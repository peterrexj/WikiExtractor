					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the brother of Saint Fursey, see <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ultan" title="Saint Ultan">Saint Ultan</a>.</div>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Ultan of Ardbraccan</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 657<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Pre-Congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">4 September</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data">children, paediatricians</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>St. Ultan of Ardbraccan</b>, also known as Ultan the scribe was an Irish saint and Abbot-Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Ardbraccan" title="Ardbraccan">Ardbraccan</a> during the 7th century. He died c. 657<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 4 September.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ultan_of_Ardbraccan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition has said he was an uncle of <a href="/wiki/Brigid_of_Kildare" title="Brigid of Kildare">St Brigit of Kildare</a>; however, this is not chronologically possible.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He collected a life of her for his pupil, St. <a href="/wiki/Brocc%C3%A1n_Cl%C3%B3en" title="Broccán Clóen">Broccán Clóen</a> of Rostuirc, in <a href="/wiki/Ossory" class="mw-redirect" title="Ossory">Ossory</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Irish_Annals" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Annals">Irish Annals</a> describe St. Ultan as of the <a href="/wiki/List_of_kings_of_Connacht#High_medieval_era" title="List of kings of Connacht">royal race of O'Connor</a>. Ultan was a disciple and kinsman of St. Declan, who made him bishop of Ardbraccan.<sup id="cite_ref-Book_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Book-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He succeeded St. Breccan as Abbot-<a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Meath#History" title="Bishop of Meath">Bishop of Ardbraccan</a> about the year 570.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ultan founded a school, educating and feeding its poor students, and was noted for his work in collecting the writings of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Brigid" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Brigid">Saint Brigid</a> and <a href="/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript" title="Illuminated manuscript">illuminating</a> them. One of his students was <a href="/wiki/T%C3%ADrech%C3%A1n" title="Tírechán">Tírechán</a>. He was also known for his beautiful <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymns</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> hymn, commencing "Christus in nostra insula", is incorporated in the Solesmes Chant books.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <i><a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9lire_%C3%93engusso" class="mw-redirect" title="Félire Óengusso">Félire Óengusso</a></i>, he is mentioned as "the great sinless prince in whom the little ones are flourishing: the children play greatly round Ultan of Ardbraccan." The annotation explains that the Yellow Plague attacked adults more than children and described the piteous scenes of human suffering witnessed during its continuance. Everywhere through the country numbers of little children, whose mothers and fathers had been carried off, were left helpless and starving. Ultan collected all the orphan babes he could find, and brought them to his <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. In one of the accounts, we are told that he often had as many as 150.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have invented a method of feeding his young charges by "procuring a number of cows' teats, which he filled with milk".<sup id="cite_ref-Book_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Book-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Having preached the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> in Ardbraccan, he went to the <a href="/wiki/Aran_Islands" title="Aran Islands">Aran Islands</a> after a short stay in <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-navan_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-navan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Ultan ended his days on one of the Aran Islands, where his tomb slab was discovered.<sup id="cite_ref-ellison_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellison-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He died on 4 September.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Annals of Clonmacnoise">Annals of Clonmacnoise</a> placed St. Ultan's death in the year 653.<sup id="cite_ref-navan_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-navan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He may also have been a bishop of the Desi of Meath.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Much mention of him is made in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a> of <a href="/wiki/Aengus" title="Aengus">Aengus</a>. He is also connected with <a href="/wiki/Killanny" title="Killanny">Killanny</a> and <a href="/wiki/Louth,_County_Louth" title="Louth, County Louth">Louth</a> both in County Louth.
</p><p>Ultan's Holy Well was originally within the <a href="/wiki/Celts" title="Celts">Celtic</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a>, and later within the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Normans" title="Anglo-Normans">Anglo Norman</a> bishop's grounds.<sup id="cite_ref-navan_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-navan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ultan_of_Ardbraccan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is now regarded as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_Saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron Saint">Patron Saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Paediatrician" class="mw-redirect" title="Paediatrician">paediatricians</a>, a well known children's hospital and a special school in Navan being named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-ellison_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellison-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ultan_of_Ardbraccan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Church_Building" title="Church Building">church</a> at Upper <a href="/wiki/Killinkere" title="Killinkere">Killinkere</a> takes its name from St. Ultan, a well-known patron of children, whose <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> was established at <a href="/wiki/Ardbraccan" title="Ardbraccan">Ardbraccan</a> between <a href="/wiki/Kells,_County_Meath" title="Kells, County Meath">Kells</a> and <a href="/wiki/Navan" title="Navan">Navan</a> in the 6th century. He is reputed to have travelled to Killinkere and founded the first Christian church in the area.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The establishment of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ultan%27s_Children%27s_Hospital" title="Saint Ultan&#39;s Children&#39;s Hospital">Saint Ultan's Children's Hospital</a> was the result of the activity of a group of female doctors and activists, including <a href="/wiki/Madeleine_ffrench-Mullen" title="Madeleine ffrench-Mullen">Madeleine ffrench-Mullen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kathleen_Lynn" title="Kathleen Lynn">Kathleen Lynn</a>, who were deeply concerned at the high level of infant mortality in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, and the rise of infant syphilis in the wake of the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a>. The hospital opened at 37 Charlemont Street on Ascension Thursday, 29 May 1919. The name of the hospital came from the seventh century Saint Ultan of Ardbraccan, bishop of Meath, who had looked after the children of Meath during an outbreak of <i>yellow plague</i>. St. Ultan's closed in 1984 and merged with the <a href="/wiki/National_Children%27s_Hospital" title="National Children&#39;s Hospital">National Children's Hospital</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
