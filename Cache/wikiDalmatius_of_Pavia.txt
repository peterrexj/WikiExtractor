					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Dalmatius of Pavia</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">possibly San Damiano Macra, Italy</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">254 or 304 AD</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">5 December</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Borgo_San_Dalmazzo" title="Borgo San Dalmazzo">Borgo San Dalmazzo</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Dalmatius of Pavia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Dalmazzo, Dalmazio</i>) (died 254 or 304 AD) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.  It is possible that Dalmatius was simply a local preacher of northern Italy, but the century in which he lived or the manner in which he died is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-sandalmazio_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sandalmazio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was venerated at what was called <i>Pedona</i> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Borgo_San_Dalmazzo" title="Borgo San Dalmazzo">Borgo San Dalmazzo</a>).  His biography was composed in the 7th or 8th centuries, its author perhaps a <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombard</a> monk of the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of Pedona who was drawing from oral tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-sandalmazio_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sandalmazio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His biography states that Dalmatius was born at <i>Forum Germarzorum</i> (present-day <a href="/wiki/San_Damiano_Macra" title="San Damiano Macra">San Damiano Macra</a>) and became a churchman and evangelizer in Pedona.<sup id="cite_ref-sandalmazio_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sandalmazio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  In the 10th century, when the area of Pedona was devastated during <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> raids, Dalmatius’ <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were carried to <a href="/wiki/Quargnento" title="Quargnento">Quargnento</a>, where an inscription on his tomb read: <i>[H]ic requiescit corpus sancti Dalmatii repositum ab Audace episcopo Astensi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-sandalmazio_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sandalmazio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In France, a tradition dating from the 9th century held that he died a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.  Later legends state that he evangelized many cities of <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>, <a href="/wiki/Emilia_(region_of_Italy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Emilia (region of Italy)">Emilia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, and was killed for his faith in 254 or 304 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-patronsaints_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patronsaints-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, based on erroneous episcopal lists, considers Dalmatius a bishop of Pavia and lists his feast day as December 5.<sup id="cite_ref-patronsaints_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patronsaints-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
