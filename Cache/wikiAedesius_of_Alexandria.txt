<p><b>Saint Aedesius of Alexandria</b> (also <b>Edese</b> or <b>Edesius</b><sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) (died 306) was an <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" title="Early Christianity">early Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> under <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Galerius Maximianus</a>. He was the brother of <a href="/wiki/Aphian" title="Aphian">Saint Aphian</a> (or Amphianus).<sup id="cite_ref-patron_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrology</a>, he publicly rebuked a judge who had been forcing <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">Christian virgins</a> to work in brothels in order to break them of their faith, so he was <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> and <a href="/wiki/Drowning" title="Drowning">drowned</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_martyrdom">Life and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aedesius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Western_tradition">Western tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aedesius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Western tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>At Alexandria, in the time of Emperor Maximian Galerius, the martyr St. Aedesius, brother of the blessed Apphian.  Because he publicly reproved the wicked judge who delivered to corruptors virgins consecrated to God, he was arrested by the soldiers, exposed to the most severe torments, and thrown into the sea for the sake of Christ our Lord.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">The Roman Martyrology</a><sup id="cite_ref-martyrology_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrology-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>The historian <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius of Caesarea</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> elaborates Aedesius' story: like his brother, he was a <a href="/wiki/Philosopher" class="mw-redirect" title="Philosopher">philosopher</a> that converted to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Perhaps because of his standing among the educated, he seems to have thought little of professing his faith before magistrates, for which he was imprisoned several times and was sentenced to work in the mines of <a href="/wiki/History_of_Palestine#Roman_Period_63_BCE–330_CE" title="History of Palestine">Palestine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He sought solitude in Egypt after his release, but found the persecution there was harsher under <a href="/wiki/Hierocles_(proconsul)" class="mw-redirect" title="Hierocles (proconsul)">Hierocles</a>. Aedesius was offended by the enslavement of consecrated virgins (who were forced to work in brothels), and so presented himself before the governor, whereupon he was seized by soldiers, tortured, and drowned.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The saint's <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acta" class="extiw" title="wikt:acta">acta</a></i> are preserved in a <a href="/wiki/Chaldea" title="Chaldea">Chaldaic</a> text. This story is probably confused,<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and perhaps <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/conflate" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:conflate">conflated</a> with that of the contemporary <a href="/wiki/Neoplatonism" title="Neoplatonism">Neoplatonist</a> philosopher, <a href="/wiki/Aedesius" title="Aedesius">Aedesius</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Eastern_tradition">Eastern tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aedesius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Eastern tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The account of the Eastern Church says Aedesius and his brother were born in <a href="/wiki/Patara_(Lycia)" title="Patara (Lycia)">Patara</a> of high-standing pagan parents.<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The brothers converted while studying in <a href="/wiki/Beirut" title="Beirut">Beirut</a>, secretly fleeing to <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Caesarea</a> to be taught by a priest named Pamphylus.<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is reported that Amphianus  gave himself up to martyrdom, having "a twenty-year-old body but the understanding and greatness of soul of a <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/centenarian" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:centenarian">centenarian</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Having tried to stop the pagan governor of the area from sacrificing to idols, he was tortured; his legs were wrapped in cotton and burned, and they threw him into the sea with a stone around his neck. Aedesius was punished by being sent to a copper mine in Palestine, and then to Egypt. In Alexandria, he spoke out against Hierocles, who had been forcing Christian "nuns, virgins and pious women" to work alongside prostitutes in brothels.<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The account says Aedesius struck the prince, for which he was tortured and drowned in the sea like his brother.<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aedesius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Aedesius' <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 8 April in the Roman Catholic Church. In Eastern Orthodox Churches, his feast is 2 April.<sup id="cite_ref-serbian_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serbian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In art, Aedesius is shown shipwrecked with his brother;<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the mention of a depiction that has his legs wrapped in oiled linen before he is burned to death is probably a reflection of the Eastern story of his brother's martyrdom.
</p>

Finally in the city of Alexandria, when he beheld the judge, who was trying the Christians, offending beyond all bounds, now insulting holy men in various ways, and again consigning women of greatest modesty and even religious virgins to procurers for shameful treatment, he acted like his brother. For as these things seemed insufferable, he went forward with bold resolve, and with his words and deeds overwhelmed the judge with shame and disgrace.
