<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Thibiuca" title="Felix of Thibiuca">St Felix</a>, the bishop of Thibiuca who was martyred in 303 along with a man named Audactus.</div>
<p><b>Felix</b> and <b>Adauctus</b> (<abbr title="died">d.</abbr>&#160;303) were according to tradition, <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyrs</a> who were said to have suffered during the <a href="/wiki/Great_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Persecution">Great Persecution</a> during the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_Roman_emperors" title="List of Roman emperors">emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>.
</p><p>The <i>Acts</i>, first published in <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado's</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a></i>, relate as follows: <b>Felix</b>, a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> priest, and brother of another priest, also named Felix, being ordered to offer sacrifice to the gods, was brought by the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> Dracus to the temples of <a href="/wiki/Serapis" title="Serapis">Serapis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mercury_(mythology)" title="Mercury (mythology)">Mercury</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Diana_(mythology)" title="Diana (mythology)">Diana</a>. But at the prayer of the saint the <a href="/wiki/Idolatry" title="Idolatry">idols</a> fell shattered to the ground. He was then led to <a href="/wiki/Execution" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution">execution</a>.  On the way an unknown person joined him, professed himself a Christian, and also received the crown of <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>. The Christians gave him the name <b>Adauctus</b> (the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> word for "added").  They were both <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.
</p><p>These <i>Acts</i> are considered a legendary embellishment of a misunderstood inscription by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus</a>. A Dracus cannot be found among the prefects of Rome; the other Felix of the legend is <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Nola" title="Felix of Nola">St. Felix of Nola</a>; and Felix of Monte Pincio is the same Felix honoured on the Garden Hill. The brother is imaginary (<i>Anal. Boll., XVI, 19-29</i>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_and_Adauctus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their veneration is very old; they are commemorated in the Sacramentary of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> and in the ancient martyrologies.
</p><p>Their church in Rome, built over their graves, in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_of_Commodilla" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb of Commodilla">catacomb of Commodilla</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ostiensis" title="Via Ostiensis">Via Ostiensis</a>, near the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls</a>, and restored by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_III" title="Pope Leo III">Pope Leo III</a>, was discovered about three hundred years ago and again unearthed in 1905 (<i>Civiltà Catt., 1905, II, 608</i>). <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a>, about 850, is said to have given their relics to Irmengard, wife of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a>; she placed them in the abbey of canonesses at Eschau in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>. 
</p><p>They were brought to the <a href="/wiki/Stephansdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephansdom">church of St. Stephen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> in 1361. The heads are claimed by <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Anjou" title="Duchy of Anjou">Anjou</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>. According to the "Chronicle of Andechs" (Donauwörth, 666877, p.&#160;69), Henry, the last count, received the relics from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> and brought them to the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Andechs" title="Andechs">Andechs</a>.
</p><p>The painter <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Innocenzo_Carlone" class="mw-redirect" title="Carlo Innocenzo Carlone">Carlo Innocenzo Carlone</a> (1686–1775) painted <i>The Glorification of Saints Felix and Adauctus</i> (1759–61), seen above. It was commissioned for the <a href="/wiki/Cupola" title="Cupola">cupola</a> of the church of San Felice del Benaco on <a href="/wiki/Lake_Garda" title="Lake Garda">Lake Garda</a>.
</p>
