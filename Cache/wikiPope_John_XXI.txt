<p><b>Pope John XXI</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes XXI</i>; <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1215</span>&#160;– 20 May 1277), born <b>Pedro Julião</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Petrus Iulianus</i>), was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 8 September 1276 to his death on 20 May 1277. Apart from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Damasus&#160;I</a> (from <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Lusitania" title="Lusitania">Lusitania</a>), he has been the only <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> pope.<sup id="cite_ref-McBrien222_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McBrien222-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is sometimes identified with the <a href="/wiki/Logic" title="Logic">logician</a> and herbalist <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Spain_(author)" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter of Spain (author)">Peter of Spain</a> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Petrus Hispanus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Pedro Hispano</i>), which would make him the only pope to have been a physician.<sup id="cite_ref-McBrien222_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McBrien222-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XXI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pedro Julião was born in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> between 1210 and 1220 to Julião Pais Rebolo, a <a href="/wiki/Physician" title="Physician">physician</a>, and his wife Mor Mendes. He started his studies at the episcopal school of <a href="/wiki/Lisbon_Cathedral" title="Lisbon Cathedral">Lisbon Cathedral</a> and later joined the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>, although some historians claim that he was educated at <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>. Wherever he studied, he concentrated on <a href="/wiki/Medicine" title="Medicine">medicine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>, <a href="/wiki/Logic" title="Logic">logic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Physics" title="Physics">physics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Metaphysics" title="Metaphysics">metaphysics</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Aristotle" title="Aristotle">Aristotle</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Dialectic" title="Dialectic">dialectic</a>. He is traditionally and usually identified with the medical author <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Spain" title="Peter of Spain">Peter of Spain</a>, an important figure in the development of logic and <a href="/wiki/Pharmacology" title="Pharmacology">pharmacology</a>. Peter of Spain taught at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Siena" title="University of Siena">University of Siena</a> in the 1240s and his <i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Summulae Logicales</i></span></i> was used as a university textbook on <a href="/wiki/Aristotelian_logic" class="mw-redirect" title="Aristotelian logic">Aristotelian logic</a> for the next three centuries. At the court in Lisbon, he was the councilor and spokesman for <a href="/wiki/Afonso_III_of_Portugal" title="Afonso III of Portugal">King Afonso III</a> in church matters. Later, he became <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of Guimarães.
</p><p>He was Archdeacon of Vermoim (Vermuy) in the Archdiocese of Braga.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He tried to become <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Lisbon" title="Patriarch of Lisbon">bishop of Lisbon</a> but was defeated. Instead, he became the Master of the school of Lisbon. Peter became the physician of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a> (1271&#8211;1276) early in his reign. In March 1273, he was elected Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a>, but did not assume that post; instead, on 3 June 1273, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a> created him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Bishop_of_Frascati" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Bishop of Frascati">Cardinal Bishop of Tusculum (Frascati)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XXI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi,_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi,_01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg/220px-Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg/330px-Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg/440px-Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi%2C_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi%2C_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2432" data-file-height="3648" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Luogo_del_primo_sepolcro_di_vari_papi,_con_resti_del_monumento_di_giovanni_xxi,_01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Pope John XXI in <a href="/wiki/Viterbo_Cathedral" title="Viterbo Cathedral">Viterbo Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>After the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_V" title="Pope Adrian V">Pope Adrian V</a> on 18 August 1276, Peter was <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_September_1276" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, September 1276">elected pope</a> on 8 September.<sup id="cite_ref-McBrien222_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McBrien222-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was crowned a week later on 20 September. One of John XXI's few acts during his brief reign was the reversal of a decree recently passed at the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyon" title="Second Council of Lyon">Second Council of Lyon</a> (1274); the decree had not only confined cardinals in solitude until they elected a successor pope, but also progressively restricted their supplies of food and wine if their deliberations took too long. Though much of John XXI's brief papacy was dominated by the powerful Cardinal Giovanni Gaetano Orsini, who succeeded him as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_III" title="Pope Nicholas III">Pope Nicholas III</a>, John attempted to launch a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, pushed for a union with the Eastern church, and did what he could to maintain peace between the Christian nations. He also launched a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">mission</a> to convert the <a href="/wiki/Tatars" title="Tatars">Tatars</a>, but he died before it could start.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To secure the necessary quiet for his medical studies, he had an apartment added to the papal palace at <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, to which he could retire when he wished to work undisturbed. On 14 May 1277, while the pope was alone in this apartment, the ceiling collapsed; John was rescued alive from beneath the rubble; however, he died of his serious injuries on 20 May, possibly an early recorded case of <a href="/wiki/Crush_syndrome" title="Crush syndrome">crush syndrome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DeSanto2021_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DeSanto2021-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_di_Viterbo" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo di Viterbo">Duomo di Viterbo</a>, where his tomb can still be seen. The original <a href="/wiki/Porphyry_(geology)" title="Porphyry (geology)">porphyry</a> sarcophagus was destroyed during the cathedral's 16th-century refurbishment, and was replaced with a more modest one in stone with the pope's effigy. In the 19th century, the <a href="/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Carlos_de_Saldanha_Oliveira_e_Daun,_1st_Duke_of_Saldanha" title="João Carlos de Saldanha Oliveira e Daun, 1st Duke of Saldanha">Duke of Saldanha</a>, as Portuguese Ambassador to the Holy See, had the pope's remains transferred to a new sarcophagus sculpted by <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Gnaccarini" class="extiw" title="it:Filippo Gnaccarini">Filippo Gnaccarini</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DeSanto2021_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DeSanto2021-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 2000, the <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> City Council, led by Mayor <a href="/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Soares_(politician)" title="João Soares (politician)">João Soares</a>, successfully had a new funeral monument built in <a href="/wiki/Lioz" title="Lioz">lioz</a> stone, topped by the original stone effigy of the pope, placed in a more condign location in the <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XXI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his death, it was rumored that John XXI had actually been a <a href="/wiki/Necromancer" class="mw-redirect" title="Necromancer">necromancer</a> (see also <a href="/wiki/Communion_of_saints" title="Communion of saints">Communion of the Saints</a>), a suspicion frequently directed towards the few scholars among medieval popes (see, e.g., <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Sylvester II</a>). It was also said that his death had been an <a href="/wiki/Act_of_God" title="Act of God">act of God</a>, stopping him from completing a <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical treatise</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Since the works of "Peter of Spain" continued to be studied and appreciated, however, <a href="/wiki/Dante_Alighieri" title="Dante Alighieri">Dante Alighieri</a> placed "Pietro Spano" in his <i><a href="/wiki/Paradiso_(Dante)" title="Paradiso (Dante)">Paradiso's</a></i> <a href="/wiki/Paradiso_(Dante)#Fourth_Sphere_(The_Sun:_The_Wise)" title="Paradiso (Dante)">Sphere of the Sun</a> with the spirits of other great religious scholars.
</p>
