					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Christian monk and saint in Egypt</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Abba</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Pitirim of Porphyry</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Abba Pitirim of Mount Porphyry, Piteroum Savants, Pityrun, Pithyrion, Pityrune</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. fourth century<br /><a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Residence</th><td class="infobox-data">Mount Porphyry, the <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Thebaid</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. fourth or fifth century<br />Egypt</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">November 29</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Influences</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Influenced</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Saint_Isidora" title="Saint Isidora">Isidora</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Abba Pitirim of Porphyry</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πιτυροῦν</span>) was an <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Egyptian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism_before_451" title="Christian monasticism before 451">Christian monastic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the fourth century, and a disciple of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is November 29 in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pitirim was an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of many monks, was the third successor of Anthony the Great in his <a href="/wiki/Monastic_cell" title="Monastic cell">hermitage</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was said to have received Anthony's virtues.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He lived on Mount Porphyry (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πορφυρίτη</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Porphyriti</i></span>) in the <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Thebaid</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> which was probably named after its proximity to the Roman <a href="/wiki/Quarry" title="Quarry">quarry</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mons_Porphyrites" title="Mons Porphyrites">Mons Porphyrites</a> that mined a rare stone by the same name.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pitirim continued the work of Anthony by instructing Christian monks along the <a href="/wiki/Nile" title="Nile">Nile</a> in the Thebaid, and lived with his followers in austere <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It was said of Pitirim that he only ate water mixed with a little bit of flour twice a week, and that sometimes he would fast from food entirely.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pitirim died in the late fourth or early fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Meeting_with_Isidora">Meeting with Isidora</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Meeting with Isidora">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Saint_Isidora" title="Saint Isidora">Saint Isidora</a></div><p>
A story of Pitirim is related in chapter 34 of <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius</a>' <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://ia802905.us.archive.org/20/items/in.ernet.dli.2015.152307/2015.152307.The-Lausiac-History-Of-Palladius_text.pdf">Lausaic History</a></i> (written 419-420).<sup id="cite_ref-:3_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> According to Palladius, Pitirim one day received a vision from an angel while praying in the mountains.</p><blockquote><p>Now an angel appeared to the holy Pitirim, an anchorite of high reputation who dwelt in Porphyrites, and said to him: "Why are you proud of yourself for being religious and dwelling in a place like this? Do you want to see a woman who is more religious than you?"<sup id="cite_ref-:3_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>The angel told him to visit the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Tabennisi" class="mw-redirect" title="Tabennisi">Tabennisi</a> (founded by <a href="/wiki/Pachomius_the_Great" title="Pachomius the Great">Pachomius the Great</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>), and there to find a woman who was more holy than himself. Pitirim did so and met <a href="/wiki/Saint_Isidora" title="Saint Isidora">Isidora</a>, a woman of extreme humility among her fellow nuns. Pitirim admonished the other nuns for treating Isidora unkindly.</p><blockquote><p>They were all amazed and said to him: "Father, do not let her insult you, she is dumb." Said Pitirim to them all: "<i>You</i> are dumb. For she is am Amma both of me and you."<sup id="cite_ref-:3_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Verses">Verses</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Verses">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Troparion_on_Tone_8.5B10.5D"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Troparion_on_Tone_8[10]">Troparion on Tone 8<sup id="cite_ref-:4_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Troparion on Tone 8[10]">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>In thee, O Father, was preserved unimpaired that which is according to God's image; for thou didst take up the Cross and follow <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>. By thine actions thou hast taught us to despise the flesh for it passes away, but to care for the soul which is thing immortal. Wherefore thy spirit holy Pitirim rejoices with the angels.
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<h3><span id="Ode_6.5B10.5D"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Ode_6[10]">Ode 6<sup id="cite_ref-:4_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Ode 6[10]">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p><b>Irmos</b><br />
Whirled about in the abyss of sin, I appeal to the unfathomable abyss of thy compassion, from corruption raise me up, O God.<br />
When the <a href="/wiki/Lord" title="Lord">Lord</a> chastened Saint Pitirim for "traveling in the cities" mentally while living as a solitary, he was instructed to seek you, O Isidora, fool for Christ.<br />
The nuns, seeing only foolishness, did not discern your unceasing prayer to Christ God whom you loved with your heart, soul, mind and strength. Focusing on the rags upon your head, they missed your resplendent crown of love.
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hymn_of_Praise">Hymn of Praise</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Hymn of Praise">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>From the <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://archive.org/details/ThePrologueFromOhrid_BishopNikolaiVelimirovich/page/n273/mode/2up?q=pitirim">Prologue of Ohrid</a></i> by Saint <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">Nikolaj Velimirović</a>.
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>In the desert, the ascetic Pitirim<br />
Prays to God and asks himself:<br />
In the world, is there anyone equal to me?<br />
Then appeared, an angel of God,<br />
Gently reproached Pitirim<br />
In thoughts, you magnify yourself O Elder<br />
As though, in the world, there is no better!<br />
Come, follow me, O Elder Pitirim,<br />
Come, follow me to see the aged woman,<br />
Isidora makes herself a <a href="/wiki/Fool_for_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Fool for Christ">fool for Christ</a>,<br />
To see her and then to be amazed:<br />
She, from God her heart does not separate,<br />
All her thoughts, to God, she binds,<br />
And not like you, who, in the body, are here,<br />
And thoughts, in the ends of the world!<br />
And all of her mortifications that you see<br />
That by shame, of the woman to be ashamed!<br />
And the wisdom of God that you glorify<br />
Which, in the weeds, nurses the roses!
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Teachings">Teachings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pitirim_of_Porphyry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Teachings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Pitirim taught that to each person's <a href="/wiki/Seven_deadly_sins" title="Seven deadly sins">passions</a> there are corresponding <a href="/wiki/Demon" title="Demon">demons</a> which incite that passion in the person through different <a href="/wiki/Temptation" title="Temptation">temptations</a>. To escape from these demons and from evil thoughts, one must first free the heart from passions.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Monks often told Pitirim of visions of demons appearing to them. He would say, "I am most afraid of demons, which nest <a href="/wiki/Pride" title="Pride">pride</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greed" title="Greed">avarice</a>, sensuality and other similar passions. These are the most dangerous demons and great care must be taken towards them."<sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
