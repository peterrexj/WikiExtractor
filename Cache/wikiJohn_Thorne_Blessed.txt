<p><b>Richard Whiting</b> (1461 – 15 November 1539) was an English Catholic priest and the last <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Glastonbury" title="Abbot of Glastonbury">Abbot of Glastonbury</a>.
</p><p>Whiting presided over <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a> at the time of the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a> (1536–1541) under King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a>. The king had him <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> after his conviction for treason for remaining loyal to Rome. He is considered a martyr by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, which <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> him on 13 May 1895.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Whiting_(abbot)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Richard Whiting was born near <a href="/wiki/Wrington" title="Wrington">Wrington</a>. He was educated at Glastonbury Abbey, and then at the <a href="/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge" title="Magdalene College, Cambridge">Monk's Hostel</a> at Cambridge,<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> graduating with an <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin)" title="Master of Arts (Oxford, Cambridge, and Dublin)">MA</a> in 1483.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Whiting_(abbot)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:263px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Glastonburyabbey.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/261px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="261" height="179" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/392px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/522px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="581" data-file-height="399" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Glastonburyabbey.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>View of Glastonbury Abbey from the former location of the North <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a> in East direction to the <a href="/wiki/Choir_(architecture)" title="Choir (architecture)">choir</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Whiting was ordained deacon in 1500 and priest in 1501.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He returned to Cambridge in 1505 to take his doctor's degree. He served as <i><a href="/wiki/Camerarius" title="Camerarius">camerarius</a></i> at Glastonbury in charge of managing the dormitory, lavatory, and wardrobe of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of the Abbot of Glastonbury, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Beere" title="Richard Beere">Richard Beere</a>, in February 1525, the community elected his successor <i>per formam compromissi</i>, which elevates the selection to a higher ranking personage&#160;– in this case Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Wolsey" title="Thomas Wolsey">Thomas Wolsey</a>. Wolsey obtained <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">King Henry's</a> permission to act and chose Richard Whiting. The first ten years of Whiting's rule were prosperous and peaceful.<sup id="cite_ref-body_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-body-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was a sober and caring spiritual leader and a good manager of the abbey's day-to-day life.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Contemporary accounts show that Whiting was held in very high esteem.
</p><p>The abbey over which Whiting presided was one of the richest and most influential in England. About one hundred monks lived in the enclosed monastery, where the sons of the nobility and gentry were educated before going on to university.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As Abbot of Glastonbury, Whiting was a peer of the realm and administrator of vast estates.
</p><p>Whiting signed his assent to the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Supremacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Supremacy">Act of Supremacy</a> when it was first presented to him and his monks in 1534. Henry sent <a href="/wiki/Richard_Layton" title="Richard Layton">Richard Layton</a> to examine Whiting and the other inhabitants of the abbey. He found all in good order, but suspended the abbot's jurisdiction over the town of <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury" title="Glastonbury">Glastonbury</a>. Small "injunctions" were given to him about the management of the abbey property. A number of times over the years which followed, Whiting was told the abbey was safe from dissolution.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, the <a href="/wiki/Suppression_of_Religious_Houses_Act_1535" title="Suppression of Religious Houses Act 1535">1535 Suppression of Religious Houses Act</a> brought about the dissolution of the lesser monasteries and provided a warning of what the future might hold.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Whiting_(abbot)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By January 1539, Glastonbury was the only monastery left in <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a>. Abbot Whiting refused to surrender the abbey, which did not fall under the Act for the suppression of the lesser houses.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 19 September of that year the royal commissioners, Layton, Richard Pollard and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Moyle" title="Thomas Moyle">Thomas Moyle</a>, arrived there without warning on the orders of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a>, presumably to find faults and thus facilitate the abbey's closure. The commissioners had expected to find considerable treasure at Glastonbury and finding comparatively little, they proceeded to a more thorough search, finding money and plate walled up in secure vaults. There also was heard rumour of other items relocated to other estates and manors belonging to the Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Whiting, by now feeble and advanced in years, was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> so that Cromwell might examine him himself. The precise charge on which he was arrested, and subsequently executed, remains uncertain, though his case is usually referred to as one of treason. Cromwell clearly acted as judge and jury: in his manuscript <i>Remembrances</i> are the entries:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Item, Certayn persons to be sent to the Tower for the further examenacyon of the Abbot, of Glaston... Item. The Abbot, of Glaston to (be) tryed at Glaston and also executyd there with his complycys... Item. Councillors to give evidence against the Abbot of Glaston, Rich. Pollard, Lewis Forstew (Forstell), Thos. Moyle.</p></blockquote>
<p>Marillac, the French Ambassador, on 25 October wrote: "The Abbot of Glastonbury. . . has lately, been put in the Tower, because, in taking the Abbey treasures, valued at 200,000 crowns, they found a written book of arguments in behalf of <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon" title="Catherine of Aragon">queen Katherine</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a member of the House of Lords, Whiting should have been <a href="/wiki/Attainder" title="Attainder">attainted</a> (condemned) by an <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Parliament" title="Act of Parliament">Act of Parliament</a> passed for that purpose, but his execution was an accomplished fact before Parliament met. Whiting was sent back to Glastonbury with Pollard, and reached <a href="/wiki/Wells,_Somerset" title="Wells, Somerset">Wells</a> on 14 November. There a trial apparently took place, and he was convicted of "robbing Glastonbury church". The next day, Saturday, 15 November, he was taken to Glastonbury with two of his monks, treasurer of the church John Thorne and sacristan Roger James, where all three were fastened upon hurdles and dragged by horses to the top of <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Tor" title="Glastonbury Tor">Glastonbury Tor</a> which overlooks the town. Here they were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>, with Whiting's head being fastened over the west gate<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> of the now deserted abbey and his limbs exposed at Wells, <a href="/wiki/Bath,_Somerset" title="Bath, Somerset">Bath</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ilchester" title="Ilchester">Ilchester</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bridgwater" title="Bridgwater">Bridgwater</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-body_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-body-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Whiting_(abbot)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Whiting was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the Pope Leo XIII on 13 May 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Whiting was the subject of a novel, <i>The Last Abbot of Glastonbury</i> (1883), by <a href="/wiki/Augustine_David_Crake" title="Augustine David Crake">Augustine David Crake</a>.</li>
<li>When <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Bligh_Bond" title="Frederick Bligh Bond">Frederick Bligh Bond</a> (1864–1945) excavated Glastonbury Abbey, he removed bones he believed belonged to Richard Whiting.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
