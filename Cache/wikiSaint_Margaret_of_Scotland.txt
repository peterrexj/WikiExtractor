<p><b>Saint Margaret of Scotland</b> (<a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Gaelic language">Scottish Gaelic</a>: <i lang="gd">Naomh Maighréad</i>; <a href="/wiki/Scots_language" title="Scots language">Scots</a>: <i lang="sco">Saunt Marget</i>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1045 – 16 November 1093</span>), also known as <b>Margaret of Wessex</b>, was an English princess and a <a href="/wiki/Queen_consort_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen consort of Scotland">Scottish queen</a>. Margaret was sometimes called "The Pearl of Scotland".<sup id="cite_ref-selden_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-selden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Born in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> to the expatriate English prince <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Exile" title="Edward the Exile">Edward the Exile</a>, Margaret and her family returned to <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">England</a> in 1057. Following the death of King Harold II at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Hastings" title="Battle of Hastings">Battle of Hastings</a> in 1066, her brother <a href="/wiki/Edgar_%C3%86theling" title="Edgar Ætheling">Edgar Ætheling</a> was elected as <a href="/wiki/King_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="King of England">King of England</a> but never crowned. After she and her family fled north, Margaret married <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_III_of_Scotland" title="Malcolm III of Scotland">Malcolm III of Scotland</a> by the end of 1070.
</p><p>Margaret was a very pious <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a>, and among many charitable works she established a ferry across the <a href="/wiki/Firth_of_Forth" title="Firth of Forth">Firth of Forth</a> in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> for pilgrims travelling to <a href="/wiki/St_Andrews" title="St Andrews">St Andrews</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>, which gave the towns of <a href="/wiki/South_Queensferry" title="South Queensferry">South Queensferry</a> and <a href="/wiki/North_Queensferry" title="North Queensferry">North Queensferry</a> their names. Margaret was the mother of three kings of Scotland, or four, if <a href="/wiki/Edmund_of_Scotland" title="Edmund of Scotland">Edmund of Scotland</a> (who ruled with his uncle, <a href="/wiki/Donald_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Donald III">Donald III</a>) is counted, and of a queen consort of England. According to the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Vita S. Margaritae (Scotorum) Reginae</i></span> (<i>Life of St. Margaret, Queen (of the Scots)</i>), attributed to <a href="/wiki/Turgot_of_Durham" title="Turgot of Durham">Turgot of Durham</a>, Margaret died at <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle" title="Edinburgh Castle">Edinburgh Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh" title="Edinburgh">Edinburgh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> in 1093, merely days after receiving the news of her husband's death in battle.
</p><p>In 1250, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> her, and her remains were reinterred in a shrine in <a href="/wiki/Dunfermline_Abbey" title="Dunfermline Abbey">Dunfermline Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. Her relics were dispersed after the <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Reformation" title="Scottish Reformation">Scottish Reformation</a> and subsequently lost. <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a>, at one time owned her head, which was subsequently preserved by <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Scots_College,_Douai" title="Scots College, Douai">Scots College, Douai</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, from where it was lost during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret was the daughter of the English prince Edward the Exile and his wife Agatha, and also the granddaughter of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Ironside" title="Edmund Ironside">Edmund Ironside</a>, King of England.<sup id="cite_ref-selden_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-selden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After the death of Ironside in 1016, <a href="/wiki/Canute" class="mw-redirect" title="Canute">Canute</a> sent the infant Edward and his brother to the court of the Swedish king, <a href="/wiki/Olof_Sk%C3%B6tkonung" title="Olof Skötkonung">Olof Skötkonung</a>, and they eventually made their way to <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a>. As an adult, he travelled to Hungary, where in 1046 he supported the successful bid of <a href="/wiki/Andrew_I_of_Hungary" title="Andrew I of Hungary">King Andrew I</a> for the Hungarian crown. The provenance of Margaret's mother, <a href="/wiki/Agatha,_wife_of_Edward_the_Exile" class="mw-redirect" title="Agatha, wife of Edward the Exile">Agatha</a>, is disputed, but Margaret was born in Hungary about 1045. Her brother <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_%C3%86theling" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Ætheling">Edgar the Ætheling</a> and sister <a href="/wiki/Cristina,_daughter_of_Edward_the_Exile" class="mw-redirect" title="Cristina, daughter of Edward the Exile">Cristina</a> were also born in Hungary around this time. Margaret grew up in a very religious environment in the Hungarian court.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_England">Return to England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Return to England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret came to England with the rest of her family when her father, <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Exile" title="Edward the Exile">Edward the Exile</a>, was recalled in 1057 as a possible successor to her great-uncle, the childless <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor">King Edward the Confessor</a>. Whether from natural or sinister causes, her father died immediately after landing, and Margaret, still a child, continued to reside at the English court where her brother, <a href="/wiki/Edgar_%C3%86theling" title="Edgar Ætheling">Edgar Ætheling</a>, was considered a possible successor to the English throne.<sup id="cite_ref-selden_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-selden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When Edward the Confessor died in January 1066, <a href="/wiki/Harold_Godwinson" title="Harold Godwinson">Harold Godwinson</a> was selected as king, possibly because Edgar was considered too young. After Harold's defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Hastings" title="Battle of Hastings">Battle of Hastings</a> later that year, Edgar was proclaimed <a href="/wiki/King_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="King of England">King of England</a>, but when the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a> advanced on <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Witenagemot" class="mw-redirect" title="Witenagemot">Witenagemot</a> presented Edgar to <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a>, who took him to <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> before returning him to England in 1068, when Edgar, Margaret, Cristina, and their mother Agatha fled north to <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Journey_to_Scotland">Journey to Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Journey to Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, the widowed Agatha decided to leave <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> with her children and return to the continent. However, a storm drove their ship north to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Scotland" title="Kingdom of Scotland">Kingdom of Scotland</a>, where they were shipwrecked in 1068. There they were given refuge by <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_III_of_Scotland" title="Malcolm III of Scotland">King Malcolm III</a>.  The locus where it is believed that they landed is known today as St Margaret's Hope. Margaret's arrival in Scotland, after the failed revolt of the Northumbrian earls, has been heavily romanticised, though <a href="/wiki/Symeon_of_Durham" title="Symeon of Durham">Symeon of Durham</a> implied that her first meeting of Malcolm III may not have been until 1070, after <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror's</a> <a href="/wiki/Harrying_of_the_North" title="Harrying of the North">Harrying of the North</a>. Other sources say they met 9 years before. According to <a href="/wiki/Orderic_Vitalis" title="Orderic Vitalis">Orderic Vitalis</a>, one of Malcolm's earliest actions as king was to travel to the court of <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor">Edward the Confessor</a> in 1059 to arrange a marriage with "Edward's kinswoman Margaret, who had arrived in England two years before from <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> If a marriage agreement was made in 1059, it was not kept, and this may explain the Scots invasion of Northumbria in 1061 when <a href="/wiki/Lindisfarne" title="Lindisfarne">Lindisfarne</a> was plundered.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Malcolm_III_of_Scotland" title="Malcolm III of Scotland">King Malcolm III</a> was a widower with two sons, Donald and Duncan, and would have been attracted to marrying one of the few remaining members of the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> royal family. The marriage of Malcolm and Margaret occurred in 1070. Subsequently, Malcolm executed several invasions of <a href="/wiki/Northumberland" title="Northumberland">Northumberland</a> to support the claim of his new brother-in-law Edgar and to increase his own power. These, however, had little effect save the devastation of the county.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarshall190755_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarshall190755-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Progeny">Progeny</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Progeny">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret and Malcolm had eight children – six sons and two daughters:
</p>
<ol><li>Edward (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1071 – 13 November 1093</span>), killed along with his father in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Alnwick_(1093)" title="Battle of Alnwick (1093)">Battle of Alnwick</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Edmund_of_Scotland" title="Edmund of Scotland">Edmund</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1071 – post-1097</span>)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Ethelred_of_Scotland" title="Ethelred of Scotland">Ethelred</a>, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Dunkeld_Cathedral" title="Dunkeld Cathedral">Dunkeld</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perth_and_Kinross" title="Perth and Kinross">Perth and Kinross</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Edgar_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar of Scotland">Edgar</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1074 – 11 January 1107</span>), king of Scotland, reigned 1097–1107</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Scotland" title="Alexander I of Scotland">Alexander I</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1078 – 23 April 1124</span>), <a href="/wiki/King_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Scotland">King of Scotland</a>, reigned 1107–24</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Edith_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Edith of Scotland">Edith</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1080 – 1 May 1118</span>), renamed Matilda, queen of England</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Scotland_(1082%E2%80%931116)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary of Scotland (1082–1116)">Mary</a> (1082–1116), countess of Boulogne</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland" title="David I of Scotland">David I</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1084 – 24 May 1153</span>), king of Scotland, reigned 1124–53</li></ol>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Piety">Piety</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Piety">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG/220px-Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="361" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG/330px-Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG/440px-Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2550" data-file-height="4181" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Malcolm_and_Margaret_at_Queensferry.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Malcolm greeting Margaret at her arrival in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>; detail of a mural by Victorian artist <a href="/wiki/William_Hole_(artist)" title="William Hole (artist)">William Hole</a></div></div></div>
<p>Margaret's biographer <a href="/wiki/Turgot_of_Durham" title="Turgot of Durham">Turgot of Durham</a>, Bishop of St. Andrew's, credits her with having a civilizing influence on her husband Malcolm by reading him narratives from the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a>. She instigated religious reform, striving to conform the worship and practices of the <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic Church">Church in Scotland</a> to those of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. This she did on the inspiration and with the guidance of <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a>, a future <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">archbishop of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-menzies_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-menzies-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She also worked to conform the practices of the <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic Church">Scottish Church</a> to those of the continental Church, which she experienced in her childhood. Due to these achievements, she was considered an exemplar of the "just ruler", and moreover influenced her husband and children, especially her youngest son, the future King <a href="/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland" title="David I of Scotland">David I of Scotland</a>, to be just and holy rulers.
</p><p>"The chroniclers all agree in depicting Queen Margaret as a strong, pure, noble character, who had very great influence over her husband, and through him over Scottish history, especially in its ecclesiastical aspects. Her religion, which was genuine and intense, was of the newest Roman style; and to her are attributed a number of reforms by which the Church [in] Scotland was considerably modified from the insular and primitive type which down to her time it had exhibited. Among those expressly mentioned are a change in the manner of observing <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a>, which thenceforward began as elsewhere on Ash Wednesday and not as previously on the following Monday, and the abolition of the old practice of observing Saturday (Sabbath), not Sunday, as the day of rest from labour (see Skene's <i>Celtic Scotland</i>, book ii chap. 8)."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The later editions of the <i>Encyclopædia Britannica</i>, however, as an example, the Eleventh Edition, remove Skene's opinion that Scottish Catholics formerly rested from work on Saturday, something for which there is no historical evidence. Skene's Celtic Scotland, vol. ii, chap. 8, pp.&#160;348–350, quotes from a contemporary document regarding Margaret's life, but his source says nothing at all of Saturday Sabbath observance, but rather says St. Margaret exhorted the Scots to cease their tendency "to neglect the due observance of the Lord's day."
</p><p>She attended to charitable works, serving orphans and the poor every day before she ate and washing the feet of the poor in imitation of Christ. She rose at midnight every night to attend the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a>. She successfully invited the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine Order</a> to establish a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Dunfermline_Abbey" title="Dunfermline Abbey">Dunfermline</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a> in 1072, and established ferries at <a href="/wiki/South_Queensferry" title="South Queensferry">Queensferry</a> and <a href="/wiki/North_Berwick" title="North Berwick">North Berwick</a> to assist pilgrims journeying from south of the <a href="/wiki/Firth_of_Forth" title="Firth of Forth">Firth of Forth</a> to St. Andrew's in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>. She used a cave on the banks of the Tower Burn in <a href="/wiki/Dunfermline" title="Dunfermline">Dunfermline</a> as a place of devotion and prayer. St. Margaret's Cave, now covered beneath a municipal car park, is open to the public.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Among other deeds, Margaret also instigated the restoration of <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> She is also known to have interceded for the release of fellow English exiles who had been forced into <a href="/wiki/Serfdom" title="Serfdom">serfdom</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dalrymple1776_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalrymple1776-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Margaret was as pious privately as she was publicly. She spent much of her time in prayer, devotional reading, and ecclesiastical embroidery. This apparently had considerable effect on the more uncouth Malcolm, who was illiterate: he so admired her piety that he had her books decorated in gold and silver. One of these, a <a href="/wiki/Pocket_gospel_book" class="mw-redirect" title="Pocket gospel book">pocket gospel book</a> with <a href="/wiki/Evangelist_portrait" title="Evangelist portrait">portraits of the Evangelists</a>, is in the <a href="/wiki/Bodleian_Library" title="Bodleian Library">Bodleian Library</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer1997_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer1997-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Malcolm was apparently largely ignorant of the long-term effects of Margaret's endeavours, not being especially religious himself. He was content for her to pursue her reforms as she desired, which was a testament to the strength of and affection in their marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her husband <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_III_of_Scotland" title="Malcolm III of Scotland">Malcolm III</a>, and their eldest son Edward, were killed in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Alnwick_(1093)" title="Battle of Alnwick (1093)">Battle of Alnwick</a> against the English on 13 November 1093. Her son <a href="/wiki/Edgar_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar of Scotland">Edgar</a> was left with the task of informing his mother of their deaths. Not yet 50 years old, Margaret died on 16 November 1093, three days after the deaths of her husband and eldest son. The cause of death was reportedly grief. She was buried before the high altar in <a href="/wiki/Dunfermline_Abbey" title="Dunfermline Abbey">Dunfermline Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. In 1250, the year of her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>, her body and that of her husband were exhumed and placed in a new shrine in the Abbey. In 1560, <a href="/wiki/Mary_Queen_of_Scots" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary Queen of Scots">Mary Queen of Scots</a> had Margaret's head removed to <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle" title="Edinburgh Castle">Edinburgh Castle</a> as a relic to assist her in childbirth. In 1597, Margaret's head ended up with the Jesuits at the <a href="/wiki/Scots_College,_Douai" title="Scots College, Douai">Scots College, Douai</a>, France, but was lost during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">King Philip of Spain</a> had the other remains of Margaret and Malcolm III transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Escorial" class="mw-redirect" title="Escorial">Escorial</a> palace in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, but their present location has not been discovered.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeene2013134_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeene2013134-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization_and_feast_day">Canonization and feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Canonization and feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:177px;"><a href="/wiki/File:StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg/175px-StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg" decoding="async" width="175" height="216" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg/263px-StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg/350px-StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="664" data-file-height="820" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:StMargareth_edinburgh_castle2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Image of Saint Margaret in a window in Edinburgh</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> canonised St. Margaret in 1250 in recognition of her personal holiness, fidelity to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, work for ecclesiastical reform, and charity. On 19 June 1250, after her canonisation, her remains were transferred to a chapel in the eastern apse of <a href="/wiki/Dunfermline_Abbey" title="Dunfermline Abbey">Dunfermline Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeene2013121_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeene2013121-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 1693 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> moved her feast day to 10 June in recognition of the birthdate of the son of <a href="/wiki/James_VII_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="James VII of Scotland">James VII of Scotland</a> and II of England.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeene2013134_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeene2013134-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1969, 16 November became free and the Church transferred her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> to 16 November, the date of her death, on which it always had been observed in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However, some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholics">traditionalist Catholics</a> continue to celebrate her feast day on 10 June.
</p><p>She is also venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>. Margaret is honored in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/November_16" title="November 16">16 November</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Institutions_bearing_her_name">Institutions bearing her name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Institutions bearing her name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Margaret_of_Scotland,_Twickenham" title="Church of St Margaret of Scotland, Twickenham">Church of St Margaret of Scotland, Twickenham</a>; <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret_the_Queen,_Buxted" title="St Margaret the Queen, Buxted">St Margaret the Queen, Buxted</a>; <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret%27s_Chapel,_Edinburgh" title="St Margaret&#39;s Chapel, Edinburgh">St Margaret's Chapel, Edinburgh</a>; and <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret_of_Scotland,_Aberdeen" title="St Margaret of Scotland, Aberdeen">St Margaret of Scotland, Aberdeen</a></div>
<p>Several churches throughout the world are dedicated in honour of St Margaret. One of the oldest is <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret%27s_Chapel,_Edinburgh" title="St Margaret&#39;s Chapel, Edinburgh">St Margaret's Chapel</a> in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh_Castle" title="Edinburgh Castle">Edinburgh Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh" title="Edinburgh">Edinburgh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, which her son <a href="/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland" title="David I of Scotland">King David I</a> founded. The Chapel was long thought to have been the oratory of Margaret herself, but is now thought to have been established in the 12th century. The oldest edifice in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh" title="Edinburgh">Edinburgh</a>, it was restored in the 19th century and refurbished in the 1990s.
</p>
