<p><b>John of Cologne</b> (Joannes van Hoornaar),  was a friar and priest of the  <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>, born in the <a href="/wiki/Electorate_of_Cologne" title="Electorate of Cologne">Electorate of Cologne</a>, part of modern Germany. He later became a <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hoornaar" title="Hoornaar">Hoornaar</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands" title="Spanish Netherlands">Spanish Netherlands</a>. He was executed for his faith in 1572 and has been declared a martyr and saint by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the period 1550-1560 the Dutch Provinces were trying to gain their independence from the Spanish empire of King Philip II.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Geuzen" title="Geuzen">Geuzen</a> (Sea Beggars) were adventurers, pirates and patriots who fought against the Spanish rule in the Dutch provinces. For several years their bases of operation included the ports of Emden (on the coast of the Dutch Province Friesland), La Rochelle (on the coast of France) and Dover (on the coast of England). The Sea Beggars attacked vessels of almost any nation as well as fishing villages and towns on the coast of the Dutch Provinces.<sup id="cite_ref-sea_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sea-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>All that is known of John's early life is that he attended the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cologne" title="University of Cologne">University of Cologne</a>, then recognized as one of the best educational institutions in Europe. He entered the Dominican Order at Cologne and received his formation there. After completing his education, John was assigned to a parish in the Netherlands village of Hoornaar, where he served for the next twenty years.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1572, <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheranism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinism</a> had spread through a great part of Europe. In the Netherlands this was followed by a struggle between the two denominations in which Calvinism was victorious. On 1 April of that year Calvinists and a group called the <a href="/wiki/Watergeuzen" class="mw-redirect" title="Watergeuzen">Watergeuzen</a> (Sea Beggars) conquered <a href="/wiki/Brielle" title="Brielle">Brielle</a> (Den Briel) and later <a href="/wiki/Vlissingen" title="Vlissingen">Flushing</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sea_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sea-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In June of that year, <a href="/wiki/Dordrecht" title="Dordrecht">Dordrecht</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gorinchem" title="Gorinchem">Gorkum</a> also fell into their hands. There they arrested all the Catholic clergy and held them in confinement, in an attempt to get them to deny the Catholic beliefs on the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Papal_primacy" title="Papal primacy">papal primacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As John became aware of what had happened to his fellow priests, he disguised himself and attempted to bring them the comfort of the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">sacraments</a>. He secretly ministered to the captives but was eventually found out and also taken captive. These nineteen were imprisoned in Gorkum from June 26 until July 6, undergoing much abuse.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Meanwhile, a letter from <a href="/wiki/William_the_Silent" title="William the Silent">William the Silent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Orange" title="Prince of Orange">Prince of Orange</a>, enjoined all those in authority to leave priests and religious unmolested. Nevertheless <a href="/wiki/William_II_de_La_Marck" title="William II de La Marck">Lumey</a>, the commander of the Watergeuzen, ordered them to be hanged on the night of 9 July,<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in a turfshed, after cruel tortures.
</p><p>They are referred by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Gorkum" title="Martyrs of Gorkum">Martyrs of Gorkum</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The group was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> in 1867. Their remains are honored at a shrine built to their honor in Brielle.
