<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Molaise of Leighlin</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">unknown<br />Ireland</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">18 April ca. 700<br />Ireland</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> (<a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a>)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">18 April<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr></tbody></table>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg/220px-Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg/330px-Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg/440px-Molaise_cave_by_Billlions.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="960" /></a><figcaption>Saint Molaise's cave on <a href="/wiki/Holy_Isle,_Firth_of_Clyde" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Isle, Firth of Clyde">Holy Isle, Firth of Clyde</a>, decorated with <a href="/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism" title="Tibetan Buddhism">Tibetan Buddhist</a> prayer flags.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint <b>Molaise</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <b>of Leighlin</b>, also <b>Laisrén</b> or <b>Laserian</b> (died ca. 639), was an early Irish saint and abbot of Lethglenn or Leithglenn, now <a href="/wiki/Old_Leighlin" title="Old Leighlin">Old Leighlin</a> in County Carlow, who is supposed to have lived in the 6th and 7th centuries.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Molaise_of_Leighlin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> and raised in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> as a young man, he lived the life of a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> on <a href="/wiki/Holy_Isle,_Firth_of_Clyde" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Isle, Firth of Clyde">Holy Isle</a> (off the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Arran" title="Isle of Arran">Isle of Arran</a>). He later visited <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> as a pilgrim and was subsequently said to have been ordained a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> there. He later entered the monastery at Old Leighlin in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> where he became <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and possibly <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. He adapted Church discipline in accordance with the practices of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Rome</a>. He is credited with introducing or advocating the Roman method of <a href="/wiki/Date_of_Easter" title="Date of Easter">dating the celebration of Easter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Kuno_Meyer" title="Kuno Meyer">Kuno Meyer</a>, he is the Laisrén who is depicted in the <a href="/wiki/Old_Irish" title="Old Irish">Old Irish</a> prose narrative <i>The Vision of Laisrén</i>, one of the earliest vernacular pieces of vision literature in Christian tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The extant fragment shows him leaving the monastery of Clúain (possibly Clonmacnois or Cloyne) to 'purify' the church of Clúain Cháin (unidentified) in Connaught. After a three nights' fast, his soul is taken up by two angels, who escort him to Hell to show him the horrors that await unredeemed sinners. The angels explain to one devil eager to take Laisrén from them that their guest is granted the vision in order that "he will give warning before us to his friends."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Molaise probably died circa 639. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 18 April.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In a note added to the <i>Félire Óengusso</i>, Molaise is said to have pulled out a hair from St Sillán's eyebrow which had the special property that anyone who saw it in the morning died instantly. Having thereby saved others, Molaise died.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Because of the fiery connection between sunrise and Molaise's name, from <i>lasair</i> "flame", the anecdote has been interpreted as relating to solar mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His monastery thrived and gave its name to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> established in 1111 at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_R%C3%A1ith_Bressail" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Ráith Bressail">Synod of Ráith Bressail</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
