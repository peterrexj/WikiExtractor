<p><b>Stanislaus Papczyński</b> (18 May 1631 &#8211; 17 September 1701), born <b>Jan Papczyński</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who founded the <a href="/wiki/Marians_of_the_Immaculate_Conception" class="mw-redirect" title="Marians of the Immaculate Conception">Marians of the Immaculate Conception</a>, the first Polish <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious order</a> for men. Prior to starting his own order, he had been a member of the <a href="/wiki/Piarists" title="Piarists">Piarist Order</a>. He took the name of "<b>Stanislaus of Jesus and Mary</b>".  Papczyński is widely remembered as a prolific religious writer; his writings include works such as <i>The Mystical Temple of God</i>.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 16 September 2007 in <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Tarcisio_Bertone" title="Tarcisio Bertone">Tarcisio Bertone</a> on the behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved a miracle attributed to him on 21 January 2016 which allowed for him to be canonized. On 15 March 2016 a consistory of cardinals scheduled his canonization to take place on 5 June 2016, which was celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jan (John) Papczyński was born on 18 May 1631, in the village of <a href="/wiki/Podegrodzie,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Podegrodzie, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Podegrodzie</a> - now near <a href="/wiki/Stary_S%C4%85cz" title="Stary Sącz">Stary Sącz</a> - as the youngest of the eight children of Tomasz Papczyński and Zofia Tacikowska. His father was a local blacksmith and also served as the village <a href="/wiki/Bailiff" title="Bailiff">bailiff</a>. As a youngster, he cared for his father's herd of sheep and came to know the countryside well.<sup id="cite_ref-founder_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-founder-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Stanislaus's first beginnings with school gave no indication of his later career as an academic. He had difficulty with studies, nevertheless he mastered his temperament, overcame material problems associated with his social standing, and advanced in studies, graduating from one school to another: in Podegrodzie, then in Nowy Sacz. At the age of fifteen he entered the Jesuit College at <a href="/wiki/Jaroslaw" class="mw-redirect" title="Jaroslaw">Jaroslaw</a>, the Piarist College of Podoliniec in 1649, and in 1650 the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> College of <a href="/wiki/Lvov" class="mw-redirect" title="Lvov">Lvov</a>, from which he was forced to flee due to an attack by <a href="/wiki/Cossack" class="mw-redirect" title="Cossack">Cossack</a> troops. At the age of twenty-three he completed his general and philosophical studies at the Jesuit College of <a href="/wiki/Rawa_Mazowiecka" title="Rawa Mazowiecka">Rawa Mazowiecka</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-founder_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-founder-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is described as a tall, slender young man, with a swarthy complexion, tall forehead and deep, pensive eyes gazing from under his eyebrows.<sup id="cite_ref-Rogalewsky_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rogalewsky-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father died while Jan was studying in Lvov.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Piarist">Piarist</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Piarist">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 2 July 1654, Papczyński joined the Piarist Order, newly established in Poland, in their monastery in Podoliniec and was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of "Stanislaus of Jesus and Mary". He performed the lowliest tasks, cleaning out stables and barns, chopping wood, and washing dishes.<sup id="cite_ref-Rogalewsky_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rogalewsky-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He made his second year of <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a>, while studying theology with the <a href="/wiki/Recollect" class="mw-redirect" title="Recollect">Recollect</a> Franciscan friars. Those were the times of the Swedish invasion known as "<a href="/wiki/Deluge_(history)" title="Deluge (history)">the Deluge</a>", that made a deep impression on the young seminarian's life. From the perspective of his whole life's experience, he thus described in his testimony one incident from those days: "I confess," he writes, "that I leave this world professing the Catholic faith for which I was ready to pour out my blood during the war with Sweden. One day my companion and I were exiting the Old Town, when a heretic soldier, his unsheathed sword in hand, attacked us near the Dominican Fathers. My companion (even though he was German) fled, and I, falling to my knees, stuck out my neck for the blow. However, by the decree of Divine Providence, I did not sustain any wounds although I was struck three times with great force. Afterward, I felt great pains for an hour and a half because of that."<sup id="cite_ref-Rogalewsky_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rogalewsky-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 22 July 1656, Papka made his profession of <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> as the first Polish member of the Piarist Congregation. He was ordained to the priesthood on 12 March 1661.<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While still a seminarian, Papczyński taught rhetoric in the Piarist colleges of Rzeszow and Podoliniec, and then after ordination he taught in a Warsaw college. He wrote a textbook entitled "Prodromus reginae artium" (The Messenger of the Queen of Arts), and used it in his lectures. This textbook underwent four reprints during subsequent years. This work merited special praise<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> for its pedagogical character as well as the selection of rhetoric which witnesses to the author's patriotic sentiments and his special devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>. Also, in this textbook he postulated the equality of all citizens before the law and took a critical stand in regards to the "<a href="/wiki/Liberum_veto" title="Liberum veto">liberum veto</a>." ("free veto" = an unexplained veto)
</p><p>As a priest Papczyński heard <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a> and gave sermons. On greater feasts he was invited to preach in the many churches of the capital. One sermon he preached on <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a>, delivered at the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominican order">Dominican</a> church, was published in 1664 under the title <i>Doctor Angelicus</i> (The Angelic Doctor). Taking advantage of Papczyński's contacts with influential people, his superiors entrusted him with gathering petitions for the beatification of the Order's founder, <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Calasanz" title="Joseph Calasanz">Joseph Calasanz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Papczyński obtained many letters. From 1663 to 1667 he directed the Confraternity of Our Lady of Grace, Patroness of Warsaw, whose image was in the Piarist's Church on Dluga Street.
</p><p>One of his penitents was the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nuncio_to_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Nuncio to Poland">Apostolic Nuncio to Poland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Pignatelli" class="mw-redirect" title="Antonio Pignatelli">Antonio Pignatelli</a>, the future Pope Innocent XII.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Leaving_the_Piarists">Leaving the Piarists</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Leaving the Piarists">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Due to differences in understanding of the Order's character, Papczyński's relations with his superiors kept getting more strained and eventually led to his leaving the Order. He strove to restore the early rigor of their way of life, while practices in the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">province</a> tended to favor laxity. Because of his views and actions, as interpreted by some of his superiors, he was the cause of confusion among Piarists, while he himself felt misunderstood. Later he used to call this period of his life "a long-lasting martyrdom". In those difficult times he wrote two series of meditations:  <i>Orator crucifixus</i> (<i>The crucified Speaker</i>) and <i>Christus patiens</i> (<i>The suffering Christ</i>).
</p><p>Not being able to find for himself, within the Piarists, the proper climate for life and work and at the same time seeing also a rift within the Province between his supporters and adversaries, Papczyński asked the <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> to release him from his religious vows.  On 11 December 1670, he left the Order. In the presence of his Piarist superiors he declared: "I offer and consecrate to God, the Almighty Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, as well as to the Mother of God, the ever-Virgin Mary, conceived without sin, my heart, my soul, my body, leaving absolutely nothing for myself, so that henceforth I may be the slave of God Almighty and of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary. Consequently, I vow to serve Them zealously, in chastity, to the end of my life, in the society of Marian Clerics of the Immaculate Conception, which by the grace of God I wish to found".
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="New_Marian_congregation">New Marian congregation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: New Marian congregation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Papczyński's mission was made more difficult by his being of common birth and advocating a very rigorous lifestyle in the prevalent aristocratic mentality. He found support for his plan from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poznan" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poznan">Bishop of Poznan</a>, Stefan Wierzbowski. Rejecting proposals from different Orders and bishops (those of Cracow and Plock), Papczyński went as a chaplain to Jakub Karski's house in Lubocza near Nowe Miasto-on-Pilica in order to prepare himself for this new undertaking. There, in September 1671, he was vested in a white <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> in honor of the Immaculate Conception of the Virgin Mary.  He also prepared the project of the future Order's statute entitled "Norma vitae" (Rule of Life), which he established as an order of <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a>.  At the same time he wrote a moral and ascetical treatise called "Templum Dei Mysticum" (The Mystical Temple of God), which underwent several reprints. In this book he expressed his belief that lay people, and not only priests and religious, are called to holiness.
</p><p>Seeking candidates for his planned order, in September 1673, Papczyński went to Puszcza Korabiewska near Skierniewice, where a former soldier, Stanislaus Krajewski, and companions lived as hermits. There, on the land donated by Krajewski, he built and organized his Institute's first house, which he called a "Retreat House".  Bishop Stanislaus Swiecicki, who came on a canonical visitation on 24 October 1673, approved this community living in accord with the "Norma vitae" under Papczyński's guidance.  He left them his own "Statutes" on the characteristics of hermits and penitentials. The Marians consider this date as the beginning of their Order.
</p><p>Wierzbowski wished to engage the newly created religious community in fulfilling of his plans of the renewal of religious life in the diocese. Particularly in the town of Gora near Warsaw where he established the holy city of Mazowsze which he called New Jerusalem. There he built many churches and chapels commemorating the Passion of Christ. To serve the arriving pilgrims, he brought over several religious orders, among them Papczyński's community. In 1677, he consigned to him the Church of the Lord's Cenacle with its adjacent monastery. The previous obligations of living a hermits' lifestyle were not in force at this institution. Papczyński lived here until his death, 24 years later.
</p><p>On 21 April 1679, Wierzbowski <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canonically</a> constituted Stanislaus's institute, composed of two houses, as an Order of diocesan right. The stated goal of the community was to spread devotion to the Virgin Mary, and to assist poor souls in <a href="/wiki/Purgatory" title="Purgatory">purgatory</a>, particularly those who died in fields of battle and victims of plagues. Papczyński also directed his spiritual sons to undertake pastoral works, especially assisting pastors by hearing confessions, as well as catechizing and delivering sermons to the faithful, particularly the most spiritually neglected in regards to religion. Papczyński introduced abstinence from drinking vodka in the order. King <a href="/wiki/John_III_Sobieski" title="John III Sobieski">John III Sobieski</a>, who was known for his kindness towards strict religious orders, took the Marians under his special protection.
</p><p>However, the simple vows which the Marians were making at the time were not strongly binding, and the fate of the Congregation depended simply on the bishop. This problem became particularly apparent after the death of Bishop Wierzbowski in 1687. For these reasons Papczyński, who was already 60 years old, set out on a journey by foot to Rome in 1690, in order to obtain papal approval for his Order. Unfortunately, he arrived there at a time when the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> was without a <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, and only procured a spiritual affiliation of the Marians with several other Orders. The approbation of "Norma vitae" as the Congregation's statutes, which took place a couple of years later, was a fruit of this journey.
</p><p>In 1699, Papczyński made another attempt to procure the approbation of his order, by sending to Rome Joachim Kozlowski as his envoy with full powers (<a href="/wiki/Plenipotentiary" title="Plenipotentiary">plenipotentiary</a>). Meeting with difficulties in obtaining the approbation of the Marian Order upon its own rule, Kozlowski approached the reformed Franciscan Minors for the "Rule of the Ten Virtues of the Blessed Virgin Mary", which included the Marians' legal dependence on this Order. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> approved the document issued by the General of Franciscans on 24 October 1699. Although the Rule was received without previous consultation with Papczyński, it did not infringe the specific features of the Marian Institute and was thus gladly accepted by the Marian Legislator. The Marians became an order with solemn vows and were freed from the authority of a local bishop, although they were dependent on the Franciscans for some time. Finally, the Marians received approbation as an order of apostolic right ridding themselves of restrictions. In their apostolic work they placed special emphasis on teaching truths of the faith to the faithful of lower classes, which the church considered was its most urgent task at the time.
</p><p>On 6 June 1701, Papczyński pronounced a solemn profession of vows in the hands of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nuncio" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Nuncio">Apostolic Nuncio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Pignatelli" title="Francesco Pignatelli">Francesco Pignatelli</a>, and then received the profession of vows from the other Marians, a very important milestone in establishing the first Polish religiouis order for men.  On the way to the realization of this goal, for a duration of 30 years, Papczyński had to overcome innumerable difficulties. A few times he doubted whether or not God truly wanted him to follow this road.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He died on 17 September 1701, and was buried at the Church of the Lord's <a href="/wiki/Cenacle" title="Cenacle">Cenacle</a> in Gora Kalwaria.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spirituality">Spirituality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Spirituality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At school, from the pulpit, in confessional, or at his desk, Papczyński proclaimed the word of God everywhere in speech and writing, emphasizing particularly the need to care for those wronged by the society. His work followed the beliefs proclaimed in "Templum Dei Mysticum": "O how admirable are those who mutually work with Christ, and, solely out of love for Him, sincerely and zealously announce all that is essential for salvation, for leading a Christian life, for rooting out vices, and increasing virtues. This is the greatest and most fruitful work of mercy".
</p><p>Two centuries before the proclamation of the Dogma of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> Stanislaus professed: "I believe everything that the holy Roman Church believes, but first of all I profess that the Most Holy Mother of God, Mary, was conceived without any stain of original sin". This privilege of the Immaculate Conception was very precious to him.  He talked about it in sermons and wrote extensively on the subject. In 1670 he made a "vow of blood" witnessing his readiness to lay down his life if necessary in honor of Mary's Immaculate Conception. Papczyński lived in times illustrated by the Polish writer Henryk Sienkiewicz in his "Trilogy", describing the Cossack wars, the Swedish invasion of Poland, and finally, the Polish army's victory at Vienna. He himself accompanied Polish troops in battles against Turks in Ukraine in 1674, and witnessed that many people died unshriven.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Process_and_Venerable">Process and Venerable</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Process and Venerable">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A cause to beatify Papczyński was started in 1767, interrupted in 1775, and reopened in 1953. But because such a long time had passed since his death, this process had to be accomplished "historically", which means that documents had to be searched for testimonies on one's life and virtues. Those documents were prepared and gathered in a 1000-page manuscript called "Positio". On 28 February 1980, the National Conference of Bishops in Poland petitioned the Pope to elevate Papczyński to the altars as a model of Christian life. Similar petitions were sent by major superiors of male and female congregations in Poland. Based on this evidence, on 28 November 1980, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for Saints</a> made a decision that Papczyński's beatification cause may be brought before the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Signatura" title="Apostolic Signatura">Apostolic Tribunal</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved this decision on 6 March 1981 which commenced sainthood proceedings and granted him the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> - the first stage in the process.
</p><p>In the years 1981–1982, a process de non cultu was conducted in Warsaw.  The decree super validitate Processus (regarding the validity of the diocesan process) was promulgated in 1990. Further evaluations of Positio super Virtutibus were also conducted. Then, on 22 January 1991, during an Ordinary Congress of the Congregation, chaired by Anthony Petti, General Promoter of the Faith, the theological consultants stated that Papczyński had practiced virtues to a heroic degree.
</p><p>On 17 March 1992, after having listened to the report delivered by Paulino Limongi, Titular Archbishop of Nice in Hemimont, the cardinals and bishops gathered at an Ordinary Congregation stated that he had practiced the theological virtues, the cardinal virtues, and other virtues associated with these, to a heroic degree.
</p><p>After the results of the research conducted by the Cardinal-Prefect, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Felici" title="Angelo Felici">Angelo Felici</a>, had been presented to the pope, he accepted the decision of the Sacred Congregation for the Causes of the Saints and ordered that the decree on Papczyński's heroic virtues be prepared.
</p><p>On 13 June 1992, the Pope invited the Cardinal-Prefect, the relators of the cause, the Secretary of the congregation, and other people who are usually summoned in such circumstances and, in the presence of all gathered with regard to the cause and its result, solemnly announced that:
</p>
<dl><dd>It is considered to be a certain thing that Blessed Stanislaus of Jesus and Mary Papczyński practiced the theological virtues of faith, hope, and charity, the cardinal virtues of prudence, justice, temperance, and fortitude, and other virtues associated with these, to a heroic degree. The Pope ordered that this decree be made public and kept in the acts of the Sacred Congregation for the Causes of Saints.</dd></dl>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:402px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/400px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" decoding="async" width="400" height="91" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/600px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/800px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg 2x" data-file-width="16382" data-file-height="3726" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Canonization Mass celebrated on 5 June 2016.</div></div></div>
<p>The miracle needed for his beatification was investigated in the 2003-2004 timeframe and received formal validation from the Sacred Congregation for the Causes of Saints on 7 May 2004. A medical board based in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> met and approved the miracle on 12 May 2005 while theologians followed suit and approved it also on 20 September 2005. The cardinal and bishop members of the Sacred Congregation for the Causes of Saints granted their formal approval on 3 October 2006, while <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved it on 16 December 2006.
</p><p>The miracle in question concerned Ursula Markowska on 4 April 2000.
</p><p>He was beatified at the Marian Shrine in Licheń by a special delegate of Pope Benedict XVI – Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Tarcisio_Bertone" title="Tarcisio Bertone">Tarcisio Bertone</a>, Secretary of State of the Holy See on 16 September 2007.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The second miracle required for his canonization was investigated in a diocesan tribunal and was ratified as being a valid process on 27 June 2014. The Rome-based medical board met and approved it on 17 September 2015 while the theologians also met and approved it on 10 November 2015. The cardinal and bishops who constituted the Sacred Congregation for the Causes of Saints also approved it on 12 January 2016.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved the miracle on 21 January 2016; a date was determined for the canonization at a consistory of cardinals on the following 15 March and he was canonized as a saint on 5 June 2016.<sup id="cite_ref-5June_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5June-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span id="From_the_last_will_and_testament_of_Stanislaus_Papczy.C5.84ski"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="From_the_last_will_and_testament_of_Stanislaus_Papczyński">From the last will and testament of Stanislaus Papczyński</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Papczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: From the last will and testament of Stanislaus Papczyński">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>"I give thanks to the Divine Majesty for graces, gifts, and goodness granted me with great generosity.  I repent from the depth of my heart and I wish out of love of the Lord to repent more and more for all my sins, which I place in the deepest wounds of Jesus Christ, my Lord and Redeemer.  I fall at the feet of Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of God, and I give myself with the entire Congregation of her Immaculate Conception to her for eternity.  I call on her gracious direction and effective care, and for the hour of my death I beg her most merciful and powerful protection from the ambushes of my enemies and all temporal and eternal Evil.
</p><p>To all those who loved and supported the Congregation of the Immaculate Conception (which I founded through God’s inspiration to support all the faithful departed), I promise double reward from God’s hand.
</p><p>I eagerly suggest to my brethren, if it be appropriate for me to say:  Sons, you ought to have love for God and neighbor, devotion to the Catholic faith, and worship, love, and obedience to the Holy See, faithful preservation of the vows, humility, patience, support the souls in purgatory, and be at peace with everyone."
</p>
