<p><b>Saint Meriasek</b> (<a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>: <i lang="br">Meriadeg</i>) was a 6th-century Cornish and <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. The legends of his life are known through <i><a href="/wiki/Beunans_Meriasek" title="Beunans Meriasek">Beunans Meriasek</a></i>, a <a href="/wiki/Cornish_language" title="Cornish language">Cornish language</a> play known from a single surviving manuscript copy dated 1504, and a few other sources. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Camborne" title="Camborne">Camborne</a>, and according to his legendary will his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is the first Friday in June (although it is celebrated in some places on 7 June).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meriasek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Until <a href="/wiki/Beunans_Ke" class="mw-redirect" title="Beunans Ke">Beunans Ke</a> (NLW MS 23849D) came to light very recently, Beunans Meriasek was the only known saint's play in Middle Cornish. It was rediscovered in the 1860s. It was most probably written down at Glasney collegiate church at Penryn, perhaps under the aegis of Master John Nans, provost of Glasney, who later moved to Camborne and died in 1508.<sup id="cite_ref-library_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meriasek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Meriasek was a <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a><sup id="cite_ref-library_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> from a ducal family. <a href="/wiki/Conan_Meriadoc" title="Conan Meriadoc">Conan Meriadoc</a>, the legendary king of Brittany at the time, wanted to arrange a political marriage for him, but Meriasek preferred to renounce his inheritance and become a priest. He performed several miraculous cures thereafter. He crossed the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">Channel</a> to found an <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> in <a href="/wiki/Camborne" title="Camborne">Camborne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>. Encountering persecution from <a href="/wiki/Tewdwr_Mawr" title="Tewdwr Mawr">King Teudar</a>, he returned to Brittany (landing at <a href="/wiki/Plougasnou" title="Plougasnou">Plougasnou</a>) to found a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Josselin" title="Josselin">Josselin</a>, in the lands of the Viscounts of <a href="/wiki/Rohan_(commune)" class="mw-redirect" title="Rohan (commune)">Rohan</a>. His reputation for miracles attracted crowds and he decided to withdraw to <a href="/wiki/Pontivy" title="Pontivy">Pontivy</a>, close to the château of Rohan.
</p><p>He assisted the Viscount in dealing with brigands who infested his lands by bringing down the fire of heaven upon them; in gratitude he founded three fairs at Noyal at the saint's request.
</p><p>He is reputed to have healed many <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">lepers</a> and disabled people, to have driven off the highwaymen of Josselin through prayer, to have made water spring from solid rock, and to have calmed a storm. He was elevated to become <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Vannes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Vannes">bishop of Vannes</a> but continued to wear a <a href="/wiki/Hair_shirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hair shirt">hair shirt</a>, practise <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>, and minister to the poor. He was buried in Vannes Cathedral. His <a href="/wiki/Holy_well" title="Holy well">sacred well</a> in Camborne was long thought to have the power of healing the insane.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meriasek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>An open-air performance of an adaptation of "Beurens Mariasek" was performed in Heartlands by well over 100 schoolchildren on 13 July 2012. The performance was the culmination of a project that introduced students to the Cornish language and the tradition of medieval Cornish drama through a series of workshops and rehearsals.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
