<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Vincent of Lérins</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vincentius Lerinensis</i>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;445</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gallic</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and author of <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">early Christian writings</a>.  One example was the <i><a href="/wiki/Commonitory" title="Commonitory">Commonitorium</a></i>, c.<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>434, which offers guidance in the <a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">orthodox teaching</a> of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>.  Suspected of <a href="/wiki/Semi-Pelagianism" title="Semi-Pelagianism">semi-Pelagianism</a>, he opposed the <a href="/wiki/Original_sin" title="Original sin">Augustinian model of grace</a> and was probably the recipient of <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a>'s <i>Responsiones ad Capitula Objectionum Vincentianarum</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholiconline_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholiconline-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is celebrated on 24&#160;May.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_L%C3%A9rins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent of Lérins was born in <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Saskatchewan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saskatchewan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> to a noble family, and he is believed to be the brother of <a href="/wiki/Lupus_of_Troyes" title="Lupus of Troyes">Lupus of Troyes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholiconline_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholiconline-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In his early life he engaged in secular pursuits; it is unclear whether these were civil or military, though the term he uses, <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">secularis militia</i></span>, may imply the latter. He entered <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins Abbey</a> on <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Saint-Honorat" title="Île Saint-Honorat">Île Saint-Honorat</a>, where under the <a href="/wiki/Pseudonym" title="Pseudonym">pseudonym</a> Peregrinus he wrote the <i><a href="/wiki/Commonitorium" class="mw-redirect" title="Commonitorium">Commonitorium</a></i> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;434</span>, about three years after the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus" title="Council of Ephesus">Council of Ephesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ghellinck1912_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ghellinck1912-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Vincent defended calling <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Mary, mother of Jesus</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a></i> (God-bearer). This opposed the teachings of <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Nestorius_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Nestorius of Constantinople">Patriarch Nestorius of Constantinople</a> which were condemned by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus" title="Council of Ephesus">Council of Ephesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saskatchewan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saskatchewan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius of Lyon</a> called him a "conspicuously eloquent and knowledgeable" holy man.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Gennadius_of_Massilia" title="Gennadius of Massilia">Gennadius of Massilia</a> wrote that Vincent died during the reigns of the Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius&#160;II</a> in the East and <a href="/wiki/Valentinian_III" title="Valentinian III">Valentinian&#160;III</a> in the West. Therefore, his death must have occurred in or before the year 450. His relics are preserved at Lérins.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> included his name in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> but <a href="/wiki/Louis-S%C3%A9bastien_Le_Nain_de_Tillemont" title="Louis-Sébastien Le Nain de Tillemont">Louis-Sébastien Le Nain de Tillemont</a> doubted whether there was sufficient reason. He is commemorated on 24&#160;May.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commonitory"><i>Commonitory</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_L%C3%A9rins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Commonitory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent wrote his <i><a href="/wiki/Commonitory" title="Commonitory">Commonitory</a></i> to provide himself with a general rule to distinguish Catholic truth from <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>, committing it to writing as a reference. It is known for Vincent's famous maxim: "Moreover, in the Catholic Church itself, all possible care must be taken, that we hold that faith which has been believed everywhere, always, by all."<sup id="cite_ref-Heurtley1894_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heurtley1894-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 132">&#58;&#8202;132&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Moxon1915_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Moxon1915-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 10">&#58;&#8202;10&#8202;</span></sup> The currently accepted idea that Vincent was a <a href="/wiki/Semipelagian" class="mw-redirect" title="Semipelagian">semipelagian</a> is attributed to a 17th-century Protestant theologian, <a href="/wiki/Gerardus_Vossius" title="Gerardus Vossius">Gerardus Vossius</a>, and developed in the 17th century by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Henry_Noris" title="Henry Noris">Henry Noris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Moxon1915_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Moxon1915-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Location: xxii">&#58;&#8202;xxii&#8202;</span></sup> Evidence of Vincent's semipelagianism, according to Reginald Moxon, is Vincent's, "great vehemence against" the doctrines of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> in <i>Commonitory</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Moxon1915_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Moxon1915-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Location: xxvii">&#58;&#8202;xxvii&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> has quoted Vincent's Commonitory on several occasions, notably his words on doctrine and the progress of doctrine: "The dogma of the Christian religion must follow these laws. It progresses, consolidating over the years, developing with time, deepening with age."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Semipelagianism"><span class="anchor" id="Semi-Pelagianism"></span>Semipelagianism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_L%C3%A9rins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Semipelagianism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Semi-Pelagianism" title="Semi-Pelagianism">Semi-Pelagianism</a> was a doctrine of grace advocated by monks in and around <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a> in Southern Gaul after 428. It aimed at a compromise between the two extremes of <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustinianism" title="Augustinianism">Augustinianism</a>, and was condemned as <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Orange" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Orange">Second Council of Orange</a> in 529&#160;AD after more than a century of disputes.<sup id="cite_ref-pohle1912_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pohle1912-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Augustine wrote of <a href="/wiki/Prevenient_grace" title="Prevenient grace">prevenient grace</a>, and expanded to a discussion of <a href="/wiki/Predestination" title="Predestination">predestination</a>. A number of monastic communities took exception to the latter because it seemed to nullify the value of asceticism practiced under their rules. <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">John Cassian</a> felt that Augustine's stress on predestination ruled out any need for human cooperation or consent.
</p><p>Vincent was suspected of Semipelagianism but whether he actually held that doctrine is not clear as it is not found in the <i>Commonitorium</i>. But it is probable that his sympathies were with those who held it. Considering that the monks of the <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Islands" title="Lérins Islands">Lérins Islands</a>&#160;– like the general body of clergy of Southern Gaul&#160;– were Semipelagians, it is not surprising that Vincent was suspected of Semipelagianism. It is also possible that Vincent held to a position closer to the Eastern Orthodox position of today, which they claim to have been virtually universal until the time of Augustine, and which may have been interpreted as Semipelagian by Augustine's followers.
</p><p>Vincent upheld tradition and seemed to have objected to much of Augustine's work as "new" theology. He shared Cassian's reservations about Augustine's views on the role of grace. In the <i>Commonitorium</i> he listed theologians and teachers who, in his view, had made significant contributions to the defense and spreading of the Gospel; he omitted Augustine from that list. Some commentators have viewed Cassian and Vincent as "Semiaugustinian" rather than Semipelagian.
</p><p>It is a matter of academic debate whether Vincent is the author of the <i>Objectiones Vincentianae</i>, a collection of sixteen inferences allegedly deduced from Augustine's writings, which is lost and only known through <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a>'s rejoinder, <i>Responsiones ad capitula objectionum Vincentianarum</i>. It is dated close to the time of the <i>Commonitorium</i> and its animus is very similar to the <i>Commonitorium</i> sections 70 and 86, making it possible that both were written by the same author.<sup id="cite_ref-Ghellinck1912_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ghellinck1912-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
