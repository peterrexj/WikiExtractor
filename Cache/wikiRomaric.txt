<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the saint. For the Ivorian football player, see <a href="/wiki/Romaric_(footballer)" title="Romaric (footballer)">Romaric (footballer)</a>.</div>
<p><b>Saint Romaric</b> (died 653) was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> nobleman who lived in <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> from the late 6th century until the middle of the 7th century. He and Amatus of Grenoble founded Remiremont Abbey.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romaric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was a former <a href="/wiki/Count_Palatine" class="mw-redirect" title="Count Palatine">Count Palatine</a> in the court of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> king, <a href="/wiki/Theodebert_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodebert II">Theodebert II</a>. His parents were killed by Queen <a href="/wiki/Brunhilda_of_Austrasia" title="Brunhilda of Austrasia">Brunhilda</a>, and because of the enmity of his family with this queen, he wandered without a home. However, with the fall of the Queen, he was received at the court of <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a> and his lands restored.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Disenchanted with life as a courtier, Romaric experienced a religious conversion through the missionary efforts of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ame" title="Saint Ame">Amatus of Grenoble</a>, a monk of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil Abbey</a>. Romaric entered Luxeuil and trained to be a monk. With the approval of <a href="/wiki/Eustace_of_Luxeuil" title="Eustace of Luxeuil">Abbot Eustace</a>, Romaric and Amatus founded a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> for men and women, later given the name of <a href="/wiki/Remiremont_Abbey" title="Remiremont Abbey">Remiremont</a> (<i>Romariki Mons</i>). The monastery was built on land belonging to St. Romaric, when he was a count palatine.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> One of the earlier monks in Remiremont was St. Romaric's friend <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">St. Arnulf</a>.
</p><p>Around 625 Romaric succeeded Amatus as abbot. He directed several members of his family who embraced religious life there: two of his daughters, Ozeltruda and Zeberga, his granddaughter Gebetruda, and his grandson Adelphus.
</p><p>Romaric died in 653.<sup id="cite_ref-MartRom_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MartRom-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Romaric is recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, with a feast day on December 10<sup id="cite_ref-MartRom_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MartRom-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or December 8.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was canonized on 3 December 1049 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
