<p><b>Hermann Joseph</b>, (c. 1150&#160;&#8211;&#32;7 April 1241) was a German <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Premonstratensian">Premonstratensian</a> <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canon regular</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a>. Never formally <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>, in 1958 his status as a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> was formally recognized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hermann was born in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>, the son of Count Lothair of <a href="/wiki/Meerbusch" title="Meerbusch">Meer</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Hildegund_(widow)" title="Hildegund (widow)">Hildegund</a>. His sister was <a href="/wiki/Hadewych_of_Meer" title="Hadewych of Meer">Hadewych of Meer</a>. Though of the nobility, the family was not overly wealthy. According to the biography by Razo Bonvisinus, a contemporary and <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of <a href="/wiki/Steinfeld_Abbey" title="Steinfeld Abbey">Steinfeld Abbey</a>, at the age of seven Hermann attended school and very early was known for devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a>. At every available moment he could be found at the church of <a href="/wiki/St._Maria_im_Kapitol" title="St. Maria im Kapitol">St. Maria im Kapitol</a>, where he would kneel rapt in prayer to Mary. Bonvisinus claims that the boy once presented an apple, saved from his own lunch, to a statue of Jesus, who accepted it.<sup id="cite_ref-Salvatorian_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salvatorian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to still another legend, on another occasion, when on a cold day he made his appearance with bare feet, Mary procured him the means of getting shoes. <sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of twelve he entered the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> of the Premonstratensian (more commonly known as <a href="/wiki/Norbertine" class="mw-redirect" title="Norbertine">Norbertine</a>) Canons Regular at Steinfeld. As he was too young to be accepted into the Order, he was sent to study, probably at Mariengaarde at <a href="/wiki/Hallum" title="Hallum">Hallum</a> in Friesland.<sup id="cite_ref-Salvatorian_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salvatorian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Upon his return, he made his <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">vows</a> and was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> and the additional name "Joseph". As a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a>, he was entrusted initially with the service of the <a href="/wiki/Refectory" title="Refectory">refectory</a> and later of the <a href="/wiki/Sacristy" title="Sacristy">sacristy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a>, Hermann was sometimes sent out to perform pastoral duties and was also in frequent demand for the making and repairing of clocks. He was also active in pastoral care outside the monastery, especially in women's monasteries in the Eifel region.<sup id="cite_ref-Huber_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Huber-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Herman was characterized by his child-like devotion to Mary. Late in his life, he had under his charge the spiritual welfare of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_nuns" title="Cistercian nuns">Cistercian nuns</a> at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hoven_(Z%C3%BClpich)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hoven (Zülpich) (page does not exist)">Hoven</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoven_(Z%C3%BClpich)" class="extiw" title="de:Hoven (Zülpich)">de</a>&#93;</span>, near <a href="/wiki/Z%C3%BClpich" title="Zülpich">Zülpich</a>, whom he served as <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a>. There he died and was buried in their <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His works include: "A Commentary on the Canticle of Canticles", which is lost; "Opuscula" (new edition, Namur, 1899), including: "Duodecim gratiarum actiones"; "Jubilus seu Hymnus de SS. undecim millibus Virginibus"; "Oratio ad Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum", taken to a great extent from the <a href="/wiki/Song_of_Songs" title="Song of Songs">Canticle of Canticles</a>; "Alia Oratio"; "Precula de quinque Gaudiis B. Mariae V." It is not quite certain whether the last three are the works of Hermann, though they are generally ascribed to him. He possibly created the oldest hymn to the Sacred Heart of Jesus (“Summi regis cor aveto”).<sup id="cite_ref-Huber_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Huber-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg/220px-Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg/330px-Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg/440px-Kloster_Steinfeld_Grab_Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3071" data-file-height="2303" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Hermann Joseph von Steinfeld, Kloster Steinfeld</figcaption></figure>
<p>Reports of miracles that are said to have happened immediately after his death testify to the veneration of the saint in the late Middle Ages. The miracle stories show well-known hagiographic patterns. Miraculous healings deal with the blind or almost blind, paralyzed and possessed, but also with everyday complaints such as toothache, sore throat, headache, etc.<sup id="cite_ref-Doring_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doring-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His body was later transferred back to Steinfeld, where his marble tomb and large picture may be seen to the present day. By custom apples are left at his tomb, in reference to a legend that Hermann once offered an apple to the Christ Child in the arms of the Madonna in the church of St. Maria im Kapitol at Cologne - who took it.<sup id="cite_ref-Salvatorian_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Salvatorian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Portions of his relics are at Cologne and at Antwerp. His grave in Steinfeld became a pilgrimage destination. In the Middle Ages especially by mothers, in modern times by children and pupils.<sup id="cite_ref-Huber_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Huber-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The Hermann Josef Festival is held at Steinfeld on the sixth Sunday after Easter.<sup id="cite_ref-Doring_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doring-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The process of his canonization was begun in 1626, at the request of Archbishop Ferdinand of Cologne and the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Ferdinand_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Ferdinand II">Emperor Ferdinand II</a>, but was interrupted. His feast, however, continued to be celebrated on 4 April, by the members of his Order and the name of Hermann was listed in the Premonstratensian supplement to the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>. They also celebrate the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translation</a> of his relics on 24 May. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> consecrated an altar in honor of the Blessed Hermann Joseph in the Roman College of the Norbertines in 1728.
</p><p>His status as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> was confirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Pius_XII" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XII">Pius XII</a> in 1958. (The <a href="/wiki/Salvatorian" class="mw-redirect" title="Salvatorian">Salvatorian</a> Fathers, who had come to occupy the abbey in Steinfeld in modern times, opted to perform this less costly and involved process—known as <i>Confirmatio Cultus</i>—rather than to carry out a full canonization process.) His current feast day on the calendar of the diocese of Cologne is 21 May. (His mother and sister are listed among the "Blessed".) 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Hermann Josef is depicted as a religious with Mary and the baby Jesus, often offering an apple; his attributes are a chalice or three roses
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_Joseph_von_Steinfeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Hermann Joseph is the patron of watchmakers,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and of children and young people.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As a former sexton, he was held up as a model for sextons, acolytes, and altar servers.
</p><p>Hermann Josef received calls from expectant women who asked for a good delivery. The patronage of expectant mothers ("patronus puerperarum") has been handed down since the 17th century in the use of "touch relics", such as needles, brooches and clasps left on the reliquary and retrieved to fastened to their hairstyle or clothes in the hope of a happy pregnancy through the intercession of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Doring_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doring-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
