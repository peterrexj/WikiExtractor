<p><b>Jaime Hilario Barbal</b> (2 January 1898 – 18 January 1937) – born <b>Manuel Barbal i Cosán</b> – was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Brother_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Brother (Christian)">professed religious</a> brother from the <a href="/wiki/Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools" class="mw-redirect" title="Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools">Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He served for almost two decades as a teacher in the schools that his order managed until being caught up in the turmoil of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a> that saw the forces of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic" title="Second Spanish Republic">Second Spanish Republic</a> execute him.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification was celebrated on 29 April 1990 after it was confirmed that Barbal was killed "in odium fidei" ("in hatred of the faith") and the confirmation of a miracle attributed to his intercession allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to canonize him as a saint of the Roman Catholic Church on 21 November 1999.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jaime_Hilario_Barbal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Manuel Barbal i Cosán was born in <a href="/wiki/Lleida" title="Lleida">Lleida</a>.
</p><p>He began his ecclesiastical studies at a religious school where <a href="/wiki/Seminarians" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminarians">seminarians</a> were educated in 1911 for the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Urgel" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Urgel">Diocese of Urgel</a>. But he soon developed hearing problems and was forced to withdraw from the institute and could not pursue a path to the priesthood as he had intended. In 1917 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools" class="mw-redirect" title="Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools">Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools</a> in <a href="/wiki/Irun" title="Irun">Irun</a> where he was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of "Jaime Hilario" and the habit on 24 February.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Until 1933 he was sent on various teaching assignments and was regarded as an exceptional teacher and <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechist</a>; he taught <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and was a believer in universal education with an emphasis on the poor. His hearing problems continued to persist and worsen and, at the beginning of the 1930s, he was forced to stop teaching and began work as a gardener at the college of Saint Joseph in <a href="/wiki/Tarragona" title="Tarragona">Tarragona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">civil war</a> in mid-1936 while traveling to visit his relations saw him arrested for being a member of a religious congregation. In December 1936, he was transferred to the prison ship "Mahon" at Tarragona. Although he could have claimed that he was a gardener, he insisted that he was a religious brother and, on 15 January 1937, he was tried and convicted for being a member of the De La Salle Brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In his trial, his defense advocate told him to cite his occupation as a gardener, but he refused to do so and said he would claim to be a religious brother, as was the case.
</p><p>Barbal was taken to Monte de los Olivos – an olive grove – in Tarragona for execution during the afternoon of 18 January 1937.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When the first two salvos from the firing squad failed to harm Barbal, the firing squad commander shot him five times at close range. His last words were: "To die for <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Christ</a>, my young friends, is to live". He was the first of 97 of his order killed in <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a> during the Spanish Civil War.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jaime_Hilario_Barbal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process started in Tarragona in 1944 and concluded a short time later in 1945 while the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> later validated this process several decades later on 7 June 1985 in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>; the postulation sent the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier to the C.C.S. in 1988 for assessment. Theological experts first approved on 24 February 1989 that he was killed "in odium fidei" ("in hatred of the faith") while the C.C.S. conceded the same thing in their meeting on 17 October 1989. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed on 21 December 1989 that Barbal was killed in hatred of his faith and then beatified Barbal on 29 April 1990.
</p><p>One miracle was required for his sanctification and when news reached the postulation about one such case it was investigated in the diocese of its origin. For one killed "in odium fidei", no miracle is needed for their beatification but one is needed for sainthood. The C.C.S. validated this process on 1 October 1993 and a medical board approved this on 26 October 1994 as did theologians on 20 March 1998 and the C.C.S. on 20 October 1998. John Paul II approved this miracle on 21 December 1998 and canonized Barbal as a saint on 21 November 1999 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
