<p><b>Saint Pantaenus the Philosopher</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πάνταινος</span>; died c. 200)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a> and a significant figure in the <a href="/wiki/Catechetical_School_of_Alexandria" title="Catechetical School of Alexandria">Catechetical School of Alexandria</a> from around AD 180. This school was the earliest <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechetical</a> school, and became influential in the development of <a href="/wiki/Christian_theology" title="Christian theology">Christian theology</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pantaenus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pantaenus was a <a href="/wiki/Stoicism" title="Stoicism">Stoic</a> philosopher teaching in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>. He was a native of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He converted to the Christian faith, and sought to reconcile his new faith with <a href="/wiki/Greek_philosophy" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek philosophy">Greek philosophy</a>. His most famous student, <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria" title="Clement of Alexandria">Clement</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> who was his successor as head of the Catechetical School, described Pantaenus as "the Sicilian bee".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Although no writings by Pantaenus are extant,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> his legacy is known by the influence of the Catechetical School on the development of Christian theology, in particular in the early debates on the interpretation of the Bible, the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Christology" title="Christology">Christology</a>. He was the main supporter of <a href="/wiki/Serapion_of_Antioch" title="Serapion of Antioch">Serapion of Antioch</a> for acting against the influence of <a href="/wiki/Gnosticism" title="Gnosticism">Gnosticism</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Silk_route.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Silk_route.jpg/250px-Silk_route.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="163" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Silk_route.jpg/375px-Silk_route.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Silk_route.jpg/500px-Silk_route.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2868" data-file-height="1866" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Silk_route.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Silk_Road" title="Silk Road">Silk Road</a> map showing ancient trade routes</div></div></div>
<p>In addition to his work as a teacher, <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius of Caesarea</a> reports that Pantaenus was for a time a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> traveling as far as <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> where, according to Eusebius, he found Christian communities using the Gospel of Matthew written in "Hebrew letters", supposedly left them by the Apostle <a href="/wiki/Bartholomew" class="mw-redirect" title="Bartholomew">Bartholomew</a> (and which might have been the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_the_Hebrews" title="Gospel of the Hebrews">Gospel of the Hebrews</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This may indicate that <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Christianity" title="Syriac Christianity">Syrian Christians</a>, using a Syriac version of the New Testament, had already evangelized parts of India by the late 2nd century. However, some writers have suggested that having difficulty with the language of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians" title="Saint Thomas Christians">Saint Thomas Christians</a>, Pantaenus misinterpreted their reference to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle" title="Thomas the Apostle">Mar Thoma</a> (the <a href="/wiki/Aramaic" title="Aramaic">Aramaic</a> term meaning <a href="/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle" title="Thomas the Apostle">Saint Thomas</a>), who is currently credited with bringing <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> to India in the 1st century<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> by the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christian_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Thomas Christian Churches">Syrian Churches</a>, as Bar Tolmai (the Hebrew name of Bartholomew). The ancient seaport <a href="/wiki/Muziris" title="Muziris">Muziris</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Malabar_Coast" title="Malabar Coast">Malabar Coast</a> (modern-day <a href="/wiki/Kerala" title="Kerala">Kerala</a> in India) was frequented by the Egyptians in the early centuries AD.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jerome">Saint Jerome</a> (c. 347 – 30 September 420), apparently relying entirely on Eusebius' evidence from <i>Historia Ecclesiastica</i>, wrote that Pantaenus visited India, “to preach Christ to the Brahmans and philosophers there.”<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> It is unlikely that Jerome has any information about Pantaenus' mission to India that is independent of Eusebius. On the other hand, his claim that "many" of Pantaenus' Biblical commentaries were still extant is probably based on Jerome's own knowledge.
</p><p>His feast day as July 7.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-auto1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Copts" title="Copts">Coptic</a> synaxarium mentions "Pantaenus and Clement" in its entry regarding the return of the relics of St <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Apostle" class="mw-redirect" title="Mark the Apostle">Mark the Apostle</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> of Rome on 15 <a href="/wiki/Paoni" title="Paoni">Paoni</a><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> but does not assign Pantaenus any specific feast date.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="19th_century_and_modern_study_on_Pantaenus">19th century and modern study on Pantaenus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pantaenus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: 19th century and modern study on Pantaenus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Universalist_Church_of_America" title="Universalist Church of America">Universalist Church of America</a> historian <a href="/wiki/J._W._Hanson" class="mw-redirect" title="J. W. Hanson">J. W. Hanson</a> (1899) argued that Pantaenus "must, beyond question" have taught Universalism to <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria" title="Clement of Alexandria">Clement of Alexandria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> However, since it is now considered that Clement of Alexandria's views contained a tension between salvation and freewill,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and that he and Origen did not clearly teach universal reconciliation of all <a href="/wiki/Immortal_soul" class="mw-redirect" title="Immortal soul">immortal souls</a> in their understanding of <a href="/wiki/Apokatastasis" class="mw-redirect" title="Apokatastasis">apokatastasis</a>, Hanson's conclusion about Pantaenus lacks a firm basis.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
