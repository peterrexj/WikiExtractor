<p><b>Margherita Colonna,</b> (c.1259 – 30 December 1284)<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Colonna_family" title="Colonna family">Colonna family</a>, which was notable in Italian history for centuries. She lived as a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, with her followers.
</p><p>She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> after the approval of her cult in 1847.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margherita_Colonna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born in <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> about 1259. Her father, Oddone Colonna, died when she was about two years old. Her mother, who was also named Margherita and who was the sister of <a href="/wiki/Matteo_Rosso_Orsini" title="Matteo Rosso Orsini">Matteo Rosso Orsini</a> and a pious woman, died when Margherita was probably a little over ten years old.<sup id="cite_ref-brentano_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brentano-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Margherita was left in the care of her older brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-donovan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The elder, Giovanni Colonna, was repeatedly a Roman senator. The earliest biography about Margaret was written by him around 1285. The younger, <a href="/wiki/Giacomo_Colonna_(cardinal)" title="Giacomo Colonna (cardinal)">Giacomo</a>, was made a cardinal in 1278 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_III" title="Pope Nicholas III">Pope Nicholas III</a>, an Orsini cousin. Cardinal Colonna was a friend and confidant of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_IV" title="Pope Honorius IV">Pope Honorius IV</a>, and a leading figure of the papal curia.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Margherita's late teens there was talk of an appropriate marriage for her, but it was not forced. According to the biography by her brother, Margaret had always lived in peculiarly sweet piety. She had long wanted to retire from the world. She was initially opposed by her brother Giovanni, but supported by her brother Giacomo, a Franciscan friar.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>She retired to Castel San Pietro, a lonely retreat near <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> where she passed her time in practices of <a href="/wiki/Piety" title="Piety">piety</a> and <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>. Her charity towards the poor was said to be unbounded, and she was thought to have worked miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-donovan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Every year, on the feast of St. John the Baptist, to whom she was very devoted, she organized a luncheon for the poor and sick.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Margherita's followers formed a loose community on the <a href="/wiki/Monti_Prenestini" title="Monti Prenestini">Monti Prenestini</a> above Palestrina.  Seven years before her death, she was afflicted with an <a href="/wiki/Peptic_ulcer" class="mw-redirect" title="Peptic ulcer">ulcer</a>, from which she suffered until her death.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She died on December 30, 1284.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margherita_Colonna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margherita was, after her death, considered a saint by the people in the areas of Colonna power, in the stretch of territory from <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Lazio" title="Subiaco, Lazio">Subiaco</a> to Palestrina and from Palestrina to <a href="/wiki/Anagni" title="Anagni">Anagni</a>. She was very much a family saint; and her sanctity had flowered, in life, on Colonna hills.<sup id="cite_ref-brentano_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brentano-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The relics of Blessed Margherita are venerated in the church of Castel San Pietro, not far from Palestrina.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1285 Pope Honorius IV granted the monastery of San Silvestro in Capite to her followers, who at that time were incorporated into a formal <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> community, adopting the rule of the Sorores minores, which had been approved for <a href="/wiki/Isabelle_of_France_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Isabelle of France (saint)">Isabelle of France</a>, the sister of <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a>, in 1263.  After the first biography written by her brother, a second was composed by a woman named Stefania, who had been a leader of the community stemming from Margherita's followers.
</p>
