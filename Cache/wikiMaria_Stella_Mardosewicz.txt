<p>The <b>Martyrs of Nowogródek</b>, also known as the <b>Blessed Martyrs of Nowogródek</b> and the Eleven Nuns of Nowogródek or Blessed Mary Stella and her Ten Companions were a group of members of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Holy_Family_of_Nazareth" title="Sisters of the Holy Family of Nazareth">Sisters of the Holy Family of Nazareth</a>, a Polish <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a>, executed by the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a> in August 1943 in <a href="/wiki/Occupied_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Occupied Poland">occupied Poland</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Navahrudak" class="mw-redirect" title="Navahrudak">Navahrudak</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarus</a>). They have been declared <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> by virtue of martyrdom by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 5 March 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Nowogr%C3%B3dek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Sisters of the Holy Family of Nazareth had arrived in <a href="/wiki/Nowogr%C3%B3dek" class="mw-redirect" title="Nowogródek">Nowogródek</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic" title="Second Polish Republic">Second Polish Republic</a>, in 1929 at the request of Zygmunt Łoziński, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">Bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Pinsk" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Pinsk">Roman Catholic Diocese of Pinsk</a>. The Sisters became an integral part of the life of the town. In 1939, Nowogrodek, located at that time in <a href="/wiki/Kresy" title="Kresy">Kresy-part of pre-war Poland</a> (nowadays central Belarus), was annexed by the Soviet Union and incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Belarusian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic" class="mw-redirect" title="Belarusian Soviet Socialist Republic">Belarusian Soviet Socialist Republic</a>. In 1941, the town was occupied by the German army as part of the <a href="/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa" title="Operation Barbarossa">Operation Barbarossa</a>.
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/History_of_Poland_(1939%E2%80%9345)" class="mw-redirect" title="History of Poland (1939–45)">Nazi and Soviet occupations of Nowogrodek</a>, the Sisters invested great effort in preparing the residents of the town for religious services – as <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgical</a> prayer became a beacon of hope amid the hopelessness of the occupation.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Nazi terror in Nowogródek began in 1942 with the extermination of the town's Jewish population as part of <a href="/wiki/Operation_Reinhard" title="Operation Reinhard">Operation Reinhard</a>. Of the 20,000 inhabitants of the town before the war, approximately half were Jews. The Germans murdered about 9,500 of the Jews in a series of "actions" and sent the remaining 550 Jews to slave labor camps. This was followed by <a href="/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_ethnic_Poles" class="mw-redirect" title="Nazi crimes against ethnic Poles">a surge in Polish arrests</a>, then the slaughter of 60 people, including two <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priests</a>. This situation was repeated on 18 July 1943, when more than 120 people were arrested and slated for execution.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The women of the town turned to the Sisters to pray for the prisoners’ release. After discussing the matter, the Sisters unanimously expressed their desire to offer their lives in sacrifice for the prisoners. The Superior of the community, Sister Maria Stella, C.S.F.N., shared the Sisters' decision with their local <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a>, Father Zienkiewicz, telling him: "My God, if sacrifice of life is needed, accept it from us and spare those who have families. We are even praying for this intention." Almost immediately, the plans for the prisoners were changed to deportation to work camps in Germany, with some of them even being released. When the life of Zienkiewicz was threatened, the Sisters renewed their offer, saying, "There is a greater need for a priest on this earth than for us. We pray that God will take us in his place, if sacrifice of life is needed."<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Execution">Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Nowogr%C3%B3dek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Without warning or provocation, on 31 July 1943, the community was summoned by the local Gestapo commander to report to the local police station, where they were held overnight. The next morning, 1 August 1943, they were loaded into a van and driven beyond the town limits.<sup id="cite_ref-11nuns_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11nuns-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At a secluded spot in the woods about 3 miles from the town, the eleven women were machine gunned to death and buried in a common grave. Before reporting to the police station, Sister Stella had asked one member of the community, Sister M. Malgorzata Banas, C.S.F.N., who worked as a nurse in the local public hospital, to stay behind at the convent, whatever happened, to take care of the church and their pastor. She was the best candidate for that among the community as she wore civilian clothing due to her job. It was days before she and the townspeople knew that the Sisters had been killed. Eventually, Banas located their grave, quietly tending to it and the parish church during the war years and during the post-war Soviet occupation, until her death in 1966.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  The Church of the Transfiguration, known as <i>Biała Fara</i> (or White Church), now contains the remains of the eleven Sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Nowogr%C3%B3dek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> process for the eleven Religious Sisters was officially opened on 18 September 1991, and, on 28 June 1999, it was announced by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> that <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> had confirmed that they were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>, having died for others in the name of the Catholic faith. Pope John Paul formally <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> them, along with a group of thirty-three others, on 5 March 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-11nuns_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11nuns-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
