<p><b>Mary Theresa Ledóchowska</b>, SSPC (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Maria Teresa Ledóchowska</i>) (29 April 1863 &#8211; 6 July 1922), was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" title="Polish people">Polish</a> Catholic religious sister and missionary who founded the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_St._Peter_Claver" title="Missionary Sisters of St. Peter Claver">Missionary Sisters of St. Peter Claver</a>, dedicated to service in Africa. She has been <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Mary Theresa was the eldest of seven children. Members of the Polish nobility, she and her siblings – including <a href="/wiki/Wlodimir_Led%C3%B3chowski" title="Wlodimir Ledóchowski">Wlodimir Ledóchowski</a>, <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, and St. <a href="/wiki/Ursula_Led%C3%B3chowska" title="Ursula Ledóchowska">Ursula Ledóchowska</a> – were born in <a href="/wiki/Loosdorf" title="Loosdorf">Loosdorf</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austrian</a> estate that belonged to their parents, Count Antoni Halka-Ledóchowski and Countess Josephine Salis-Zizers.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a young girl, Ledóchowska exhibited a great love of the arts and displayed talent as a writer. She loved society life and would dress in her finest attire to attend the balls which were part of the family's social life. She was educated by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Loreto" title="Sisters of Loreto">Sisters of Loreto</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sankt_P%C3%B6lten" title="Sankt Pölten">Sankt Pölten</a> and displayed a strong Catholic piety, as was typical in her family. This social life continued until both she and father contracted <a href="/wiki/Smallpox" title="Smallpox">smallpox</a> in 1885.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was nursed back to health, but her father succumbed to the disease. After his death, their uncle, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Mieczys%C5%82aw_Halka_Led%C3%B3chowski" class="mw-redirect" title="Mieczysław Halka Ledóchowski">Mieczysław Halka Ledóchowski</a>, took charge of their care.<sup id="cite_ref-na_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-na-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1885 to 1890, in order to help her family, which had fallen into economic difficulties, she obtained the position of <a href="/wiki/Lady-in-waiting" title="Lady-in-waiting">lady-in-waiting</a> to <a href="/wiki/Princess_Alice_of_Parma_(1849%E2%80%931935)" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Alice of Parma (1849–1935)">Princess Alice of Parma</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_of_Tuscany" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duchess of Tuscany">Grand Duchess of Tuscany</a>, at the imperial palace in <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>. While living at court, she attended concerts and balls often. At the same time, she maintained a strict commitment to her faith. Under the guidance of a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who served as <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> to both the princess and her, she was admitted to the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, following its spirituality and its emphasis on venerating the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion of Christ</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after her arrival, two members of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Missionaries_of_Mary" title="Franciscan Missionaries of Mary">Franciscan Missionaries of Mary</a> came to the court seeking financial help for their missionary work. Two <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sister" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sister">religious sisters</a> shared their experiences of working with lepers in Madagascar. The following year, sisters of the same congregation arrived at the court with the same purpose. Their accounts of work in the overseas missions sparked a desire in her to commit herself to similar work. Her interest in the missions increased when she read a pamphlet on Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Charles_Lavigerie" title="Charles Lavigerie">Charles Lavigerie</a>'s anti-slavery campaign.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> had entrusted the <a href="/wiki/Evangelization" class="mw-redirect" title="Evangelization">evangelization</a> of Africa to Lavigerie. She began to publicize his cause, and soon attracted donors.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_service">Missionary service</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Missionary service">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1889 Princess Alice arranged for Ledóchowska to meet Lavigerie. He encouraged her to establish committees throughout Europe in order to combat African slavery. She proceeded to do so, starting them in Salzburg, Sankt Pölten, <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Krakow" class="mw-redirect" title="Krakow">Krakow</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She began to use her literary talent to oppose slavery and protest the inhuman treatment of women then prevalent in Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-CMS_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CMS-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She wrote a novel entitled <i>Zaida</i> to show the terrible consequences of slavery, especially for women.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the same time, she began a mission page in a Catholic periodical. These mission features, called <i>Echo From Africa</i>, were based on letters from missionaries serving in Africa. The page of letters evolved into a monthly magazine, which made its debut in 1889, with her as the publisher, rare for a woman in the 19th century.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The magazine soon became a full-time job, and Ledóchowska left her duties at the imperial court in 1891 so as to devote all of her time and energy to the missions.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ledóchowska took up residence with a community of the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_St._Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Daughters of Charity of St. Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of St. Vincent de Paul</a> in Salzburg. Struggling to find financial support for her project, she lived in near poverty, surviving on a <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebend</a> granted to her by <a href="/wiki/Empress_Elisabeth_of_Austria" title="Empress Elisabeth of Austria">Empress Elisabeth of Austria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundress">Foundress</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundress">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>As the work expanded, Ledóchowska's vision took shape gradually. She began to recruit other women as "auxiliary missionaries," whom she organized in 1894 as the <a href="/wiki/Sodality" title="Sodality">Sodality</a> of St. <a href="/wiki/Peter_Claver" title="Peter Claver">Peter Claver</a> for the African Missions and the Liberation of Slaves, an association of <a href="/wiki/Laywomen" class="mw-redirect" title="Laywomen">laywomen</a>. She placed her work – publicizing the needs of the missions in Africa and raising funds for them – under the patronage of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> missionary, <a href="/wiki/Peter_Claver" title="Peter Claver">Peter Claver</a>, who spent a lifetime in service to the enslaved African people brought to South America, which earned him the title of "Apostle to the Slaves"; he had recently been declared a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-na_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-na-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On April 29, 1894, Pope Leo XIII formally blessed the enterprise, approving the St. Peter Claver Sodality as a <a href="/wiki/Associations_of_the_faithful" class="mw-redirect" title="Associations of the faithful">Pious Association of the Faithful</a>. Out of this society, the auxiliary missionaries developed into a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a>. On 8 September 1897 (the Feast of the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_Mary" title="Nativity of Mary">Nativity of Mary</a>, but also the anniversary of Claver's death), she and her first companions <a href="/wiki/Religious_profession" title="Religious profession">professed</a> their permanent <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> as Missionary Sisters of St. Peter Claver. They adopted the Jesuit Constitutions for their own use.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The new foundress traveled throughout Europe, addressing various conferences and international gatherings of Catholics to speak about the evils of slavery. She became aware of the lack of printed resources available in African languages. Her publishing house began to produce books to answer this need, ranging from <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bibles</a> and dictionaries to hymnals. The number of sisters began to grow and the congregation began to open houses in Africa and around the world.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ledóchowska moved to Rome in order to run her order better from there. She developed <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> but continued to serve the needs of the missions and her congregation. She died on July 6, 1922, in the congregation's <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">motherhouse</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today, the Sisters of St. Peter Claver serve in 23 countries around the globe.<sup id="cite_ref-CMS_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CMS-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The periodical <i>Echo from Africa</i> still appears regularly in several languages.<sup id="cite_ref-na_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-na-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During her own lifetime, Ledóchowska had become known as the "Mother of the African missions." The cause for her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> was opened about 1930. As part of the process, her remains were exhumed and transferred to the chapel of the General Motherhouse in 1934.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> her on October 19, 1975, together with <a href="/wiki/Arnold_Janssen" title="Arnold Janssen">Arnold Janssen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Freinademetz" title="Joseph Freinademetz">Josef Freinademetz</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod" title="Eugène de Mazenod">Eugene de Mazenod</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is the sixth of July.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
