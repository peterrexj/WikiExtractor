<p><b>Bernhard II of Baden</b> (1428 or 1429 at <a href="/wiki/Hohenbaden_Castle" title="Hohenbaden Castle">Hohenbaden Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Baden-Baden" title="Baden-Baden">Baden-Baden</a> &#8211; 15 July 1458 in <a href="/wiki/Moncalieri" title="Moncalieri">Moncalieri</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>), was the second son of Margrave <a href="/wiki/Jacob,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden" title="Jacob, Margrave of Baden-Baden">Jacob of Baden</a> and his wife, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Lorraine,_Margravine_of_Baden-Baden" title="Catherine of Lorraine, Margravine of Baden-Baden">Catherine of Lorraine</a>.  He was born in the late 1420s at Hohenbaden Castle in <a href="/wiki/Baden-Baden" title="Baden-Baden">Baden-Baden</a>.  His exact birth date is unknown.  He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> in 1769.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernhard_II,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bernard II of Baden grew up in a deeply religious family. His father, for example, founded <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fremersberg_Abbey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fremersberg Abbey (page does not exist)">Fremersberg Abbey</a> and expanded the Collegiate Church in Baden-Baden.
</p><p>Bernard II received a careful education, which would prepare him for his later role as a sovereign.  The intent was that he would be Margrave of <a href="/wiki/Pforzheim" title="Pforzheim">Pforzheim</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_of_Eberstein" title="County of Eberstein">Eberstein</a>, <a href="/wiki/Besigheim" title="Besigheim">Besigheim</a> and several <a href="/wiki/Amt_(country_subdivision)" class="mw-redirect" title="Amt (country subdivision)">districts</a> in the northern part of the Margraviate.
</p><p>Bernard II was related to the <a href="/wiki/Habsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Habsburg">Habsburg</a> dynasty via his older brother <a href="/wiki/Karl_I,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden" class="mw-redirect" title="Karl I, Margrave of Baden-Baden">Karl I</a>, who had married <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Austria_(1420%E2%80%931493)" title="Catherine of Austria (1420–1493)">Catherine of Austria</a>, a sister of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick III, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick III</a>.  This relationship should give Bernard access to the imperial court.  But first, he assisted his uncle <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou" title="René of Anjou">René of Anjou</a> in an armed conflict in northern Italy.  According to contemporary sources, he fought bravely.  After his father's death in 1453, he returned to Baden, where he agreed with his brother to give up his claim to part of the margraviate.<sup id="cite_ref-hasenauer_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hasenauer-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Instead, he became Frederick III's personal envoy, despite his young age.
</p><p>Bernard II saw a number of disgraceful situations and tried to alleviate hardship and poverty wherever he could.  He spent most of his income assisting the poor and those in need.  Even during his lifetime he impressed his contemporaries with his unusual piety.
</p><p>Under pressure, after the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople" title="Fall of Constantinople">fall of Constantinople</a> to the Turks in 1453, the imperial Habsburg family began preparing a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> against the expanding <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>.  Bernard II was sent to the European princely houses to promote this project.  He died of the <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a> during one of these visits, on 15 July 1458 in <a href="/wiki/Moncalieri" title="Moncalieri">Moncalieri</a> in northern Italy.  <i>Bernardo</i>, as they call him, is revered to this day by many people in this region.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aftermath">Aftermath</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernhard_II,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Aftermath">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg/220px-Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg/330px-Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg/440px-Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bh_Stadtkirche_Innen_Altar.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>High altar in the church in <a href="/wiki/Babenhausen,_Hesse" title="Babenhausen, Hesse">Babenhausen</a>, donated by Sibylle of Baden.  Bernhard II of Baden is on  the left wing on the inside</div></div></div>
<p>His grave in the St. Mary's Church in <a href="/wiki/Moncalieri" title="Moncalieri">Moncalieri</a> quickly became the destination of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimages</a> by devout Christians.  Allegedly, many miraculous healings occurred at his grave.  He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1769.  On the occasion of Bernard's beatification, Margrave <a href="/wiki/August_Georg_Simpert,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden" class="mw-redirect" title="August Georg Simpert, Margrave of Baden-Baden">August Georg Simpert</a> constructed the <i>Bernhardus</i> fountain in <a href="/wiki/Rastatt" title="Rastatt">Rastatt</a>.  Allegedly, having Bernhard <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> was considered too expensive.<sup id="cite_ref-wettstein_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wettstein-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his beatification, the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Margraviate_of_Baden-Baden" title="Margraviate of Baden-Baden">Margraviate of Baden-Baden</a> chose him as its patron saint and celebrated this event on 24 July 1770;<sup id="cite_ref-wettstein_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wettstein-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Freiburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Freiburg">Archdiocese of Freiburg</a> still reveres him today as its <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a>.  At least one miracle is said to have taken place in Freiburg.<sup id="cite_ref-bz_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bz-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  His feast day is 15 July.
</p><p>His great-niece, <a href="/wiki/Sibylle_of_Baden" title="Sibylle of Baden">Sibylle of Baden</a>, who was married to Count <a href="/wiki/Philipp_III,_Count_of_Hanau-Lichtenberg" title="Philipp III, Count of Hanau-Lichtenberg">Philipp III of Hanau-Lichtenberg</a>, founded a high altar in the St. Nicholas church in <a href="/wiki/Babenhausen,_Hesse" title="Babenhausen, Hesse">Babenhausen</a> on which Bernhard is shown on its left wing on the inside.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernhard_II,_Margrave_of_Baden-Baden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> is still being pursued.  On 16 May 2011, Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Robert_Zollitsch" title="Robert Zollitsch">Robert Zollitsch</a> of Freiburg published a public appeal in the official journal of his archdiocese.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Public discussion started on 17 June 2011. On 8 November 2017 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> authorized the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> to issue a decree recognising Bernhard's <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p>
