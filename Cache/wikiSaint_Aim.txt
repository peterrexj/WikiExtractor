<p><b>Saint Amatus</b>, also called <b>St. Aimé</b> or <b>Aimé of <a href="/wiki/Sion,_Switzerland" title="Sion, Switzerland">Sion</a></b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Aim%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born of a wealthy family, Aimé took the monastic habit at the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Maurice,_Agaunum" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Maurice, Agaunum">Abbey of St. Maurice, Agaunum</a>, where with the leave of the abbot, he dwelt in a little cell cut in a rock, with an oratory adjoining, which is now called our Lady's in the rock.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>About the year 669, after serving as <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> Amatus was chosen bishop of <a href="/wiki/Sion,_Switzerland" title="Sion, Switzerland">Sion</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Valais" title="Valais">Valais</a>. He was an accomplished pastor, and here he was abled to distribute alms more plentifully among the poor. He had governed his diocese almost five years, when certain calumnies were spread about him. It was said that he had spoken negatively concerning the Mayor of the Palace, <a href="/wiki/Ebroin" title="Ebroin">Ebroin</a>. Despite the fact that no synod had been assembled to hear him, no sentence of deposition issued out, nor had he been charged with any crime, King <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theuderic III</a> banished him to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fursey" title="Saint Fursey">Saint Fursey</a>’s monastery at <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9ronne,_Somme" title="Péronne, Somme">Péronne</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ultan" title="Saint Ultan">Ultan</a>, the abbot, received him with all respect. Relieved of the responsibility of managing a diocese, Bishop Aimé found tranquility in exile.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Ultan, Abbot Mauront was charged with the custody of the bishop, and took him first to the monastery of Hamaye; but soon after built a new abbey upon an estate of his own, at a place called Breüil in Flanders. Aimé removed with him to Breüil. Mauront welcomed him as a guest, and delegated to him the government of that abbey. Aimé, having settled the house in excellent order, shut himself up in a little cell near the church. Thus he lived five years with these monks, and died in 690.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
Some time before his death in 691, Theuderic came to regret his treatment of Aimé, and in satisfaction made several donations to the abbey of Breüil.   
</p><p>Around 700 <a href="/wiki/Saint_Bain" title="Saint Bain">Saint Bain</a> translated the body of St. Amatus from Merville to the church which St. Maurout had built at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douay</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Aimé is commemorated on September 13.
</p>
