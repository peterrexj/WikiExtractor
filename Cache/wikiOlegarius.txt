<p><b>Olegarius Bonestruga</b> (from <a href="/wiki/Germanic_languages" title="Germanic languages">Germanic</a> <i>Oldegar</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ollegarius, Oligarius</i>, <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Oleguer</i>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Olegario</i>; 1060 – 6 March 1137) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Barcelona">Bishop of Barcelona</a> from 1116 and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Tarragona" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Tarragona">Archbishop of Tarragona</a> from 1118 until his death. He was an intimate of <a href="/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_III,_Count_of_Barcelona" title="Ramon Berenguer III, Count of Barcelona">Ramon Berenguer III, Count of Barcelona</a>, and often accompanied the count on military ventures.
</p><p>Olegarius was canonised in 1675 and his major shrine and sepulchre is in the side chapel of Christ of Lepanto in the cathedral of Barcelona. His feast is celebrated the date of his death: 6 March. An unreliable <i><a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">vita</a></i> was composed for his canonisation, based on a fourteenth-century <i>Vitae sancti Ollegarii</i>, which is based on a lost twelfth-century <i>vita</i> often ascribed to Olegarius' contemporary of Barcelona, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Renald_the_Grammarian&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Renald the Grammarian (page does not exist)">Renald the Grammarian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank160_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank160-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_ecclesiastical_career">Early ecclesiastical career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Olegarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early ecclesiastical career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Olegarius was born to a noble family of <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>. His father was a follower of <a href="/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_I,_Count_of_Barcelona" title="Ramon Berenguer I, Count of Barcelona">Ramon Berenguer I, Count of Barcelona</a>; his mother was Guilia. At the age of ten, Olegarius entered the guild of <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon priests</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Barcelona">Cathedral of Barcelona</a>. He later served as superior (<a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a>) of the canonries of Barcelona and then <a href="/wiki/Sant_Adri%C3%A0_de_Bes%C3%B3s" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant Adrià de Besós">Sant Adrià de Besós</a> (1095–1108), and later as <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> monastery of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint-Ruf&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint-Ruf (page does not exist)">Saint-Ruf</a> (Saint Rufus) in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> (1113–1118).<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank162_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank162-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As abbot of Saint-Ruf, Olegarius had mediated the Mediterranean alliance between the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa" title="Republic of Pisa">Republic of Pisa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giudicato_of_Cagliari" class="mw-redirect" title="Giudicato of Cagliari">Kingdom of Cagliari</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_of_Provence" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Provence">County of Provence</a>, and Barcelona against the <a href="/wiki/Almoravid" class="mw-redirect" title="Almoravid">Almoravid</a> pirates based on the <a href="/wiki/Balearic_Islands" title="Balearic Islands">Balearic Islands</a>, resulting in the <a href="/wiki/1113%E2%80%9315_Balearic_Islands_expedition" class="mw-redirect" title="1113–15 Balearic Islands expedition">expedition of 1113–15</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank162_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank162-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In the <i>Gesta triumphalla per Pisanos, facta de captione Hierusalem et civitatis Mayoricarum</i> of the Pisan deacon Enric (not, as sometimes alleged, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorenzo_Verones&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lorenzo Verones (page does not exist)">Lorenzo Verones</a>), Olegarius' name is misspelled as <i>Nogelarius</i> or <i>Nigelarius</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank162_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank162-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At some point he joined the <i>cofradía</i> (confraternity) of <a href="/w/index.php?title=San_Pedro_de_la_Portella&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="San Pedro de la Portella (page does not exist)">San Pedro de la Portella</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Raymond Berenguer III named him bishop of Barcelona in 1116, and he was consecrated by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Boso_of_Sant%27Anastasia" title="Boso of Sant&#39;Anastasia">Boso of Sant'Anastasia</a> in the cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Maguelone" class="mw-redirect" title="Maguelone">Maguelone</a> in <a href="/wiki/Occitania" title="Occitania">Occitania</a> during the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Paschal_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Paschal II">Paschal II</a>. In 1117 he went to Rome to pay homage to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_II" title="Pope Gelasius II">Pope Gelasius II</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesiastical_reformer_and_leader">Ecclesiastical reformer and leader</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Olegarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ecclesiastical reformer and leader">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>As a churchman Olegarius was of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_reforms" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian reforms">reforming tradition</a>. He was often present at papal synods. He attended <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Toulouse" title="Council of Toulouse">Toulouse</a> in 1119, <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Reims" title="Council of Reims">Rheims</a> in 1120, <a href="/wiki/First_Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="First Lateran Council">First Lateran</a> in 1123, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Narbonne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Narbonne (page does not exist)">Narbonne</a> in 1129, <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont" title="Council of Clermont">Clermont</a> in 1130, and <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Reims" title="Council of Reims">Rheims</a> in 1131.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher43_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher43-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At First Lateran he had been declared legate <i>a latere</i> over the <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">Crusade</a> in New Catalonia (i.e., the province of Tarragone) and began to take the title <i>dispensator</i> or <i>rector</i> of Tarragona.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> At Narbonne the council confirmed the interprovincial archconfraternity (<i>confratrium</i>) for the restoration of the church of Tarragona which Olegarius had established on a more local level a year earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank167_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank167-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Members of the confraternity, lay and ecclesiastical, noble or otherwise, paid membership dues which went to Olegarius' archdiocese.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> At Clermont he probably met <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> and his arguments were influential in the condemnation of <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Anacletus_II" title="Antipope Anacletus II">Antipope Anacletus II</a>. He attended the council of <a href="/w/index.php?title=San_Zoilo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="San Zoilo (page does not exist)">San Zoilo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile" title="Kingdom of Castile">Castile</a> on 4 February 1130.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165n27_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165n27-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1120s Olegarius reformed the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Eulalia_of_Barcelona" title="Eulalia of Barcelona">Santa Eulàlia</a> outside Barcelona, turning it into a community of Augustinian canons.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Indeed, he was extensively involved in the Augustinian reform of the Catalan monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank162_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank162-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1132 he excommunicated the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_de_Ripoll" title="Santa Maria de Ripoll">Santa Maria de Ripoll</a> over the right to exercise justice for crimes committed on the monastery's land.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1133 Olegarius granted the sheets and beds of all deceased clergy to the hospital of <a href="/w/index.php?title=En_Guitard&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="En Guitard (page does not exist)">En Guitard</a> in Barcelona.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Restoration_of_Tarragona">Restoration of Tarragona</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Olegarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Restoration of Tarragona">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After <a href="/wiki/Tarragona" title="Tarragona">Tarragona</a> was <a href="/wiki/Reconquista" title="Reconquista">re-conquered</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a>, on 8 March 1118 Olegarius was consecrated archbishop of Tarragona (remaining bishop of Barcelona) by Gelasius, who as a monk had lived at Saint-Ruf under Olegarius.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank163n17_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank163n17-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He received the bull of confirmation and the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> on 21 March.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank163n17_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank163n17-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He was granted full jurisdiction over <a href="/wiki/Tarragona" title="Tarragona">Tarragona</a> and its countryside by Ramon Berenguer III—through a process, agreed on 23 January 1118, whereby the secular lordship was granted to the church pending reconquest<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>—and also received ecclesiastical administrative rights over the projected diocese of <a href="/wiki/Tortosa" title="Tortosa">Tortosa</a> (which had not yet been conquered) from Pope Gelasius.<sup id="cite_ref-Bisson_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bisson-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He was a close counsellor to Ramon Berenguer III and <a href="/wiki/Ramon_Berenguer_IV,_Count_of_Barcelona" title="Ramon Berenguer IV, Count of Barcelona">Ramon Berenguer IV</a>.
</p><p>At some point after the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Corbins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Battle of Corbins (page does not exist)">Battle of Corbins</a>—a great Catalan defeat—in 1124, Olegarius is said to have gone on a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. He cut his stay short at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> because of concern for Tarragona and had returned by 1127.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Between 1126 and 1130 Olegarius was very active in rebuilding Tarragona, its churches especially.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> He also actively encouraged resettlement and colonisation and laboured to bring in knights and other soldiers for the new territory's defence.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> He "conferred benefices regularly", according to his <i>vita</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In 1126–1127, the period of his greatest activity in New Catalonia, he began encouraging a second Crusade effort. He began by compensating <a href="/wiki/William_V_of_Montpellier" title="William V of Montpellier">William V of Montpellier</a> for the knights he had lent to Barcelona in 1124–1125 and by reconciling William with his son, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernard_IV_of_Melgueil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bernard IV of Melgueil (page does not exist)">Bernard IV of Melgueil</a>, in order to strengthen the anti-Almoravid alliance.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> On 14 March 1129 he ceded this secular authority in the district of Tarragona to <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bordet" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Bordet">Robert Bordet</a>, with whom he had an antagonistic relationship, with the title of <i>princeps Tarraconensis</i>, effectively the archiepiscopate's <a href="/wiki/Vidame" title="Vidame">vidame</a> or <i>defensor</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank167_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank167-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Bisson_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bisson-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Instead Olegarius concentrated on restoring the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan (religion)">metropolitan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bisson_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bisson-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diplomatic_activity">Diplomatic activity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Olegarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Diplomatic activity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1129 Olegarius was drawn into the <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a> then raging between <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">Papacy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Empire</a> and he returned to southern France to be with the pope in exile.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank165_18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank165-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> He was briefly in Barcelona and then in Castile (at San Zoilo) in 1130 before returning to France. He was back in Barcelona for the <i><a href="/wiki/Parliament" title="Parliament">cort</a></i> of 1131, whereat Olegarius successfully petitioned for a restoration of the tithe on the revenues from Barcelona's port, which a new treaty he had negotiated with the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa" title="Republic of Genoa">Republic of Genoa</a> had recently augmented.<sup id="cite_ref-McCrank166_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCrank166-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olegarius helped establish the <a href="/wiki/Knights_Templar" title="Knights Templar">Knights Templar</a> in Catalonia "to serve God and fight in our land" in 1134.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher43_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher43-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1122 he was a signatory at <a href="/wiki/Montearag%C3%B3n,_Toledo" title="Montearagón, Toledo">Montearagón</a> to the foundation charter of the military <a href="/wiki/Confraternity_of_Belchite" title="Confraternity of Belchite">confraternity of Belchite</a>, founded by <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_the_Battler" title="Alfonso the Battler">Alfonso the Battler</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher46_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher46-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> He played an important role in December 1134 when, at <a href="/wiki/Zaragoza" title="Zaragoza">Zaragoza</a>, he brokered a peace between <a href="/wiki/Ramiro_II_of_Aragon" title="Ramiro II of Aragon">Ramiro II of Aragon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VII_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VII of Castile">Alfonso VII of Castile</a>. He also negotiated the marriage alliances between <a href="/wiki/Douce_I,_Countess_of_Provence" title="Douce I, Countess of Provence">Douce I, Countess of Provence</a> and Ramon Berenguer III and of <a href="/wiki/Petronila_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Petronila of Aragon">Petronila of Aragon</a> to Ramon Berenguer IV.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
