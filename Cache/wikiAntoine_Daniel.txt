<div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">17th-century Jesuit missionary and martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the video maker, see <a href="/wiki/Antoine_Daniel_(YouTuber)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antoine Daniel (YouTuber)">Antoine Daniel (YouTuber)</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Antoine Daniel</b> (27 May, 1601 – 4 July, 1648) was a French <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> missionary in North America, at <a href="/wiki/Sainte-Marie_among_the_Hurons" title="Sainte-Marie among the Hurons">Sainte-Marie among the Hurons</a>, and one of the eight <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Martyrs" title="Canadian Martyrs">Canadian Martyrs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoine_Daniel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Daniel was born at <a href="/wiki/Dieppe,_Seine-Maritime" class="mw-redirect" title="Dieppe, Seine-Maritime">Dieppe</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, on 27 May, 1601. After two years' study of <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> and one year of <a href="/wiki/Law" title="Law">law</a>, Daniel entered the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> on 1 October, 1621. He was a teacher of junior classes at the Collège in Rouen from 1623 to 1627.<sup id="cite_ref-Pouliot_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pouliot-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1627 he was sent to the College of Clermont in Paris to study theology. In 1630, Daniel was ordained to the priesthood. He then taught at the College at Eu.<sup id="cite_ref-Rye_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rye-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1632, Daniel and Ambroise Davost set sail for New France. Daniel's brother Charles was a sea-captain in the employ of the De Caen Company of France, representing Protestant-Huguenot interests. Captain Daniel had a French fort on <a href="/wiki/Cape_Breton_Island" title="Cape Breton Island">Cape Breton Island</a> in 1629.<sup id="cite_ref-Spillane_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spillane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They arrived at St. Anne's Bay, Cape Breton, where the two Jesuits remained for a year ministering to the French who had settled there.<sup id="cite_ref-Rye_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rye-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the spring of 1633, Daniel and Davost joined Captain Morieult on his way to Quebec, and arrived there on 24 June. Davost stopped at <a href="/wiki/Tadoussac" title="Tadoussac">Tadoussac</a> on the way, a French trading settlement at the confluence of the Taddoussac and St. Lawrence rivers.<sup id="cite_ref-Rye_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rye-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1634 Daniel travelled to <a href="/wiki/Wyandot_people" title="Wyandot people">Wendake</a> with <a href="/wiki/Jean_de_Br%C3%A9beuf" title="Jean de Brébeuf">Jean de Brébeuf</a> and Daoust. Daniel studied the <a href="/wiki/Wyandot_language" title="Wyandot language">Wendat (Huron)</a> language and made rapid progress. He translated the <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Lord's Prayer</a>, <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">the Creed</a> and other prayers into the Huron native tongue and set them to music. For two years, in what is now <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, he had charge of a school for Indian boys. He returned to Huronia in 1638 to relieve Brébeuf at the new mission.<sup id="cite_ref-ads_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ads-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He returned to Teanaostaye, the chief town of the Huron, in July 1648. Shortly thereafter on 4 July,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Iroquois" title="Iroquois">Iroquois</a> made a sudden attack on the mission while most of the Huron men were away in Quebec trading. The priest rallied the defenders. Before the palisades had been scaled, he hurried to the chapel where the women, children, and old men were gathered. He gave them general <a href="/wiki/Absolution" title="Absolution">absolution</a> and, immersing his handkerchief in a bowl of water, he shook it over them, baptizing the catechumens by aspersion.<sup id="cite_ref-ads_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ads-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daniel, still in his vestments, took up a cross and walked toward the advancing Iroquois. The Iroquois halted for a moment, then fired on him. They put Daniel's body into the chapel, which they had set on fire. Many of the Huron escaped during this incident.<sup id="cite_ref-ads_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ads-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daniel was the first martyr of the missionaries to the Hurons.<sup id="cite_ref-Pouliot_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pouliot-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Father Ragueneau, his superior, wrote of him in a letter to the Superior General of the Jesuits as "a truly remarkable man, humble, obedient, united with God, of never failing patience and indomitable courage in adversity."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoine_Daniel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Daniel and seven other martyrs were <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 29 June 1930.<sup id="cite_ref-Pouliot_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pouliot-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The liturgical celebration of the Holy Martyrs of Canada takes place on 26 September in Canada and 19 October in the universal Church.
</p><p>St. Anthony Daniel Roman Catholic church and parish, Kitchener, Ontario, is named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Elementary schools in Victoria Harbour, Ontario and Willowdale, Ontario are also named after the saint. <sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
