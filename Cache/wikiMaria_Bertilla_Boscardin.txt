<p><b>Maria Bertilla Boscardin</b> (6 October 1888 – 20 October 1922) was an Italian nun and nurse who displayed a pronounced devotion to duty in working with sick children and victims of the <a href="/wiki/Airstrike" title="Airstrike">air raids</a> of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>. She was later <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Bertilla_Boscardin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Bertilla_Boscardin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was born <b>Anna Francesca Boscardin</b> at <a href="/wiki/Brendola" title="Brendola">Brendola</a>, <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>. In her family and town she was known as Annette.<sup id="cite_ref-sicari_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicari-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was a member of a <a href="/wiki/Peasant" title="Peasant">peasant</a> family. Her father, Angelo Boscardin, would testify during her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> process that he was jealous, violent, and frequently drunk. As a child she could only attend school irregularly, as she was needed to help at home and in the fields. When she did attend school she also worked as a servant in a nearby home. She did not display any particular talents, was thought to be not particularly intelligent, and was often the target of insulting jokes. These included being referred to as a "goose" for her slowness by a local clergyman.<sup id="cite_ref-Patrick_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was allowed to make her First Holy Communion at eight and a half years old, when the authorized age in those years was eleven. At twelve years old, she was accepted into the parish association of the “Children of Mary” association.<sup id="cite_ref-sicari_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicari-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The parish priest gave her a catechism as a gift. They found it in the pocket of her habit, when she died, at 34 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-sicari_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicari-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vicenza">Vicenza</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Bertilla_Boscardin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Vicenza">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After being rejected for admission to one order because of her slowness, she was accepted as a member of the Teachers of Saint Dorothy, Daughters of the Sacred Heart at <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a> in 1904, taking the name "Maria Bertilla". She herself internalized some of her earlier criticism, telling the novice-mistress of the order, "I can't do anything. I'm a poor thing, a goose. Teach me. I want to be a saint."<sup id="cite_ref-Patrick_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She worked there as a <a href="/wiki/Kitchen_maid_(domestic_worker)" title="Kitchen maid (domestic worker)">kitchen maid</a> and laundress for three years.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Treviso">Treviso</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Bertilla_Boscardin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Treviso">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Treviso" title="Treviso">Treviso</a> to learn nursing at the municipal hospital there, which was under the direction of her order. During her training period, she was once placed to work in the kitchen. However, upon completing her training, she was promoted to working with victims of <a href="/wiki/Diphtheria" title="Diphtheria">diphtheria</a> in the hospital's children's ward. During the <a href="/wiki/Strategic_bombing" title="Strategic bombing">air raids</a> of Treviso following the disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Caporetto" title="Battle of Caporetto">Battle of Caporetto</a>, the hospital fell under the control of the military. Sister Bertilla was noted for her unwavering care of her patients, particularly those who were too ill to be moved to safety.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This devotion to duty attracted the attention of the authorities of a local military hospital. However, her <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">superioress</a> did not appreciate Sister Bertilla's work and reassigned her to work in the laundry, a position she remained in for four months until being reassigned by a higher superior, who put Sister Bertilla in charge of the children's isolation ward at the hospital.  Shortly thereafter, Sister Bertilla's already poor health got worse. A painful <a href="/wiki/Tumor" class="mw-redirect" title="Tumor">tumor</a> which she had had for several years had progressed to the point of requiring an operation, which she did not survive. She died in 1922.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Bertilla_Boscardin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her reputation for simplicity and devoted, caring hard work had left a deep impression on those who knew her. A memorial plaque placed on her tomb refers to her as "a chosen soul of heroic goodness ... an angelic alleviator of human suffering in this place."<sup id="cite_ref-Patrick_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Crowds flocked to her first grave at Treviso. After a tomb was erected for her at <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a>, it became a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> site where several miracles of healing were said to have taken place.
</p><p>In 1961, 39 years after her death, she was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. The crowd in attendance included members of her family as well as some of her patients.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is October 20.
</p>
