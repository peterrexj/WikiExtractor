<p><b>Saint Brendan of Birr</b> (died c. 572) was one of the early <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Irish monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a>. He was a monk and later an abbot, of the 6th century. He is known as "St Brendan the Elder" to distinguish him from his contemporary and friend St <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan</a> the Navigator of Clonfert.<sup id="cite_ref-bhs_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bhs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Flood_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flood-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> a friend and disciple of <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Saint Columba</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_of_Birr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Early_Christian_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christian Ireland">early Christian Ireland</a> the druid tradition collapsed with the spread of the new faith. Study of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> learning and <a href="/wiki/Christian_theology" title="Christian theology">Christian theology</a> in monasteries flourished. Brendan became a pupil at the monastic school at <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard Abbey</a>. During the sixth century, some of the most significant names in the history of Irish <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> studied at the Clonard monastery. It is said that the average number of scholars under instruction at Clonard was 3,000.<sup id="cite_ref-Flood_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flood-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Twelve students who studied under <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Saint Finian</a> became known as the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>; Brendan of Birr was one of these.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_of_Birr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brendan of Birr is said to have been of a noble Munster family.<sup id="cite_ref-lanigan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanigan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was at Clonard that Brendan became a friend and companion of <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Ciarán of Saigir</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan of Clonfert</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He founded the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Birr,_County_Offaly" title="Birr, County Offaly">Birr</a><sup id="cite_ref-duffy_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in central Ireland in about 540, serving as its abbot.  He emerges from early Irish writings as a man of generous hospitality with a reputation for sanctity and spirituality who was an intuitive judge of character.<sup id="cite_ref-bhs_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bhs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was considered one of the chief prophets of Ireland.  This is evidenced both in his title ('Prophet of Ireland'), and by his attendance at the synod of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Meath" title="Kingdom of Meath">Meltown</a>, in which <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Saint Columba</a> was brought to trial over his role in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_C%C3%BAl_Dreimhne" title="Battle of Cúl Dreimhne">Battle of Cúl Dreimhne</a> in 561.  Brendan spoke on Columba's behalf, prompting the assembled clerics to sentence Columba with exile rather than excommunication.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His friendship and support for Columba resulted in important connections between Birr and the Columban foundations.<sup id="cite_ref-bhs_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bhs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> An adviser of Columba said that the saint saw a vision of Brendan's soul being carried away by angels after his death.  He thereupon ordered for a mass to be said in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-Iona1995_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Iona1995-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The feast day of Brendan of Birr is 29 November.
</p><p>Brendan's monastery at Birr was later to produce the <a href="/w/index.php?title=MacRegol_Gospels&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="MacRegol Gospels (page does not exist)">MacRegol Gospels</a>, which are now housed at the <a href="/wiki/Bodleian_Library" title="Bodleian Library">Bodleian Library</a> in Oxford.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
