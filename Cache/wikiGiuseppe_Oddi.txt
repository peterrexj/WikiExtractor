<p><b>Giuseppe Oddi</b> (6 June 1839 - 3 June 1919) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> professed religious - though not a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> - of the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>. He felt drawn to religious life after his adolescence and was resolved to become a Franciscan upon seeing the example that Mariano da Roccacasale set. Upon his profession of vows he assumed the religious name of "<b>Diego from Vallinfreda</b>".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oddi was beatified - alongside Mariano da Roccacasale - on 3 October 1999.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Oddi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Giuseppe Oddi was born in <a href="/wiki/Vallinfreda" title="Vallinfreda">Vallinfreda</a> on 6 June 1839 to the poor but pious Vincenzo Oddi and Bernardina Pasquali. During his childhood he had a limited education despite the fact he was receptive to learning about his faith to which he became devoted to.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of 20 - in 1859 - he felt as if he were being called to the religious life and announced his desire to his parents to become a professed religious. His parents met this with fierce opposition and denied Oddi this chance. In tears he confronted his father and said: "What am I doing? I want to go to become a saint". After sometime in 1863 while on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> he met Mariano da Roccacasale and was inspired with Mariano's example; this inspired Oddi to become a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> of the Franciscans.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Oddi went to the convent of the Franciscans in <a href="/wiki/Bellegra" title="Bellegra">Bellegra</a> in 1871 and was welcomed as a postulant; he later made his solemn vows in 1889.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oddi was known among his fellow Franciscans for his bright temperament and for his obvious and enriching faith.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His personal holiness spread in reputation throughout Rome and the surrounding areas to the extent that even <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> said of him: "This is a true son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Francis_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Francis of Assisi">Saint Francis</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oddi died at sunset on 3 June 1919 not long before he was to turn 80. His remains were exhumed on 12 November 1931 and were transferred to a new resting place in the presence of 3000 people.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Oddi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The process for Oddi's beatification commenced in <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Lazio" title="Subiaco, Lazio">Subiaco</a> with a diocesan process that opened in 1933 and spanned until 1937; this process was initiated in order to evaluate Oddi's life and to investigate the extent to which he exercised the virtues and led a life of personal holiness. This process occurred despite the fact that the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Rites">Congregation of Rites</a> - under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> - did not grant their formal approval to the initiation of the cause until 1 July 1964 - this conferred upon Oddi the posthumous title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. Not long after another process opened in 1968 and continued the work of the first process until it closed on 26 May 1972.
</p><p>The postulation then compiled the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> as a result of previous investigations and drafted the dossier to attest to his cause and also in order to provide biographical details to Rome who would begin their own investigations; this was sent to Rome in 1987. On 22 January 1991 he was proclaimed to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> after <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> acknowledged the fact that he had lived a model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The miracle required for his beatification was investigated in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Sabina-Poggio_Mirteto" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Sabina-Poggio Mirteto">Sabina-Poggio Mirteto</a> in 1960 and was ratified as being a valid process that completed the work assigned to the set criteria on 27 September 1985. John Paul II approved it on 6 April 1998 as being a legitimate miracle and beatified Oddi on 3 October 1999.
</p>
