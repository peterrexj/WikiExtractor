<p><b>John Rochester</b>, O.Cart (c. 1498–1537) was an English <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He was hanged at York for refusing to concede King Henry VIII's supremacy over the church.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Rochester_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The government was at first anxious to secure the public acquiescence of the monks of the London Charterhouse regarding royal supremacy in ecclesiastical matters, since for the austerity and sincerity of their mode of life they enjoyed great prestige. Having failed in this, the only alternative was to annihilate the resistance since a refusal engaged the prestige of the monks in the opposite sense. On 4 May 1535 the authorities sent to their death at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn_Tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn Tree">Tyburn Tree</a> three leading English <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusians</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Houghton_(martyr)" title="John Houghton (martyr)">John Houghton</a>, prior of the London house, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a>, respectively priors of <a href="/wiki/Beauvale_Charterhouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Beauvale Charterhouse">Beauvale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Axholme" class="mw-redirect" title="Axholme">Axholme</a>. Little more than a month later, it was the turn of three leading monks of the London house: <a href="/wiki/Humphrey_Middlemore" title="Humphrey Middlemore">Humphrey Middlemore</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_Exmew" title="William Exmew">William Exmew</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sebastian_Newdigate" title="Sebastian Newdigate">Sebastian Newdigate</a>, who were to die at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn_Tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn Tree">Tyburn Tree</a> on 19 June. This process of attrition was to claim as its victims no less than fifteen of the London Carthusians.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Rochester_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was the third son of John Rochester, of <a href="/wiki/Terling" title="Terling">Terling</a>, <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a>, and Grisold Writtle, daughter of Walter Writtle, of <a href="/wiki/Bobbingworth" title="Bobbingworth">Bobbingworth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMetcalfe1879622_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfe1879622-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was the brother of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Rochester" title="Robert Rochester">Sir Robert Rochester</a>, <a href="/wiki/Comptroller_of_the_Household" title="Comptroller of the Household">Comptroller of the Household</a> and a member of the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a> under <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Queen Mary</a>.
</p><p>He joined the Carthusians, was a choir monk of the Charterhouse in London, and strenuously opposed the new doctrine of the royal supremacy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Four more monks of the London community were seized; two being taken to the Carthusian house at <a href="/wiki/Beauvale" title="Beauvale">Beauvale</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nottinghamshire" title="Nottinghamshire">Nottinghamshire</a>, while Dom John Rochester and Dom James Walworth were taken to the <a href="/wiki/Charterhouse_of_St._Michael_in_Hull" class="mw-redirect" title="Charterhouse of St. Michael in Hull">Charterhouse of St. Michael in Hull</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pilgrimage_of_Grace">Pilgrimage of Grace</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Rochester_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pilgrimage of Grace">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>That autumn, the government had just succeeded in putting down a rising in <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>, when on 13 October 1536, the far more serious <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage_of_Grace" title="Pilgrimage of Grace">Pilgrimage of Grace</a> broke out, mustering an enormous multitude of adherents, perhaps as many as 40,000. This time, having dealt with the problem, the government was desperate to stamp out any centres of resistance. Since one of the flashpoints had been the Northern capital of York, it was necessary for the government to mount a lesson in the city.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Executions">Executions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Rochester_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Executions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The two London monks were brought from Hull to York and brought before the Lord President of the North, the Duke of Norfolk, on trumped up treason charges. Condemned to death, they provided the desired menacing spectacle for the city when on 11 May 1537 both were hanged and their bodies hung in chains from the city battlements<sup id="cite_ref-brown_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brown-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> until they fell to pieces.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From arrest to death the two monks were inseparable companions in the same fate. They were both beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-brown_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brown-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
