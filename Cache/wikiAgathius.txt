<p><b>Saint Agathius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Ἅγιος Ἀκάκιος; died 303), also known as <b>Acacius of Byzantium</b>, <b>Achatius</b>, or <b>Agathonas</b> to Christian tradition, was a <a href="/wiki/Cappadocian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cappadocian">Cappadocian</a> Greek centurion of the imperial army, martyred around 304. A church existed in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> associated with Acacius and possibly named after him: the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Acacius" title="Church of St Acacius">Church of St Acacius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agathius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agathius was arrested on charges for being a Christian by Tribune Firmus in <a href="/wiki/Perinthus" title="Perinthus">Perinthus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thrace" title="Thrace">Thrace</a>, tortured and then brought to <a href="/wiki/Byzantium" title="Byzantium">Byzantium</a> where he was <a href="/wiki/Scourge" title="Scourge">scourged</a> and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>, being made a martyr because he would not renounce his Christian faith.
</p><p>The date of his martyrdom is traditionally May 8, when his feast is observed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agathius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His relics were relocated <i>ca.</i> 630 to a spring at <a href="/wiki/Squillace" title="Squillace">Squillace</a>, close by the Vivarium, the monastery founded in the previous century by <a href="/wiki/Cassiodorus" title="Cassiodorus">Cassiodorus</a> in the heel of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-geo_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-geo-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  He was known in <a href="/wiki/Squillace" title="Squillace">Squillace</a> as <i>San Agario</i>.  A relic of his arm was brought to <a href="/wiki/Guardavalle" title="Guardavalle">Guardavalle</a> in 1584 by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Squillace" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Squillace">bishop of Squillace</a>, Marcello Sirleto, hence Agathius' patronage of this city.  Relics from Squillace were also brought to <a href="/wiki/Cuenca,_Spain" title="Cuenca, Spain">Cuenca</a> and <a href="/wiki/%C3%81vila,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Ávila, Spain">Ávila</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, where he is known as <i>San Acato</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Agathius is also venerated in <a href="/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia">Slovenia</a>, where numerous churches and chapels are dedicated to him; this popular veneration goes back to the 16th century, when he was considered the patron saint of the fighters against the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Turks" title="Ottoman Turks">Ottoman Turks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> For the same reason he became popular among the <a href="/wiki/Maniots" title="Maniots">Maniots</a>, inhabitants of the <a href="/wiki/Mani_Peninsula" title="Mani Peninsula">Mani Peninsula</a> in <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>, who took up his confrontation of the Pagan Roman authorities as a symbol of their own long lasting resistance of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>'s rule.
</p><p>St. Achatius is one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a> or Auxiliary Saints.
</p>
