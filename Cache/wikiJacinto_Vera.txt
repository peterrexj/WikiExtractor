<p><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <b>Jacinto Vera Durán</b> (Atlantic Ocean, 3 July 1813 – Pan de Azúcar, Uruguay, 6 May 1881) was a <a href="/wiki/Uruguayan_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Uruguayan people">Uruguayan</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> prelate who served as the first bishop of Montevideo.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was an active minister in <a href="/wiki/Uruguay" title="Uruguay">Uruguay</a>, although his efforts to renew the priesthood and other ecclesial initiatives brought him into conflict with the Uruguayan government; this was the cause of his exile from the country, establishing himself in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a> between October 1862 and August 1863, at which point a political change in Uruguay made his return possible. The popularity showcased by his reception continued throughout the rest of his life due to his intense and prolonged missionary work all over the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Montevideo" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Montevideo">diocese of Montevideo</a> (at that time the only one for the whole country), of which he was made bishop upon its creation in 1878. A <a href="/wiki/Jacinto_Vera,_Montevideo" title="Jacinto Vera, Montevideo">neighborhood of Montevideo</a> was named after him in 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His fame of holiness led to the opening of his canonization process in 1935. On 5 May 2015, Pope Francis signed the decree of his <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtues</a>, conferring him the title of <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> upon him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On December 17, 2022, a miracle obtained through his intercession was approved<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and his beatification authorized; it was celebrated on 6 May 2023, in Montevideo.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacinto_Vera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jacinto Vera y Durán was born in mid-1813 on board a boat on the <a href="/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean" title="Atlantic Ocean">Atlantic Ocean</a> to Gerardo Vera and Josefa Durán; the boat had been taking his parents from their place of origin in the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a> to <a href="/wiki/Uruguay" title="Uruguay">Uruguay</a>. The infant was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> during a boat stop at Nossa Senhora do Desterro in <a href="/wiki/Florian%C3%B3polis" title="Florianópolis">Florianópolis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>. His siblings included his sisters María Teodora and Marianna and his brothers Dionisio Antonio de los Dolores and Francisco who died while in Brazil.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1813 Vera lived on a leased farm until his parents purchased their own farm in 1819. He made his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> in the chapel of Our Lady of Carmen called Doña Ana and received his <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(sacrament)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (sacrament)">Confirmation</a> around this stage as was the custom of the time.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1832 he felt the call to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> and from 1836 to 1841 studied under the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> (at the Colegio San Ignacio) in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a> where he became known for his intelligence as well as for his sharp and cheerful persona. He was elevated into the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> on 28 May 1841 and received his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> on 5 June from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Buenos_Aires" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Buenos Aires">Bishop of Buenos Aires</a> Mariano Medrano y Cabrera. Vera celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 6 June at the church of the Catalinas in Buenos Aires before returning to Uruguay.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 4 October 1859 Vera was appointed as the Vicar Apostolic of Montevideo and he took office on 14 December. He sought renewal among priests and in January 1860 summoned all priests for the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises</a> which he wanted to make a regular thing for their own growth as priests. He made a missionary trip from 25 April 1860 to January 1861 across the nation in order to meet with various people, preach and administer. But complications arose in Montevideo which caused him to be exiled to Buenos Aires from 8 October 1862 until 23 August 1863.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> But he was soon invited to return to his home nation after <a href="/wiki/Venancio_Flores" title="Venancio Flores">Venancio Flores</a> put the offer to him; he was met with a grand welcome upon his return. It was upon his return that the <a href="/wiki/President_of_Uruguay" title="President of Uruguay">Acting President</a> <a href="/wiki/Atanasio_Aguirre" title="Atanasio Aguirre">Atanasio Aguirre</a> asked <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> to name Vera as a bishop. The pope accepted this recommendation in 1864 when he named Vera as the Titular Bishop of Megara on 22 September which prompted Vera to receive his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> later on 16 July 1865.
</p><p>In 1867 Vera left for <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to participate in the XIX centennial of the death of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and went on a long tour of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> while also visiting neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>. It was also around this point that he made a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. In 1869 he set off to Rome again to participate in the <a href="/wiki/First_Vatican_Council" title="First Vatican Council">First Vatican Council</a> and was there until the council closed in 1870 with the loss of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 10-17 July 1871 Vera carried out a peace mission between General Timoteo Aparicio and President <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_Batlle_y_Grau" title="Lorenzo Batlle y Grau">Lorenzo Batlle y Grau</a> but this mediation failed and led to the <a href="/wiki/Revolution_of_the_Lances" title="Revolution of the Lances">Revolution of the Lances</a>. Vera supported the return of the Jesuits to the nation who settled in Montevideo on 3 September 1872 while also allowing for the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a> to come into the country with that order arriving on 26 December 1876; Bishop Vera sometimes corresponded with <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">Saint Giovanni Bosco</a>. Bishop Vera's posting as the vicar apostolic and the titular bishop ended on 15 July 1878 when the new <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> named him as the first <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Montevideo" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Montevideo">Bishop of Montevideo</a> after establishing the diocese and dissolving the apostolic vicariate.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bishop died in 1881 while on a mission and his funeral attracted hundreds of individuals who had hailed him as an energetic pastor and a saint known for his intelligence and his personal holiness.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_process">Beatification process</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacinto_Vera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification process">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg/250px-Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="201" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg/375px-Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg/500px-Monse%C3%B1or_Jacinto_Vera_3.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3394" data-file-height="2735" /></a><figcaption>Tomb.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The beatification process opened in the Montevideo archdiocese in an informative process that spanned from 27 July 1935 until its closure not long after in 1942; the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> were given all boxes of documentation at the end of the process but the cause remained inactive until 28 February 1992 when the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated the informative phase. The postulation later compiled and submitted the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> to the C.C.S. in 2012 for further assessment with the dossier spanning well over 2000 pages. Historians approved the cause on 19 February 2013 as did theologians on 18 September 2014 and the C.C.S. members on 5 May 2015.
</p><p>Just moments after the C.C.S. approved the cause it was taken to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> for approval. The pope&#160;— on 5 May 2015 - confirmed that Vera had led a model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p>On 17 December 2022, Pope Francis recognized the miracle attributed to the late bishop's intercession, enabling his for beatification to take place. He was beatified in Montevideo on 6 May 2023 by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Paulo_Cezar_Costa" title="Paulo Cezar Costa">Paulo Cezar Costa</a>, who presided over the celebration on the pontiff's behalf.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is Carlo Calloni.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Debunked_miracle">Debunked miracle</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacinto_Vera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Debunked miracle">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There was a supposed healing of a man dating back to 2005 in Montevideo that some believed to be a miracle from the intercession of the late bishop that could be investigated for his beatification. The Montevideo archdiocese opened a diocesan tribunal for this supposed miracle on 2 August 2015 though doubts about whether the healing was a miracle caused the investigation to cease since it was ruled that there existed evidence that could refute the supposed miraculous circumstances of the case.
</p>
