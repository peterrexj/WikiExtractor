<p><b>Canzo</b> (<small>Italian pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="it-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian" title="Help:IPA/Italian">[ˈkantso]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Lombard_language" title="Lombard language">Lombard</a>: <i lang="lmo">Canz</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="lmo-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Lombard" title="Help:IPA/Lombard">[ˈkãː(t)s]</a></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Canz%C3%A9s" class="mw-redirect" title="Canzés">locally</a></small> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="lmo-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Lombard" title="Help:IPA/Lombard">[ˈkaːnts]</a></span>) is a <i><a href="/wiki/Comune" title="Comune">comune</a></i> (municipality) of the Italian <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Como" title="Province of Como">province of Como</a>. It is the last town north of the historical <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a> region of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, capital of the <a href="/wiki/Lake_Como" title="Lake Como">Lake Como</a> Triangle community and a regional tourism destination.
</p><p>It has 5,192 citizens and an area of 11.8 square kilometres (4.6&#160;sq&#160;mi), a density of 445 persons/km<sup>2</sup> (1,100/sq mi).
</p><p>Its history began in the 5th century BC, when it was founded by <a href="/wiki/Celts" title="Celts">Celts</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gauls" title="Gauls">Gaulish</a> <a href="/wiki/Insubres" title="Insubres">Insubres</a>. Prehistoric settlements date to the <a href="/wiki/Mesolithic" title="Mesolithic">Mesolithic</a> period and the <a href="/wiki/Copper_Age" class="mw-redirect" title="Copper Age">Copper Age</a>. The name of the town comes from the <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> <i>Cantius</i>, itself from the <a href="/wiki/Celtic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic language">Celtic</a> root meaning <i>edge</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is known in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> for its mountains, particularly the <a href="/wiki/Corni_di_Canzo" title="Corni di Canzo">Corni di Canzo</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> (Canzo's Horns) and the <a href="/wiki/Cornizzolo" title="Cornizzolo">Cornizzolo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The surroundings are rich in watercourses and springs. <a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lago del Segrino</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Glacial_lake" title="Glacial lake">glacial lake</a> fed by underground springs.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" </p><p>According to a <a href="/wiki/Statistic" title="Statistic">statistic</a> of the major <a href="/wiki/List_of_business_newspapers" title="List of business newspapers">financial newspaper</a> of Italy,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Canzo is a leader place of <a href="/wiki/Hobby" title="Hobby">hobbies</a> and <a href="/wiki/Security" title="Security">security</a> and the 1st borough for <a href="/wiki/Participation_(decision_making)" class="mw-redirect" title="Participation (decision making)">social vitality</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Geography_of_Italy" title="Geography of Italy">Italian mountains</a>.

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founding_and_pre-Christian_period">Founding and pre-Christian period</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Founding and pre-Christian period">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Lago_del_Segrino_(aprile_2013)_02.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG/250px-Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG/375px-Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG/500px-Lago_del_Segrino_%28aprile_2013%29_02.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2832" data-file-height="2128" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lago del Segrino</a> from the <i>Budracch</i> path, one of the first places near Canzo that was colonized during the <a href="/wiki/Mesolithic" title="Mesolithic">Mesolithic</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The first settlements were between <a href="/wiki/Cornizzolo" title="Cornizzolo">Cornizzolo</a>, Mount Raj and <a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Segrino lake</a>. This site remained inhabited until the 1950s, under the later names of <i>Canza</i> and <i>Sitt di Budracch</i>. The later <a href="/wiki/Celts" title="Celts">Celtic</a> and first <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> foundation were transferred to the valley under the <a href="/wiki/Corni_di_Canzo" title="Corni di Canzo">Corni di Canzo</a> (Canzo's Horns), formed by the course of the river Ravella.
</p><p>The ancient town was on the right side of this river. The oldest extant streets are: <i>Lunaa</i> (from a <a href="/wiki/Celtic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic language">Celtic</a> root meaning "swampland" was nearest to the river); <i>Casargh</i> (probably from <i>Casearium</i>, place for production of cheese and milk-derivatives) was where people lived and <i>Sumbich</i> (<i>summum vicum</i> – the elevated camp) was where the Roman cohorts settled.
</p><p>The presence of Roman soldiers and their integration within the <a href="/wiki/Insubres" title="Insubres">Insubric</a>/Lepontian (partially Orobic) population is still evidenced by toponyms such as <a href="/wiki/Castelmarte" title="Castelmarte">Castelmarte</a> ("the Castle of <a href="/wiki/Mars_(mythology)" title="Mars (mythology)">Mars</a>", the <a href="/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Rome" title="Religion in ancient Rome">Roman god</a> of war) and Martesana (possibly the root of the cult of <a href="/wiki/St._Michael" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Michael">St. Michael</a>, as in the <i>lazzaretto</i> near to the springs of the river Valett). Evidence of pre-Roman cults is found at mountain sites, including stones dedicated to propitiatory <a href="/wiki/Fertility_rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Fertility rites">fertility rites</a> and to female divinities of the waters (see <a href="/wiki/Water_sprite" class="mw-redirect" title="Water sprite">water sprite</a>).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="4th_to_10th_centuries">4th to 10th centuries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: 4th to 10th centuries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A legend says that the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Milan">Church of Milan</a> was founded by <a href="/wiki/St._Barnabas" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Barnabas">St. Barnabas</a>. This is commonly rejected by historians (because apparently <a href="/wiki/St._Barnabas" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Barnabas">St. Barnabas</a> never left <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a> after <a href="/wiki/St._Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Paul">St. Paul</a>'s departure), <a href="/wiki/Archaeology" title="Archaeology">archaeological</a> proof of a Christian presence in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> during <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_times" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic times">apostolic times</a> has been found. The conversion of rural areas came after that of the cities: missionaries were sent into the country where they founded religious colleges called <a href="/wiki/Pieve" title="Pieve">pieve</a> that supplied priests and deacons for the countryside.
</p><p>From the 3rd to the 5th centuries, Christianity was consolidated across Canzo. A church was dedicated to <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stephen">St. Stephen</a>. Another product of this period was widespread veneration of <a href="/wiki/Maternus_(bishop_of_Milan)" title="Maternus (bishop of Milan)">St. Maternus</a>, one of the bishops under whom the area had been converted. During the 3rd century, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Monas" class="mw-redirect" title="Monas">Monas</a> organized the diocese.
</p><p>At this time Canzo probably belonged to <a href="/wiki/Erba_(CO)" class="mw-redirect" title="Erba (CO)">Incino</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Pieve" title="Pieve">pieve</a>, but later the <i><a href="/wiki/Curtis_Casalensis" title="Curtis Casalensis">Curtis Casalensis</a></i> (or Canzo's Team) confederation was established and retained relative political autonomy throughout the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. This pieve or confederation of communes comprised the villages of <a href="/wiki/Caslino_d%27Erba" title="Caslino d&#39;Erba">Caslino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Castelmarte" title="Castelmarte">Castelmarte</a>, <a href="/wiki/Proserpio" title="Proserpio">Proserpio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eupilio" title="Eupilio">Eupilio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Longone_al_Segrino" title="Longone al Segrino">Longone</a> and some <a href="/wiki/Frazioni" class="mw-redirect" title="Frazioni">frazioni</a> (hamlets) of the current <a href="/wiki/Comune" title="Comune">commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Erba_(CO)" class="mw-redirect" title="Erba (CO)">Erba</a>, with Canzo as its capital.
</p><p>The Old Town of Canzo preserves the sites of the ancient communal and pieval system: the seat of the governor and of the federal jails were in the street later named <i>Cuntrada dal pretòri</i>; the communal assembly was in a place that gave its name to the <i>Cuntrada dal cuèrc</i> (street of the porch); a fort, later occupied by the Spanish rulers in their first phase, was probably not far from the <i>pretòri</i>, in the street now known as <i>Turèta</i> (little tower street); another tower was on an elevated site looking toward <i>Crann</i> and the little valley in front of it.
</p><p>The coat of arms of Corte di Casale was similar to others of the zone (such as <a href="/wiki/Pieve" title="Pieve">pieve</a> <a href="/wiki/Valassina" title="Valassina">Vallassina</a>), with diagonal white and red stripes, forming peaks with the top upward and the imperial eagle at the top.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png/170px-Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png" decoding="async" width="170" height="230" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png/255px-Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png/340px-Corte_de_Casale_Canz_Canzo_02.png 2x" data-file-width="857" data-file-height="1161" /></a><figcaption>Coat of arms of <i>Corte di Casale</i>, the confederation led by Canzo during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="11th_to_14th_century">11th to 14th century</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: 11th to 14th century">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During these centuries the rule of Corte di Casale continued.  On 27 April 1162, in an act signed in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, recognized that the possessions of St. Peter's Abbey (built on the east side of mount <a href="/wiki/Cornizzolo" title="Cornizzolo">Cornizzolo</a>) included some fields in Canzo. Contemporaneous documents attest that other acreages formally belonged to <a href="/wiki/Monza" title="Monza">Monza</a>'s chapter; one of them was rented by Nicola Prina. Most of the toponyms remained unchanged until modern times, such as <i>Gèpp longh</i> and <i>Ravèl(a)</i>. The documents testify to the limitation of the feudal power of the <a href="/wiki/Duke" title="Duke">dukes</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishops</a> of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in favour of the communal autonomy of the Corte di Casale, until 1414. This is the reason why this pieve had a different name from the others of western Lombardy: "corte" is the term for a system of families, farms and possessions established from below, rather than a district established by a superior power.
</p><p>In the 14th century Canzo's history was dominated by the life of the Blessed Mir, a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrim</a> recognized as a saint by the population of Canzo and by those he visited during his travels. He was born in Canzo, in the mountainous frazione of <i>Segunt Alp,</i> to the family of Paredis (meaning <i>relatives</i>), probably a hybrid of <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> with the local people who settled on Canzo's Horns and still widely present. After the death of his elderly parents, he was educated by an older hermit who lived in the frazione named Scarenna. In Canzo he served as a spiritual father and peacekeeper, living in the mountains near the springs of the River Ravella, where a church was posthumously dedicated to him. He dedicated a long period to a walking pilgrimage in total poverty to Rome, where he is said to have visited the Pope and (possibly) Saint <a href="/wiki/Bridget_of_Sweden" title="Bridget of Sweden">Bridget of Sweden</a>. He then returned to Canzo, passing by the Lomellina region near <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, where he worked his first attested miracle, summoning rain by his prayers. He worked the same miracle in Canzo when he started toward the north side of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Como" title="Lake Como">Lake Como</a> (where his mother was born). He died in <a href="/wiki/Sorico" title="Sorico">Sorico</a>, where another church was dedicated to him. He may have belonged to the newborn movement of <a href="/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Francis of Assisi">St. Francis of Assisi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Renaissance_and_Spanish_rule">Renaissance and Spanish rule</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Renaissance and Spanish rule">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 7 July 1414, <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Maria_Visconti" title="Filippo Maria Visconti">Filippo Maria Visconti</a>, duke of Milan, brought the former Corte di Casale under the temporal jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a>, and it ceased to be an autonomous authority. This happened with the consent of the men of Canzo, who swore fidelity to the legitimate Archbishop, <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Visconti_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Visconti (archbishop)">Giovanni Visconti</a> and provided him a refuge after he was forced to flee by a pretender to his office. Thus Canzo became the seat of the Archbishop and his court for at least five years.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg/170px-Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="159" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg/255px-Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg/340px-Canzo_in_Leonardo_da_Vinci%27s_manuscript.jpg 2x" data-file-width="571" data-file-height="533" /></a><figcaption>Canzo and Lake Segrino represented in a manuscript by <a href="/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci" title="Leonardo da Vinci">Leonardo da Vinci</a> (mirrored). Notice the mysterious shape of a (flying?) person where Canzo lies.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Canzo's economy had been based on wool weaving, but under its <a href="/wiki/Vassal" title="Vassal">vassalship</a> to the Negronis family, nicknamed "Missaglia", it became the official source of iron for the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan" title="Duchy of Milan">Duchy of Milan</a>'s army, enriching the town. Two schools were established. In this period the commune devised a <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> (which had previously used that of the Corte di Casale): a blue field (also found in Prina's coat of arms) with golden stars and hooves for iron transformation, shaped like beehives.
</p><p>While <a href="/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci" title="Leonardo da Vinci">Leonardo da Vinci</a> was painting <i><a href="/wiki/The_Last_Supper" class="mw-redirect" title="The Last Supper">The Last Supper</a></i> in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, the artistic wave of the <a href="/wiki/Renaissance" title="Renaissance">Renaissance</a> was represented in Canzo by wood carver Giovanni da Canzo and his colleagues Giovanni Ambrogio da Longone, Cristoforo da Proserpio and Francesco Vignarca. They were employed in the construction of the <a href="/wiki/Pipe_organ" title="Pipe organ">pipe organ</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo of Milan">Duomo of Milan</a>. Other wood artists of the former Squadra di Canzo were the Contis from Mariaga and Stefano de Bernardi from Caslino.
</p><p>The wealth of Lombardy attracted the armies of France and <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> into a lengthy battle there in the late 15th and early 16th century. In this period Niccolò Pelliccione, the Canzese captain of a <a href="/wiki/Free_company" title="Free company">free company</a>, fought under the sight of <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Sforza" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco Sforza">Francesco Sforza</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gian_Giacomo_Medici" title="Gian Giacomo Medici">Gian Giacomo Medici</a>. In 1526 Canzo was the theatre of a clash between his company and the <a href="/wiki/Arquebus" title="Arquebus">arquebusiers</a> sent by <a href="/wiki/Antonio_de_Leyva,_Duke_of_Terranova" title="Antonio de Leyva, Duke of Terranova">Antonio de Leyva</a>. After the decisive <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Pavia" title="Battle of Pavia">Battle of Pavia</a>, the Duchy of Milan became a possession of the <a href="/wiki/Habsburgs" class="mw-redirect" title="Habsburgs">Habsburgs</a>. Canzo became part of "the empire where the sun never sets" (after the conquest of the Americas). The new rulers did little to improve the economy, instead imposing taxes to support their wars. Spanish soldiers had a base in Canzo. Three tales of rebellion against Spanish rule by Canzo's population have been handed down from this period. One tells about a man who became a hero, <i>Mornerin</i>, whose name is preserved as the name of a street in the Old Town.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="18th_and_19th_centuries">18th and 19th centuries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: 18th and 19th centuries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Canzo became a centre of iron production for Europe in the 18th and 19th centuries. Thereafter Canzo became an art centre known for its <a href="/wiki/Quality_of_life" title="Quality of life">quality of life</a>. In 1728 the construction of the <a href="/wiki/Baroque" title="Baroque">baroque</a> Parochial <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">Basilica</a> started, expanding an existing church. Its realistic wooden <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a> was praised by <a href="/wiki/St._Charles_Borromeo" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Charles Borromeo">St. Charles Borromeo</a> of Milan during his pastoral visit. The church displayed marble confessionals, three monumental marble statues by Elia Vincenzo Buzzi (an artist of the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo of Milan">Duomo of Milan</a>), a triumphal arch and <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">frescos</a> representing the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stephen">St. Stephen</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Four_evangelists" class="mw-redirect" title="Four evangelists">four evangelists</a>. It also had three portals, two pulpits, two sacristies, many marble balusters and several <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> busts. In the 19th century it was enriched by its pipe organ (1828) provided by Serassi from Bergamo and by the mural pictures of David Beghè. On 21 April, the pope enlarged the titles granted to the parish, giving its vicars the titles of <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monsignore" class="mw-redirect" title="Monsignore">monsignore</a> and encouraging the Archbishop of Milan, <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari" title="Andrea Carlo Ferrari">Andrea Carlo Ferrari</a>, to revive Corte di Casale's rule in matters of ecclesiastical administration. Two important painters of the later 19th century, Carlo Gerosa and <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Segantini" title="Giovanni Segantini">Giovanni Segantini</a>, lived and worked in Canzo. Artistic chapels and monumental graves of the 18th and 19th century are visible in the cemetery. Poets and writers like <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni" title="Alessandro Manzoni">Alessandro Manzoni</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Porta" title="Carlo Porta">Carlo Porta</a> were inspired by this place during visits to their friends' <a href="/wiki/Villa" title="Villa">villas</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg/250px-Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="139" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg/375px-Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg/500px-Segantini_Die_beiden_M%C3%BCtter.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1392" data-file-height="776" /></a><figcaption>"The two mothers", painting by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Segantini" title="Giovanni Segantini">Giovanni Segantini</a>, made within the boundaries of Canzo during his residence.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Canzo became one of the major sites of silk production in the Western world, led by the Marques Crivelli. Spinning mills belonging to the Gavazzi and especially the Verza families were central to this development. Entire families moved there from <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a> as manpower for these factories, receiving an education in the small workers' city created by Giuseppe and Alessandro Verza.
</p><p>In the 19th century the Vallassinese Bank arose from the capital of the Prina family and silk worker Magno Magni who pioneered the Italian chemical industry. In <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a> he founded the society that later invented commercial plastics.
</p><p>The strong moral ideas of <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Parini" title="Giuseppe Parini">Giuseppe Parini</a>, educated in this district, encouraged economist Verri to explain the laws of <a href="/wiki/Supply_and_demand" title="Supply and demand">supply and demand</a> and elaborate the system of modern revenue service, later adopted across the globe. He was a precursor of both <a href="/wiki/Adam_Smith" title="Adam Smith">Adam Smith</a> and <a href="/wiki/Marginalism" title="Marginalism">marginalism</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Philosopher Beccaria published his theory about "punishment as reeducation", proposing the abolition of <a href="/wiki/Capital_punishment" title="Capital punishment">capital punishment</a>. The linguistic work of <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni" title="Alessandro Manzoni">Manzoni</a> and the syndical/political work of <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Turati" title="Filippo Turati">Filippo Turati</a>, born in Canzo in 1857, contributed to the moral unification of Italy that followed the 1861 political change. Many Canzese soldiers participated in the unification under the orders of <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi" title="Giuseppe Garibaldi">Giuseppe Garibaldi</a>. During the 1848 revolution against Austria, Duroni, a patriot from Canzo, was the first to hoist the <a href="/wiki/Italian_flag" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian flag">Italian flag</a> on the highest spire of the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_di_Milano" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo di Milano">Duomo di Milano</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="20th_and_21st_centuries">20th and 21st centuries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: 20th and 21st centuries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1904, deputy <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Turati" title="Filippo Turati">Filippo Turati</a>'s party's support in Parliament to <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Giolitti" title="Giovanni Giolitti">Giovanni Giolitti</a>'s Italian Government led to the approval of important innovations: laws for the protection of female and young workers, on work accidents, illness, old age, data collection/analysis and acceptance of cooperative societies. In 1915, Italy entered the <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> in alliance with France and Great Britain, reconquering the Italian territories of Trentino and Friuli, then occupied by the Austro-Hungarian empire. Many Canzeses died and a monument to them was built in Garibaldi square. Patriotism was strong just after the war. In 1924 veterans' club Gruppo Alpini Canzo was founded, serving as a major cultural, recreational and social feature. It is also involved in ministering to the natural environment.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Autarky" title="Autarky">autarky</a> stimulated the creation of many urban vegetable gardens in Canzo. After <a href="/wiki/Benito_Mussolini" title="Benito Mussolini">Mussolini</a>'s alliance with <a href="/wiki/Adolf_Hitler" title="Adolf Hitler">Hitler</a>, Italy enacted race laws, but the population of Canzo, like those of many other Italian cities, acted to protect their Jewish neighbors. Orlando Prina, official of the <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a>, served in the <a href="/wiki/Greco-Italian_War" title="Greco-Italian War">Greek campaign</a>, then returned to Canzo after the armistice. He worked clandestinely in the <a href="/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement" title="Italian resistance movement">Resistance</a> against German occupation, in spite of the presence of two headquarters of SS corps. After the Nazi's retreat he organized the new Commune, providing for <a href="/wiki/Displaced_person" class="mw-redirect" title="Displaced person">displaced persons</a> (sfollati) bombed out of their homes. The Canzese welcomed those searching for refuge. A moral example was given by parish priest don Pozzoli, when he assisted deserters destined for execution just a few days before Liberation.
</p><p>After the war, the educated class of Canzo restored its quality of life, adding infrastructure and restarting production. Despite a national battle between Catholics and Marxists, in Canzo moderate and peaceful <a href="/wiki/Freedom_of_thought" title="Freedom of thought">freedom of thought</a> was maintained, together with honest collaboration in social matters. The population grew and its density increased, so new districts and houses were built, especially in the plain of the River Ravella, in the areas known as <i>Parisùn</i> and <i>Gerètt</i>. New enterprises were opened, such as the Porroni stone carving company (which created the capitals for Como's Portici Plinio), and manufacturing industries, such as iron scissors. Canzo became Italy's centre of this kind of production together with the nearby village of <a href="/wiki/Premana" title="Premana">Premana</a>. Every street had at least one scissor maker.
</p><p>At the same time sensibility to nature grew. In particular, <a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lake Segrino</a> was protected by ceasing an international <a href="/wiki/Water_skiing" title="Water skiing">water skiing</a> competition, at the expense of immediate financial advantage.
</p><p>In the last quarter of the century Canzo became a centre for organic farming. In 1984 the first example of what would become the <i>BIOFERA</i> began<i>.</i> In 1987, the oldest of Italy's three major "organic festivals" launched.
</p><p>Historic buildings were restored, including St. Francis church, St. Mir's hermitage and Villa Meda. A new chapel was built by the Hunters' Association near the site of the hamlet where Blessed Mir was born.
</p><p>In the 2000s the Alpini founded a polyphonic chorus, and a solidarity fund was established for people touched by the international financial crisis.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Topography">Topography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Topography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The borough of Canzo is situated in front of the Valassina valley, beginning at the foot of the <a href="/wiki/Corni_di_Canzo" title="Corni di Canzo">Corni di Canzo</a> mountain, so called (Corni meaning Horns) for its crest, as bifurcate and rocky, ash-grey, in the shape and color of two horns. After the plain of Erba, Canzo is at the confluence of the two small valleys formed by Mount Barzaghino, Mount Scioscia and Mount Cornizzolo. The first is the site of <a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lake Segrino</a>, formed by glacial movements. The second hosts the town of <a href="/wiki/Caslino_d%27Erba" title="Caslino d&#39;Erba">Caslino</a>. An intermittent spring rises from the side of Corni di Canzo and flows into the river <a href="/wiki/Lambro" title="Lambro">Lambro</a>, whose waters flow south to the <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a> region, pass <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and reach the <a href="/wiki/Po_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Po River">Po River</a>. Elevations range from 360 to 1,371 m above sea level.
</p><p>Geographically, Canzo is roughly the centre of the Larian peninsula (or Lake Como Triangle). The land lies between the two ramifications of Lake Como. One leads to <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a> and one goes straight south and, within the boundaries of <a href="/wiki/Lecco" title="Lecco">Lecco</a>, transforms itself into the second part of the <a href="/wiki/Adda_(river)" title="Adda (river)">Adda</a> River. The third side of the triangle consists of many small lakes, of which <a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lake Segrino</a> is smallest. The mountains of the triangle are pre-Alpine, topped by Mount San Primo at 1,686&#160;m.
</p><p>The classification of the Larian peninsula's mountain range is: Western Alps &gt; North-Western Alps &gt; Lugano Pre-Alps &gt; Como Pre-Alps &gt; Larian Triangle. The chain is divided into three groups: the Mount Palanzone group; Mount San Primo's massif (with sub-group Oriolo) and the Horns group, comprising Canzo's Horns (with the surrounding mountains) and the sub-group of Mount Barro and Mount Crocione (situated over the boundaries of the Larian peninsula). Other mountains are Mount Bollettone, Mount Moregallo, Pizzo dell'Asino, Mount Raj, Mount Boletto, Punta Tre Termini and Mount Broncino.
</p><p>The terrain is mainly composed of <a href="/wiki/Limestone" title="Limestone">limestone</a> strata, rich in caves and caverns, the most famous of which is the "Lead Hole" (Bus del Piomb, Buco del Piombo in Italian). A particular characteristic of this area is the presence of isolated <a href="/wiki/Erratic_boulder" class="mw-redirect" title="Erratic boulder">erratic boulders</a>, pushed down by glaciers that in <a href="/wiki/Prehistory" title="Prehistory">prehistory</a> occupied the Valmasino and Valmalenco valleys. Some were excavated by the primitive population to make tombs ("massi avelli").
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Climate">Climate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Climate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_(Chalet_Segrino).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg/250px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="169" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg/375px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg/500px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_3_%28Chalet_Segrino%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4042" data-file-height="2728" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lake Segrino</a> has a role in moderating hot temperatures in summer and in tempering cold ones during winter.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Canzo lies on a boundary between three climatic typologies: <a href="/wiki/Continental_climate" title="Continental climate">continental climate</a> of Brianza, <a href="/wiki/Alpine_climate" title="Alpine climate">alpine climate</a> produced by the presence of mountains over 1,500 m, and <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean_climate" title="Mediterranean climate">Mediterranean climate</a> of Lake Como.
</p><p>Rains are abundant, exceeding 2&#160;m a year. The rainfall is caused by the <a href="/wiki/Orographic_lift" title="Orographic lift">Stau effect</a> that occurs within depressions, causing strong southern winds and forcing air to rise along the slopes of mountains and to release its humidity as precipitation. During the winter precipitation often falls as snow. Winter is a relatively drier season, while the other three seasons have a uniformly high humidity, with the exception of the dry month of July. Fog may appear for a few days a year.
</p><p>The most frequent winds are from the West. During the spring and autumn, southern winds are responsible for massive precipitation. During winter, winds from the north or east bring dry and cold days; sometimes their interaction with wet tepid masses from the Atlantic or Mediterranean creates the conditions for heavy snowfall. <a href="/wiki/Foehn" class="mw-redirect" title="Foehn">Foehn</a> is a fall or winter wind that can provoke sudden higher temperature. The main local winds are: <i>Breva dal Segrin</i> (from the south), <i>Breva da Caslin</i> (from the west), <i>Vent dal Sant Prim</i> (from the north), and <i>Ariasc di Alp</i> (from the east).
</p><p>Summer is warm, with temperatures around 30&#160;°C. Winters are cold on the whole, and by night the temperature goes below 0&#160;°C, but the protective effect of surrounding mountains and the mitigating effect of Lake Como make the winter cold less severe, with temperatures only a few degrees below 0. During spring and autumn temperatures vary suddenly, but generally are moderate.
</p><p>The flora is composed of oaks, chestnut trees, ironwoods, alders, elms, maples, spruce and firs, with a particularly rich underbrush: strawberries, blueberries and mushrooms. Veronica is relatively warm and suitable for cultivation of grapevines.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lake_and_rivers">Lake and rivers</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Lake and rivers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Lake_Segrino">Lake Segrino</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Lake Segrino">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1096954695/mw-parser-output/.tmulti"><div class="thumb tmulti tleft"><div class="thumbinner multiimageinner" style="width:408px;max-width:408px"><div class="trow"><div class="tsingle" style="width:202px;max-width:202px"><div class="thumbimage"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg/200px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="133" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg/300px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg/400px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4288" data-file-height="2848" /></a></span></div></div><div class="tsingle" style="width:202px;max-width:202px"><div class="thumbimage"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg/200px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="133" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg/300px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg/400px-Lago_del_Segrino_-_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4288" data-file-height="2848" /></a></span></div></div></div><div class="trow" style="display:flow-root"><div class="thumbcaption" style="text-align:center;background-color:"><a href="/wiki/Lago_del_Segrino" title="Lago del Segrino">Lake Segrino</a> shores with reed thickets.</div></div></div></div>
<p>The rocks of Lake Segrino are <a href="/wiki/Calcareous" title="Calcareous">calcareous</a>, produced by the gradual solidification of an ancient <a href="/wiki/Seabed" title="Seabed">seabed</a>. Afterwards, with the continents' geological motion and with the push of Africa against the European plate, the rocks were raised into mountains. "Flutes" are signs of the channels of sediment flow that formed these rocks. Lake Segrino was appreciated by <a href="/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci" title="Leonardo da Vinci">Leonardo da Vinci</a>, and by the Italian writers <a href="/wiki/Ippolito_Nievo" title="Ippolito Nievo">Ippolito Nievo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Fogazzaro" title="Antonio Fogazzaro">Antonio Fogazzaro</a>, who set some of their romances there. At the bridge called <i>Punt d'Inach</i>, the habitat is rich in plant and animal species. In June and August waterlilies bloom on the surface and a community of reed-maces diffuses on the shores. In a place called <i>Schiuntina</i>, on the eastern side of the lake, water birds such as coots, gallinules, wild ducks, herons, nycticoraxes, <i>cannareccione</i>, dunnocks, nightingales and bitterns live. <i>Emis orbicularis</i> is the indigenous <a href="/wiki/Green_turtle" class="mw-redirect" title="Green turtle">green turtle</a>. Myriophylla in the deeper water offer food for birds and a nesting site for perch, jackfish, rudd, <i>savettas</i>, tench, carp and eel. In late summer, myriophylla bloom on the surface. The shelter in the middle of the road offers a panoramic view of the lake's western part. This lake is one of the few habitats of the <a href="/wiki/Italian_agile_frog" title="Italian agile frog">Lataste frog</a> (protected by the EU and UN). The former presence of the sea is revealed by fossils of <a href="/wiki/Radiolarians" class="mw-redirect" title="Radiolarians">radiolarians</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ammonites" class="mw-redirect" title="Ammonites">ammonites</a>. The <i>Fons Sacer</i>, or sacred spring, the main underground source of Lake Segrino's water, gave its name to the entire lake. In this part of the lake common toads nest, and grebes (or loons) swim with their joeys and nightingales sing. In the air are kestrels (or windhovers), hawks and buzzards and eagles. This lake is the clearest and least polluted lake in Europe, due to the absence of influent rivers and the advanced plant-based purification system installed on the northern side. The north end of the lake is marked by the Roman <a href="/wiki/Milestone" title="Milestone">milestone</a>, on which a two sided niche has been constructed, a memorial of the time (in the early 19th century, during the so-called <a href="/wiki/Little_Ice_Age" title="Little Ice Age">Little Ice Age</a>) when a carter fell asleep and his oxen carried his wagon, loaded with stone, onto the ice-covered lake, but the ice did not break.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ravella.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Ravella.jpg/200px-Ravella.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="299" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Ravella.jpg/300px-Ravella.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Ravella.jpg/400px-Ravella.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3872" /></a><figcaption>The River Ravella, a mountain stream ideal for climbing and for geological excursions, photographed not far from Gajum springs.</figcaption></figure>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="River_Lambro_and_River_Ravella">River Lambro and River Ravella</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: River Lambro and River Ravella">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The River Lambro rises in the mountains of the Mt. San Primo group, not far from <a href="/wiki/Madonna_del_Ghisallo" title="Madonna del Ghisallo">Madonna del Ghisallo</a>. The springs are <a href="/wiki/Karst_spring" title="Karst spring">karstic</a> and their name <i>Menaresta</i> means "flows and stops". They rise from a particular stone siphon, situated underground in calcareous rock. Every eight minutes, as observed by <a href="/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder" title="Pliny the Elder">Pliny the Elder</a>, the water fills the siphon and then flows out, producing an intermittent stream for the village of <a href="/wiki/Magreglio" title="Magreglio">Magreglio</a>.
</p><p>The first affluent of Lambro is called Lambretto (coming from Crezzo). It enters Lambro by the village of <a href="/wiki/Lasnigo" title="Lasnigo">Lasnigo</a>. It then runs faster and "cuts" the Vallassina valley, leading to <a href="/wiki/Asso" title="Asso">Asso</a>, where it drives three old flour mills. The Vallassina ends when it receives the flow of the River Foce (coming from Valbrona), which descends in an unexpected waterfall that also marks the beginning of the commune of Canzo.
</p><p>In Canzo the peripheral course of the River Lambro is an important source of irrigation for the Canzese plain frazione of Terra Rossa and for the Canzese part of Scarenna, extending to the edge of Caslino. In Canzo, as in many boroughs of Brianza, the word <i>Lambar</i> became a synonym of "river". Its clarity is proverbial. "Clear as <a href="/wiki/Lambro" title="Lambro">Lambro</a>" is a common saying. Its waters are relatively fresh and constant, compared with those of Ravella.
</p><p>Ravella is a stream from the southern side of the <a href="/wiki/Corni_di_Canzo" title="Corni di Canzo">Corni di Canzo</a>, at about 1,000 m altitude, on the lower edge of Colma dei Corni, where glacial clay crops out from under a mantle of alluvial sediment. It flows down into the Old Town of Canzo. Its basin, lengthened in an east-west direction, is bounded by Cranno's crest, Colletta, Corni, Colma dei Corni (north), and by Maschèrpa and Mt. Prasanto (east). To the south, the <a href="/wiki/Drainage_basin" title="Drainage basin">basin</a> of Ravella includes parts of Mount Raj, Mount Cornizzolo and Pesora. Its affluents are the small rivers of St. Mir and of Valett, the last flowing down from Pesora and entering Ravella not far from St. Francis' church, passing between the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>'s lands and the <i>curt di Sant</i>, flowing under an ancient bridge and entering Ravella between the former Prina and Arcellazzi palaces. The River Ravella, in its upper course, often flows through steep limestone canyons, resulting in small waterfalls and with characteristic "giants' mufflers", produced by the action of glaciers, parallel to the <a href="/wiki/Anticline" title="Anticline">anticline</a> and <a href="/wiki/Syncline" title="Syncline">syncline</a> folds forming the structure of the Lombard pre-Alps.
</p><p>The environment is completely wild as far as Gajum, and is made up of <a href="/wiki/Coppices" class="mw-redirect" title="Coppices">coppices</a>; the quality of its waters is demonstrated by the presence of stream <a href="/wiki/Trout" title="Trout">trout</a>, sometimes also visible under the town bridges in Canzo. The view of the river valley is dominated by the promontory named <i>Cepp da l'Angua</i> or <i>Scalfìn dal diaul</i>, in the shape of an enormous heel, while on the right side you can see, although covered by vegetation, the terraces of the moraines, where <i>Prim Alp</i>, <i>Segunt Alp</i> and <i>Terz Alp</i> are built. A geological nature trail traces the valley, whose attractions are the green rocks ("massi erratici") of <i>serpentite</i> and <i>serizz</i>. After passing the town, the River Ravella flows through the plain of Lambro, and in the frazione of <a href="/wiki/Castelmarte" title="Castelmarte">Castelmarte</a>, also called Ravella, it flows into the Lambro River.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Gajum_Springs">Gajum Springs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Gajum Springs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The springs of Gajum at 485 m are a source of quality drinking water, in the past bottled by a local cooperative. Their water is distributed freely to the local population from two fountains, one near to the source and one in Villa Meda. The springs have been a famous resort for excursionists since the 19th century. Tables and chairs were carved from rocks, built by early tourists. In the woods above the source an ancient chapel is dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Sorrows" title="Our Lady of Sorrows">Our Lady of Sorrows</a> (<i>Madòna di Sètt Duluur</i>). The springs are at the crossroads of the <i>Alp</i> path and St. Mir's path. Their name comes from the local <a href="/wiki/Lombard_language" title="Lombard language">Lombard</a> word <i>gaumm</i> /ga'ym/ (from Celtic <i>ga-</i>, womb), meaning "husk, walnut husk or hull", because a walnut tree overlooked the main fountain. Its nuts fell into the basin of water, floating on it and when ripe, coloring it black. The water attracts people from southern <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Milan" title="Province of Milan">Province of Milan</a> to take quantities home with them.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Architecture">Architecture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Architecture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1096954695/mw-parser-output/.tmulti"><div class="thumb tmulti tright"><div class="thumbinner multiimageinner" <p>Villa Meda is composed of two neoclassical courtyards. St. Francis square features buildings from many architectural periods, particularly the <a href="/wiki/Baroque_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Baroque church">Baroque church</a>, the <i>curt da Pinòla</i> (a medieval poor courtyard), and the ramparts and arch of Villa Meda.
</p><p>The chapel of St. Stephen's church hosts relics and a Renaissance crucifix.
</p><p>The area has two war memorials. City Hall was designed as a modern <i><a href="/wiki/Broletto" title="Broletto">Broletto</a></i> with a massive freestone civil clock tower<i> </i>linked to the ancient Palazzo Tentorio and its park<i>. </i>Old Town is formed by streets called <i>cuntrada</i>, of different ages. Structures feature a variety of portals and other particulars such as <a href="/wiki/Mullion" title="Mullion">mullioned</a> windows, ogives, ribbings, tower-shaped garrets and the Old Theatre.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg/200px-CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg/300px-CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg/400px-CO-Canzo-Santo-Stefano-03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1224" data-file-height="1632" /></a><figcaption>Façade of St. Stephen's parish church.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The surviving 18th-century silk factory is informally known as <i>Filandùn</i> (with the annexed Villa Verza). Other factories include the Pellizzoni factory and the spinning mill near to the water hammer, now a Youth <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">Oratory</a> and Park.
</p><p>Nearby land is terraced with farm cottages named <i>Fabrica</i>, <i>Cà Quarantina</i>, <i>Cà Duglia</i> and <i>Cà Miglia</i>, <i>Cà Bianca</i>. Notable sights include the old market's facade and <a href="/wiki/Portico" title="Portico">portico</a> and the block of houses facing the church. The church's main part is now the Convent of the Nuns, but in the past was composed of notables' palaces (generally 18th century businessmen).
</p><p>Notable villas include Villa Zucchelli, Villa Conti-Ponti, Villa Raverta, Villa Arcellazzi, Villa Gavazzi.
</p><p>Local lifestyles include a reproduction of Milanese living, as in cafés such as Albergo Canzo, Ponti and Citterio. Neoclassical villas span the last three centuries, especially near the boundaries of the southern train station. The stations themselves are neoclassical, with Liberty influences, built around 1900. Another major building of this style is the Teatro Sociale (Social Theatre), built at the end of the 18th century (during Austrian domination) by the architects and artists of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>'s <a href="/wiki/La_Scala" title="La Scala">La Scala</a>. Many palaces in the Old Town preserve on their walls accurate engravings and the typical <a href="/wiki/Ochre" title="Ochre">ochre</a> colour. The Magno Magni villa (1903–1906), built into a park extending from <i>Mirabèla</i> and <i>Grimèll</i>, is a product of the architect Pietro Fenoglio, in a neo-medieval style; the exterior is characterized by its Vicentine stones and by the garden, which contains structures such as medieval-style towers surrounding an elaborate interior. Another style is that of "new urbanization", called  <i>Parisùn</i> or <i>Parisone</i>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Parks">Parks</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Parks">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Parco Barni is the former park of a rich family, separated from the Commune after <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>. It is surrounded by a wall. The original gate is on Via Volta, while two other gates are on Via Roma and on Piazzale Remo Sordo. The interior presents many features of the old villas' parks, such as hiding places, artificial lakes and rivers, and exotic or monumental woods. Its western gate is near to the southern railway station, while the other two gates are near Garibaldi Square. A hidden door links the park with the Social Theatre's yard.
</p><p>Parco Giochi (Games Park) is situated in front of the new market square, named Piazza <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Giovanni XXIII</a>. It contains a game area for children and a sports village with soccer, tennis, basketball and volleyball fields and a café-restaurant-<a href="/wiki/Pizzeria" title="Pizzeria">pizzeria</a>. Nearby Piazzetta <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Turati" title="Filippo Turati">Turati</a> is a small park adjoining the park of Villa Meda. Other parks are in the Parisone area, containing two soccer and athletics stadia. The local teams are US Canzese for the former and ATL for the latter. A children's courtyard, the "Campetto", is furnished for basketball and free play. The <i>Jesus with Children</i> Chapel and the Holy Cross Chapel are there.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Culture">Culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Languages">Languages</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=19" title="Edit section: Languages">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The official language is Italian. About 25% of the Canzese people also speak the <a href="/wiki/Lombard_language" title="Lombard language">Lombard language</a>. Other languages are <a href="/wiki/Arab_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab language">Arabic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romanian_language" title="Romanian language">Romanian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>.
</p><p>Local pronunciation of Italian is similar to that in Milan, with a prevalence of open vowels in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Closed_sillables&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Closed sillables (page does not exist)">closed sillables</a>, as in <i>quèsto</i> (as against Standard Italian <i>quésto</i>, "this (one)") or <i>murètto</i> (as against Standard Italian <i>murétto</i>, "little wall"), the wide use of voiced /dz/ instead of Standard Italian /ts/, as in /ˈdzukːero/ instead of /ˈtsukːero/ ("sugar"), and the softening of <a href="/wiki/Geminates" class="mw-redirect" title="Geminates">geminates</a>. In common with Standard Italian, it strongly distinguishes voiceless from voiced consonants. It is unlike Central and Southern pronunciations, which are unbalanced toward voiced ones.
</p><p>Due to the different pronunciation of <a href="/wiki/Lombard_language" title="Lombard language">Lombard</a>, the people of Canzo do not show the typical Milanese nasal accent. Other regional pronunciations of Italian are also present (especially those of <a href="/wiki/Central_Italy" title="Central Italy">Central Italy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Apulia" title="Apulia">Apulia</a>), along with <a href="/wiki/Venetian_language" title="Venetian language">Venetian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Neapolitan_language" title="Neapolitan language">Neapolitan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sicilian_language" title="Sicilian language">Sicilian languages</a> in a family context.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> is also used in plaques and inscriptions and in liturgical and academic contexts. Almost all schools in the region teach English as a first foreign language, with French, German or Spanish as a second. Knowledge of <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Ancient Greek</a> is widespread. It is taught in <i><a href="/wiki/Liceo_classico" title="Liceo classico">liceo classico</a></i> secondary schools and because of the presence of Byzantine Greek fixed formulas in the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite" title="Ambrosian Rite">Ambrosian Rite</a> of the Catholic liturgy. Traditional songs in <a href="/wiki/Piedmontese_language" title="Piedmontese language">Piedmontese</a>, <a href="/wiki/Venetian_language" title="Venetian language">Venetian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Friulian_language" title="Friulian language">Friulian</a> are sung.
</p><p>The local speech is called <i><a href="/wiki/Canz%C3%A9s_dialect" title="Canzés dialect">Canzés</a></i> or <i>Canzées</i>, a dialect of the <a href="/wiki/Western_Lombard_dialect" title="Western Lombard dialect">Insubric or Western Lombard language</a> It is recognized by <a href="/wiki/UNESCO" title="UNESCO">UNESCO</a> as one of the two branches of Lombard. The central variety of Western Lombard is the <a href="/wiki/Milanese_dialect" title="Milanese dialect">Milanese dialect</a>, which is used as a <a href="/wiki/Lingua_franca" title="Lingua franca">lingua franca</a> for all Western Lombard speakers. The Canzés dialect culturally belongs to the <a href="/wiki/Brianz%C3%B6%C3%B6_dialect" title="Brianzöö dialect">Brianzöö dialect</a>'s family, as influenced by 19th century Milanese, <a href="/wiki/Comasco_dialect" title="Comasco dialect">Comasco</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lecchese_dialect" title="Lecchese dialect">Lecchese dialects</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Substrata_(linguistics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Substrata (linguistics)">substratum</a> of Canzo's primitive population further separates it from other Brianzöö dialects. For example, in phonetics you can see the prevalence of the vowel /a/ instead of Milanese /e/ or common Brianzoeu /u/. Its lexicon is conservative in comparison with surrounding dialects and modern Milanese. Its grammar employs a wide variety of registers, e.g. <i>noble</i> (similar to Old Milanese, but with strong idioms and conservative pronunciation), <i>commercial</i> (with many influences, especially from Lecchese and southern Brianzöö), <i>peasant</i> (prevalence of idioms and few similarities to Erba and Asso dialects), and <i>alpée</i> (with proper phonetic and lexical idioms). A major difference from Milanese pronunciation is the absence of vowel nasalization, rendered in Canzés with the <a href="/wiki/Velar" class="mw-redirect" title="Velar">velar</a> nasal consonant, and the conservation of /ts/ instead of Milanese /s/, as in the pronunciation of the <a href="/wiki/Toponym" class="mw-redirect" title="Toponym">toponym</a> <i>Canz</i>, along with the absence of Milanese half-geminates. Like the other conservative Brianzoeu dialects, Canzés has a certain number of original Lombard terminations in /tʃ/ (spelled as "cc"): typically, <i>lacc</i> ("milk"), <i>frècc</i> ("cold"), <i>tècc</i> ("roof"), <i>nòcc</i> ("night"), <i>tücc</i> ("everybody").
</p>
<h3><span id="Art.2C_literature.2C_and_performing_arts"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Art,_literature,_and_performing_arts">Art, literature, and performing arts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=20" title="Edit section: Art, literature, and performing arts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Western_Lombard_literature" title="Western Lombard literature">Western Lombard literature</a></div>
<p>Theatre troupes include <i>Filodrammatica Canzese</i> and <i>Mobeel</i>. The Social Theatre hosts theatre companies acting in Italian, Venetian, Lombard, Neapolitan, German languages. Every year on the last Thursday of January Cumpagnia di Nost's <i>Giubiana</i> is presented in the streets, with a traditional script in Lombard.
</p><p>Orchestras and bands include <i>Amicinbanda</i>, <i>Corale e Coretto S. Stefano</i>, <i>Coro e Orchestra MM. M. e C. Colombo</i>, <i>Coro Alpini Canzo</i> and <i>Quijcacanta</i>. Operetta outdoor concerts take place in Parco Barni.
</p><p>Live-music genres that are part of the town's cultural heritage include Alpine Polyphonic Chant, Lyric Music, <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Chant" class="mw-redirect" title="Ambrosian Chant">Ambrosian Chant</a> and Baroque music.
</p><p>Canzese painters include <a href="/wiki/Salvatore_Fiume" title="Salvatore Fiume">Salvatore Fiume</a>, Testa, Raverta, Cremonini (two paintings by whom are sited outdoors in Canzo) and Gerosa. <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Segantini" title="Giovanni Segantini">Giovanni Segantini</a> painted some of his works there.
</p><p>The masterpieces of Canzese literature in the Lombard language are <i>"In ucasiun"</i> by Tiziano Corti, for poetry (monolingual), and <i>"Al föch"</i> by Cinzia Valli, for prose (bilingual with Italian). The former is a collection of circumstance poems, including one bilingual poem (<i>Un restauro preciso e sapiente...</i>), while the latter is an essay on popular wisdom, based on the element of fire.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Festivals">Festivals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Festivals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG/200px-Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="276" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG/300px-Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG/400px-Giubiana_da_Canz_N2007.JPG 2x" data-file-width="720" data-file-height="994" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Giubiana" title="Giubiana">Giubiana</a>'s mask, during her transport to the courthouse.</figcaption></figure>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Giubiana">Giubiana</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=22" title="Edit section: Giubiana">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>One of the most traditional events in Canzo is the <a href="/wiki/Giubiana" title="Giubiana">Giubiana</a>, which is celebrated on the last Thursday of January. It dates to the <a href="/wiki/Insubres" title="Insubres">Insubric people</a> (7th–2nd century BC) and consists of a propitiatory rite for the end of winter. Its name is derived from <a href="/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology)" title="Jupiter (mythology)">Jupiter</a>, the Roman "translation" of an unknown Insubric god of spring. The rite immolates a puppet on a bonfire. The puppet carries all the evils of the past year (similar to the Hebrew "<a href="/wiki/Scapegoat" title="Scapegoat">scapegoat</a>"). During the last two millennia, the manifestation has absorbed other symbolic values, such as the renunciation of idols and the stigmatization of old age. It is diffused throughout <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Varese" title="Province of Varese">Province of Varese</a> and some parts of <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>. In Canzo this feast experienced a revival in the 1980s, thanks to the ethnological research of Cumpagnia di Nost; and in recent decades Canzo's Giubiana has been imitated by many of <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a>'s villages. The three aspects of the nighttime feast, held in the Old Town are:
</p>
<ul><li>a procession from <i>Cipiloeu da San Rocch</i>, in which many characters are seen, such as Anguana (water sprite), devils, witches and symbolic characters such as the Hunter and the Bear. The children follow the cortege with faces coloured white or black, with little bells or iron pots.</li>
<li>a criminal trial of Gubiana the puppet, conducted in Lombard, with witnesses for the prosecution and defence, the harangue of Giubiana's counsel and the reading of Giubiana's threatening last will, the judgement of the <i>Regiuu</i>s (the heads of Canzo's families and Town senators) and the pronouncing of the verdict.</li>
<li>the bonfire and a supper based on <i><a href="/wiki/Risotto" title="Risotto">risotto</a></i> with sausage (<i>lugànega</i>) and <a href="/wiki/Mulled_wine" title="Mulled wine">mulled wine</a> (<i>vin brulé</i>).</li></ul>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="BIOFERA"><i>BIOFERA</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=23" title="Edit section: BIOFERA">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In Villa Meda on the first weekend of September <i>BIOFERA</i> takes place. It is one of the main <a href="/wiki/Organic_farming" title="Organic farming">organic farming</a> events in Italy (with over 10,000 visitors) and a festival of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombard</a> culture. It began in 1988 as an evolution from the Festa di Nost (1983) by the Canzese association Cumpagnia di Nost. <i>BIOFERA</i> offers organic food and drink, with exhibitors from across Europe. Historical, ethnological and geographical books are for sale. Other events include a dramatization laboratory, a ludoteque, performances of traditional songs and exhibitions, games, theatre shows and music laboratories for children. It has an area dedicated to handicrafts with environmentally compatible items, a refreshment area with both vegan and traditional cuisines, and a wellbeing area. Inside the fair free conferences on cultural or spiritual themes take place.
</p>
<h4><span id="Fera_di_.C3.BCsei"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Fera_di_üsei">Fera di üsei</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=24" title="Edit section: Fera di üsei">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The oldest tourist event in Canzo is "Fera di üsei", started in 1963 by the Hunters' Association. It occurs in August, in the area of Piazzale Giovanni XXIII. It consists of a bird exposition, with exhibitors from all Europe. Hunting dog competitions, household pet shows and farm animals such as chicken and turkey. On the first morning a competition of singing birds takes place in Parco Barni. It greatly aided Canzo's tourist development in the second half of the 20th century, along with entertainment events of Azienda Autonoma di Soggiorno, the local section of the Public Tourism Agency, now extinct (with the participation of <a href="/wiki/Mike_Bongiorno" title="Mike Bongiorno">Mike Bongiorno</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adriano_Celentano" title="Adriano Celentano">Adriano Celentano</a> and other stars of the moment). It was later imitated by other towns and villages of Brianza, with minor fairs. Today it attracts people from across Lombardy.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Mir_celebrations">St. Mir celebrations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=25" title="Edit section: St. Mir celebrations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In May, the religious feast of St. Mir takes place on 10 May (according to the <i>Martyrologium Nocomiensis</i>), 21 May (probably the day on which the first recognition of St. Mir's relics occurred) or the second Friday of the month. During the last few decades, the feast has been celebrated on Sunday, with a Mass in the parish church, in which the monastic community of St. Mirett participates. On that occasion, during an evening programme at the Social Theatre, a Committee composed of members of the Commune and of the parish awards a prize to a deserving resident. Every Thursday and Sunday in July and August, a Eucharist is celebrated in the mountain hermitage of St. Mir.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Gruppo_Alpini_Canzo">Gruppo Alpini Canzo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=26" title="Edit section: Gruppo Alpini Canzo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Gruppo <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a> Canzo organizes other public and tourist events, particularly the Chestnut festival in October–November and the meetings on Mount <a href="/wiki/Cornizzolo" title="Cornizzolo">Cornizzolo</a>, along with concerts of its Polyphonic Choir of Alpine songs. Fireworks, concerts, open-air dramas, comic shows are held during summer. Other yearly events are the Feast of the Volunteers (by SOS Canzo), the Patronal feast of <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stephen">St. Stephen</a> (26 December), the <i>Magg</i> (in May, the carrying of a fir from the wood to the town by eighteen-year-olds), the Day of the Sun (<a href="/wiki/Lughnasadh" title="Lughnasadh">Lughnasadh</a>, the traditional men's day and celebration of the agriculture of the mountains) and the <i>Cargà i Alp</i> (route in the mountains, recalling the ancient practice of annually moving animals to the upper stables).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cuisine">Cuisine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=27" title="Edit section: Cuisine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1096954695/mw-parser-output/.tmulti"><div class="thumb tmulti tright"><div class="thumbinner multiimageinner" style="width:348px;max-width:348px"><div class="trow"><div class="tsingle" style="width:172px;max-width:172px"><div class="thumbimage"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg/170px-Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="127" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg/255px-Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg/340px-Abelas_Bastavales_Galicia_011.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="1499" /></a></span></div></div><div class="tsingle" style="width:172px;max-width:172px"><div class="thumbimage"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg/170px-Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="128" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg/255px-Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg/340px-Illicium_verum_macro_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="2736" /></a></span></div></div></div><div class="trow" style="display:flow-root"><div class="thumbcaption" style="text-align:center;background-color:">Left: Hazelnuts, whose flour is necessary to produce <i>Nocciolini di Canzo</i>. Right: <i>Illiucium verum</i>, one of the many aromas of <i>Vespetrò</i>.</div></div></div></div>
<p>The cuisine of Northern <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a> is based upon maize (used in <i><a href="/wiki/Polenta" title="Polenta">polenta</a></i> and cakes), Italian vegetables (used especially in <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">minestras</i></span> and <i><a href="/wiki/Minestrone" title="Minestrone">minestrone</a></i>), pork (<i><a href="/wiki/Salami" title="Salami">salami</a></i> is typical), wine (like that of Montevecchia), <a href="/wiki/List_of_Italian_cheeses" title="List of Italian cheeses">cheeses</a>, milk and butter (or lard), rice (as in <i><a href="/wiki/Risotto" title="Risotto">risotto</a></i>), game and freshwater fish, and other primary products. Canzo has a time-honoured tradition in salami and cold meat production; Canzo's wine, cultivated in the <i>Veronica</i> district, is called <span title="Lombard-language text"><i lang="lmo">ragett</i></span>. Local chicken, cow, goat, rabbit and frogs are typical ingredients. Meals include <i><a href="/wiki/Cassoeula" title="Cassoeula">cassoeula</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Tripe" title="Tripe">tripe</a> (<i>busecca</i>) and salmì, carpione or dry fish; various kinds of soup or broth, sometimes with rice. Birds are eaten with <i>polenta</i>; mushrooms also with <i>polenta</i>, or in a <i><a href="/wiki/Risotto" title="Risotto">risotto</a></i>.
</p><p><i>A</i> typical Canzese dish based on mushrooms is <i>funghi trifolati</i>. One kind of pasta is made with pumpkin instead of wheat flour. A sweet bread, called <i><a href="/wiki/Pan_meino" title="Pan meino">pan meino</a></i>, is characteristic of northern Brianza, along with a kind of fritter, named <i>paradèll</i> in Canzo; fruit mustard is usual with meat. Native products featured in Canzo's dishes include roast throstles, little birds with <i>polenta</i> and <i>poccen de salsa e fongs secch</i>. In the <i>Terz Alp</i> and mountains <i>pulénta e lacc</i> and hard hot <i>polenta</i> in cold milk are favored. <a href="/wiki/Coq-au-vin" class="mw-redirect" title="Coq-au-vin">Coq-au-vin</a> and Boeuf-à-la-mode have special forms there. <span title="Lombard-language text"><i lang="lmo">Témpia cui sciger</i></span> is the temple of a pig cooked in a soup of chickpeas, a dish for <a href="/wiki/All_Souls%27_Day" title="All Souls&#39; Day">All Souls' Day</a>.
</p><p>Canzo is famous for:
</p>
<ul><li>Nocciolini di Canzo (a kind of biscuit)</li>
<li>Vespetrò (a kind of liqueur)</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religion">Religion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=28" title="Edit section: Religion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The prevalent and traditional religion of Canzo's population is <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholicism</a>.
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Men expect from the various religions answers to the unsolved riddles of the human condition, which today, even as in former times, deeply stir the hearts of men: What is man? What is the meaning, the aim of our life? What is moral good, what is sin? Whence suffering and what purpose does it serve? Which is the road to true happiness? What are death, judgment and retribution after death? What, finally, is that ultimate inexpressible mystery which encompasses our existence: whence do we come, and where are we going?</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/Nostra_aetate" title="Nostra aetate">Nostra aetate</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> (1965)</cite></div></blockquote>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg/200px-Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="299" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg/300px-Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg/400px-Statua_chiesa_canzo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3872" /></a><figcaption>White marble sculpture of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> winning against the devil (~1740), in one of the four minor altars of Canzo parish church.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Canzo has a deep heritage of religious witnesses. It is characterized by the <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Commune" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval Commune">Communal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ambrose_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ambrose of Milan">Ambrosian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Natural_law" title="Natural law">Natural law</a> and St. Mir's spiritualities.
</p><p>The main author of the Communal spirituality is Bonvesin de la Riva. One of his verses, transcribed in classic orthography reads:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>Ove l'omm no mètt el coeur e l'ingegn, nient var.</i><br />Where a man does not put his heart and genius, there is no value.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite>Bonvesin de la Riva, <i>Libro delle Tre Scritture</i> (1274)</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>This hymn is an exemplar of Ambrosian spirituality:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>Splendor paternae gloriae, de luce lucem proferens, lux lucis et fons luminis, diem dies illuminans.</i><br />O splendor of my Father's glory, Light brought from light, Light of lights and fountain of light, The day that enlightens day.<br /><i>...in Patre totus Filius, et totus in Verbo Pater.</i><br />...in the Father all the Son, and the whole Father in the Word (<a href="/wiki/Logos" title="Logos">Logos</a>).</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><a href="/wiki/Saint_Ambrose_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ambrose of Milan">Saint Ambrose of Milan</a>, Hymn <i>Splendor paternae gloriae</i> (~350)</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>An example of Natural law doctrine is found in the Italian Constitution is:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Art. 2: The Republic recognises and guarantees the inviolable rights of the person, both as an individual and in the social groups where human personality is expressed.<br />The Republic expects that the fundamental duties of political, economic and social solidarity be fulfilled.<br />Art. 29: The Republic recognises the rights of the family as a natural society founded on marriage.[...]</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy" title="Constitution of Italy">Constitution of the Italian Republic</a> (1945)</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Saint Mir served as an example of love for the poor. The rock on the River Ravella under which he slept is visible next to the path connecting St. Mir's hermitage to <i>Terz Alp</i>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Toponymy">Toponymy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=29" title="Edit section: Toponymy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Place names help reveal the ethnological topography of the area, which concerns the conservation and analysis of the region's <a href="/wiki/Toponomastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Toponomastic">toponomastic</a> heritage. Some of these names are Celtic, some Latin, and other from Longobard, with a specific <a href="/wiki/Western_Lombard_dialects" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Lombard dialects">Western Lombard</a> pronunciation.
</p><p>The Commune of Canzo and the Cumpagnia di Nost created three maps displaying the places of Canzo with their local language <a href="/wiki/Toponyms" class="mw-redirect" title="Toponyms">toponyms</a>. The first map (<i>Mapa di sitt</i>) covers the communal territory and gives the names of the mountains and lesser peaks, of hunting or digging localities, of the springs and rocks, and of ancient main paths and old <i>frazioni</i> (hamlets). The second (<i>Mapa di lööch</i>) gives the toponymy of agricultural fields, while the third (<i>Mapa dal paés vecc</i>) portrays streets, quarters and remarkable places of the Old Town. Some of these are <i>Cèpp da l'Angua</i>, alias <i>Scalfìn dal Diaul</i>, with a double (Celtic and Christian) denomination, the <i>Sass dal Prim Fiöö</i> (stone of the first son), a trace of an ancient cult for successful delivery of children, <i>Crann</i> (a Celtic root meaning "hard"), <i>Alpèt</i>, <i>Alp a vòlt</i>, <i>Repussìn</i> and so on. Some famous fields in Canzo are <i>Tèra russa</i> (Red Earth) and <i>Lagüsc</i> (Little Lake); <i>Parisùn</i> and <i>Gerascia</i>; <i>Castèll</i> and <i>Cà bianca</i>; <i>Grimèll</i>, <i>Mirabèla</i> and <i>Cèpp</i>; <i>Cà Milia</i>, <i>Cà Dulia</i> and <i>Cà növa</i> (<i>Cà</i> means "house" or "farmhouse").  <i>Cuntrada da San Mirètt</i>, <i>Cuntrada dal Cuèrc</i>, <i>Pretòri</i>, <i>Portacinées</i>, <i>Turèta</i>, <i>Bergamasca</i>, <i>Caravazz</i> are a few examples of the Old Town's toponyms.
</p><p>The oldest surnames of Canzo are Carpani, Masciadri, Pellizzoni, Ponti, Prina and Locatelli. The Carpani were one of the main families in the Corte de Casale administration, and have a house in Caslino; a castle appears in their coat of arms. The Masciadri surname is derived from the vocation of wandering peddler; Pellizzoni, in the modern shape, or Pelliccione, as it was during the <a href="/wiki/Cinquecento" title="Cinquecento">Cinquecento</a>, is the surname of a famous free captain who acted virtually as a vassal of Canzo during his life. Ponti/Conti-Ponti was a wealthy family linked with the silk industry. Prina is the oldest surname testified to by a written document, in which they appear as the owners of one third of Canzese territory, in name of the Cathedral of Monza's Capitle. They kept the land until the first years of the 20th century. Locatelli is probably the modern form of the surname Catelli/Catella, found in some medieval documents among Canzo's heads of families and also in a female saint, who was born in Canzo. Nowadays, Paredi and Pina are the most widespread and typical surnames: the first may have originally been the common denomination of a Welsh clan established in Canzo's mountains by the emperors as a border defence. It is famous because Saint Mir belonged to this clan. Pina is said to be a hybrid Spanish-Canzese family (Piña), or a Canzese family that had links with the Spanish administration, and became the most common.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Civic_life">Civic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=34" title="Edit section: Civic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nine sports associations, five performing arts associations and four naturalists' associations exist. Notables:
</p>
<ul><li><i>Gruppo Alpini Canzo.</i> Promotes care of the natural environment, for the spiritual and intellectual education of new generations and volunteers in missions of civil protection. The members are former soldiers of the <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a> corps. They also organize tourist events.</li>
<li><i>Pubblica assistenza SOS.</i> Ambulance and medical aid services using over 150 volunteers that is inspired by principles of Christian charity and solidarity. It was founded in 1979.</li>
<li><i>Gruppo Naturalistico della Brianza.</i> Study, understand and help to resolve environmental problems, emphasizing environmental education. They give lessons in the schools, meet with other associations, and give exhibits, contests and observational trips. It was founded in 1960 by Giorgio Achermann and Adolfo Rancati.</li>
<li><i>Oratorio.</i> An institution of the Catholic Church for youth pastoral care. It offers prayer, catechesis, community experiences, play and sport, cultural openmindedness, charity and social diligence. It has links with the transmission of faith project within the parish community, but is open to all.</li>
<li><i>CAI Canzo.</i> Services for mountain safety and cultural and sports activities concerning alpinism and the mountains.</li>
<li><i>Nonsoloturismo.</i> Centered on tourist promotion. Collaborates with communal agencies in the organization of cultural events, entertainment and the Social Theatre.</li>
<li><i>Fondazione Raverta.</i> A foundation begun in 1997 to conserve the artistic heritage of late watercolour painter Giuseppe Raverta. It cares for the promotion of the arts by organizing conferences, publications, exhibits and concerts.</li>
<li><i>Cumpagnia di Nost.</i> Rescue and defend local culture as an element of exchange with other identities.</li>
<li><i>Associazione Nazionale Carabinieri – Canzo.</i> Active and retired <a href="/wiki/Carabinieri" title="Carabinieri">Carabinieri</a> and their families who promote devotion to the fatherland and the memory of the fallen, and to encourage social responsibility.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sports">Sports</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canzo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=35" title="Edit section: Sports">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><i>Moto Club Canzo</i>, existing since the '50s and refounded in 1975, is the most prestigious <a href="/wiki/Motorcycle_trials" title="Motorcycle trials">motorcycle trials</a> institution in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. It has been a pioneer club in this sport. In 1985 it held the first official <a href="/wiki/Trial_des_Nations" title="Trial des Nations">Trial des Nations</a>, who has been consequently expanded, by proposal of the canzese club itself, to an annual worldwide event. In 1988 it also held the <a href="/wiki/FIM_Trial_World_Championship" title="FIM Trial World Championship">World Championship</a> and in 1992 <a href="/wiki/Tommi_Ahvala" title="Tommi Ahvala">Tommi Ahvala</a>, racer of Moto Club Canzo, was world champion in this discipline. Since 1995 it also helds an unofficial European race called <i>Old Trial Cup</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Among its racers, <a href="/wiki/Tommi_Ahvala" title="Tommi Ahvala">Tommi Ahvala</a>, Xavier Miquel and Donato Miglio.
</p>
