<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For places in Iran, see <a href="/wiki/Ernan,_Iran_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Ernan, Iran (disambiguation)">Ernan, Iran</a>.</div>
<p><b>Ernan</b> (variants include <b>Ernain, Ernin, Ethernanus</b>) is the name of four <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="St._Ernan.2C_Son_of_Eogan"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Ernan,_Son_of_Eogan">St. Ernan, Son of Eogan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ernan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: St. Ernan, Son of Eogan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ernan, Son of Eogan, died around 640. Mentioned in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i> on 1 January, he was a nephew of <a href="/wiki/St._Columba" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Columba">St. Columba</a> and sometime missionary to the Picts. <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His monastery in Ireland was at Druim-Tomma in the district of <a href="/wiki/Drumhome" class="mw-redirect" title="Drumhome">Drumhome</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a>. He is venerated as the patron saint of Killernan, though he may not have visited <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> and also as patron of the parish of Drumhome, where a school has been dedicated to him.  Kilviceuen ("church of the son of Eogan") in <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Mull" title="Isle of Mull">Mull</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and of Kilearnadale in <a href="/wiki/Jura,_Scotland" title="Jura, Scotland">Jura</a>, may have been dedicated in his honor.  In the <i>Scottish Kalendars</i>, his commemoration is assigned to 21 and 22 December.<sup id="cite_ref-edmonds_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edmonds-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Ernan.2C_Abbot_of_Hinba_.28Ernan_of_Hinba.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Ernan,_Abbot_of_Hinba_(Ernan_of_Hinba)">St. Ernan, Abbot of Hinba (Ernan of Hinba)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ernan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: St. Ernan, Abbot of Hinba (Ernan of Hinba)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ernan, Abbot of Hinba lived in the sixth century. An uncle of St. Columba, Ernan was one of the twelve who accompanied Columba from Ireland to <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>. He was appointed head of the community which Columba had established on the island of "<a href="/wiki/Hinba" title="Hinba">Hinba</a>" -which may have been <a href="/wiki/Canna,_Scotland" title="Canna, Scotland">Canna</a>, about four miles northwest of <a href="/wiki/R%C3%B9m" title="Rùm">Rùm</a>, but it may also have been <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Jura" class="mw-redirect" title="Isle of Jura">Jura</a> or <a href="/wiki/Eileach_an_Naoimh" title="Eileach an Naoimh">Eileach an Naoimh</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Garvellachs" title="Garvellachs">Garvellachs</a>, between <a href="/wiki/Scarba" title="Scarba">Scarba</a> and Mull. <i>St Ernan's Kirk</i>, <a href="/wiki/Aridhglas" title="Aridhglas">Aridhglas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Mull" title="Isle of Mull">Isle of Mull</a>, is dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Columba's biographer, Ernán was only abbot for a few days. In the story recorded, he was told by his nephew before leaving to Hinba that he did not expect to see him again in this life, and several days later, Ernán became sick and went back to Iona to see his nephew, according to his own wish. When Columba was told his uncle had returned, Columba happily went out to meet him in the harbour, but when they were only 50 yards distant, Ernán fell down dead.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Ernan_of_Cluvain-Deoghra">St. Ernan of Cluvain-Deoghra</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ernan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: St. Ernan of Cluvain-Deoghra">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Ernan of Cluvain-Deoghra lived in the sixth or seventh century.  Cluvain-Deoghra may have been in <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">Meath</a> or in <a href="/wiki/County_Longford" title="County Longford">County Longford</a>.  His feast day falls on 11 January in the <i>Martyrology of Tallagh</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Ernan_of_Torach">St. Ernan of Torach</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ernan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: St. Ernan of Torach">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ernan of Torach died on 17 August <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;650</span>. He is considered by some scholars one of the disciples of St. Columba. Columba had founded a church and monastery on island of Torach or Tory, off the northwest coast of Donegal. He may have accompanied Columba initially to Torach.  He was chosen to be its <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. He has been identified with <b>Ernan of Cluvain-Deoghra</b>, perhaps erroneously.  Of him, the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> states: "It has been conjectured that this Ernan is identical with the Ernan whose name appears in the epistle of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_IV" title="Pope John IV">John, the pope-elect</a>, to the prelates of northern Ireland in 640. If this be so, he must have been a person of some importance."<sup id="cite_ref-edmonds_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-edmonds-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
