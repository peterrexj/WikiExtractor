<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Trophimus of Arles</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-trophime.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Saint-trophime.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Saint-trophime.jpg/200px-Saint-trophime.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Saint-trophime.jpg/300px-Saint-trophime.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Saint-trophime.jpg/400px-Saint-trophime.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2736" data-file-height="3648" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">Statue of Trophimus. <p>According to <a href="/wiki/Christian_mythology" title="Christian mythology">Catholic lore</a>, <b>Trophimus of Arles</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Trophime</i>) was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arles">bishop of Arles</a>, in today's southern France.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Trophimus_of_Arles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It was an early tradition of the Catholic Church<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> that under the co-Emperors <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> and  <a href="/wiki/Herennius_Etruscus" title="Herennius Etruscus">Herennius Etruscus</a> (251 AD), <a href="/wiki/Pope_Fabian" title="Pope Fabian">Pope Fabian</a> sent out seven bishops from <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, to preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>: <a href="/wiki/Gatianus_of_Tours" title="Gatianus of Tours">Gatien</a> to <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>, Trophimus to Arles, <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Narbonne" title="Paul of Narbonne">Paul</a> to <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saturnin" title="Saturnin">Saturninus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Denis</a> to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austromoine" title="Austromoine">Austromoine</a> to <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Martial" title="Saint Martial">Martial</a> to <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, apparently quoting from the <i>Acta</i> of Saint Saturninus, says in effect that Trophimus arrived in Gaul with the first bishops of Tours, Paris, and other cities after the middle of 3rd century, in the consulate of Decius and Gratus.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From the mid-fifth century<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> local tradition has assimilated Trophimus of Arles with the <a href="/wiki/Trophimus" title="Trophimus">Trophimus</a> mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a> as a companion of <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul the Apostle</a>. The <i>Martyrium romanum</i> identifies him as the disciple of Paul, but the identification is spurious.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> Trophîme, as he is in French, does not rate a biography in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, but the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Trophime" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Trophime">church at Arles dedicated to him</a>, built from the 12th century onwards over a third-century <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>, is one of the monuments of <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque architecture</a> and sculpture in <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a>. In its cloister a corner figure in the north gallery, dated about 1180, represents Trophimus.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Trophimus is considered by the Catholic Church the protector of those with <a href="/wiki/Gout" title="Gout">gout</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pie7916_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pie7916-8">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notes">Notes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Trophimus_of_Arles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Notes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1011085734">.mw-parser-output .reflist{font-size:90%;margin-bottom:0.5em;list-style-type:decimal}.mw-parser-output .reflist .references{font-size:100%;margin-bottom:0;list-style-type:inherit}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-2{column-width:30em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-3{column-width:25em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns{margin-top:0.3em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns ol{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns li{page-break-inside:avoid;break-inside:avoid-column}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-alpha{list-style-type:upper-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-roman{list-style-type:upper-roman}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-alpha{list-style-type:lower-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-greek{list-style-type:lower-greek}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-roman{list-style-type:lower-roman}</style><div class="reflist reflist-lower-alpha">
<div class="mw-references-wrap"><ol class="references">
<li id="cite_note-3"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-3">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">In 417 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zosimus" title="Pope Zosimus">Pope Zosimus</a> wrote letters to the bishops of Gaul, in favour of Patroclus, bishop of Arles. In one he mentions that the Holy See had sent Trophimus into Gaul, where he was the source of "true faith", which implicitly contrasts Trophimus with the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a> current among the Goths in control of Arles at the time Zosimus was writing.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-5"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-5">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The earliest conflation noted by Sabine Baring-Gould, <i>The Lives of the Saints</i>, <i>sub</i> 29 December, was in 450, when the official deputation from Arles reported to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Leo the Great">Pope Leo the Great</a> that Trophimus had been sent out by Saint Peter.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-6"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-6">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Compare <a href="/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis" title="Saint-Denis, Seine-Saint-Denis">Saint Denis</a> of Paris, identified with <a href="/wiki/St_Denis_the_Areopagite" class="mw-redirect" title="St Denis the Areopagite">St Denis the Areopagite</a>.</span>
</li>
</ol></div></div>
