<p><b>Peregrina Mogas Fontcuberta</b> (13 January 1827 - 3 July 1886) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> in the name of "<b>María Ana</b>" and the founder of the Franciscan Missionaries of the Mother of the Divine Shepherd.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mogas Fontcuberta was in a <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Capuchin order">Capuchin</a> congregation before establishing her own order and was under the guidance of <a href="/wiki/Josep_Tous_Soler" title="Josep Tous Soler">Josep Tous Soler</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The beatification cause commenced under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 11 June 1977 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified her on 6 October 1996.<sup id="cite_ref-ET_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ET-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peregrina_Mogas_Fontcuberta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peregrina Mogas Fontcuberta was born in <a href="/wiki/Granollers" title="Granollers">Granollers</a>, a textile and farming city about thirty miles from <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> in Spain's <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a> region on 13 January 1827. She was the third of four children of Llorenç Mogas – a farmer and innkeeper – and Magdalena Fontcuberta.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> mere hours after her birth and made her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> either at the age of six or seven.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her father died when she was seven and her mother only survived him by six years.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her parents died, her childless and widowed paternal aunt Dona Maria Mogas – who was also her godmother – raised Fontcuberta.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The teenager joined many parish activities at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Mar,_Barcelona" title="Santa Maria del Mar, Barcelona">Santa Maria del Mar</a>, and the parish <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> Mosen Gorgas became her confessor.
</p><p>During the political tumult in Barcelona in 1848, Fontcuberta met two nuns of the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Capuchin order">Capuchin</a> congregation living together in a rented flat, and through them met <a href="/wiki/Josep_Tous_Soler" title="Josep Tous Soler">Josep Tous Soler</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Vic" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Vic">Bishop of Vic</a> Luciano Casadevall had allowed them to open a school in <a href="/wiki/Ripoll,_Girona" class="mw-redirect" title="Ripoll, Girona">Ripoll</a>. Impressed by Father Soler's charisma as well, as their joint dedication to the path of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, Fontcuberta decided to join the religious at the school, and left all she had to follow this calling.<sup id="cite_ref-ET_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ET-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thus, in 1850, despite her confessor's concerns, Fontcuberta went to Ripoll, and was formally clothed in the habit of the Capuchins of the Divine Shepherdess.<sup id="cite_ref-ET_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ET-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The school opened on 27 May 1850. Despite still being in the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> and lacking a formal teaching diploma, less than a month later (on 13 June 1850), Fontcuberta was named the school's superior and formally became such in September 1851. Nonetheless, she returned to the cloistered life in October 1851 to complete her novitiate.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 25 January 1851 she made her profession into the order. She received her teaching <a href="/wiki/Diploma" title="Diploma">diploma</a> in March 1853, then assumed her new religious name of "María Ana" on 25 June.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fontcuberta also met and discussed teaching with <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Maria_Claret" class="mw-redirect" title="Antonio Maria Claret">Antonio Maria Claret</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 10 December 1865 she accompanied four nuns to assist <a href="/wiki/Antonia_de_Oviedo_Sch%C3%B6ntal" title="Antonia de Oviedo Schöntal">Antonia de Oviedo Schöntal</a> with her new religious congregation. In 1868 Fontcubra led a group of religious to Spain's capital city, <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, where they founded a kindergarten.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Despite political unrest (particularly after the assassination of the progressive Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Juan_Prim" title="Juan Prim">Juan Prim</a> in December 1870), the religious persisted in their mission to educate the city's poor and help the sick. Spain's Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Toledo" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Toledo">Archbishop of Toledo</a> (the country's leading prelate) <a href="/wiki/Cirilo_de_Alameda_y_Brea" title="Cirilo de Alameda y Brea">Cirilo de Alameda y Brea</a> formally approved Fontcuberta's founding a new religious order, recognizing it on 16 January 1872 as the Franciscan Missionaries of the Mother of the Divine Shepherd.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peregrina_Mogas_Fontcuberta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fontcuberta died at midnight on 3 July 1886 due to increasing seizures which she had begun suffering in 1878, and which led her to retire to <a href="/wiki/Fuencarral" title="Fuencarral">Fuencarral</a> in May 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:46px;"><a href="/wiki/File:FMMDP-CAYADO.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a7/FMMDP-CAYADO.jpg" decoding="async" width="44" height="162" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="44" data-file-height="162" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:FMMDP-CAYADO.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Logo of the order c. 1900.</div></div></div>
<p>Her order grew and eventually pursued recognition for the foundress. Her order was aggregated with other <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan Orders</a> on 19 June 1906. As of 2005, the order included 669 religious in 105 houses in locations as diverse as <a href="/wiki/Benin" title="Benin">Benin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.
</p><p>Fontcubra received a papal decree of praise on 22 September 1894, and diocesan approval for her potential beatification came on 4 July 1896. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> issued formal approval of the potential cause on 8 August 1899.<sup id="cite_ref-ET_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ET-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Several sisters of the order were martyred for their faith and religious activities in 1936 during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The informative process concerning Mogas Fontcubra commenced in Madrid on 5 March 1949 and concluded on 28 May 1963. Theologians examined all of her theological writings and declared that the latter complied with the magisterium of the faith, as an official decree declared on 21 October 1965.
</p><p>Pope <a href="/wiki/Paul_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul VI">Paul VI</a> declared Fontcuberta a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> on 11 June 1977, after the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> (C.C.S.) began the formal cause. A diocesan historical commission had begun an investigation which took from 1978 to 1988. Historians met on 9 May 1989 to approve the cause, and the C.C.S. validated their process on 1 December 1989. The C.C.S. received the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> in 1990 and passed it onto consulting theologians on 3 June 1994 for their approval. The C.C.S. approved the cause on 18 October 1994. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed M Fontcubra as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 15 December 1994 after confirming her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The process of investigating a miracle spanned from 1989 until 1990 and received C.C.S. validation on 12 February 1993. A medical board approved the miracle on 14 June 1995, and theologians came to the same conclusion on 1 November 1995. After the C.C.S. voted in favor on 5 March 1996, Pope John Paul II approved the result on 25 June 1996 and beatified her on 6 October 1996.
</p><p>A church in Madrid is named to honor Maria Ana Mogas Fontcubra.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
