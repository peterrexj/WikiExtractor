<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Margareta Ebner</b> (1291 – 20 June 1351) was a <a href="/wiki/German_people" class="mw-redirect" title="German people">German</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominican Nuns</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Ebner – from 1311 – experienced a series of spiritual visions in which <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> gave her messages which she recorded in letters and a <a href="/wiki/Diary" title="Diary">journal</a> at the behest of her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a>; she was ill for well over a decade as she experienced these visions. The backdrop of much of Ebner's religious life was the bitter fighting between <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a> and Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Bavarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis the Bavarian">Louis the Bavarian</a>, in which she and her convent faithfully backed Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ebner's beatification cause began in the 1600s well after her death though stalled for a time until 1910 when the initial process was concluded; <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified Ebner in 1979 after confirming her longstanding "cultus" – or popular devotion to her – rather than recognizing a miracle as would be the norm.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margareta_Ebner&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margareta Ebner was born circa 1291 in <a href="/wiki/Donauw%C3%B6rth" title="Donauwörth">Donauwörth</a> to aristocratics; she received a thorough education in her home.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In about 1305 she entered the Kloster Mödingen <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominican Nuns</a> near <a href="/wiki/Dillingen_an_der_Donau" title="Dillingen an der Donau">Dillingen</a> and made her profession around 1306.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1312 to 1325 she suffered a grave illness and in her later <i>Revelations</i> described how she had "no control over herself" and often would laugh or weep on a constant level with sometimes little reprieve. This illness was the stimulus for her conversion to a deeper spiritual life of devotion to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This illness seemed to even put her at the point of death on occasion and even when she seemed to recover she still remained in bed for well over a decade. It was from 1311 that she began to experience her visions of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> who graced her with messages.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ebner became prone to further bouts of illness for the remainder of her life. But she could exercise her desire for <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification</a> via <a href="/wiki/Abstinence" title="Abstinence">abstinence</a> from <a href="/wiki/Wine" title="Wine">wine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fruit" title="Fruit">fruit</a> as well as bathing which were considered some of the greatest pleasures of life in that time.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1320s, the perennial feud between pope and Holy Roman Emperor ramped up again. John XXII excommunicated Louis the Bavarian and placed the empire under an interdict; Louis appointed his own pope. The convent's members were forced to disperse for safety during <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Louis IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Louis IV's</a> campaign against the papal forces.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Ebner took refuge at her old home with her relations. Upon her return her nurse died and she grieved without consolation until the <a href="/wiki/Secular_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Secular priest">secular priest</a> <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_N%C3%B6rdlingen" title="Henry of Nördlingen">Heinrich von Nördlingen</a> assumed her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual direction</a> in 1332.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>But her confessor was often absent due to his personal allegiance to the pope. The correspondence that passed between them is the first collection of this kind in the <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German language</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At his command – beginning during the <a href="/wiki/Advent" title="Advent">Advent</a> of 1344 – she began to write with her own hand a full account of all her revelations and her conversation with the <a href="/wiki/Infant_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Infant Jesus">Infant Jesus</a> as well as all the answers that she had received from him including those given to her in her sleep. Ebner also wrote her visions in the <a href="/wiki/Swabian_dialect" class="mw-redirect" title="Swabian dialect">Swabian dialect</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This <a href="/wiki/Diary" title="Diary">journal</a> is preserved in a <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscript</a> in 1353 at Medingen. Ebner also had extensive correspondence with the noted Dominican <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a> <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Tauler" title="Johannes Tauler">Johannes Tauler</a> as well as <a href="/wiki/Christina_Ebner" title="Christina Ebner">Christina Ebner</a> (no relation). Tauler was considered the leader of the spiritual movement dubbed the <a href="/wiki/Friends_of_God" title="Friends of God">Friends of God</a>. Through her connection with him she has become identified as part of this movement.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From her letters and journal it is learned that she never abandoned her compassion for the Emperor Louis whose soul she learned in a vision had been saved.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Ebner died on 20 June 1351. Her remains are now interred in her old convent in a chapel that had been constructed in 1755.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margareta_Ebner&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG/200px-M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="300" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG/300px-M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG/400px-M%C3%B6dingen_Kloster_Maria_Medingen_Ebner_808.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2520" data-file-height="3776" /></a><figcaption>Ebner's tomb in her old convent.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The beatification cause opened in <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a> in an informative process in 1686 though this seemed to be stalled at some stage until it was later closed centuries later in 1910; theologians had to investigate each of her writings and letters in order to determine those writings adhered to official doctrine. Such contraventions in her writings would have suspended the cause though the theologians approved these on 14 March 1952 having found no fault in them. Historians assigned to the cause also approved it on 12 June 1963 before the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> and their consultants approved the cause on 9 May 1978. The C.C.S. alone then approved the cause on 7 November 1978.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Ebner was beatified on 24 February 1979 after <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed her longstanding and local "cultus" – or popular devotion" – which meant that no miracle would be required for her to be beatified as is the norm.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spiritual_works">Spiritual works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margareta_Ebner&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Spiritual works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ebner's experiences are mainly recorded in the <i>Revelations</i> (or "<i>Offenbarungen</i>") which she composed in 1344–48 with the encouragement of her spiritual advisor Heinrich; it was in this that she recounted the spiritual graces she had received between 1312 and 1348. There are about seven manuscripts which have survived.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There also remain 56 letters that Heinrich had written to Ebner which survive in a single late manuscript though one of her letters to him still survives.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The <i>Revelations</i> preserved in Ebner's own hand became widely known in the 18th century thanks to a selection of her manuscript letters and memoirs compiled by Eustachius Eichenhut. His work is one of the earliest printed expositions of the mystic's doctrines and work.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
