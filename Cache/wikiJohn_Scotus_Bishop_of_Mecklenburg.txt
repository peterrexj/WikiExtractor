					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Scotus_(Bishop_of_Mecklenburg)&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="John Scotus (Bishop of Mecklenburg)">John Scotus (Bishop of Mecklenburg)</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
<b>John Scotus</b> (approx. <a href="/wiki/Anno_Domini" title="Anno Domini">A.D.</a> 990 – 10 November 1066) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Mecklenburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Mecklenburg">Bishop of Mecklenburg</a> from <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. It is likely this John can be identified as the John who was allegedly made <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Glasgow" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Glasgow">Bishop of Glasgow</a> sometime between 1055 and 1060 and possibly the same John allegedly holding the title of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Orkney" title="Bishop of Orkney">Bishop of Orkney</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Scotus_(bishop_of_Mecklenburg)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From approximately 500 AD <a href="/wiki/Slavic_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavic people">Slavonic</a> tribes poured into <a href="/wiki/Mecklenburg" title="Mecklenburg">Mecklenburg</a>. By about 600, they had complete possession of the land. The chief god was Radegast Zuarasici, whose sanctuary at Rethra was the centre of his worship for the whole of Mecklenburg. Charlemagne's conquests in this region were lost soon after his death. <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I of Germany">Henry I of Germany</a> (916-36) was the first to force the Slavonic territory again to pay tribute (circa 928); he also placed it under the jurisdiction of Saxon counts. With the dominion of the Germans, Christianity found ingress into the land.  However, antagonism to the tribute to the empire and the Saxon dukes led to a heathen reaction.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was killed in 1066 during a <a href="/wiki/Wends" title="Wends">Wendish</a> revolt against <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, when he was sacrificed to <a href="/wiki/Radegast_(god)" class="mw-redirect" title="Radegast (god)">Radegast</a>, the god of hospitality.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
