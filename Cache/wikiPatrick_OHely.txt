<p>

<b>Patrick O'Hely</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Pádraig Ó hÉilí</i>) (born between 1543 - 1546, died 31 August, <a href="/wiki/1579_in_Ireland" title="1579 in Ireland">1579</a>) was an Irish Roman Catholic bishop of Mayo, Ireland, who was executed by the English secular authorities.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patrick_O%27Hely&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Dromahair" title="Dromahair">Dromahair</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Leitrim" title="County Leitrim">County Leitrim</a>, and a native of <a href="/wiki/Connacht" title="Connacht">Connacht</a>, Patrick O'Hely joined the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a> at an early age. Four years after his profession he was sent to the Spanish <a href="/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1" title="University of Alcalá">University of Alcalá</a>, where he surpassed his contemporaries in sacred studies.<sup id="cite_ref-ohely_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ohely-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Summoned to Rome, he was promoted in 1576 to the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Mayo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Mayo">bishopric of Mayo</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Webb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> later merged in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Tuam" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Tuam">archbishopric of Tuam</a>. Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory XIII">Gregory XIII</a> empowered him to officiate in adjoining dioceses, if no Catholic bishop were at hand, and supplied him generously with money. At Paris he took part in public disputations at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Sorbonne</a> university, amazing his hearers by his mastery of patristic and controversial theology, as well as of Scotist philosophy.
</p><p>In autumn, 1579, he sailed from <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> and arrived off the coast of <a href="/wiki/County_Kerry" title="County Kerry">Kerry</a> after <a href="/wiki/James_Fitzmaurice_Fitzgerald" class="mw-redirect" title="James Fitzmaurice Fitzgerald">James Fitzmaurice</a> had landed at <a href="/wiki/Smerwick" class="mw-redirect" title="Smerwick">Smerwick</a> from Portugal with the remnant of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Stukeley" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Stukeley">Thomas Stukeley</a>'s expedition. All <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> was then in arms. 
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/FitzGerald_dynasty" title="FitzGerald dynasty">House of Desmond</a> was divided, and the Earl had withdrawn from the scene of action. The bishop and his companion, <b>Conn O'Rourke</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish:</a> <i>Conn Ó Ruairc,</i> born c. 1549), a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Brian_O%27Rourke" title="Brian O&#39;Rourke">Brian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_Breifne" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of Breifne">Lord of Breifne</a>, came ashore at <a href="/wiki/Dingle_Peninsula" title="Dingle Peninsula">Corca Dhuibhne</a> and sought hospitality at <a href="/wiki/Askeaton" title="Askeaton">Askeaton</a> Castle, where, in the Earl's absence, the Countess entertained them. Next day, they departed for <a href="/wiki/Limerick" title="Limerick">Limerick</a>; but the Countess, probably so instructed, for the <a href="/wiki/Gerald_FitzGerald,_15th_Earl_of_Desmond" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerald FitzGerald, 15th Earl of Desmond">Rebel Earl</a> later claimed the merit afterwards, gave information to the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Mayor" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Mayor">Lord Mayor</a> of <a href="/wiki/Limerick" title="Limerick">Limerick</a>, who three days later seized the two ecclesiastics and sent them to <a href="/wiki/Kilmallock" title="Kilmallock">Kilmallock</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Lords_Justices_of_Ireland" title="Lords Justices of Ireland">Lord Justice</a> Sir <a href="/wiki/William_Drury" title="William Drury">William Drury</a> then was with an army.<sup id="cite_ref-Webb_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As <a href="/wiki/Lord_President_of_Munster" title="Lord President of Munster">Lord President of Munster</a>, Drury had taken severe measures, in one year executing four hundred persons "by justice and <a href="/wiki/Martial_law" title="Martial law">martial law</a>"; some he sentenced "by <a href="/wiki/Natural_law" title="Natural law">natural law</a>, for that he found no law to try them by in the realm". At first, he offered to secure Bishop Ó hÉilí in his see for the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> if he would take the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Supremacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Supremacy">Act of Supremacy</a> and disclose all business of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. The bishop replied that he could not barter his faith for life or honours; his business was to do a bishop's part in advancing the Catholic religion and saving souls. To questions about the plans of the Pope and King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a> for invading Ireland he made no answer, and thereupon was delivered to torture. As he still remained silent, he and Ó Ruairc were sent to instant execution by martial law.
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Conn O'Rourke<br />(Irish: Conn Ó Ruairc)</div><br /><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">O.F.M.</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Franciscan friar, Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1549<br />Brefney, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">31 August 1579 (aged 29 - 30)<br />Outside one of the gates of Kilmallock</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">27 September 1992 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">31 August</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>They were hanged outside one of the gates of Kilmallock on 31 August 1579 and their bodies allowed to remain suspended from the gallows for fourteen days.<sup id="cite_ref-Webb_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

</p>
