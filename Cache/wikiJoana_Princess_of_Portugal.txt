<p><b>Joanna of Portugal</b> (6 February 1452 &#8211; 12 May 1490; <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Santa Joana Princesa</i>, <span style="font-size:85%;">Portuguese pronunciation:&#160;</span><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pt-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese" title="Help:IPA/Portuguese">[ˈsɐ̃tɐ ʒuˈɐnɐ pɾĩˈsezɐ]</a></span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> regent <a href="/wiki/Princess" title="Princess">princess</a> of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Aviz" title="House of Aviz">House of Aviz</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/List_of_Portuguese_monarchs" title="List of Portuguese monarchs">King</a> <a href="/wiki/Afonso_V_of_Portugal" title="Afonso V of Portugal">Afonso V of Portugal</a> and his first wife <a href="/wiki/Isabel_of_Coimbra" title="Isabel of Coimbra">Isabel of Coimbra</a>. She served as regent during the absence of her father in 1471. She is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joanna,_Princess_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Joanna was the second child of Afonso, but after the early death of her older brother <a href="/wiki/John,_Hereditary_Prince_of_Portugal_(1451)" title="John, Hereditary Prince of Portugal (1451)">John</a> in 1451, she was recognized as heir presumptive and given the title of <a href="/wiki/Princess_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess of Portugal">Princess of Portugal</a>. Other children of the king were <a href="/wiki/Infantes" class="mw-redirect" title="Infantes">infantes</a>. Upon the birth of her younger brother, the future <a href="/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal" title="John II of Portugal">John II of Portugal</a> in 1455, she ceased to be heir presumptive, but among the people she continued to be known as Princess Joanna.
</p><p>From a young age, Joanna expressed a desire to become a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>; however, as she was second-in-line to the throne, her father did not allow it.<sup id="cite_ref-Capes_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Capes-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>During his military expedition to <a href="/wiki/Tangier" title="Tangier">Tangier</a> in 1471, Joanna served as Regent of the <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_Empire" title="Portuguese Empire">Portuguese Kingdom</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_proposals">Marriage proposals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joanna,_Princess_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage proposals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After vehemently refusing several proposals of marriage, Joanna joined the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominican order">Dominican</a> Convent of Jesus in <a href="/wiki/Aveiro,_Portugal" title="Aveiro, Portugal">Aveiro</a> in 1475.<sup id="cite_ref-Capes_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Capes-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Her brother had, by then, been given an heir, so the family line was no longer in danger of extinction, and thus she entered the convent that same year her nephew was born in 1475. Still, she was compelled several times to leave the convent and return to court. She turned down an offer of marriage from <a href="/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France" title="Charles VIII of France">Charles VIII of France</a>, 18 years her junior. Her father abdicated in 1477, died in 1481, and was succeeded by her brother. 
</p><p>In 1485, she received another offer, from the recently widowed <a href="/wiki/Richard_III_of_England" title="Richard III of England">Richard III of England</a>, who was only 8 months younger. This was to be part of a double marital alliance, with his niece <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_York" title="Elizabeth of York">Elizabeth of York</a> marrying her cousin, the future <a href="/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal" title="Manuel I of Portugal">Manuel I</a>. However, his death in battle, of which Joanna allegedly had a prophetic dream, halted these plans.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Late_life">Late life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joanna,_Princess_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Late life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She continued to be a great supporter of her brother, <a href="/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal" title="John II of Portugal">John II of Portugal</a>, throughout his reign and her life.
</p><p>Joanna died on 12 May 1490 in Aveiro and was buried in the Convent of Jesus in Aveiro. She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1693 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a>. In honor of her beatification, an official account of her life was issued in Italian.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Although she has not been canonized, in Portugal she is known as the Princess Saint Joanna.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Revival">Revival</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joanna,_Princess_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Revival">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the early 18th century, the <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_nobility" title="Portuguese nobility">Portuguese nobility</a>, clergy, and court had a revival in interest in the princess. During this time, the Portuguese artist Manuel Ferreira e Sousa was the most famous artist in this revival. He was contracted by various religious institutions, noblemen, and even the royal family to paint scenes from her life.
</p>
