<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Pelagia</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Holy_virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy virgin">Virgin</a> &amp; <p><b>Pelagia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Πελαγία</span></span>), distinguished as <b>Pelagia of Antioch</b> and <b>Pelagia the Virgin</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">saint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">virgin</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyr</a> who leapt to her death during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> rather than be forced by <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_soldier" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman soldier">soldiers</a> to offer a public <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrifice</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Roman religion">pagan gods</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or to do "something unspeakable (for she was a virgin)", typically inferred as the Roman soldiers attempting to rape her. <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was 15 years old. Originally, her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was celebrated on October 8,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in common with SS&#160;<a href="/wiki/Pelagia_the_Harlot" class="mw-redirect" title="Pelagia the Harlot">Pelagia the Harlot</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pelagia_of_Tarsus" title="Pelagia of Tarsus">Pelagia of Tarsus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, it came to be celebrated on June 9<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and, at <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, she is celebrated on October 5.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1866&quot;8_October:_St._Pelagia,_Penitent&quot;_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1866&quot;8_October:_St._Pelagia,_Penitent&quot;-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span><span class="anchor" id="History"></span><span class="anchor" id="Legend"></span>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pelagia_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pelagia is mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and was the subject of two sermons by <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-chrys_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chrys-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> She was home alone during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian Persecution">Diocletian Persecution</a> when <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_soldier" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman soldier">soldiers</a> arrived. She came out to meet them and, discovering they intended to compel her to participate in a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Roman religion">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrifice</a>, she received permission to change her clothes. She went to the roof of her house and threw herself into the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea" title="Mediterranean Sea">sea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">patristic</a> sources treat this as a sacred <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">martyrdom</a> rather than an ignoble <a href="/wiki/Suicide" title="Suicide">suicide</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pelagia_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pelagia's story was the probable basis for the later dubious accounts of <a href="/wiki/Pelagia_of_Tarsus" title="Pelagia of Tarsus">Pelagia of Tarsus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
