<p><b>Joseph Freinademetz, <a href="/wiki/Divine_Word_Missionaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Divine Word Missionaries">S.V.D.</a>,</b> (<a href="/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters" title="Simplified Chinese characters">simplified Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh-Hans">圣福若瑟</span>; <a href="/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters" title="Traditional Chinese characters">traditional Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh-Hant">聖福若瑟</span>; <a href="/wiki/Pinyin" title="Pinyin">pinyin</a>&#58; <i><span lang="zh-Latn-pinyin">Shèng Fú Ruòsè</span></i>) (April 15, 1852 - January 28, 1908) was a <a href="/wiki/Ladin_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Ladin people">Ladin</a> Roman Catholic priest and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> in <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-October2019_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-October2019-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He has been declared a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Freinademetz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Freinademetz was born the fourth among the 13 children of Giovanmattia and Anna Maria Freinademetz<sup id="cite_ref-nlvn.net_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nlvn.net-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> in Oies, a section of the town of <a href="/wiki/Badia,_South_Tyrol" title="Badia, South Tyrol">Badia</a>, which was then in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">County of Tyrol</a>, a part of the <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Empire" title="Austrian Empire">Austrian Empire</a>, now a part of Italy. He studied theology in the diocesan seminary of <a href="/wiki/Brixen" title="Brixen">Brixen</a> and was ordained a priest on July 25, 1875. He was assigned to the community of <a href="/wiki/San_Martin_de_Tor" title="San Martin de Tor">San Martin de Tor</a>, not far from his own home.
</p><p>During his studies and the three years in San Martino, Freinademetz always felt a calling to be a missionary. He contacted <a href="/wiki/Arnold_Janssen" title="Arnold Janssen">Arnold Janssen</a>, founder of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Divine_Word" title="Society of the Divine Word">Society of the Divine Word</a>, a missionary <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> based in <a href="/wiki/Steyl" title="Steyl">Steyl</a>, <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>. With the permission of his parents and his bishop, he moved to Steyl in August 1878, where he received training as a missionary.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_work">Missionary work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Freinademetz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Missionary work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In March 1879, he and his confrere <a href="/wiki/Johann_Baptist_von_Anzer" title="Johann Baptist von Anzer">Johann Baptist von Anzer</a> boarded a ship to <a href="/wiki/Hong_Kong" title="Hong Kong">Hong Kong</a>, where they arrived five weeks later. They stayed there for two years. Freinademetz was based in <a href="/wiki/Sai_Kung_Peninsula" title="Sai Kung Peninsula">Sai Kung Peninsula</a> until 1880<sup id="cite_ref-nlvn.net_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nlvn.net-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and set up a chapel on the island of <a href="/wiki/Yim_Tin_Tsai_(Sai_Kung)" class="mw-redirect" title="Yim Tin Tsai (Sai Kung)">Yim Tin Tsai</a> in 1879.<sup id="cite_ref-YTT_Village_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-YTT_Village-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 1881 they moved to the southern region of the Province of <a href="/wiki/Shantung" class="mw-redirect" title="Shantung">Shantung</a>, to which they had been assigned. At the time of their arrival, there were 12&#160;million people living in that province, of which 158 had been baptized.
</p><p>Freinademetz was very active in the education of Chinese laymen and priests.  He wrote a <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> in <a href="/wiki/Chinese_language" title="Chinese language">Chinese</a>, which he considered a crucial part of their missionary effort. In 1898, he was sick with <a href="/wiki/Laryngitis" title="Laryngitis">laryngitis</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>, so Anzer, who had become the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">bishop</a> of the region, and other priests convinced him to go to Japan to recuperate. He returned, but was still not fully cured. When Anzer had to leave China for a journey to Europe in 1907, the administration of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> was assigned to Freinademetz.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Freinademetz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There was an outbreak of <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a> in this time, and he helped wherever he could, until he himself became infected. He returned to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Daijiazhuang&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Daijiazhuang (page does not exist)">Daijiazhuang</a> (<a href="/wiki/Chinese_language" title="Chinese language">Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%88%B4" class="extiw" title="wikt:戴">戴</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%AE%B6" class="extiw" title="wikt:家">家</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%BA%84" class="extiw" title="wikt:庄">庄</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Pinyin" title="Pinyin">pinyin</a>&#58; <i><span lang="zh-Latn-pinyin"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/D%C3%A0i" class="extiw" title="wikt:Dài">Dài</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ji%C4%81" class="extiw" title="wikt:jiā">jiā</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/zhu%C4%81ng" class="extiw" title="wikt:zhuāng">zhuāng</a></span></i>, historically spelled "Taikia" or "Taichia"), <a href="/wiki/Rencheng_District" title="Rencheng District">Rencheng District</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jining" title="Jining">Jining</a>, South <a href="/wiki/Shandong" title="Shandong">Shandong</a>, where he died from typhus.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in Daijiazhuang, at the twelfth station on the <a href="/wiki/Way_of_the_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Way of the Cross">Way of the Cross</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-October2019_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-October2019-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Freinademetz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Freinademetz, together with Arnold Janssen, the founder of his Society, was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on October 5, 2003, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, as was <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Comboni" class="mw-redirect" title="Daniel Comboni">Daniel Comboni</a>, the founder of the Comboni Missionaries, which works in Africa.
</p><p>Under his <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patronage</a> is the <a href="/wiki/St._Joseph_Freinademetz_German_National_Parish,_Beijing" title="St. Joseph Freinademetz German National Parish, Beijing">St. Joseph Freinademetz German National Parish</a> in <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> for German-speaking residents and visitors.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
