<p><b>Peter Faber</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Pierre Lefevre or Favre</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Petrus Faver</i>) (13 April 1506 – 1 August 1546)<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theologian</a>, who was also a co-founder of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> announced his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> in 2013.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Faber was born in 1506 to a peasant family in the village of Villaret, in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy" title="Duchy of Savoy">Duchy of Savoy</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Jean-de-Sixt" title="Saint-Jean-de-Sixt">Saint-Jean-de-Sixt</a> in the <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> Department of <a href="/wiki/Haute-Savoie" title="Haute-Savoie">Haute-Savoie</a>). As a boy, he was a shepherd in the high pastures of the <a href="/wiki/French_Alps" title="French Alps">French Alps</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had little education, but a remarkable memory; he could hear a sermon in the morning and then repeat it verbatim in the afternoon for his friends.<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Two of his uncles were <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> priors.<sup id="cite_ref-leitner_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-leitner-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At first, he was entrusted to the care of a priest at <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%B4nes" title="Thônes">Thônes</a> and later to a school in the neighboring village of <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Roche-sur-Foron" class="extiw" title="fr:La Roche-sur-Foron">La Roche-sur-Foron</a>.
</p><p>In 1525, Faber went to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> to pursue his studies. He was admitted to the <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Sainte-Barbe" title="Collège Sainte-Barbe">Collège Sainte-Barbe</a>, the oldest school in the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>, where he shared his lodgings with <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There Faber's spiritual views began to develop, influenced by a combination of popular devotion, Christian humanism, and late medieval scholasticism.<sup id="cite_ref-leitner_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-leitner-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Faber and Xavier became close friends and both received the degree of <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts" title="Master of Arts">Master of Arts</a> on the same day in 1530. At the university, Faber also met <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a> and became one of his associates. He tutored Loyola in the philosophy of <a href="/wiki/Aristotle" title="Aristotle">Aristotle</a>, while Loyola tutored Faber in spiritual matters.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Faber wrote of Loyola's counsel: "He gave me an understanding of my conscience and of the temptations and scruples I have had for so long without either understanding them or seeing the way by which I would be able to get peace."<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Xavier, Faber, and Loyola all became roommates at the University of Paris and are all recognized by the Jesuits as founders of the Society of Jesus.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesuit_preacher">Jesuit preacher</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Jesuit preacher">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pierre_Favre_(1506-1546).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg/170px-Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="236" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg/255px-Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg/340px-Pierre_Favre_%281506-1546%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="419" data-file-height="582" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pierre_Favre_(1506-1546).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Peter Faber, S.J.</div></div></div>
<p>Faber was the first among the small circle of men who formed the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> to be <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a>. Having become a priest on 30 May 1534, he received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> of Ignatius and his five companions at <a href="/wiki/Montmartre" title="Montmartre">Montmartre</a> on 15 August.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon graduation, Ignatius returned to Spain for a period of convalescence, after instructing his companions to meet in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> and charging Faber with conducting them there.<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After Loyola himself, Faber was the one whom Xavier and his companions esteemed the most.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Leaving Paris on 15 November 1536, Faber and his companions rejoined Loyola at Venice in January 1537. When war between Venice and the Turks prevented them from evangelizing the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> as they planned,<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> they decided to form the community that became the Society of Jesus, also known as the Jesuit Order. The group then traveled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> where they put themselves at the disposal of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a>. After Faber spent some months preaching and teaching, the Pope sent him to <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a> and <a href="/wiki/Piacenza" title="Piacenza">Piacenza</a>, where he brought about a revival of Christian piety.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Recalled to Rome in 1540, Faber was sent to Germany to uphold the position of the Catholic Church at the <a href="/wiki/Diet_of_Worms" title="Diet of Worms">Diet of Worms</a> and then at the <a href="/wiki/Diet_of_Ratisbon_(1541)" class="mw-redirect" title="Diet of Ratisbon (1541)">Diet of Ratisbon</a> in 1541.<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Another Catholic theologian, <a href="/wiki/Johann_Cochlaeus" title="Johann Cochlaeus">Johann Cochlaeus</a>, reported that Faber avoided theological debate and emphasized personal reformation, calling him "a master of the life of the affections".<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Faber was startled by the unrest that the Protestant movement had stirred up in Germany and by the decadence he found in the Catholic hierarchy. He decided that the remedy did not lie in discussions with the Protestants but in the reform of the Roman Catholic, especially of the clergy. For ten months, at <a href="/wiki/Speyer" title="Speyer">Speyer</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Ratisbon" class="mw-redirect" title="Ratisbon">Ratisbon</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>, he conducted himself with gentleness with all those with whom he dealt. He influenced princes, prelates, and priests who opened themselves to him and amazed people by the effectiveness of his outreach.<sup id="cite_ref-dublin_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dublin-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Faber possessed the gift of friendship to a remarkable degree. He was famous not for his preaching, but for his engaging conversations and his guidance of souls. He crisscrossed Europe on foot, guiding bishops, priests, nobles, and common people alike in the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises" title="Spiritual Exercises">Spiritual Exercises</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ucan_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ucan-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a lone Jesuit often on the move, Faber never felt alone because he walked in a world whose denizens included saints and <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angels</a>. He would ask the saint of the day and all the saints "to obtain for us not only virtues and salvation for our spirits but in particular whatever can strengthen, heal, and preserve the body and each of its parts". His <a href="/wiki/Guardian_angel" title="Guardian angel">guardian angel</a>, above all, became his chief ally. He sought support from the saints and angels both for his personal sanctification and in his evangelization of communities. Whenever he entered a new town or region, Faber implored the aid of the particular angels and saints associated with that place. Through the intercession of his allies, Faber could enter even a potentially hostile region assured of a spiritual army at his side. As he desired to bring each person he met to a closer relationship through spiritual friendship and conversation, he would invoke the intercession of the person's guardian angel.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Called to Spain by Loyola, he visited Barcelona, Zaragoza, Medinaceli, Madrid, and Toledo.<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In January 1542 the pope ordered him to Germany again. For the next nineteen months, Faber worked for the reform of Speyer, Mainz, and <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Cologne" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Cologne">Archbishop of Cologne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hermann_of_Wied" title="Hermann of Wied">Hermann of Wied</a>, favored Lutheranism, which he later publicly embraced. Faber gradually gained the confidence of the clergy and recruited many young men to the Jesuits, among them <a href="/wiki/Peter_Canisius" title="Peter Canisius">Peter Canisius</a>. After spending some months at <a href="/wiki/Leuven" title="Leuven">Leuven</a> in 1543, where he implanted the seeds of numerous vocations among the young, he returned to Cologne. Between 1544 and 1546, Faber continued his work in Portugal and Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Through his influence while at the <a href="/wiki/Royal_court" title="Royal court">royal court</a> of Lisbon, Faber was instrumental in establishing the Society of Jesus in Portugal. There and in Spain, he was a fervent and effective preacher. He was called to preach in the principal cities of Spain, where he aroused fervor among the local populations and fostered vocations to the clergy. Among them there was <a href="/wiki/Francis_Borgia" title="Francis Borgia">Francis Borgia</a>, another significant future Jesuit. King <a href="/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal" title="John III of Portugal">John III of Portugal</a> wanted Faber made <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarchate_of_Ethiopia" title="Latin Patriarchate of Ethiopia">Patriarch of Ethiopia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dublin_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dublin-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sim%C3%A3o_Rodrigues" title="Simão Rodrigues">Simão Rodrigues</a>, co-founder of the Jesuit order, wrote that Faber was "endowed with charming grace in dealing with people, which up to now I must confess I have not seen in anyone else. Somehow he entered into friendship in such a way, bit by bit coming to influence others in such a manner, that his very way of living and gracious conversation powerfully drew to the love of God all those with whom he dealt."<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He then worked in several Spanish cities, including Valladolid, Salamanca, Toledo, Galapagar, Alcalá, and Madrid.<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>In 1546, Faber was appointed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a> to act as a <i><a href="/wiki/Peritus" title="Peritus">peritus</a></i> (expert) on behalf of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>. Faber, at age 40, was exhausted by his incessant efforts and his unceasing journeys, always made on foot. In April 1546, he left Spain to attend the Council and reached Rome, weakened by fever, on 17 July 1546. He died, reportedly in the arms of Loyola, on 1 August 1546.<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Faber's body was initially buried at the Church of Our Lady of the Way, which served as a center for the Jesuit community. When that church was demolished to allow for the construction of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Ges%C3%B9" title="Church of the Gesù">Church of the Gesù</a>, his remains and those of others among the first Jesuits were exhumed.<sup id="cite_ref-tylenda_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tylenda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His remains are now in the crypt near the entrance to the Gesù.<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Faber kept a diary of his spiritual life known as his <i>Memoriale</i>. Most of it dates from June 1542 to July 1543, with some additional entries from 1545 and a final brief entry made in January 1546. It begins with a quotation from <i>Psalms</i>: "Bless the Lord, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits." It takes the form of a series of conversations, mostly between God and Faber with occasional contributions on the part of various saints and Faber's colleagues.<sup id="cite_ref-padberg_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padberg-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter Faber authored "The Blessed Sacrament" which proffers a strong argument for the existence and nature of God.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Those who had known Faber in life already invoked him as a saint. <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">Francis de Sales</a>, whose character recalled that of Faber's, never spoke of him except as a saint. He is remembered for his travels through Europe promoting Catholic renewal and his great skill in directing the Spiritual Exercises. Faber was beatified on 5 September 1872.<sup id="cite_ref-dublin_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dublin-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 2 August by the Society of Jesus. Faber was honored as part of the 2006 Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christianity)">Jubilee</a> Year which celebrated the 500th anniversary of the birth of <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>, the 500th anniversary of the birth of Peter Faber, and the 450th anniversary of the death of <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Ignatius Loyola">Ignatius Loyola</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>, on his own 77th birthday, 17 December 2013, announced Faber's canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He used a process known as <i>equipollent canonization</i> that dispenses with the standard judicial procedures and ceremonies in the case of someone long venerated. Faber is regarded as one of Pope Francis' favorite saints. A few weeks earlier, Francis had praised Faber's "dialogue with all, even the most remote and even with his opponents; his simple piety, a certain naïveté perhaps, his being available straightaway, his careful interior discernment, the fact that he was a man capable of great and strong decisions but also capable of being so gentle and loving."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Francis also gave thanks for Faber's canonization when he celebrated Mass on 3 January 2014, at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Ges%C3%B9" title="Church of the Gesù">Church of the Gesù</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Faber&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Saint Peter Faber Jesuit Community at <a href="/wiki/Boston_College" title="Boston College">Boston College</a> is a residence for Jesuits in formation.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Creighton_University" title="Creighton University">Creighton University</a> confers the Blessed Peter Faber Integrity Award on a student, faculty or staff member who is involved in activities that promote integrity, social justice, peace, and religious, racial, and cultural harmony and is able to inspire and lead others to distill their values and integrity.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Peter Faber House at <a href="/wiki/Gonzaga_University" title="Gonzaga University">Gonzaga University</a> is an extension of the University Ministry office reserved for preparing retreats and further developing University Ministry programs.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Faber Center for Ignatian Spirituality was adopted as a ministry of <a href="/wiki/Marquette_University" title="Marquette University">Marquette University</a> in November 2005.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Peter Faber Chapel serves as the central space for the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Scranton" title="University of Scranton">University of Scranton</a>'s Retreat Center at Chapman Lake, about 30 minutes north of Scranton, PA. <sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The St. Peter Faber conference room in Loyola Hall at Manresa House of Retreats, Convent, Louisiana, is the location where men on retreat are directed through the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola</a><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The School of Business at Australian Catholic University is known as the Peter Faber School of Business.
</p><p>Faber Hall at Fordham University in the Bronx, New York, is a residence hall and administrative building.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was announced in <i><a href="/wiki/Publishers_Weekly" title="Publishers Weekly">Publishers Weekly</a></i> on 26 October 2016 that <a href="/wiki/Loyola_Press" title="Loyola Press">Loyola Press</a> has contracted <a href="/wiki/Jon_M._Sweeney" title="Jon M. Sweeney">Jon M. Sweeney</a>, the author of <i>The Pope Who Quit</i> and other historical books, to write a new narrative life of Saint Peter Faber.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
