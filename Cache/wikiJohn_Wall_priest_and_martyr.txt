<p><b>Saint John Wall,</b> <i>(aliases John Marsh, Francis Johnson or Dormore or Webb, religious name "Joachim of St. Ann")</i> <b><a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a></b>, (1620 – 22 August 1679) was an English <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, who is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. Wall served on the English mission in Worcestershire for twenty-two years before being arrested and executed at the time of <a href="/wiki/Titus_Oates" title="Titus Oates">Titus Oates</a>'s alleged plot.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Wall_(priest_and_martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Preston,_Lancashire" title="Preston, Lancashire">Preston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a>, the son of wealthy and staunch Lancashire Catholics. His brother William, also a priest, became a Benedictine monk. William was later arrested, and condemned for being a priest, but was reprieved and survived.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> John Wall was sent when very young to the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College, Douai</a>. As the English government had spies and informers on the continent, Wall used the name "John Marsh".<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He entered the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Rome" title="English College, Rome">English College, Rome</a>, on 5 November 1641 and was ordained a Catholic priest on 3 December 1645. He was sent on the English Mission on 12 May 1648 under the aliases of Francis Johnson, and Dormore. For several years he said Mass for recusant households. He returned to Douai and on 1 January 1651, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at St. Bonaventure's Friary, taking the name Friar Joachim of St. Ann. He was soon named <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Master of novices</a>, serving in that office until 1656, when he returned to England, under the name Francis Webb and settled in <a href="/wiki/Worcestershire" title="Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There he became a Governor (<a href="/wiki/Six_Master" class="mw-redirect" title="Six Master">Six Master</a>) of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School_Worcester" title="Royal Grammar School Worcester">Royal Grammar School Worcester</a>. Father Wall usually made his home at <a href="/wiki/Harvington_Hall" title="Harvington Hall">Harvington Hall</a>, which had a number of <a href="/wiki/Priest-hole" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest-hole">priest-holes</a>, thought to be the work of <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Owen_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicholas Owen (martyr)">Nicholas Owen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG/220px-Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="144" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG/330px-Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG/440px-Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="980" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Exterior_of_Harvington_Hall.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Exterior of Harvington Hall.</div></div></div>
<p>After 22 years of ministry to the Catholics of the area, he was apprehended in December 1678, at Rushock Court near Bromsgrove, where the sheriff's man had come to seek a debtor. He was tendered the Oath of Supremacy, and was committed to Worcester Gaol for refusing it. His trial was on 25 April 1679. A man whose vices he had reproved bore testimony to his priesthood, and he received sentence.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was then sent to London, and four times examined by <a href="/wiki/Titus_Oates" title="Titus Oates">Oates</a>, Bedloe, and others in the hope of implicating him in the pretended plot; but was declared innocent of all plotting and offered his life if he would abjure his religion. He was brought back to Worcester and executed on <a href="/wiki/Red_Hill,_Worcester" title="Red Hill, Worcester">Red Hill</a> 22 August 1679.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Wall was a much respected local figure and the crowd's reaction showed that their sympathies were entirely with him.<sup id="cite_ref-kenyon_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kenyon-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Many of the onlookers, who were mostly Protestants, wept, and the Sheriff reportedly cried out "Will this end Popery? This is the way to make us all Papists!"<sup id="cite_ref-kenyon_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kenyon-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was an outstanding academic, perhaps the most intellectually distinguished English Catholic priest of his generation.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Wall_(priest_and_martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His quartered body was given to his friends and was buried in the cemetery adjoining the Church of St. Oswald of Worcester,<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> while the head was taken to the Franciscan friary of Douai, to which the martyr belonged.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Previously, his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was observed within the Franciscan Order on the date of his death, 22 August. It has been moved and is currently observed on 12 July, a date he shares with his brother friar and fellow martyr, <a href="/wiki/John_Jones_(martyr)" title="John Jones (martyr)">St. John Jones, O.F.M.</a> In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Birmingham" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Birmingham">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Birmingham</a>, his feast day is celebrated on 23 August. <a href="/wiki/St_John_Wall_Catholic_School" title="St John Wall Catholic School">St John Wall Catholic School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Birmingham" title="Birmingham">Birmingham</a> is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
