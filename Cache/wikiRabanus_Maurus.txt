<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>

<p><b>Rabanus Maurus Magnentius</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 780 – 4 February 856), also known as <b>Hrabanus</b> or <b>Rhabanus</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk, theologian, poet, encyclopedist and military writer who became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">archbishop of Mainz</a> in <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Francia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the author of the encyclopaedia <i>De rerum naturis</i> (<i>"On the Natures of Things"</i>). He also wrote treatises on education and grammar and commentaries on the Bible. He was one of the most prominent teachers and writers of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> age,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and was called "Praeceptor Germaniae", or "the teacher of Germany". In the most recent edition of the Roman Martyrology (<i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Romanum" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologium Romanum">Martyrologium Romanum</a></i>, 2004, pp.&#160;133), his feast is given as 4 February and he is qualified as a Saint ('sanctus').
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg/220px-Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg/330px-Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg/440px-Hrabanus-Maurus_De-rerum-naturis_nach-842.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="890" /></a><figcaption>Page from Rabanus' <i>De rerum naturis</i>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Rabanus was born of noble parents in <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>. The date of his birth remains uncertain, but in 801 he was ordained a deacon at <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Fulda_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Fulda monastery">Abbey of Fulda</a> in Hesse, where he had been sent to school and had become a monk. At the insistence of <a href="/wiki/Ratgar" title="Ratgar">Ratgar</a>, his abbot, he went together with <a href="/wiki/Haymo_of_Halberstadt" title="Haymo of Halberstadt">Haimo</a> (later <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Halberstadt" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Halberstadt">of Halberstadt</a>) to complete his studies at <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>. There he studied under <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a>, who in recognition of his diligence and purity gave him the surname of Maurus, after the favourite disciple of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Benedict</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurus" title="Saint Maurus">Saint Maurus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hymnary_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hymnary-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning to <a href="/wiki/Fulda" title="Fulda">Fulda</a>, in 803 he was entrusted with the principal charge of the abbey school,<sup id="cite_ref-hymnary_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hymnary-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which under his direction became one of the most preeminent centers of scholarship and book production in Europe, and sent forth such pupils as <a href="/wiki/Walafrid_Strabo" title="Walafrid Strabo">Walafrid Strabo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lupus_Servatus" title="Lupus Servatus">Servatus Lupus of Ferrières</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Otfrid_of_Weissenburg" title="Otfrid of Weissenburg">Otfrid of Weissenburg</a>. It was probably at this period that he compiled his excerpt from the grammar of <a href="/wiki/Priscian" title="Priscian">Priscian</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> a popular textbook during the Middle Ages. According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>'s <i>Lives of the Saints,</i> Rabanus ate no meat and drank no wine.
</p><p>In 814 Rabanus was ordained a priest. Shortly afterwards, apparently on account of disagreement with Abbot Ratgar, he withdrew for a time from Fulda. This banishment has long been understood to have occasioned a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, based on an allusion in his commentary on <a href="/wiki/Joshua" title="Joshua">Joshua</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, the passage in question is taken from <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>'s <i>Homily</i> xiv <i>In Librum Jesu Nave</i>. Hence, it was Origen, not Rabanus, who visited Palestine.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Rabanus returned to Fulda in 817 on the election of a new abbot, <a href="/wiki/Eigil_of_Fulda" title="Eigil of Fulda">Eigil</a>, and at Eigil's death in 822, Rabanus himself became abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He handled this position efficiently and successfully, but in 842 he resigned so as to have greater leisure for study and prayer, retiring to the neighbouring monastery of <a href="/wiki/Petersberg,_Hesse" title="Petersberg, Hesse">St Petersberg</a>.
</p><p>In 847 Rabanus was constrained to return to public life when he was elected to succeed <a href="/wiki/Otgar_of_Mainz" title="Otgar of Mainz">Otgar</a> as <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Mainz">Archbishop of Mainz</a>. He died at <a href="/wiki/Oestrich-Winkel" title="Oestrich-Winkel">Winkel</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a> in 856.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hymns">Hymns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hymns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rabanus composed a number of hymns, the most famous of which is the <i><a href="/wiki/Veni_Creator_Spiritus" title="Veni Creator Spiritus">Veni Creator Spiritus</a></i>. This is a hymn to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Spirit (Christianity)">Holy Spirit</a> often sung at <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> and at ordinations. It is known in English through many translations, including <i>Come, Holy Ghost, our souls inspire</i>; <i>Come, Holy Ghost, Creator blest</i>; and <i>Creator Spirit, by whose aid.</i><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <i>Veni Creator Spiritus</i> was used by <a href="/wiki/Gustav_Mahler" title="Gustav Mahler">Gustav Mahler</a> as the first chorale of his <a href="/wiki/Symphony_No._8_(Mahler)" title="Symphony No. 8 (Mahler)">eighth symphony</a>.
</p><p>Another of Rabanus' hymns, <i>Christ, the fair glory of the holy angels</i> (<i>Christe, sanctorum decus Angelorum</i>), sung for the commemoration of <a href="/wiki/Michaelmas" title="Michaelmas">Saint Michael and All Angels</a>, and to include the <a href="/wiki/Archangels" class="mw-redirect" title="Archangels">archangels</a> <a href="/wiki/Archangel_Gabriel" class="mw-redirect" title="Archangel Gabriel">Gabriel</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archangel_Raphael" class="mw-redirect" title="Archangel Raphael">Raphael</a>, is found in English translation in <a href="/wiki/The_Hymnal_1982" title="The Hymnal 1982">The Hymnal 1982</a> (of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church</a>), and was harmonized by <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Vaughan_Williams" title="Ralph Vaughan Williams">Ralph Vaughan Williams</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Toscana_(forse),_rabano_mauro,_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis,_xiii_secolo,_01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg/300px-Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg/450px-Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg/600px-Toscana_%28forse%29%2C_rabano_mauro%2C_in_honorem_sanctae_crucis%2C_xiii_secolo%2C_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2732" data-file-height="1824" /></a><figcaption><i>In Honorem Sanctae Crucis</i>, 13th century, <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Medicea_Laurenziana" class="mw-redirect" title="Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana">Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana</a>, Florence</figcaption></figure>
<p>Rabanus' works, many of which as of 1911<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup> remained unpublished, comprise commentaries on scripture (<a href="/wiki/Book_of_Genesis" title="Book of Genesis">Genesis</a> to <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Judges" title="Book of Judges">Judges</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Ruth" title="Book of Ruth">Ruth</a>, <a href="/wiki/Books_of_Kings" title="Books of Kings">Kings</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Chronicles" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of Chronicles">Chronicles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Judith" title="Book of Judith">Judith</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Esther" title="Book of Esther">Esther</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canticles" class="mw-redirect" title="Canticles">Canticles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Proverbs" title="Book of Proverbs">Proverbs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Wisdom" title="Book of Wisdom">Wisdom</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wisdom_of_Sirach" class="mw-redirect" title="Wisdom of Sirach">Sirach</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Jeremiah" title="Book of Jeremiah">Jeremiah</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Lamentations" title="Book of Lamentations">Lamentations</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Ezekiel" title="Book of Ezekiel">Ezekiel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Maccabees" title="Maccabees">Maccabees</a>, <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Matthew" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of Matthew">Matthew</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Epistles_of_St_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Epistles of St Paul">Epistles of St Paul</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews" title="Epistle to the Hebrews">Hebrews</a>); and various treatises relating to doctrinal and practical subjects, including more than one series of <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homilies</a>. In <i>De institutione clericorum</i> he brought into prominence the views of Augustine and Gregory the Great as to the training which was requisite for a right discharge of the clerical function.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> One of his most popular and enduring works is a spectacular collection of poems centered on the cross, called <i>De laudibus sanctae crucis</i> or <i>In honorem sanctae crucis,</i> a set of highly sophisticated poems that present the cross (and, in the last poem, Rabanus himself kneeling before it) in word and image, even in numbers.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Among the others may be mentioned the <i>De universo libri xxii., sive etymologiarum opus</i>, a kind of dictionary or encyclopedia, heavily dependent upon <a href="/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville" title="Isidore of Seville">Isidore of Seville</a>'s <i>Etymologies</i>, designed as a help towards the <a href="/wiki/Typology_(theology)" title="Typology (theology)">typological</a>, historical and mystical interpretation of Scripture, the <i>De sacris ordinibus</i>, the <i>De disciplina ecclesiastica</i> and the <i>Martyrologium</i>. All of them are characterized by erudition (he knew even some <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He also published an annotated version of <i><a href="/wiki/De_re_militari" title="De re militari">De re militari</a></i> to improve Frankish warfare.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the annals of German philology a special interest attaches to the <i>Glossaria Latino-Theodisca</i>. A commentary, <i>Super Porphyrium</i>, printed by <a href="/wiki/Victor_Cousin" title="Victor Cousin">Cousin</a> in 1836 among the <i>Ouvrages inédits d'Abélard</i>, and assigned both by that editor and by Haurau to Hrabantis Maurus, is now generally believed to have been the work of a disciple.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2006 Germans marked the 1150th anniversary of his death, especially in Mainz and in Fulda. Highlights of the celebrations included the display of Codex Vaticanus Reginensis latinus 124, an extremely rare loan by the Vatican to Mainz of a spectacular manuscript containing <i>De laudibus sanctae crucis</i>. The anniversary also saw the publication of no fewer than three book-length studies of Maurus and his work.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marcomannic_runes">Marcomannic runes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Marcomannic runes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Marcomannic.PNG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Marcomannic.PNG/310px-Marcomannic.PNG" decoding="async" width="310" height="128" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Marcomannic.PNG/465px-Marcomannic.PNG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Marcomannic.PNG/620px-Marcomannic.PNG 2x" data-file-width="904" data-file-height="372" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Marcomanni" title="Marcomanni">Marcomannic</a> runes</figcaption></figure>
<p>A runic alphabet recorded in a treatise called <i>De Inventione Litterarum</i> has been ascribed to Rabanus. It consisted of a mixture of <a href="/wiki/Elder_Futhark" title="Elder Futhark">Elder Futhark</a> with <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_runes" title="Anglo-Saxon runes">Anglo-Saxon runes</a> and is preserved in 8th and 9th-century manuscripts mainly from the southern part of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a> (<a href="/wiki/Alemannia" class="mw-redirect" title="Alemannia">Alemannia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>). The manuscript text attributes the runes to the <i>Marcomanni, quos nos Nordmannos vocamus</i> (and hence traditionally, the alphabet is called "Marcomannic runes") but it has no connection with the <a href="/wiki/Marcomanni" title="Marcomanni">Marcomanni</a>, and rather is an attempt of Carolingian scholars to represent all letters of the Latin alphabet with runic equivalents.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm" title="Wilhelm Grimm">Wilhelm Grimm</a> discussed these runes in 1821.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bibliography">Bibliography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rabanus_Maurus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Bibliography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first nominally complete edition of the works of Hrabanus Maurus was that of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Georges_Colvener&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Georges Colvener (page does not exist)">Georges Colvener</a> (Cologne, 6 vols. fol., 1627). The <i>Opera omnia</i> form vols. cvii–cxii of <a href="/wiki/Jacques_Paul_Migne" title="Jacques Paul Migne">Migne</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologiae cursus completus</a></i>. The <i>De universo</i> is the subject of <i>Compendium der Naturwissenschaften an der Schule zu Fulda im IX. Jahrhundert</i> (Berlin, 1880).
</p><p>Recent critical editions and translations are available of some of his works:
</p>
<ul><li><i>De sermonum proprietate sive Opus de universo</i>, ed. and tr. by Priscilla Throop, <i>Hrabanus Maurus: De Universo: the peculiar properties of words and their mystical significance</i>, 2 vols. Charlotte, VT: MedievalMS, 2009.</li>
<li><i>Expositio in Matthaeum</i>, edited by B. Löfstedt, 2 vols. <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christianorum" title="Corpus Christianorum">Corpus Christianorum</a>, continuatio medievalis 174-174A. Turnhout: Brepols, 2000.</li>
<li><i>In honorem sanctae crucis</i>, edited by M. Perrin, 2 vols. Corpus Christianorum, continuatio medievalis 100-100A. Turnhout: Brepols, 1997.</li>
<li><i>De magicis artibus,</i> partial English translation in <i>European Magic and Witchcraft: a reader</i>, tr. Martha Rampton (2018), pp. 143-45</li>
<li><i>Martyrologium. Liber de computo</i>, edited by <a href="/wiki/Judith_McCulloh" title="Judith McCulloh">J. McCulloh</a> and W. Stevens, Corpus Christianorum, continuatio mediaevalis 44. Turnhout: Brepols, 1997.</li>
<li><i>Hrabanus Maurus: De institutione clericorum; Studien und Edition</i>, Freiburger Beitraege zur mittelalterlichen Geschichte 7. Frankfurt am Main: 1996. (An edition (with German translation?) of the 'De Institutione Clericorum' is listed as 'in preparation' by Brepols.)</li></ul>
