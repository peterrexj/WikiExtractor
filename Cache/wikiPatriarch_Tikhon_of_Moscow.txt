<p><b>Tikhon of Moscow</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Тихон Московский</span>, 31 January&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 19 January&#93;&#160;1865 – 7 April&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 25 March&#93;&#160;1925), born <b>Vasily Ivanovich Bellavin</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Василий Иванович Беллавин</span>), was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> (ROC). On 5 November 1917 (<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">OS</a>) he was selected the 11th <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Moscow">Patriarch of Moscow and All Russia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;Note 1&#93;</a></sup> after a period of about 200 years of the <a href="/wiki/Most_Holy_Synod" title="Most Holy Synod">Synodal rule</a> in the ROC. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Confessor_of_the_Faith" title="Confessor of the Faith">confessor</a> by the ROC in 1989.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From 1878 to 1884, Bellavin studied at the <a href="/wiki/Pskov" title="Pskov">Pskov</a> Theological Seminary.  In 1888, at the age of 23, he graduated from the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Theological_Academy" title="Saint Petersburg Theological Academy">Saint Petersburg Theological Academy</a> as a layman. He then returned to the Pskov Seminary and became an instructor of Moral and Dogmatic Theology.  In 1891, at the age of 26, he took <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monastic vows</a> and was given the name Tikhon in honor of St. <a href="/wiki/Tikhon_of_Zadonsk" title="Tikhon of Zadonsk">Tikhon of Zadonsk</a>.  Tikhon was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lublin" title="Lublin">Lublin</a> on 19 October 1897.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_in_the_United_States">Bishop in the United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop in the United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 14 September 1898, he was appointed Bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America_Diocese_of_Alaska" title="Orthodox Church in America Diocese of Alaska">Aleutian Islands and Alaska</a>. He went to the United States, and eventually became a naturalized American citizen. The peripatetic bishop visited emerging Orthodox emigrant communities in various American cities, including <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a> and the coal and steel-making cities in Pennsylvania and Ohio. 
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg/350px-Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="247" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg/525px-Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg/700px-Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1128" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fond-du-lac-circus.preview.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The consecration of <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Heber_Weller" title="Reginald Heber Weller">Reginald Heber Weller</a> as an <a href="/wiki/Anglican_bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican bishop">Anglican bishop</a> at <a href="/wiki/St._Paul%27s_Cathedral_(Fond_du_Lac,_Wisconsin)" title="St. Paul&#39;s Cathedral (Fond du Lac, Wisconsin)">St. Paul's Cathedral</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_Fond_du_Lac" title="Episcopal Diocese of Fond du Lac">Episcopal Diocese of Fond du Lac</a>, with Anthony Kozlowski of the <a href="/wiki/Polish_National_Catholic_Church" title="Polish National Catholic Church">Polish National Catholic Church</a> and Tikhon of Moscow (along with his chaplains <a href="/wiki/John_Kochurov" title="John Kochurov">John Kochurov</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sevastijan_Dabovi%C4%87" title="Sevastijan Dabović">Sevastijan Dabović</a>) of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> present</div></div></div>
<p>As head of the ROC diocese in North America, in 1900 he reorganized the diocese and changed its name from the "Of the Aleutian Islands and Alaska" to "Diocese of the Aleutian Islands and North America". He had two <a href="/wiki/Auxiliary_bishop" title="Auxiliary bishop">auxiliary bishops</a> in the United States: Bishop Innocent (Pustynsky) in Alaska, and <a href="/wiki/Raphael_of_Brooklyn" title="Raphael of Brooklyn">St. Raphael</a> (Hawaweeny) in Brooklyn. On 22 May 1901, he blessed the cornerstone for <a href="/wiki/St._Nicholas_Russian_Orthodox_Cathedral_(Manhattan)" title="St. Nicholas Russian Orthodox Cathedral (Manhattan)">St. Nicholas Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> (fundraising for which had begun in 1894 and to which Tsar <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia" title="Nicholas II of Russia">Nicholas II</a> contributed $5,000 in 1900) in a great ceremony attended by New York Mayor <a href="/wiki/Seth_Low" title="Seth Low">Seth Low</a>, Russian diplomats and sailors, and enthusiastic worshippers. He celebrated the first liturgy in the new building's basement on July 20, 1902, and in the main hall on November 10, 1902.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Tikhon was also involved in building other churches in North America, and establishing a dialog with Greek Orthodox churches in America. On 9 November 1902, he consecrated the church of St. Nicholas in <a href="/wiki/Brooklyn" title="Brooklyn">Brooklyn</a> for <a href="/wiki/Antiochian_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiochian Orthodox Church">Syrian Antiochian Orthodox</a> immigrants.
</p><p>While in the United States, Tikhon became aware of the country's tradition of religious diversity, as well as the growing <a href="/wiki/Ecumenism" title="Ecumenism">ecumenical</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pan-Slavism" title="Pan-Slavism">Pan-Slav</a> movements, and the needs of a wide variety of eastern and southern European immigrants. Before his arrival, in 1890, a delegation of Carpatho-Rusyns also had approached the Russian consul in San Francisco and requested a bishop, since the Latin Catholic archbishop of Minnesota, <a href="/wiki/John_Ireland_(bishop)" title="John Ireland (bishop)">John Ireland</a> was attempting to force their assimilation, although <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a> <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Catholics</a> had previously been accorded certain dispensations from the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> practices of the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a>. Their <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church">Byzantine (Ruthenian) Catholic</a> priest, <a href="/wiki/Alexis_Toth" title="Alexis Toth">Alexis Toth</a>, was formally accepted into the Russian Orthodox Church in 1892. In 1900, Tikhon attended the consecration of <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Heber_Weller" title="Reginald Heber Weller">Reginald Heber Weller</a> as <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">coadjutor bishop</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_Fond_du_Lac" title="Episcopal Diocese of Fond du Lac">Episcopal Diocese of Fond du Lac</a>, though he was not one of the consecrating bishops (all Episcopalians).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> More Byzantine Catholics joined the Eastern Orthodox fold, especially priests after 1907, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> published <i><a href="/wiki/Ea_Semper" title="Ea Semper">Ea Semper</a></i>, restricting their previously recognized right to ordain married men.
</p><p>In 1905, the title of Archbishop was bestowed on him, and he moved his formal residence and diocesan office from San Francisco to New York.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In June 1905, the new archbishop gave his blessing for the establishment of a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Pennsylvania" title="Pennsylvania">Pennsylvania</a>, St. Tikhon's Monastery which in the 1930s supported the founding of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tikhon%27s_Orthodox_Theological_Seminary" title="Saint Tikhon&#39;s Orthodox Theological Seminary">Saint Tikhon's Orthodox Theological Seminary</a>. On September 17, &#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> September 4, &#93;&#160;1905 The Holy Trinity Russian (Greek) Orthodox church was consecrated in <a href="/wiki/Winnipeg" title="Winnipeg">Winnipeg</a>, Manitoba, Canada, on the corner of McKenzie Street and Manitoba Avenue by the new Archbishop Tikhon, Head of the Russian Orthodox Mission in North America and soon to become Patriarch of Moscow and All Russia. Ukrainian teachers studying at the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenians" title="Ruthenians">Ruthenian</a> Training School sang at the consecration ceremony.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Russia_and_restoration_of_the_Patriarchate">Return to Russia and restoration of the Patriarchate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Return to Russia and restoration of the Patriarchate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1907, Tikhon returned to Russia, having been appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Yaroslavl" title="Diocese of Yaroslavl">See of Yaroslavl</a>. On 22 December 1913, he was transferred to the diocese of <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilno</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>.
</p><p>However, the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution" title="Russian Revolution">Russian Revolution</a> created turmoil in his native land. On 21 June 1917, the Diocesan Congress of clergy and laity elected Tikhon the ruling bishop of <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>.  On 14 August 1917 (<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">OS</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Tikhon was raised to the dignity of <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Moscow">Metropolitan of Moscow</a>. On the next day, the <a href="/wiki/1917%E2%80%9318_Local_Council_of_the_Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="1917–18 Local Council of the Russian Orthodox Church">Local Council</a> of the ROC, the first such convention since the late 17th century, opened in Moscow. The council's major decision that passed on 28 October 1917, days after the <a href="/wiki/October_Revolution" title="October Revolution">Bolsheviks seized power</a> in Petrograd, was to restore the Patriarchy in the ROC. On 5 November 1917, after his election by vote as one of the three candidates for the reinstated Moscow Patriarchate, Metropolitan <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Bogoyavlensky" title="Vladimir Bogoyavlensky">Vladimir of Kiev</a> announced that Metropolitan Tikhon had been selected for the position after a <a href="/wiki/Cleromancy" title="Cleromancy">drawing of lots</a> as the new Patriarch of Moscow and All Russia.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patriarchate">Patriarchate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patriarchate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Civil_War" title="Russian Civil War">Russian Civil War</a> the patriarch, like the entire Russian Church, was widely considered anti-Bolshevik and many bishops were exiled, imprisoned or even executed by the new regime.  Tikhon openly condemned the <a href="/wiki/Shooting_of_the_Romanov_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Shooting of the Romanov family">killings of the tsar's family</a> in 1918, and protested against violent attacks by the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</a> on the church. In 1920, he granted autonomy to what became the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> and other dioceses of the Church of Russia that were cut off from the governance of the highest church authority (i.e. the patriarch), until such time as normal relations with the highest church authority could be resumed.
</p><p>In November 1921, many of the Russian bishops, who had fled Russia in 1919–1920, had gathered in <a href="/wiki/Sremski_Karlovci" title="Sremski Karlovci">Sremski Karlovci</a>, <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>, and adopted a number of anti-Bolshevik statements; in May the following year Tikhon and his Synod formally dissolved the exile group, which nevertheless continued to exist as a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">separate Russian ecclesiastical entity</a>, viewed as schismatic by the Moscow Patriarchate until 2007.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg/220px-Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="271" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg/330px-Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg/440px-Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2103" data-file-height="2595" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow_near_Donskoy_Monastery_Jul_1923.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Patriarch Tikhon of Moscow near <a href="/wiki/Donskoy_Monastery" title="Donskoy Monastery">Donskoy Monastery</a> Jul 1923</div></div></div>
<p>In 1922 Russia was afflicted with <a href="/wiki/Russian_famine_of_1921%E2%80%9322" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian famine of 1921–22">mass famine</a>. The Communist government used the situation to <a href="/wiki/1922_confiscation_of_Russian_Orthodox_Church_property" class="mw-redirect" title="1922 confiscation of Russian Orthodox Church property">confiscate ecclesiastical property</a> and to accuse the patriarch of being a saboteur. From April 1922 until June 1923 he resided under <a href="/wiki/House_arrest" title="House arrest">house arrest</a> in <a href="/wiki/Donskoy_Monastery" title="Donskoy Monastery">Donskoy Monastery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His public protest against nationalization of Church property was reframed as a criminal act. This caused international resonance and several countries sent formal protest notes to the Soviet government.
</p><p>Under pressure from the authorities, Tikhon issued several messages to the believers in which he stated in part that he was <i>"no longer an enemy to the Soviet power"</i>. Textual analysis of these messages shows considerable similarity with a number of documents exchanged in the <a href="/wiki/Politburo" title="Politburo">Politburo</a> on the "Tikhon's Affair".  Despite his declaration of loyalty, he continued to enjoy the trust of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> community in <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>. In 1923 Tikhon was "deposed" by a Soviet-sponsored<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> council of the so-called <a href="/wiki/Living_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Living Church">Living Church</a>, which decreed that he was "henceforth a simple citizen—Vasily Bellavin." This deposition has never been recognized as an act of the Russian Orthodox Church, and is therefore considered invalid by both the Russian Orthodox Church and the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Federation" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Federation">Russian Federation</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1924 the patriarch fell ill and was hospitalized.  On 5 April 1925, he served his last <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a>, and died two days later, 25 March (<a href="/wiki/Old_Style" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Style">OS</a>)/7 April, the Feast of the <a href="/wiki/Annunciation" title="Annunciation">Annunciation</a>.  He was buried on 12 April in the winter church of <a href="/wiki/Donskoy_Monastery" title="Donskoy Monastery">Donskoy Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>. From the time of his death, he was widely considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> or <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> for the faith.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg/250px-SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg" decoding="async" width="250" height="414" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg/375px-SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg/500px-SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="806" data-file-height="1335" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SaintTikhonOfMoscow.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Icon with reliquary of Saint Tikhon of Moscow in the <a href="/wiki/Katholikon" title="Katholikon">Katholikon</a> of the Monastery of St. Tikhon of Zadonsk in <a href="/wiki/South_Canaan_Township,_Wayne_County,_Pennsylvania" title="South Canaan Township, Wayne County, Pennsylvania">South Canaan, Pennsylvania</a></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg/150px-St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="233" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg/225px-St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg/300px-St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="3536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Tikhon_Wooden_Carving.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Wooden carving of St. Tikhon of Moscow at the Monastery of St. <a href="/wiki/Tikhon_of_Zadonsk" title="Tikhon of Zadonsk">Tikhon of Zadonsk</a></div></div></div>
<p>Tikhon was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">glorified</a> (canonized) a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Bishops_(Orthodox)" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Bishops (Orthodox)">Synod of Bishops</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> in conjunction with the great glorification of the <a href="/wiki/New-martyr" class="mw-redirect" title="New-martyr">New Martyrs and Confessors of the Soviet Yoke</a> on 1 November&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 19 October&#93;&#160;1981. He was later glorified by the <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Moscow Patriarchate">Moscow Patriarchate</a> during the Bishop's Council of 9–11 October 1989. This later canonization process is generally considered an example of the thaw in <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">church</a>–<a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> relations during the <a href="/wiki/Glasnost" title="Glasnost">Glasnost</a> era. The American <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church</a> along with the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church_in_North_America" title="Anglican Church in North America">Anglican Church in North America</a> remember Tikhon with a feast day on their liturgical calendars on the anniversary of his death, April 7.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Tikhon's <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were believed lost, but on 19 February 1992 his coffin was found in a hidden crypt in the <a href="/wiki/Donskoy_Monastery" title="Donskoy Monastery">Donskoy Monastery</a> and on 22 February, the coffin was opened and his relics were discovered to be almost entirely <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The relics were placed in a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> and on 5 April&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 23 March&#93;&#160;1992, fifty bishops solemnly transferred them to the <a href="/wiki/Katholikon" title="Katholikon">Katholikon</a> (main church) of the Donskoy Monastery in a place of honour by the <a href="/wiki/Soleas" title="Soleas">soleas</a> (close to the sanctuary).
</p>
