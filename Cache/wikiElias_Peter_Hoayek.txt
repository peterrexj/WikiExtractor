<p><b>Elias Peter Hoayek</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">الياس بطرس الحويّك</span>; 4 December 1843 – 24 December 1931; also spelled <b>Hoyek, Hwayek, Huayek, Juayek, Hawayek, Houwayek</b>) was the 72nd <a href="/wiki/List_of_Maronite_Patriarchs" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Maronite Patriarchs">Patriarch of Antioch for the Maronites</a>, the largest Christian Catholic community in the <a href="/wiki/Levant" title="Levant">Levant</a>, from 1898 to 1931 when he died.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Elias <a href="/wiki/Hawayek" class="mw-redirect" title="Hawayek">Hoayek</a> was born in <a href="/wiki/Helta" title="Helta">Helta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Batroun" title="Batroun">Batroun</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Governorate" title="North Governorate">North Lebanon</a> on December 4, 1843.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He studied at the Seminary College of St. John Maroun in North Lebanon. He later went to the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic seminary">seminary</a> of Ghazir in 1859, where he studied languages (French, Arabic, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>, <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>) as well as philosophy. In 1866, he went to Rome to study <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Evangelization_of_Peoples" title="Congregation for the Evangelization of Peoples">Propaganda Fide</a>. In 1870, he was made a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> and returned to Lebanon. Upon his return, he taught <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at his old school the Seminary of St. John Maroun. He was appointed secretary and moved to the patriarchal residence in 1872, where he remained until 1898 when he was elected <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a>. He was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular bishop</a> of Arca on December 14, 1889<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> by Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Paul_Peter_Massad" title="Paul Peter Massad">Paul Peter Massad</a>.
</p><p>As vicar of Patriarch <a href="/wiki/John_Peter_El_Hajj" title="John Peter El Hajj">John Peter El Hajj</a>, he was commissioned to raise the necessary funds for the construction of a residence to the Maronites in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> and for the construction of the new Maronite seminary in Rome, which opened in 1893. In 1895, Hoayek founded the Congregation of the Maronite Sisters of the Holy Family.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Becoming_Patriarch">Becoming Patriarch</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Becoming Patriarch">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Elie_Hoyek_1899.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Elie_Hoyek_1899.jpg/220px-Elie_Hoyek_1899.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="296" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Elie_Hoyek_1899.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="265" data-file-height="357" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Elie_Hoyek_1899.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Patriarch Hoayek in 1899</div></div></div>
<p>The previous patriarch, John el Hajj, died on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Eve" title="Christmas Eve">Christmas Eve</a> in 1898; Hoayek was requested to return to Lebanon from a trip to Rome. On January 6, 1899, he was elected Patriarch and, in the consistory of June 19, 1899, Hoayek was ratified as the choice of Maronite bishops by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>, who granted him the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hoayek adopted the middle name Peter (Boutros) to signify him being the successor to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a>, first Bishop of Antioch.
</p><p>Patriarch Hoayek's residence was simple, consisting of three rooms: a bedroom/office, a reception room and a chapel. He was known to particularly revere the readings of the confessions of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, the Eternal Maxims and the <a href="/wiki/Imitation_of_Christ" title="Imitation of Christ">Imitation of Christ</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Among his first acts as patriarch was the construction of a new residence in <a href="/wiki/Bkerk%C3%A9" title="Bkerké">Bkerké</a>, replacing the former one: the foundation stone was laid on September 29, 1899. In 1893, he began the construction of the new Maronite College of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, inaugurated on February 7, 1904. In 1906, he obtained from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> the division of the eparchy of Tyre and Sidon in two separate dioceses.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Between 1906 and 1908, to commemorate the fiftieth anniversary of the proclamation of the dogma of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mary</a>, he ordered the construction of the statue of the Madonna in Harissa that still dominates the region.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Support_for_the_diaspora">Support for the diaspora</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Support for the diaspora">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was behind the creation of a Patriarchal Vicariate in <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a> in 1904, the first Maronite ecclesiastical structure after 1736. 
He was the founder of the church in <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, delegating Shikrallah Khoury and Peter Chebly. 
He was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Eparchy" title="Eparchy">independent dioceses</a> in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States of America</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> in 1920.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="World_War_I">World War I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: World War I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Kafno" class="mw-redirect" title="Kafno">Kafno</a></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Kafno_Hoayek.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Kafno_Hoayek.jpg/220px-Kafno_Hoayek.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Kafno_Hoayek.jpg/330px-Kafno_Hoayek.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Kafno_Hoayek.jpg/440px-Kafno_Hoayek.jpg 2x" data-file-width="960" data-file-height="642" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Kafno_Hoayek.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Patriarch Hoayek distributing bread to the hungry during the <a href="/wiki/Kafno" class="mw-redirect" title="Kafno">Kafno</a></div></div></div>
<p>The First World War saw a lot of persecution of the Christians in the Middle East.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Vagueness" title="Wikipedia:Vagueness"><span title="This information is too vague. (January 2015)">vague</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Some sources cite one and a half million of <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenians</a> were killed by <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> forces in the war. The Ottoman Empire was allied to Germany. The Allies (France and Great Britain) set up a blockage of the Lebanese and Syrian coastline against the Ottoman Empire which leads to the start of a famine. <a href="/wiki/Jamal_Pasha" class="mw-redirect" title="Jamal Pasha">Jamal Pasha</a>, Ottoman military governor took steps to exile Hoayek because of his relations with France. In 1915, the blockade as well as a large locust infestation had resulted in diminished food supplies. While the granaries are almost empty, the Ottoman Empire requisitions Lebanese foodstuffs. As a result, a third of the Lebanese population died of starvation. This situation continued until 1919. The Patriarch received funds from Lebanese Diaspora and the French government. Again, Jamal Pasha attempted to exile the Patriarch.
</p><p>The HM British army entered <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a> while the Ottoman (and their German allies) retreated to the north. This ended four hundred years of Ottoman rule in Lebanon. A transitional governance body was installed by the allied British and French forces.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Peace_Congress_in_Versailles">Peace Congress in Versailles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Peace Congress in Versailles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1919, Patriarch Hoayek headed a second Lebanese delegation at the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919" class="mw-redirect" title="Paris Peace Conference, 1919">Paris Peace Conference</a>. 
<a href="/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq" title="Faisal I of Iraq">Prince Faisal</a> had hoped for an Arab kingdom to encompass Lebanon, Syria, Iraq, Palestine, and the Arabian Peninsula. Hoayek fought to be able to see Lebanon free from the Ottoman Empire and had no desire to see it become part of an Arab monarchy.
</p><p>The Lebanese delegation led by Hoayek presented the Lebanese aspirations as follows:   
</p>
<ul><li>The extension of the frontiers of Lebanon to include the cities of <a href="/wiki/Beirut" title="Beirut">Beirut</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon" title="Tyre, Lebanon">Tyre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sidon" title="Sidon">Sidon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tripoli,_Lebanon" title="Tripoli, Lebanon">Tripoli</a> and the districts of Akkar, Beqaa, and Southern Lebanon. These cities and districts were natural parts of Lebanon but were administratively separated by the Ottoman rule.</li>
<li>The recognition of Lebanon's full independence</li>
<li>French protectorate over Lebanon.</li></ul>
<p>On October 25, he presented the assembly of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919" class="mw-redirect" title="Paris Peace Conference, 1919">Peace Congress</a> with this memorandum.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Independence_of_Lebanon">Independence of Lebanon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Independence of Lebanon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg/220px-Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="139" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg/330px-Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg/440px-Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="539" data-file-height="341" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Proclamation_of_the_state_of_Greater_Lebanon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Proclamation of the state of Greater Lebanon</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Henri_Gouraud_(soldier)" class="mw-redirect" title="Henri Gouraud (soldier)">General Gouraud</a> announced a declaration by France of the independence of Lebanon on September 1, 1920. He outlined the country's boundaries to include the area from Ras-al-Naqurah (Naqurah peninsula) in the south to <a href="/wiki/Nahr_al-Kabir" title="Nahr al-Kabir">Nahr-el-Kabir</a> in the north and from the summits of the Anti-Lebanon mountain in the east to the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean" class="mw-redirect" title="Mediterranean">Mediterranean</a> sea in the west. The new entity under French <a href="/wiki/Mandate_(international_law)" title="Mandate (international law)">mandate</a> was initially called <a href="/wiki/Greater_Lebanon" title="Greater Lebanon">The State of Greater Lebanon</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Patriarch Elias Hoayek died on December 24, 1931, in Bkerké.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> authorized the promulgation of decrees on the heroic virtues of Patriarch Elias Howayek, declaring him Venerable on July 5, 2019.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotation">Quotation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Peter_Hoayek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Quotation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hoyek wrote: "Both national feeling and religion make it an obligation for you to respect and love everybody whatever may be his belief.  Reason leads you to fraternize with the person you live with under the same sky and on the same land." (Hoayek, Al-Zakhair Al-Saniyyat, Jounieh, 1931 p.&#160;501)
</p>
