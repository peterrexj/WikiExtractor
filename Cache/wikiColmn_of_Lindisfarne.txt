<p><b>Colmán of Lindisfarne</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 605 – 18 February 675 AD) also known as <b>Saint Colmán</b> was <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lindisfarne" title="Lindisfarne">Lindisfarne</a> from 661 until 664.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colm%C3%A1n_of_Lindisfarne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Colman was a native of the west of Ireland and had received his education on Iona.<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was probably a nobleman of Canmaicne.<sup id="cite_ref-museums_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museums-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Colman succeeded <a href="/wiki/Aidan_of_Lindisfarne" title="Aidan of Lindisfarne">Aidan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Finan_of_Lindisfarne" title="Finan of Lindisfarne">Finan</a> as bishop of Lindisfarne,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> being appointed in 661.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook219_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook219-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Colman resigned the Bishopric of Lindisfarne after the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> called by King <a href="/wiki/Oswiu_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswiu of Northumbria">Oswiu of Northumbria</a> decided to calculate <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> using the method of the <a href="/wiki/First_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="First Ecumenical Council">First Ecumenical Council</a> instead of his preferred <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic</a> method. The change to the Roman Method led Colmán to leave and travel back to Scotland and eventually, back to Iona.
</p><p>Later tradition states that between the years 665 and 667, Colman founded several churches in Scotland before returning to Iona. However, there are no seventh-century records of such activity by him. From Iona he sailed for Ireland, settling at <a href="/wiki/Inishbofin,_County_Galway" title="Inishbofin, County Galway">Inishbofin</a> in 668 AD<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where he founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, the <a href="/wiki/School_of_Mayo" class="mw-redirect" title="School of Mayo">School of Mayo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh127_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh127-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When Colman came to Mayo he brought with him half the relics of Lindisfarne, including the bones of St. Aidan and a part of the true cross. This was reputed to be in Mayo Abbey until its vanishment during the Reformation in 1537.<sup id="cite_ref-museums_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museums-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Colman was stepping into a landscape that had been decimated by the plague of 664–665. He may have been reviving an earlier church on the island or one in the area in central Connacht where Mag Éo was founded later. On Inishbofin a rift occurred between the Irish and the English "because in summer the Irish went off to wander on their own around places they knew instead of assisting at harvest, and then, as winter approached, came back and wanted to share whatever the English monks had gathered."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>What was the reason for their intermittent absence? Earlier commentators suspected that the two nations came from different agricultural backgrounds and that the Irish intermittently removed themselves from the island with the monastery's livestock for the purpose of ‘booleying’, a form of <a href="/wiki/Transhumance" title="Transhumance">transhumance</a>. It is also possible that the Irish visited their kinsfolk on the mainland. Returning to the island in winter, they helped to consume the fruits of the Saxons' labours. This situation inevitably led to tensions within the community. Disputes arose between the Saxon and Irish monks after a short time. Colman brought his Saxon followers onto the mainland and founded a monastery for them at <a href="/wiki/Mayo,_County_Mayo" title="Mayo, County Mayo">"Magh Eó"</a> - the Plain of Yew Trees,<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> subsequently known as "Mayo of the Saxons".<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Colman's last days were spent on the island of Inishbofin, where he died in 674.<sup id="cite_ref-brett_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brett-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His feast is celebrated on 8 August<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and on 13 November.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
