<p><b>René Goupil</b>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">S.J.</a> (15 May 1608 – 29 September 1642), was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> (in French "donné", "given" or "one who offers himself") who became a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> shortly before his death. He was the first of the eight <a href="/wiki/North_American_Martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="North American Martyrs">North American Martyrs</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> to receive the crown of martyrdom and the first canonized Catholic martyr in North America.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ren%C3%A9_Goupil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Goupil was baptized in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Martin-du-Bois,_Maine-et-Loire" title="Saint-Martin-du-Bois, Maine-et-Loire">St-Martin-du-Bois</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>, in the ancient Province of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Anjou" title="Duchy of Anjou">Anjou</a>, on 15 May 1608, the son of Hippolite Goupil and Luce Provost.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-aana_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aana-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He was working as a <a href="/wiki/Surgeon" title="Surgeon">surgeon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a> before entering the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> (Jesuits) in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> on 16 March 1639. He had to leave the novitiate due to deafness.<sup id="cite_ref-DictionaryOfCanadian_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictionaryOfCanadian-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Goupil volunteered to serve as a <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> missionary working to assist the Jesuit Fathers. In 1640 he arrived in <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DictionaryOfCanadian_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictionaryOfCanadian-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> From 1640 to 1642, he served at the Saint-Joseph de Sillery Mission, near <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, where he was charged with caring for the sick and wounded at the hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-DictionaryOfCanadian_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictionaryOfCanadian-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His work primarily involved wound dressings and bloodlettings.<sup id="cite_ref-aana_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aana-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1642 Goupil traveled to the <a href="/wiki/Wyandot_people" title="Wyandot people">Huron</a> missions with about forty other persons, including several Huron chiefs and Jesuit Father <a href="/wiki/Isaac_Jogues" title="Isaac Jogues">Isaac Jogues</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DictionaryOfCanadian_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictionaryOfCanadian-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They were captured by the <a href="/wiki/Mohawk_people" title="Mohawk people">Mohawk</a>, taken to their easternmost village of Ossernenon (about 9 miles west of present-day <a href="/wiki/Auriesville,_New_York" title="Auriesville, New York">Auriesville, New York</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-snow_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-snow-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and tortured. After teaching a Mohawk boy the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">sign of the cross</a>, Goupil was killed on the Feast of St. Michael the Archangel, 29 September 1642, by a blow to the head with a tomahawk. He died uttering the Holy Name of Jesus, as he had practiced in case of martyrdom. Fr. Jogues was present and gave Goupil absolution before expiring. Before being martyred, Goupil had professed <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> as a Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> before Fr. Jogues.<sup id="cite_ref-DictionaryOfCanadian_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictionaryOfCanadian-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Many of the 24 Huron accompanying Goupil were baptized Catholic converts. Traditional enemies of the Mohawk, they were slowly tortured per Iroquois ritual before being killed.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ren%C3%A9_Goupil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Goupil is venerated as the first Jesuit martyr of Canada and one of three martyrs of the present United States territory. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 29 June 1930 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> along with the seven other <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Martyrs" title="Canadian Martyrs">Canadian Martyrs</a> or "North American Martyrs." He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Anesthesia" title="Anesthesia">anesthetists</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-aana_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aana-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At <a href="/wiki/Fordham_University" title="Fordham University">Fordham University</a>'s Rose Hill Campus in the Bronx, New York, a freshman <a href="/wiki/Dormitory" title="Dormitory">dormitory</a>—Martyrs' Court—has three sections, which are named for the three US martyr-saints: René Goupil, Isaac Jogues, and <a href="/wiki/Jean_Lalande" class="mw-redirect" title="Jean Lalande">Jean Lalande</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Goupil is also honored at the Catholic youth camp <a href="/wiki/Camp_Ondessonk" title="Camp Ondessonk">Camp Ondessonk</a>, where a unit is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
