<p><b>Willibrord</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Villibrordus</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 658 – 7 November AD 739) was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> missionary and saint, known as the "Apostle to the <a href="/wiki/Frisians" title="Frisians">Frisians</a>" in the modern <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>. He became the first <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Utrecht_(695%E2%80%931580)" title="Diocese of Utrecht (695–1580)">bishop of Utrecht</a> and died at <a href="/wiki/Echternach" title="Echternach">Echternach</a>, Luxembourg.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibrord&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His father, named <a href="/wiki/Wilgils" title="Wilgils">Wilgils</a> or Hilgis,<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was styled by <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Saxon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>. Newly converted to Christianity, Wilgils entrusted his son as an oblate to the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Ripon" title="Ripon">Ripon</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and withdrew from the world, constructing a small oratory, near the mouth of the <a href="/wiki/Humber" title="Humber">Humber</a>, dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Saint Andrew</a>. The king and nobles of the district endowed him with estates until he was at last able to build a church, over which Alcuin afterwards ruled.
</p><p>Willibrord grew up under the influence of <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>, Bishop of York. Later he joined the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictines</a>. He spent the years between the ages of 20 and 32 in the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Rath_Melsigi" title="Rath Melsigi">Rath Melsigi</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in County Carlow, Ireland, which was a centre of European learning in the 7th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Frisia">Frisia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibrord&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Frisia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During this time he studied under <a href="/wiki/Ecgberht_of_Ripon" title="Ecgberht of Ripon">Ecgberht of Ripon</a>, who sent him and eleven companions to Christianise the pagan Frisians of the North Sea coast at the request of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Herstal" title="Pepin of Herstal">Pepin of Herstal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasian</a> <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_palace" title="Mayor of the palace">mayor of the palace</a>, who had nominal <a href="/wiki/Suzerainty" title="Suzerainty">suzerainty</a> over that region. Willibrord travelled to Rome twice.  Both of these trips to Rome have historical significance.  
</p><p>As <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> tells us, Willibrord was not the only Anglo-Saxon to travel to Rome.  The way in which he described the visit and its purpose is important; unlike all the others, Willibrord was not on the usual pilgrimage to the graves of the apostles Peter and Paul and the martyrs. Rather, "he made haste to Rome, where Pope Sergius then presided over the apostolical see, that he might undertake the desired work of preaching the Gospel to the Gentiles, with his licence and blessing".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  As such he came to the pope not as a pilgrim but specifically as a missionary.  The second time he went to Rome, on 21 November 695, in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Pope Sergius I</a> gave him a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> and consecrated him as bishop of the Frisians.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He returned to <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a> to preach and establish churches, among them a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a>, where he built his <a href="/wiki/St._Martin%27s_Cathedral,_Utrecht" title="St. Martin&#39;s Cathedral, Utrecht">cathedral</a>. Willibrord is counted the first <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Utrecht_(695%E2%80%931580)" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Utrecht (695–1580)">bishop of Utrecht</a>.
</p><p>In 698 he established the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Echternach" title="Abbey of Echternach">Abbey of Echternach</a> on the site of a Roman villa in <a href="/wiki/Echternach" title="Echternach">Echternach</a>, which was donated to him by Pepin's mother-in-law, <a href="/wiki/Irmina_of_Oeren" title="Irmina of Oeren">Irmina of Oeren</a>, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Seneschal" title="Seneschal">seneschal</a> and Count Palatine <a href="/wiki/Hugobert" title="Hugobert">Hugobert</a>. After Hugobert died, Irmina founded a Benedictine convent at Horren in <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>. When a plague threatened her community, she gained the help of Willibrord; and when the pestilence passed by the convent, she gave Willibrord the lands for his abbey in Echternach.<sup id="cite_ref-Willibrordus_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Willibrordus-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pepin of Heristal died in 714. In 716 the pagan <a href="/wiki/Radbod,_king_of_the_Frisians" class="mw-redirect" title="Radbod, king of the Frisians">Radbod, king of the Frisians</a>, retook possession of Frisia, burning churches and killing many missionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Willibrord and his monks were forced to flee. After the death of Radbod in 719, Willibrord returned to resume his work, under the protection of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>. Winfrid, better known as <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a>, joined Willibrord and stayed for three years, before travelling on to preach in Frankish territory.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibrord&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Willibrosscrine.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Willibrosscrine.JPG/220px-Willibrosscrine.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Willibrosscrine.JPG/330px-Willibrosscrine.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Willibrosscrine.JPG/440px-Willibrosscrine.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Willibrosscrine.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Willibrord in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Echternach" title="Abbey of Echternach">Abbey of Echternach</a></div></div></div>
<p>Willibrord died on 7 November 739 at the age of 81, and according to his wish, was buried in Echternach. He was quickly judged to be a saint. Willibrord wells, which skirted his missionary routes, were visited by the people, to solicit the healing of various nervous diseases, especially of children.<sup id="cite_ref-Willibrordus_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Willibrordus-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the Catholic Church his feast day is celebrated on <a href="/wiki/November_7" title="November 7">7 November</a> outside England, but on 29 November in England, by order of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. Willibrord is honoured in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church (US)</a> on 7 November.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Numerous miracles and relics have been attributed to him. On one occasion, the transport of his relics was celebrated thus "the five bishops in full pontificals assisted; engaged in the dance were 2 Swiss guards, 16 standard-bearers, 3,045 singers, 136 priests, 426 musicians, 15,085 dancers, and 2,032 players".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Dancing_procession_of_Echternach" title="Dancing procession of Echternach">dancing procession</a> continues to be held in Echternach every year on Whit Tuesday, and attracts thousands of participants and an equal number of spectators, to honour the memory of a saint who is often called the apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Benelux" title="Benelux">Benelux</a> countries (Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg).<sup id="cite_ref-Willibrordus_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Willibrordus-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg/260px-Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="195" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg/390px-Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg/520px-Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2304" data-file-height="1728" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gravelines_-_Eglise_Saint-Willibrord_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Willibrord's Church at <a href="/wiki/Gravelines" title="Gravelines">Gravelines</a></div></div></div><p>At <a href="/wiki/Gravelines" title="Gravelines">Gravelines</a> in northern France, where Willibrord is said to have landed after crossing the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">Channel</a> on his mission to the Frisians, the church in the old town is dedicated to him. Gravelines grew up after ca. 800 around the chapel commemorating Willibrord's mission.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a 13th-century chapel dedicated to Willibrord at <a href="/wiki/Weissenburg_Abbey,_Alsace" title="Weissenburg Abbey, Alsace">Weissenburg Abbey, Alsace</a>, where Willibrord's benefactress <a href="/wiki/Irmina_of_Oeren" title="Irmina of Oeren">Irmina of Oeren</a> was also venerated.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibrord&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography"><i>Life</i></a> was written by <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> and dedicated to the Abbot of Echternach. Alcuin probably made use of an older one written by a British monk, which is now lost.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Bede also makes mention of Willibrord.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_(vers_657-739).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG/220px-Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG/330px-Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG/440px-Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_%28vers_657-739%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2352" data-file-height="1568" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_de_Willibrord_d%27Utrecht_(vers_657-739).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Saint Willibrord in <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a></div></div></div>
<p>Nothing written by Willibrord can be found save a marginal note in the Calendar of Echternach giving some chronological data.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Echternach_Gospels" title="Echternach Gospels">Echternach Gospels</a>, a copy of the Gospels (<a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_nationale_de_France" title="Bibliothèque nationale de France">Bibliothèque nationale</a>, Paris, 9389) under the name of Willibrord is an Irish <a href="/wiki/Codex" title="Codex">codex</a> no doubt brought by Willibrord from Ireland.
</p><p>In 752/753 <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a> wrote a letter to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Pope Stephen II</a>, in which he says that Willibrord destroyed the Frisian pagan sanctuaries and temples.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In Alcuin's <i>Life of Willibrord</i> there are two texts about Willibrord and pagan places of worship. In one he arrived with his companions in <a href="/wiki/Walcheren" title="Walcheren">Walcheren</a> in the Netherlands, where he smashed a sculpture of the ancient religion.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In the second text passage Willibord arrived on an island called Fositesland (possibly <a href="/wiki/Heligoland" title="Heligoland">Heligoland</a>) where a pagan god named <a href="/wiki/Forseti" title="Forseti">Fosite</a> was worshipped. Here he despoiled this god of its sanctity by using the god's sacred well for baptisms and the sacred cattle for food.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
