<p><b>Saint Servatius</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Sint Servaas</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Servais</i>; <a href="/wiki/Limburgish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Limburgish language">Limburgish</a>: <i lang="li">Sintervaos</i>; <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Սուրբ Սերվատիոս</span> <i>Surb Servatios</i>) (born in <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a>, died in <a href="/wiki/Maastricht" title="Maastricht">Maastricht</a>, traditionally on 13 May 384) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tongeren" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tongeren">bishop of Tongeren</a> &#8212;Latin: <i>Atuatuca Tungrorum</i>, the capital of the <a href="/wiki/Tungri" title="Tungri">Tungri</a>&#8212;. Servatius is <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the city of <a href="/wiki/Maastricht" title="Maastricht">Maastricht</a> and the towns of <a href="/wiki/Schijndel" title="Schijndel">Schijndel</a> and <a href="/wiki/Grimbergen" title="Grimbergen">Grimbergen</a>. He is one of the <a href="/wiki/Ice_Saints" title="Ice Saints">Ice Saints</a>. His feast day is May 13.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg/170px-20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="224" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg/255px-20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg/340px-20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2160" data-file-height="2844" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:20180531_Maastricht_Heiligdomsvaart_16.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Saint Servatius in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius" title="Basilica of Saint Servatius">Basilica of St Servatius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Maastricht" title="Maastricht">Maastricht</a></div></div></div>
<p>A widely travelled diplomat and a determined opponent of <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>, the presence of Servatius is recorded at several <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synods</a> and church councils. In 343, <i>Sarbatios</i> - Greek texts rendering <i>v</i> as <i>b</i> - was present at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Sardica" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Sardica">Council of Sardica</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Sofia" title="Sofia">Sofia</a>). In the debates, Servatius represented the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinitarian</a> view, which clashed with the Arian view of most Eastern bishops. According to <a href="/wiki/Sulpicius_Severus" title="Sulpicius Severus">Sulpicius Severus</a>, Servatius again eloquently denounced Arianism at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Rimini" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Rimini">Council of Rimini</a> in 359.
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Athanasius" class="mw-redirect" title="Athanasius">Athanasius</a>, the leading opponent of Arianism, was exiled to <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a> (in 336 or 343), he may have met with Servatius, because both men campaigned against Arian bishops and priests in the region.
</p><p>According to a medieval legend, Servatius took part a Council of Cologne in 346, testifying that Euphrates, bishop of Cologne, "denied the divinity of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>, this even happened in the presence of Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Euphrates was historically known as an opponent of Arianism and alongside Athanasius and Servatius took part in the Council of Sardica.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The legend, compiled in Trier, probably aimed at reducing the status of the church of Cologne, with which Trier was in conflict over ecclesiastical predominance.
</p><p>After co-emperor <a href="/wiki/Constans" title="Constans">Constans</a> had been assassinated in 350, Servatius was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> in <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>, the capital of <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Mesopotamia" title="Armenian Mesopotamia">Armenian Mesopotamia</a>, as an envoy of the <a href="/wiki/Usurper" title="Usurper">usurper</a> <a href="/wiki/Magnentius" title="Magnentius">Magnentius</a> to represent the late Constans as an unworthy tyrant and oppressor, in the hope of obtaining Constantius's recognition of Magnentius as co-ruler. The mission failed and the resulting civil war ended with the death of Magnentius in 353. The mission can be seen as a sign of the high standing of Servatius.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Servatius_and_the_Huns">Servatius and the Huns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Servatius and the Huns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>An important source about the life of Saint Servatius, albeit not a contemporary source, is <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>' <i>Glory of the Confessors</i> and <i>History of the Franks</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In his late 6th-century account, Gregory writes about <i>Aravatius</i> (identified by most scholars as Servatius), who was a bishop of Tongeren and died in Maastricht. According to the Frankish bishop and historian, Aravatius lived at the time when the <a href="/wiki/Huns" title="Huns">Huns</a> threatened Tongeren (5th century), which does not match the 4th-century dates of the synods mentioned above. It is not always clear how much of Gregory's account is history and how much is fiction. Gregory describes how Aravatius, during a vigil at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a>'s tomb in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, had a vision in which the destruction of Tongeren was forecast (because of their sinfulness). Peter then handed the <a href="/wiki/Keys_of_Heaven" title="Keys of Heaven">Keys of Heaven</a> to Aravatius, transferring to him the power to forgive sins. According to Gregory, Aravatius returned to Tongeren, brought the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of his predecessors to Maastricht, where he died and was buried alongside the Roman road, near the bridge.
</p><p>As a bishop, Servatius may have been the founder of several early Christian churches in the diocese of Tongeren. Two likely candidates are the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady,_Tongeren" title="Basilica of Our Lady, Tongeren">Basilica of Our Lady</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tongeren" title="Tongeren">Tongeren</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady,_Maastricht" title="Basilica of Our Lady, Maastricht">Basilica of Our Lady</a> in <a href="/wiki/Maastricht" title="Maastricht">Maastricht</a>. In the case of Tongeren, this traditional claim was supported by excavations in the 1980s, which revealed under the medieval church remains of a 4th-century church, possibly the original cathedral of the diocese. The origins of the Maastricht church of Our Lady remain uncertain, since no excavations have ever been carried out inside this church. In another Maastricht church however, the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius" title="Basilica of Saint Servatius">Basilica of Saint Servatius</a>, excavations in the 1990s have revealed the remains of a 6th-century church (built by bishop <a href="/wiki/Monulph" title="Monulph">Monulph</a> and described by Gregory of Tours as a <i>magnum templum</i>), with at its center a late <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> structure, possibly the tomb of Servatius.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Westfalia,_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna,_xv_secolo_01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg/260px-Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="135" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg/390px-Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg/520px-Westfalia%2C_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna%2C_xv_secolo_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3048" data-file-height="1588" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Westfalia,_geneaologia_di_sant%27anna,_xv_secolo_01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The extended family of Saint Servatius, including Jesus, Mary, John the Baptist and Saint Anne (16th-century panel, <a href="/wiki/Treasury_of_the_Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius" title="Treasury of the Basilica of Saint Servatius">Treasury of the Basilica of Saint Servatius</a>)</div></div></div>
<p>Over the centuries legends accumulated around the historical figure of the bishop of Tongeren. Two early <i>vitae</i> (<a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">biographies</a>) place Servatius' birth in <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a> and make him a cousin of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>, and thus a distant relative of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> (neither were mentioned by Gregory of Tours).
</p><p>Around  1075, the French priest Jocundus was commissioned by the <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">chapter</a> of Saint Servatius to write another <i>Vita sancti Servatii</i>. Jocundus is also the author of the <i>Miracula sancti Servatii</i>, a sequel to the vita, describing all the miracles that happened after Servatius' death. According to some historians, both works were composed to quell doubts about the genealogy of Servatius and his Armenian descent. These doubts had been raised at the Council of Mainz in 1049. When envoys from the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors" title="List of Byzantine emperors">Byzantine emperor</a> arrived at the Council of Mainz, confirming accounts by a certain Alagrecus who had testified that Servatius was Armenian, and asserting that his birthplace was <i>Fenuste</i>, southeast of <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>, this helped to erase some doubts but Servatius' kinship to Jesus was never confirmed by an official council.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the end of the 12th century the poet <a href="/wiki/Henric_van_Veldeke" class="mw-redirect" title="Henric van Veldeke">Henric van Veldeke</a> wrote a new legend of Saint Servatius, based on the earlier accounts by Gregory of Tours and Jocundus, to which he added several more miracles, thus emphasizing Saint Servatius' saintliness. The work is considered one of the earliest works of <a href="/wiki/Dutch_literature" class="mw-redirect" title="Dutch literature">Dutch literature</a>, even though it was written in <a href="/wiki/Limburgish" title="Limburgish">Limburgish</a>, the most divergent of the 4 major dialects that comprises <a href="/wiki/Middle_Dutch" title="Middle Dutch">Middle Dutch</a>.
</p><p>In the 17th century, the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandists</a> tried to separate some of the facts and myths surrounding Servatius. They managed to calculate the exact date of his death (13 May 384), which for a long time was accepted as a historical fact.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Maastricht">In Maastricht</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In Maastricht">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to tradition the saint's remains are buried in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius" title="Basilica of Saint Servatius">Basilica of Saint Servatius</a> in Maastricht, where they lie in a crypt dating from the 6th century. His tomb has been a place of pilgrimage for many centuries. Famous visitors include <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V (Holy Roman Emperor)</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. In Maastricht, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> belonging to the Russian Exarchate of the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> is also dedicated to Saint Servatius. The <a href="/wiki/Sint_Servaasbrug" title="Sint Servaasbrug">Sint Servaasbrug</a>, the oldest bridge over the river <a href="/wiki/Meuse" title="Meuse">Meuse</a> in Maastricht, was named after Saint Servatius. The name 'Servaas' was a popular given name in Maastricht and surroundings for many centuries.
</p><p>The 12th-century gilded <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary chest</a> in the Basilica of Saint Servatius, containing the saint's relics, is a major work of <a href="/wiki/Mosan_art" title="Mosan art">Mosan art</a> and became known as the 'Chest of Distress' (Dutch: <i>Noodkist</i>) as it was carried around town in times of distress. A <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> with the relics of Saint Servatius and other saints takes place every seven years: the <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage_of_the_Relics,_Maastricht" title="Pilgrimage of the Relics, Maastricht">Maastricht Pilgrimage of the Relics</a> (Dutch: <i>Heiligdomsvaart</i>). The <i>Noodkist</i> is normally kept in the <a href="/wiki/Treasury_of_the_Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius" title="Treasury of the Basilica of Saint Servatius">Treasury of the Basilica of Saint Servatius</a>, along with the so-called 'Servatiana' (objects that are associated with the saint, such as his <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim%27s_staff" title="Pilgrim&#39;s staff">pilgrim's staff</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Pectoral_cross" title="Pectoral cross">pectoral cross</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Chalice_(cup)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chalice (cup)">chalice</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Paten" title="Paten">paten</a> and a symbolic key to heaven).
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Elsewhere">Elsewhere</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servatius_of_Tongeren&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Elsewhere">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Other historic churches in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> were dedicated to the saint, e.g. the collegiate churches of <a href="/wiki/Grimbergen_Abbey" title="Grimbergen Abbey">Grimbergen Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg_Abbey" title="Quedlinburg Abbey">Quedlinburg Abbey</a>. In the <a href="/wiki/Theft_of_medieval_art_from_Quedlinburg" title="Theft of medieval art from Quedlinburg">Quedlinburg Treasury</a> important relics of Saint Servatius are kept. In many churches around the world, reliquaries, statues, stained glass windows, altar pieces and paintings of Servatius are revered. St. Servatius Church in Kampung Sawah, <a href="/wiki/Indonesia" title="Indonesia">Indonesia</a> received the relics of Servatius from Maastricht in its establishment date on October 6, 1996. Since then, highly infused by <a href="/wiki/Betawi_people" title="Betawi people">Betawi</a> culture, the festival honoring the relics has been celebrated annually by the parishioners of the church.
</p><p>A mid-15th-century wooden sculpture of Memelia (the ancestor linking Servatius to Jesus) with the infant Servatius in her arms (identifiable by the infant wearing a bishop's mitre) in the Vendsyssel Historiske Museum in <a href="/wiki/Hj%C3%B8rring" title="Hjørring">Hjørring</a>, <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a>, is iconographically so similar to sculptures of the <a href="/wiki/Madonna_and_Child" class="mw-redirect" title="Madonna and Child">Madonna and Child</a>, that it was long misattributed.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Sri_Lanka" title="Sri Lanka">Sri Lanka</a> , <a href="/wiki/St._Servatius%27_College" title="St. Servatius&#39; College">St. Servatius' College</a> was built  around 1897 by a Belgian priest, Father Augustus Standard, on the bank of the river Nilwala at Pallimulla, <a href="/wiki/Matara,_Sri_Lanka" title="Matara, Sri Lanka">Matara</a>.
</p>
