<p><b>Remigius</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr"><b>Remi</b></i> or <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr"><b>Rémi</b></i></span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;437</span> – 13 January 533) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Reims">Bishop of Reims</a> and "Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>". On 25 December 496, he <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptised</a> <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_Frankish_monarchs" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Frankish monarchs">King of the Franks</a>. The baptism, leading to about 3000 additional converts, was an important event in the <a href="/wiki/Christianization_of_the_Franks" title="Christianization of the Franks">Christianization of the Franks</a>. Because of Clovis's efforts, a large number of churches were established in the formerly <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> lands of the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish empire">Frankish empire</a>, establishing a distinct <a href="/wiki/Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism">Catholic</a> variety of Christianity for the first time in Germanic lands, most of whom had been converted to <a href="/wiki/Arian_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Arian Christianity">Arian Christianity</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Remigius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Remigius was born, traditionally, at <a href="/wiki/Cerny-en-Laonnois" title="Cerny-en-Laonnois">Cerny-en-Laonnois</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>, into the highest levels of <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> society. He is said to have been son of Emilius, count of Laon (who is not otherwise attested) and of Celina, daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Soissons">Bishop of Soissons</a>, which Clovis had conquered in 487. He studied at Reims and soon became so noted for his learning and sanctity, and his high status, that he was elected <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Reims">Bishop of Reims</a> at age 21, though still a layman.<sup id="cite_ref-Dedieu_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dedieu-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He was both <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chancellor of France">Lord Chancellor of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9f%C3%A9rendaire_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Référendaire of France">Référendaire of France</a>.
</p><p>The story of the return of the sacred vessels (most notably the <a href="/wiki/Vase_of_Soissons" title="Vase of Soissons">Vase of Soissons</a>), which had been stolen from the church of Soissons, testifies to the friendly relations existing between him and Clovis, King of the Franks, whom he converted to Christianity with the assistance of <a href="/wiki/Vedast" title="Vedast">Vedast</a> (Vedastus, Vaast, Waast) and <a href="/wiki/Clotilde" title="Clotilde">Clotilde</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundian</a> princess who was wife to Clovis. Even before he embraced Christianity, Clovis had showered benefits upon Remigius and the Christians of Reims, and after his victory over the <a href="/wiki/Alamanni" class="mw-redirect" title="Alamanni">Alamanni</a> in the battle of Tolbiac at around 497 AD, he requested Remigius to baptize him at Reims (December 25, 496) in the presence of a large company of Franks and Alamanni; according to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, 3,000 Franks were baptized with Clovis.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>King Clovis granted Remigius stretches of territory, in which Remigius established and endowed many churches. He erected bishoprics at <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a>; <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a>; <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9rouanne" title="Thérouanne">Thérouanne</a>, where he personally ordained the first bishop in 499; <a href="/wiki/Arras,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Arras, France">Arras</a>, where he installed St. Vedast; and Laon, which he gave to his niece's husband Gunband. In 530 he consecrated <a href="/wiki/Medardus" title="Medardus">Medardus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Noyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Noyon">Bishop of Noyon</a>. Remigius' brother Principius was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Soissons">Bishop of Soissons</a> and also corresponded with <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_Apollinaris" title="Sidonius Apollinaris">Sidonius Apollinaris</a>, whose letters give a sense of the highly cultivated courtly literary Gallo-Roman style all three men shared.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
  <div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg/220px-Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="338" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg/330px-Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9a/Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg 2x" data-file-width="379" data-file-height="583" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bateme_de_Clovis_par_St_Remy-edit.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Baptism of Clovis</i> by <a href="/wiki/Paul_Dubois_(sculptor)" title="Paul Dubois (sculptor)">Paul Dubois</a>, 1896, at the side of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Remi" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Remi">Abbey of Saint-Remi</a>, in Reims</div></div></div>
<p>The chroniclers of <i>"<a href="/wiki/Gallia_Christiana" title="Gallia Christiana">Gallia Christiana</a>"</i> record that numerous donations were made to Remigius by the Frankish nobles, which he presented to the cathedral at Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-Dedieu_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dedieu-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though Remigius never attended any of the church councils, in 517 he held a synod at Reims, at which after a heated discussion he converted a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> views.<sup id="cite_ref-Dedieu_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dedieu-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Although Remigius's influence over people and prelates was extraordinary, upon one occasion his condoning of the offences of one Claudius, a priest whom Remigius had consecrated, brought upon him the rebukes of his episcopal brethren, who deemed Claudius deserving of degradation. The reply of Remigius, still extant, is able and convincing.
</p><p>Few authentic works of Remigius remain: his <i>"Declamations"</i> were elaborately admired by Sidonius Apollinaris, in a finely turned letter to Remigius, but are now lost.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Four letters survive in the collection known as the <i><a href="/wiki/Epistulae_Austrasicae" class="mw-redirect" title="Epistulae Austrasicae">Epistulae Austrasicae</a></i>: one containing his defence in the matter of Claudius, two written to Clovis, and a fourth to Bishop <a href="/wiki/Falco_of_Tongres" class="mw-redirect" title="Falco of Tongres">Falco of Tongres</a>. The <i>"Testament of Saint Remigius"</i> is apocryphal. A brief and strictly legendary <i>"Vita"</i> was formerly ascribed to <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>. Another, according to <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>, was written by Ignatius, bishop of Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A letter congratulating <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hormisdas" title="Pope Hormisdas">Pope Hormisdas</a> upon his election (523) is apocryphal, and "the letter in which Pope Hormisdas appears to have appointed him vicar of the kingdom of Clovis is proved to be spurious; it is presumed to have been an attempt of Hincmar to base his pretensions for the elevation of Reims to the primacy, following the alleged precedent of Remigius."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A Commentary on the Pauline Epistles (edited Villalpandus, 1699) is not his work, but that of <a href="/wiki/Remigius_of_Auxerre" title="Remigius of Auxerre">Remigius of Auxerre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Remigius' relics were kept in the <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre-Dame de Reims">Cathedral of Reims</a>, whence Hincmar had them <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/%C3%89pernay" title="Épernay">Épernay</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Viking</a> invasions and thence, in 1099 to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-R%C3%A9my" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Rémy">Abbey of Saint-Rémy</a>.
</p><p>His tomb in Reims was deliberastely desecrated on 7 October 1793 by a Commissioner of the Convention during the French Revolution due to the link between the tomb and royal patronage.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Christian_Recognition">Christian Recognition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Remigius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Christian Recognition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast day in France is celebrated on 1 October.
</p><p>Remigius is honored in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/1_October" class="mw-redirect" title="1 October">1 October</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
