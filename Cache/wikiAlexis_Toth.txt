<p><b>Alexis Georgievich Toth</b> (<b>Алексий Георгиевич Товт</b> or <b>Alexis of Wilkes-Barre</b>; March 18, 1853 &#8211; May 7, 1909) was a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox</a> church leader in the <a href="/wiki/Midwestern_United_States" title="Midwestern United States">Midwestern United States</a> who, having resigned his position as a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Catholic</a> priest in the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church">Ruthenian Catholic Church</a>, became responsible for the conversions of approximately 20,000 <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Rite Catholics</a> to the Russian Orthodox Church, which contributed to the growth of <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Eastern Orthodoxy</a> in the United States and the eventual establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the Orthodox Church in 1994.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexis_Toth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alexis Georgievich Toth was born to George and Cecilia Toth (or Tovt) on March 14, 1853,  in <a href="/wiki/Kobylnice_(Svidn%C3%ADk_District)" title="Kobylnice (Svidník District)">Kobylnice</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov" title="Prešov">Prešov</a> in the Szepes county of <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a> (then a part of the <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Empire" title="Austrian Empire">Austrian Empire</a>) during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Franz_Joseph" class="mw-redirect" title="Franz Joseph">Franz Joseph</a>.  Having completed his primary schooling, he attended a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_(term)" title="Roman Catholic (term)">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> for one year, followed by three years in a <a href="/wiki/Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Catholic">Greek Catholic</a> seminary and additional time at the <a href="/wiki/Charles_University_in_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles University in Prague">University of Prague</a>, where he graduated with a degree in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>.
</p><p>Toth married Rosalie Mihalics on April 18, 1878, and was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a> in 1878 by <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">Bishop</a> Nicholas Toth, the Greek Catholic Bishop of Prešov. Following the death of his wife and child a few years later, he served in local parishes, as <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_chancery" title="Diocesan chancery">diocesan chancellor</a>, and as professor and director at the Greek Catholic seminary of Prešov. In 1889, Fr. Alexis' bishop received a petition from the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church">Ruthenian Catholic Church</a> in the United States, asking that Toth be sent to them as a priest. He arrived on November 15, 1889, and by the 27th of that month was holding services at St. Mary's Greek Catholic Church in <a href="/wiki/Minneapolis,_Minnesota" class="mw-redirect" title="Minneapolis, Minnesota">Minneapolis, Minnesota</a>. Finding the church barely furnished and deeply in debt, he set about rectifying the situation, ultimately bringing the parish to a place of fiscal stability whilst never drawing a salary.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conflict_with_Bishop_John_Ireland">Conflict with Bishop John Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexis_Toth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Conflict with Bishop John Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As an Eastern Rite Catholic, Toth honored the custom of paying a visit to the local <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> in his area, since there was no Eastern Rite Catholic bishop serving in the United States at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The ordinary of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Saint_Paul_and_Minneapolis" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis</a> was <a href="/wiki/John_Ireland_(bishop)" title="John Ireland (bishop)">John Ireland</a>, who had been attempting to "<a href="/wiki/Americanization_(immigration)" title="Americanization (immigration)">Americanize</a>" German and other Catholic immigrants, and was hostile to ethnic parishes such as the one in which Toth served.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When speaking of their meeting, Toth later claimed that Ireland became angry and threw Toth's priestly credentials onto his table while ardently protesting his presence in the city. Toth reported that Ireland said he did not consider Toth or his bishop to be truly Catholic,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in clear contradiction of the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Uzhhorod" title="Union of Uzhhorod">Union of Uzhhorod</a> and papal decrees to the contrary.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Toth reported that the conversation became more heated as it progressed, with both men losing their tempers.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ireland refused to give Toth permission to serve as a priest in Minneapolis, and furthermore ordered his parishes and priests not to have anything to do with the Ruthenian Catholic priest or his parishioners. Although Toth sent letters to his bishop in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>, detailing his experience and requesting specific instructions, he reportedly never received a reply.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Toth came to believe that he and other Eastern Rite Catholic priests in North America were to be recalled to <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>, and their parishioners folded into existing Roman Catholic congregations in their respective cities.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="From_Rome_to_Russian_Orthodoxy">From Rome to Russian Orthodoxy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexis_Toth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: From Rome to Russian Orthodoxy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Having heard nothing from his own bishop, he and other Eastern Rite Catholic priests who had shared similar experiences began to cast about for a solution to their dilemma. In December 1890, they contacted the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian</a> <a href="/wiki/Consul_(representative)" title="Consul (representative)">consul</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Francisco,_California" class="mw-redirect" title="San Francisco, California">San Francisco, California</a>, asking to be put in touch with a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Orthodox_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Orthodox Church)">Russian Orthodox bishop</a>. Correspondence and personal meetings with Bishop Vladimir Sokolovsky of San Francisco followed, culminating in Toth's decision to formally enter the Russian Orthodox Church in March 1892. Toth was accompanied by 361 fellow Eastern Rite Catholics; thousands more would follow in the years to come, largely due to his own efforts to evangelize them toward this move.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Following his conversion to Orthodoxy, Toth tirelessly preached his new faith to other Eastern Rite Catholics in North America. This, combined with further demands by U.S. <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishops" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishops">bishops</a> against <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Rite</a> parishes facilitated the conversion of over 20,000 Eastern-rite Catholics to Russian Orthodoxy by the time Toth died in 1909. The <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> has claimed that by 1916 the Latin Catholic Church had lost 163 Eastern Rite parishes, with over 100,000 <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">faithful</a>, to the Russian missionary diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_glorification">Death and glorification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexis_Toth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and glorification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg/220px-Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="285" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg/330px-Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Saint_Alexis_of_Wilkes-Barre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="440" data-file-height="569" /></a><figcaption>Icon. Saint Alexis of Wilkes-Barre, 2012</figcaption></figure>
<p>Toth was elevated to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Protopresbyter" class="mw-redirect" title="Protopresbyter">protopresbyter</a> later in life, continuing his efforts to convert the Eastern Catholics of North America to Eastern Orthodoxy.  He died on May 7, 1909, in <a href="/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania" title="Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania">Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania</a>, and was honored with a special shrine at <a href="/wiki/St._Tikhon%27s_Orthodox_Theological_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Tikhon&#39;s Orthodox Theological Seminary">St. Tikhon's Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_Canaan_Township,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="South Canaan Township, Pennsylvania">South Canaan, Pennsylvania</a>. On May 29, 1994, Toth was <a href="/wiki/Glorification#In_Orthodox_Christianity" title="Glorification">glorified</a> (canonized) as Saint Alexis of Wilkes-Barre by the Orthodox Church in America, whose establishment and membership numbers are largely traceable to his efforts.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
