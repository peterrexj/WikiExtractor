<p><b>Francis Fernández</b> (or <b>Ferdinand</b>) <b>de Capillas</b> (15 August 1607 – 15 January 1648) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who went as a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> to Asia. He died in China as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 1 October 2000, as one of the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyr_Saints_of_China" title="Martyr Saints of China">120 Martyrs of China</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>De Capillas is honored by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> among the missionaries in <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>, and is considered the glory and pride of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Ferdinand_de_Capillas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>De Capillas was born in <a href="/wiki/Baquer%C3%ADn_de_Campos" title="Baquerín de Campos">Baquerín de Campos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palencia,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Palencia, Spain">Palencia, Spain</a>, on 14 August 1607. At the age of 17 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Order of Preachers</a>, receiving the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> in the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">Priory</a> of St. Paul in <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a>. While still a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> he was sent by his Order to do <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> work in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, landing in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> during February 1631. Shortly after his arrival he was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> De Capillas remained there for the next decade, working alongside his fellow friars. His own field of labor was the district of Tuao, <a href="/wiki/Cagayan_Valley" title="Cagayan Valley">Cagayan Valley</a>, on the island of <a href="/wiki/Luzon" title="Luzon">Luzon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>De Capillas considered that time spent in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> as a period of preparation for a mission to China. At the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">Provincial</a> <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">Chapter</a> held by the friars of the Order in Manila in 1641, he was given permission to transfer to the Order's mission there, soon transferring to that island, along with a friend, Francisco Díez. He was one of the last Spanish missionaries in Taiwan before they were ousted from the island by the Dutch later that same year.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The two friars arrived in the Province of <a href="/wiki/Fujian" title="Fujian">Fujian</a>, on mainland China, in March 1642, where they joined a fellow Dominican who had survived an earlier period of persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> They then engaged in <a href="/wiki/Evangelization" class="mw-redirect" title="Evangelization">evangelization</a> among the Chinese people of the region, especially in the cities of Fogan (<a href="/wiki/Fu%27an" title="Fu&#39;an">Fu'an</a>) and Ting-Moyang Ten. They were so successful that they were able to establish a community of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic" title="Third Order of Saint Dominic">Third Order of Saint Dominic</a>. On 4 November 1644, there was a huge change of fortune for the mission. That day, his friend Francisco Diez died of natural causes. Later the same day, the <a href="/wiki/Manchuria" title="Manchuria">Manchurians</a> invaded the city of <a href="/wiki/Fuan" class="mw-redirect" title="Fuan">Fuan</a>, where the missionaries were based, in their <a href="/wiki/Qing_conquest_of_the_Ming" class="mw-redirect" title="Qing conquest of the Ming">conquest of the Ming Dynasty</a>. The new dynasty was hostile to Christianity and immediately began to persecute the <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 13 November 1647, De Capillas was captured while returning from Fogan, where he had gone to administer the sacraments to a sick person. Enduring many insults, he was taken to the worst local prison, where he suffered the torture of having his ankles crushed while being dragged.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He was scourged, repeatedly bloodied, but he endured the tortures without cries of pain, so that judges and torturers were surprised at the end.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He was moved, almost dying, to a prison where they locked up those criminals condemned to death.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> His conduct was uplifting, and aroused the admiration of others sentenced to death and even the prison guards themselves, who allowed food to be brought to him, that he not die of hunger.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>While in prison, he wrote
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I am here with other prisoners and we have developed a fellowship. They ask me about the Gospel of the Lord. I am not concerned about getting out of here because here I know I am doing the will of God. They do not let me stay up at night to pray, so I pray in bed before dawn. I live here in great joy without any worry, knowing that I am here because of Jesus Christ. The pearls I have found here these days are not always easy to find.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>On 15 January 1648 De Capillas was sentenced to death on charges of disseminating false doctrines and inciting the people against the new Emperor. His death sentence, by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitation</a>, was carried out at Fogan the same day. He thus became the first martyr within the vast Chinese empire.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Ferdinand_de_Capillas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>De Capillas was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>, 2 May 1909, along with 14 Chinese <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">laypeople</a> who had also died as martyrs. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as part of a group of 120 martyrs of China on 1 October 2000, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. Their collective memory is remembered on 9 July, while the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> of Francis Fernández de Capillas is observed on 15 January. He is considered a <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>.
</p>
