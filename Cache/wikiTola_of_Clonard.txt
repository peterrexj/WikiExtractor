<p><b>Saint Tola</b> is the name of a seventh-century <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Orthodox Christian">Orthodox Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, also referred to as "a good soldier of Christ".
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tola_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tola, the reputed son of Donchad,<sup id="cite_ref-lanigan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is also referred to as Thola or Tolanus. He was born, after the middle of the seventh century.<sup id="cite_ref-aoh_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He lived for many years the life of a hermit at Disert Tola, and founded a monastery there at the latter 7th or early 8th century.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard</a> in <a href="/wiki/Meath_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Meath (province)">Meath</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> ascribed to him now resides in the <a href="/wiki/National_Museum_of_Ireland" title="National Museum of Ireland">National Museum of Ireland</a>, in Dublin. About the year 700, he established a monastic community in northwestern <a href="/wiki/County_Clare" title="County Clare">County Clare</a>, between the <a href="/wiki/River_Fergus" title="River Fergus">River Fergus</a> and <a href="/wiki/The_Burren" title="The Burren">the Burren</a>. He sent missionaries to Europe and also helped the expansion of various scholarly studies. Later, Bishop Tola was called to preside over Clonard, County Meath until his death sometime between 734 and 737.<sup id="cite_ref-Westropp_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Westropp-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Portions of his original church, a very early church, or at least portions of its foundations, were incorporated into the 11th-century Romanesque Church that lies in ruins near <a href="/wiki/Corofin,_County_Clare" title="Corofin, County Clare">Corofin</a>.  The <a href="/wiki/High_cross" title="High cross">high cross</a> at <a href="/wiki/Dysert_O%27Dea_Monastery" title="Dysert O&#39;Dea Monastery">Dysert O'Dea Monastery</a> is referred to as Tola's Cross, Crusha Baunala or "the cross of blessing". It is from the 11th or 12th century, however, and there is no direct connect between the saint and this particular, late, high cross although one of the figures on the cross appears to be that of a bishop, perhaps St. Tola.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tola_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 30 March.<sup id="cite_ref-Westropp_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Westropp-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Tola was the patron saint of "toothaches", The cross had a portion that could be taken out and was placed near the tooth in order to assist in healing.
</p><p>In the year 1034, Coirten Ua Maebuain, Lord of Dealbhna, was killed by some of his clansmen, on the threshold of Disert Tola church. In punishment for this sacrilegious and unnatural murder, the perpetrator of the crime was killed the very same hour he perpetrated the treacherous act.<sup id="cite_ref-aoh_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
