<p><b>Gaius Vibius Volusianus</b> (died August 253) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> from 251 to 253, ruling with his father <a href="/wiki/Trebonianus_Gallus" title="Trebonianus Gallus">Trebonianus Gallus</a>.
</p><p>After Emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> and his son and co-ruler <a href="/wiki/Herennius_Etruscus" title="Herennius Etruscus">Herennius Etruscus</a> died in battle in June 251, Trebonianus Gallus was elected emperor in the field by the legion. Gallus raised <a href="/wiki/Hostilian" title="Hostilian">Hostilian</a>, the younger son of Decius, to <i><a href="/wiki/Augustus_(title)" title="Augustus (title)">augustus</a></i> (co-emperor) and elevated Volusianus to <i><a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">caesar</a></i>. After the death of Hostilian in July or August 251, Volusianus was raised to <i>augustus</i>. The short reign of Gallus and Volusianus was notable for the outbreak of a plague, which is said by some to be the reason for Hostilian's death, and for hostilities with the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_Empire" title="Sasanian Empire">Sasanian Empire</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a>. Volusianus and his father were killed in August 253 by their own soldiers, who were terrified of the forces of the usurper <a href="/wiki/Aemilian" class="mw-redirect" title="Aemilian">Aemilian</a> which were marching towards <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Volusianus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gaius Vibius Afinius Gallus Veldumnianus Volusianus was the son of <a href="/wiki/Trebonianus_Gallus" title="Trebonianus Gallus">Trebonianus Gallus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> who was named emperor in July 251 after his predecessors, Decius and <a href="/wiki/Herennius_Etruscus" title="Herennius Etruscus">Herennius Etruscus</a>, died fighting the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Abritus" title="Battle of Abritus">Battle of Abritus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBunson2014255–256_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunson2014255–256-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESalisburyMattingly192416_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalisburyMattingly192416-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Elected by the troops in the field, Gallus signed a treaty, decried by contemporary historians as "shameful", which promised tribute to the Goths if they abstained from raiding.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He subsequently made a bid for popularity by declaring <a href="/wiki/Hostilian" title="Hostilian">Hostilian</a>, the surviving son of Decius, <i>augustus</i> (emperor) with him, voluntarily sharing his power with the prior imperial family. Later, still in July 251, Gallus elevated Volusianus to <i><a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">caesar</a></i> (heir-apparent).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Volusianus was wed to Hostilian's sister, of an unknown name.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVagi2000342_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVagi2000342-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hostilian died in July or August 251.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPeachin199033–34_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeachin199033–34-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The reason for his death is disputed:<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESalisburyMattingly192416_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESalisburyMattingly192416-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Aurelius_Victor" title="Aurelius Victor">Aurelius Victor</a> and the author of the <i><a href="/wiki/Epitome_de_Caesaribus" title="Epitome de Caesaribus">Epitome de Caesaribus</a></i> both say that Hostilian died of a plague, but <a href="/wiki/Zosimus_(historian)" title="Zosimus (historian)">Zosimus</a> claims that Trebonianus Gallus murdered the young <i>augustus</i> so that Volusianus could take his place.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEManders201218_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEManders201218-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Regardless of circumstances, Volusianus subsequently received the rank of <i>augustus</i>. He was made consul in 252, alongside Trebonianus Gallus, and in 253, alongside <a href="/wiki/Lucius_Valerius_Poplicola_Balbinus_Maximus" title="Lucius Valerius Poplicola Balbinus Maximus">Valerius Maximus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECooley2012477_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECooley2012477-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECooley2012498_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECooley2012498-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The same plague that killed Hostilian devastated the rest of Rome, although Trebonianus Gallus gained much popularity by ensuring that all of the plague victims were given proper burials, regardless of their social status.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> During the reign of Trebonianus Gallus and Volusianus, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_persecution_of_Christians" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a> was not as extreme as it was under Decius, although <a href="/wiki/Pope_Cornelius" title="Pope Cornelius">Pope Cornelius</a> was exiled in 252<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>AD.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConway195712_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConway195712-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Novatian" title="Novatian">Novatian</a> was also forced to flee Rome during this period of persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarthaler2003464_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarthaler2003464-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Trebonianus Gallus and Volusianus issued only two imperial <a href="/wiki/Rescript" title="Rescript">rescripts</a> during their reign.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAndo2012195_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAndo2012195-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the shared reign of Trebonianus Gallus and Volusianus, the Roman Empire was invaded by both the Goths and the <a href="/wiki/Sassanids" class="mw-redirect" title="Sassanids">Sassanids</a>. Both co-emperors chose to stay in Rome rather than confront the invasions themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVagi2000342_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVagi2000342-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton2014826_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton2014826-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETruhart2000347_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETruhart2000347-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBird1993138_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBird1993138-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The Sassanids attacked in 252, quickly overrunning <a href="/wiki/Roman_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a>, and defeated the Romans at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Barbalissos" title="Battle of Barbalissos">Battle of Barbalissos</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Barbalissos" title="Barbalissos">Barbalissos</a> in the province of <a href="/wiki/Euphratensis" title="Euphratensis">Euphratensis</a> (modern day <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>). They advanced into Roman territory as far as <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, which was captured in 253 after a prolonged siege.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 253, the Goths invaded <a href="/wiki/Moesia_Inferior" class="mw-redirect" title="Moesia Inferior">Moesia Inferior</a>, as the new governor, <a href="/wiki/Aemilian" class="mw-redirect" title="Aemilian">Aemilian</a>, had refused to pay the tribute to them. The Goths split into two bands, with one raiding the cities of Moesia Inferior and <a href="/wiki/Thracia" title="Thracia">Thracia</a>, and the other crossing into <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a> as far as <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aemilian succeeded in repelling the Goths, slaughtering many and forcing the rest back across the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a>. The prestige of this victory was so great that Aemilian's soldiers spontaneously declared him emperor, in opposition to Trebonianus Gallus and Volusianus.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanFrey2012355-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When the news reached Rome, Gallus and Volusianus called for reinforcements from <a href="/wiki/Valerian_(emperor)" title="Valerian (emperor)">Valerian</a>, the future emperor, who had been strengthening the empire's defences on the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>. Aemilian marched to Italy at a rapid pace, arriving before Valerian could such send any assistance. The troops available to the co-emperors feared fighting the much stronger forces of Aemilian. To forestall the battle, they mutinied, killing Gallus and Volusianus at <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Interamna</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, in August 253.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdkinsAdkins201428-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton2014826_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton2014826-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETruhart2000347_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETruhart2000347-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Chronography_of_354" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronography of 354">Chronography of 354</a></i> says the co-emperors ruled for a total of two years, four months, and nine days.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBird1993138_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBird1993138-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Numismatics">Numismatics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Volusianus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Numismatics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Aureus_Volusianus_(obverse).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Aureus_Volusianus_%28obverse%29.jpg/220px-Aureus_Volusianus_%28obverse%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Aureus_Volusianus_%28obverse%29.jpg/330px-Aureus_Volusianus_%28obverse%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/Aureus_Volusianus_%28obverse%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="352" data-file-height="352" /></a><figcaption>Coin featuring Volusianus</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Aurei" class="mw-redirect" title="Aurei">aurei</a></i> of Volusianus fell into two types. There were five styles of coins which featured his <a href="/wiki/Bust_(sculpture)" title="Bust (sculpture)">bust</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Obverse" class="mw-redirect" title="Obverse">obverse</a>, with the <a href="/wiki/Reverse_(coin)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reverse (coin)">reverse</a> showing: <a href="/wiki/Aequitas" title="Aequitas">Aequitas</a> sitting, <a href="/wiki/Aeternitas" title="Aeternitas">Aeternitas</a> standing, <a href="/wiki/Apollo" title="Apollo">Apollo</a> standing, <a href="/wiki/Juno_(mythology)" title="Juno (mythology)">Juno</a> sitting inside a rounded temple, or <a href="/wiki/Victoria_(mythology)" title="Victoria (mythology)">Victoria</a> standing. There were a further six styles of coins which featured his bust with a <a href="/wiki/Radiate_crown" title="Radiate crown">Radiate</a> on the obverse, with the reverse displaying: <a href="/wiki/Concordia_(mythology)" title="Concordia (mythology)">Concordia</a> sitting, <a href="/wiki/Felicitas" title="Felicitas">Felicitas</a> standing, <a href="/wiki/Libertas" title="Libertas">Libertas</a> standing, <a href="/wiki/Providentia" title="Providentia">Providence</a> standing, <a href="/wiki/Salus" title="Salus">Salus</a> standing, or a helmeted <a href="/wiki/Virtus_(deity)" title="Virtus (deity)">Virtus</a> standing.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFriedbergFriedbergFriedberg201748_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFriedbergFriedbergFriedberg201748-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The coins of Volusianus occasionally bore the inscription <i>Saeculum nouum</i> (new age), alongside the traditional inscriptions <i>Romae aeternae</i> (eternal Rome) and <i>Pax aeternae</i> (eternal peace).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrent2010163_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrent2010163-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
