<p><b>Rita of Cascia</b>, born <b>Margherita Lotti</b> (1381 – 22 May 1457), was an <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> widow and <a href="/wiki/Augustinian_nuns" title="Augustinian nuns">Augustinian nun</a> venerated as a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Catholic_saints" title="List of Catholic saints">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>After Rita's husband died, she joined an <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> community of <a href="/wiki/Nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuns">religious sisters</a>, where she was known both for practicing <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a><sup id="cite_ref-mershman_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and for the efficacy of her prayers. Various miracles are attributed to her intercession, and she is often portrayed with a bleeding wound on her forehead, which is understood to indicate a partial <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Rita on 24 May 1900. Her feast day is celebrated on 22 May. At her canonization ceremony, she was bestowed the title of <i>Patroness of Impossible Causes</i>, while in many <a href="/wiki/Catholic_countries" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic countries">Catholic countries</a>, Rita came to be known as the <a href="/wiki/Patroness" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness">patroness</a> of abused wives and heartbroken women. Her <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt body</a> remains in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Santa_Rita_da_Cascia" title="Basilica of Santa Rita da Cascia">Basilica of Santa Rita da Cascia</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Rita-Roccap.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Rita-Roccap.jpg/220px-Rita-Roccap.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="172" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Rita-Roccap.jpg/330px-Rita-Roccap.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Rita-Roccap.jpg/440px-Rita-Roccap.jpg 2x" data-file-width="550" data-file-height="429" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Rita-Roccap.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sanctuary of Saint Rita at <a href="/wiki/Roccaporena" title="Roccaporena">Roccaporena</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg/220px-CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg/330px-CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg/440px-CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2736" data-file-height="3648" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:CasciaBasilicaSantaRita1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Basilica of Saint Rita at <a href="/wiki/Cascia" title="Cascia">Cascia</a></div></div></div>
<p>Margherita Lotti was born in 1381 in the city of <a href="/wiki/Roccaporena" title="Roccaporena">Roccaporena</a>, a small suburb of <a href="/wiki/Cascia" title="Cascia">Cascia</a> (near <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-digregorio_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-digregorio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where various sites connected with her are the focus of pilgrimages. Her name, <a href="/wiki/Margaret" title="Margaret">Margherita</a>, means "pearl". She was affectionately called Rita, the short form of her baptismal name. Her parents, Antonio and Amata Ferri Lotti, were known to be noble, charitable persons, who gained the epithet <i>Conciliatori di Cristo</i> (English: <i>Peacemakers of Christ</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
According to pious accounts, Rita was originally pursued by a <a href="/wiki/Notary" title="Notary">notary</a> named <i>Gubbio</i> but she resisted his offer. She was married at age twelve to a nobleman named Paolo Mancini. Her parents arranged her marriage, a common practice at the time, despite her repeated requests to be allowed to enter a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of religious sisters.  Her husband, Paolo Mancini, was known to be a rich, quick-tempered, immoral man, who had many enemies in the region of Cascia. The marriage lasted for eighteen years, during which she is remembered for her <a href="/wiki/Christian_values" title="Christian values">Christian values</a> as a model wife and mother who made efforts to convert her husband from his abusive behavior.
</p><p>Rita endured his insults, physical abuse and infidelities for many years. According to popular tales, through humility, kindness and patience, Rita was able to convert her husband into a better person, more specifically renouncing a family <a href="/wiki/Feud" title="Feud">feud</a> known at the time as <i>La Vendetta</i>. Rita eventually bore two sons, Giangiacomo (Giovanni) Antonio and Paulo Maria, and brought them up in the Christian faith. As time went by and the family feud between the Chiqui and Mancini families became more intense, Paolo Mancini became congenial, but his allies betrayed him and he was violently stabbed to death<sup id="cite_ref-digregorio_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-digregorio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> by Guido Chiqui, a member of the feuding family.
</p><p>Rita gave a public pardon at Paolo's funeral to her husband's murderers.<sup id="cite_ref-digregorio_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-digregorio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Paolo Mancini's brother, Bernardo, was said to have continued the feud and hoped to convince Rita's sons to seek revenge. Bernardo convinced Rita's sons to leave their <a href="/wiki/Manorialism" title="Manorialism">manor</a> and live at the Mancini <a href="/wiki/Villa" title="Villa">villa</a> ancestral home. As her sons grew, their characters began to change as Bernardo became their tutor. Rita's sons wished to avenge their father's murder. Rita, fearing that her sons would lose their souls, tried to dissuade them from retaliating, but to no avail. She asked God to remove her sons from the cycle of vendettas and prevent <a href="/wiki/Mortal_sin" title="Mortal sin">mortal sin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Murder" title="Murder">murder</a>. Her sons died of <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a> a year later, which pious Catholics believe was God's answer to her prayer, taking them by natural death rather than risk them committing a mortal sin punishable by <a href="/wiki/Christian_views_on_Hell" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian views on Hell">Hell</a>.
</p><p>After the deaths of her husband and sons, Rita desired to enter the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Saint Mary Magdalene</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cascia" title="Cascia">Cascia</a>, but was turned away. Although the convent acknowledged Rita's good character and piety, the nuns were afraid of being associated with her due to the scandal of her husband's violent death and because she was not a virgin.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, Rita persisted in her cause and was given a condition before the convent could accept her: the task of reconciling her family with her husband's murderers. She implored her three patron saints (<a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>, <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_of_Tolentino" title="Nicholas of Tolentino">Nicholas of Tolentino</a>) to assist her, and she set about the task of establishing peace between the hostile parties of Cascia.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Popular religious tales recall that the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>, which ravaged Italy at the time, infected Bernardo Mancini, causing him to relinquish his desire to feud any longer with the Chiqui family. She was able to resolve the conflicts between the families and, at the age of thirty-six, was allowed to enter the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pious Catholic legends later recount that Rita was transported into the monastery of Saint Magdalene via <a href="/wiki/Levitation" title="Levitation">levitation</a> at night into the garden courtyard by her three patron saints. She remained at the monastery, living by the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians#The_Augustinian_Rule" title="Augustinians">Augustinian Rule</a>, until her death from tuberculosis on 22 May 1457.<sup id="cite_ref-rotelle_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotelle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Rita-sep.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Rita-sep.jpg/220px-Rita-sep.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Rita-sep.jpg/330px-Rita-sep.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Rita-sep.jpg/440px-Rita-sep.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1063" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Rita-sep.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Rita's tomb with her <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt body</a> at the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Cascia" title="Cascia">Cascia</a></div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> Father Agostino Cavallucci from <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a> wrote the first biography of Rita based on oral tradition. The <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a> was published in 1610 by Matteo Florimi in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>. The work was composed long before her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>, but the title page nevertheless refers to Rita as already ‘blessed’.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Another <i>"Acta</i>" or life story of the woman was compiled by the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> priest Jacob Carelicci.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Rita was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> in 1626.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The pope's private secretary, <a href="/wiki/Fausto_Poli" title="Fausto Poli">Fausto Poli</a>, had been born some fifteen kilometers (nine miles) from her birthplace and much of the impetus behind her cult is due to his enthusiasm. Rita was also mentioned in a French volume on important <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinians</a> by Simplicien Saint-Martin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 24 May, 1900<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 22 May. On the 100th anniversary of her canonization in 2000, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> noted her remarkable qualities as a Christian woman: "Rita interpreted well the 'feminine genius' by living it intensely in both physical and spiritual motherhood."<sup id="cite_ref-cna_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rita has acquired the reputation, together with St. Jude, as a saint of impossible causes. She is also the patron saint of sterility, abuse victims, loneliness, marriage difficulties, parenthood, widows, the sick, bodily ills, and wounds.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rita's body, which has remained incorrupt over the centuries, is venerated today in the shrine at Cascia, which bears her name.<sup id="cite_ref-rotelle_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotelle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Many people visit her tomb each year from all over the world.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> French painter <a href="/wiki/Yves_Klein" title="Yves Klein">Yves Klein</a> had been dedicated to her as an infant. In 1961, he created a Shrine of St. Rita, which is in Cascia Convent.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg/220px-S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="319" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg/330px-S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg/440px-S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="561" data-file-height="813" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:S.Rita_da_Cascia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A popular religious depiction of Saint Rita during her partial <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">Stigmata</a>. The artist depicts her dressed in a black <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> habit, which is historically inaccurate as she would have worn the brown robes and white veil of the Monastery of Saint Mary Magdalene from the 13th century.</div></div></div>
<p>Various religious symbols are related to Rita. She is depicted holding a thorn (a symbol of her penance and stigmata), holding a large <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">Crucifix</a>, holding a Palm leaf with three crowns (representing her two sons and husband), flanked by two small children (her sons), holding a Gospel book, holding a skull (a symbol of mortality), and holding a <a href="/wiki/Flagellation" title="Flagellation">flagellum</a> whip (a symbol of her <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_forehead_wound">The forehead wound</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The forehead wound">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When Rita was approximately sixty years of age, she was meditating before an image of Christ crucified. Suddenly, a small wound appeared on her forehead, as though a thorn from the crown that encircled Christ's head had loosened itself and penetrated her own flesh. It was considered to be a <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">partial Stigmata</a>, and she bore this external sign of union with Christ until her death in 1457. At the time of her death, the sisters of the convent bathed and dressed her body for burial. They noticed that her forehead wound remained the same, with drops of blood still reflecting light. When her body was later exhumed, it was noted that her forehead wound still remained the same, with the glistening light reflected from the drops of blood. Her body showed no signs of deterioration. Over several years, her body was exhumed two more times. Each time, her body appeared the same. She was declared an incorruptible after the third exhumation. Relics were taken at that time as is the custom in the Catholic Church in preparation for sainthood.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Roses">Roses</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Roses">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>It is said that near the end of her life Rita was bedridden at the convent. While visiting her, a cousin asked if she desired anything from her old home. Rita responded by asking for a rose from the garden. It was January, and her cousin did not expect to find one due to the season. However, when her relative went to the house, a single blooming rose was found in the garden, and her cousin brought it back to Rita at the convent.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> St. Rita is often depicted holding roses or with roses nearby. On her feast day, churches and shrines of St. Rita provide roses to the congregation that are blessed by the priest during <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Bees">The Bees</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: The Bees">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Novena_to_Saint_Rita_of_Cascia_(Tagalog).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/9/98/Novena_to_Saint_Rita_of_Cascia_%28Tagalog%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="348" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="200" data-file-height="348" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Novena_to_Saint_Rita_of_Cascia_(Tagalog).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A <a href="/wiki/Tagalog_language" title="Tagalog language">Tagalog</a> <a href="/wiki/Novena" title="Novena">novena</a> to Saint Rita, published by Catholic Trade Manila in 1981</div></div></div>
<p>In the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Laarne" title="Laarne">Laarne</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, there is a statue of Rita in which several <a href="/wiki/Bee" title="Bee">bees</a> are featured. This depiction originates from the story of her <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> as an infant. On the day after her baptism, her family noticed a swarm of white bees flying around her as she slept in her crib. However, the bees peacefully entered and exited her mouth without causing her any harm or injury. Instead of being alarmed for her safety, her family was mystified by this sight. According to Butler, this was taken to indicate that the career of the child was to be marked by industry, virtue, and devotion.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rita_of_Cascia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 20th century, a large sanctuary was built for Rita in Cascia. The sanctuary and the house where she was born are among the most active pilgrimage sites of <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>.
</p><p>The National Shrine of Saint Rita of Cascia in <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia" title="Philadelphia">Philadelphia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pennsylvania" title="Pennsylvania">Pennsylvania</a>, was built in 1907 and is a popular pilgrimage and devotional site.
</p><p>French singer <a href="/wiki/Mireille_Mathieu" title="Mireille Mathieu">Mireille Mathieu</a> adopted Rita as her <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> on the advice of her <a href="/wiki/Paternal_grandmother" class="mw-redirect" title="Paternal grandmother">paternal grandmother</a>. In her autobiography, Mathieu describes buying a candle for Rita using her last <a href="/wiki/Franc" title="Franc">franc</a>. Though Mathieu claims that her prayers were not always answered, she testifies that they inspired her to become a strong and determined woman.<sup id="cite_ref-mimi_autobiography_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mimi_autobiography-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1943, <i><a href="/wiki/Rita_of_Cascia_(film)" title="Rita of Cascia (film)">Rita of Cascia</a></i>, a film based on Rita's life, was made, starring <a href="/wiki/Elena_Zareschi" title="Elena Zareschi">Elena Zareschi</a>. The story of Rita increased in popularity due to a 2004 film titled <i>Santa Rita da Cascia</i>, filmed in <a href="/wiki/Florence,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Florence, Italy">Florence, Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The latter film altered the facts of Rita's early life.
</p><p>Rita is often credited as also being the unofficial patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Baseball" title="Baseball">baseball</a> due to a reference made to her in the 2002 film <i><a href="/wiki/The_Rookie_(2002_film)" title="The Rookie (2002 film)">The Rookie</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The 2019 science fiction novella <i><a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Vast_Black" title="Sisters of the Vast Black">Sisters of the Vast Black</a></i> features a fictional group of nuns known as the Order of Saint Rita.
</p><p>St. Rita's Church is located in Nanthirickal, Kollam district, in the state of Kerala, India. It is the only church in Asia to have relics of Saint Rita. It is the only Catholic church in Kerala named after St. Rita.
</p>
