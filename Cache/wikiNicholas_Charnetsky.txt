font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Nicholas Charnetsky</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Charnetsky.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Blessed Charnetsky.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b4/Blessed_Charnetsky.jpg/250px-Blessed_Charnetsky.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/b/b4/Blessed_Charnetsky.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="273" data-file-height="364" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">Bishop Nicholas Charnetsky</div></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Bishop and Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">December 14, 1884<br />Semakivtsi, <a href="/wiki/Kolomyia_Raion" title="Kolomyia Raion">Kolomyia Raion</a>, Ukraine</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">April 2, 1959<br />Ukraine</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data">Catholic Church</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">27 June 2001, Ukraine by Pope John Paul II</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">2 April</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Nicholas Charnetsky</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-Yorkton_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorkton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <b>Mykolai Charnetskyi</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Mykolay Charnetsky</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Миколай Чарнецький</span>; December 14, 1884 – April 2, 1959) was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Redemptorists</a> (Congregation of the Holy Redeemer), a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>; he is considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_Background">Family Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mykolai Charnetskyi was born in the village of Semakivtsia, a hamlet in <a href="/wiki/Kolomyia_Raion" title="Kolomyia Raion">Kolomyia Raion</a> in <a href="/wiki/Western_Ukraine" title="Western Ukraine">western Ukraine</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Yorkton_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorkton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> on 14 December 1884. He came from a large family and was the eldest of nine children. Alexander and Parasceva Charnetsky and their children were devout members of the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church">Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church</a> which is in communion with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a> and is distinct from the <a href="/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Ukraine" title="History of Christianity in Ukraine">Ukrainian Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ukrainian_seminary_and_priesthood">Ukrainian seminary and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ukrainian seminary and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From a young age, Charnetsky had expressed a desire to become a priest and when he was 18 years of age, the Ukrainian Catholic Bishop <a href="/wiki/Hryhory_Khomyshyn" class="mw-redirect" title="Hryhory Khomyshyn">Hryhory Khomyshyn</a> (who was himself to be martyred) sent him to study at the Ukrainian College in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. After his ordination to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priesthood">Catholic priesthood</a> in 1909, Charnetsky returned from Ukraine to Rome so that he might complete a Doctorate in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">Theology</a>, which he did the following year.<sup id="cite_ref-Yorkton_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorkton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon completion of his doctorate, Charnetsky returned to his homeland in order to teach <a href="/wiki/Dogmatic_theology" title="Dogmatic theology">dogmatic theology</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> at the Ukrainian Catholic <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Ivano-Frankivsk</a> (then called Stanislaviv) where he remained for the next nine years, also serving as <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> to any student who wished.<sup id="cite_ref-ca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry_as_a_Redemptorist">Ministry as a Redemptorist</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ministry as a Redemptorist">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>For some time, Charnetsky had desired to live a more austere life than that of a seminary professor. In 1913 the Belgian province of the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Redemptorists</a> had established a mission in Ukraine and this included a <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> near <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a> for those interested in joining the congregation. Like <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Ziatyk" title="Ivan Ziatyk">Ivan Ziatyk</a> who was to follow him some years later, Charnetsky entered the novitiate in 1919.<sup id="cite_ref-London_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-London-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As he was already an <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> priest, after his first profession in 1920, Charnetsky immediately began working in a nearby parish before being sent to teach at the minor seminary (for students in their teens) run by the Redemptorists.<sup id="cite_ref-Yorkton_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorkton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, in 1926 the congregation opened a mission in the <a href="/wiki/Volhynia" title="Volhynia">Volhynia</a> region of northern Ukraine (then part of Poland), the main purpose of which was to promote a better relationship between <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> Ukrainians. As Charnetsky had been ordained in the Ukrainian Catholic Rite, he was well acquainted with the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Spirituality" title="Spirituality">spirituality</a> as lived by those of the Orthodox churches and this gained him much respect amongst their people and <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">clergy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His devotion to the people together with his tireless efforts at fostering Orthodox-Catholic relations caused <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> to name him as <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular bishop</a> of Lebed and <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Visitor" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Visitor">Apostolic Visitor</a> to Ukrainian Catholics in the Volhynia region  as well as those in <a href="/wiki/Podlaskie" class="mw-redirect" title="Podlaskie">Podlaskie</a> (Ukr: Pidlashia) in southern <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>. He was ordained to the episcopacy by Bishop Gregory Khomyshyn in Rome on February 2, 1931.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> From 1931 to 1939, he ministered to the people of Volyn, Polisia, Pidliasia, and Belorussia.<sup id="cite_ref-London_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-London-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Charnetsky was invited by Irish <a href="/wiki/Redemptorist" class="mw-redirect" title="Redemptorist">Redemptorists</a> to the 1932 Eucharistic Congress. He lodged for a fortnight in the guest-house of the Redemptoristine monastery on Saint Alphonsus Road and offered the Divine Liturgy in the monastery church each morning. On the second day of the Eucharistic Congress, Charnetsky celebrated a Pontifical Divine Liturgy for all the Congress participants in the Jesuit Church on Gardiner Street; an icon-screen with hand-painted icons was especially made and installed for this purpose; it is not known what became of the screen. A choir directed by Paul Mailleux (later Rector of the Pontifical Russian College in Rome) sang the Pontifical Liturgy in Church-Slavonic. <a href="/wiki/Fulton_J._Sheen" title="Fulton J. Sheen">Fulton J. Sheen</a> of the United States was among the participating clergy.
</p><p>After the Eucharistic Congress, Charnetsky remained in Ireland for several weeks, visiting various Redemptorist churches and schools. He then went back to <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>. Unfortunately, Charnetsky never had an opportunity to return to Ireland; <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> persecution of the church severely restricted his activity.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Soviet_invasion_and_imprisonment">Soviet invasion and imprisonment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Soviet invasion and imprisonment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1939 Soviet armed forces invaded western Ukraine, causing the Redemptorists to flee to Lviv. Two years later, Charnetsky took up a professorship at Lviv Theological Academy<sup id="cite_ref-London_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-London-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (now the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Catholic_University" title="Ukrainian Catholic University">Ukrainian Catholic University</a>) which was revived in 1941, after Nazi Germany occupied the city.
</p><p>In 1944 the Soviets invaded for a second time and the following year all the Ukrainian Greek Catholic bishops were placed under arrest as part of the Soviet plan to suppress the church and transfer its property to the state-sanctioned Russian Orthodox Church.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> During his time in prison Charnetsky endured frequent violent interrogations. Charnetsky was arrested on April 11, 1945.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He was charged with collaborating with and being an agent of a foreign power i.e. the Vatican;<sup id="cite_ref-London_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-London-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as a result he was sentenced to <a href="/wiki/Hard_labour" class="mw-redirect" title="Hard labour">hard labour</a>.
</p><p>Initially, one of his prison compatriots was the noted Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Josyf_Slipyj" title="Josyf Slipyj">Josyf Slipyj</a> when both were imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Mariinsk" title="Mariinsk">Mariinsk</a>, southern <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberia</a>. Between his arrest in 1945 and his release eleven years later, Charnetsky was moved around to about thirty a prisons. It was reported that, during all this time, he maintained a dignified, gentle and calming presence in spite of enduring over 600 hours of interrogation, which included torture.<sup id="cite_ref-ca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Release_and_death">Release and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Release and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By 1956, Charnetsky's health was in such a dismal state that a shroud had already been prepared; the prison authorities then decided to release him in order that he die elsewhere.
<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> However, he made enough of a surprising partial recovery that he was able to pastor the Ukrainian Catholic community, which was then operating clandestinely. Although he lived under constant surveillance, one of his most important acts was to secretly prepare and ordain young men called to the priesthood.
</p><p>On 2 April 1959 Charnetsky died and was buried in Lviv two days later. Due to many regarding him as a saint, people began visit his grave and to ask for his heavenly intercession immediately. Today people continue to claim that miracles happen through his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-ca_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
On 23 April 2001 Charnetsky's martyrdom was verified by the assembly of Cardinals.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> during his pastoral visit to Ukraine on 27 June 2001. This date was significant as it is the feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Perpetual_Help" title="Our Lady of Perpetual Help">Our Lady of Perpetual Help</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patroness" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness">patroness</a> of the Redemptorists.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Testimony_of_Vasyl_Voronovskyi">Testimony of Vasyl Voronovskyi</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Charnetsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Testimony of Vasyl Voronovskyi">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>“I saw him. He was a very humble person. The first time I came for instruction from the bishop, he was sweeping the house. I wanted to help him, to take the broom, but he didn’t let me. He himself swept. ‘Have a seat,’ he said. I was embarrassed that the bishop was sweeping, but I was sitting, because he wouldn’t let me. He told how many priests who had signed over to Orthodoxy, came to him to confess nearly 300 priests, they repented and came to him.”</i> – From an interview with Father Vasyl Voronovskyi.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
