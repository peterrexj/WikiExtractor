					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Mexican Catholic lawyer, martyr, and Blessed (1888–1927)</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">In this <a href="/wiki/Spanish_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish name">Spanish name</a>, the first or paternal&#32;<a href="/wiki/Surname" title="Surname">surname</a> is <i> González</i>&#32;and the second or maternal family name is <i> Flores</i>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Anacleto González Flores</b> (July 13, 1888 – April 1, 1927) was a Mexican <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">layman</a> and lawyer who was tortured and executed during the persecution of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> under Mexican President <a href="/wiki/Plutarco_El%C3%ADas_Calles" title="Plutarco Elías Calles">Plutarco Elías Calles</a>.
</p><p>González was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XVI">Benedict XVI</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> on 20 2005.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anacleto_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Flores&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When González was killed, Mexico was under the rule of President <a href="/wiki/Plutarco_El%C3%ADas_Calles" title="Plutarco Elías Calles">Plutarco Elías Calles</a>, who was fiercely <a href="/wiki/Anticlerical" class="mw-redirect" title="Anticlerical">anticlerical</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anti-Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Catholic">anti-Catholic</a>. Mexico was undergoing what the British author <a href="/wiki/Graham_Greene" title="Graham Greene">Graham Greene</a> called the "fiercest persecution of religion anywhere since the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anacleto_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Flores&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The second of twelve children born to the poor family of Valentín González Sánchez and María Flores Navarro, Anacleto González Flores was baptized the day after his birth. A <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic priest">Roman Catholic priest</a> who was a friend of the family recognized Gonzáles's intelligence and recommended him for the <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a>. There, Gonzáles excelled and earned the nickname "Maestro." After deciding that he did not have the calling to <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a>, González began the study of law at <a href="/wiki/Escuela_Libre_de_Derecho" title="Escuela Libre de Derecho">Escuela Libre de Derecho</a> in Guadalajara and became an attorney in 1922.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200340_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200340-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He married María Concepción Guerrero, and they had two children.
</p><p>González attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mass (Catholic)">Mass</a> daily and engaged in numerous works of charity, including visiting prisoners and teaching the <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200341_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200341-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anacleto_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Flores&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>González became an activist, led the Catholic Association of Mexican Youth (ACJM), and founded the magazine <i>La Palabra</i>, which attacked the anticlerical and anti-Catholic articles of the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitution of 1917">Constitution of 1917</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200341_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200341-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was the founder and president of the Popular Union (UP), which organized Catholics to resist the persecution of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200342_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200342-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Originally, González supported <a href="/wiki/Passive_resistance" class="mw-redirect" title="Passive resistance">passive resistance</a> against the government since he had studied the methods of <a href="/wiki/Gandhi" class="mw-redirect" title="Gandhi">Gandhi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200341_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200341-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  However, in 1926, he learned of the murder of four members of the Catholic Association of Mexican Youth, joined the <a href="/wiki/National_League_for_the_Defense_of_Religious_Freedom" class="mw-redirect" title="National League for the Defense of Religious Freedom">National League for the Defense of Religious Freedom</a>, and supported the coming rebellion.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican.va_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican.va-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He wrote, "the country is a jail for the Catholic Church.... We are not worried about defending our material interests because these come and go; but our spiritual interests, these we will defend because they are necessary to obtain our salvation."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200342_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200342-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January 1927, after they had endured <a href="/wiki/Religious_persecution" title="Religious persecution">religious persecution</a> and <a href="/wiki/State_atheism" title="State atheism">state atheism</a>, Mexican Catholics took up arms and set off the <a href="/wiki/Cristero_War" title="Cristero War">Cristero War</a>. González did not take up arms but gave speeches that encouraged Catholics to support the Cristeros with money, food, accommodation, and clothing. He wrote pamphlets and gave speeches that supported his opposition to the anticlerical government.
</p><p>Seeking to crush the rebellion, the government sought to capture the leaders of the Popular Union and the National League for the Defence of Religious Freedom.  González was captured and framed with charges that he murdered an American, Edgar Wilkens, but the government knew that Wilkens had been killed by a robber, Guadalupe Zuno.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200342_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200342-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anacleto_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Flores&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>González was tortured, including being hung by his thumbs pulling them out of their sockets, having his shoulder fractured with a rifle butt, and having the bottom of his feet slashed.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican.va_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican.va-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> On April 1, 1927, he was executed by firing squad.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican.va_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican.va-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Echoing the words of the assassinated Ecuadorian President <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_Garc%C3%ADa_Moreno" title="Gabriel García Moreno">Gabriel García Moreno</a>, who defied the forces seeking to suppress his faith, González's last words were "Hear Americas for the second time: I die but God does not! <i>Viva Cristo Rey</i>!"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wilkens's widow, who knew that González had been framed, wrote a letter of protest to <a href="/wiki/Washington,_DC" class="mw-redirect" title="Washington, DC">Washington, DC</a>, which exonerated him. A letter staying his execution arrived shortly after he had been shot.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECruz200342_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECruz200342-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anacleto_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Flores&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

</p>
