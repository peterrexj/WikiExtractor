<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png/220px-Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png/330px-Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png/440px-Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png 2x" data-file-width="2466" data-file-height="1844" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Egypt_with_the_dwelling_places_of_Anoub_highlighted.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Anoub lived in Terenuthis and then moved to Scetis</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ab_(Semitic)" title="Ab (Semitic)"><b>Abba</b></a> <b>Anoub</b>, also known as <b>Anoub of Scetis</b> or <b>Anoubius</b>, was an Egyptian <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> Christian Saint, <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a> who lived during the 4th and 5th centuries in <a href="/wiki/Scetis" class="mw-redirect" title="Scetis">Scetis</a> (<i>sometimes spelled Sketis</i>), <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a> (<i>modern day Wadi El Natrun</i>). Abba Anoub is mentioned in the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>, also called the <i>Apophthegmata</i> or the <i>Gerontikon</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">Saint Nikolaj Velimirović</a> says, "Anoubius was one of the great Egyptian monks."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Abba Anoub was one of the <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Fathers</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Troparion" title="Troparion">Verse</a>: <i>"Anoub performed signs and died gracefully, as one alive he hitherto shows forth to be living."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anoub&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anoub was one of the seven blood-brothers of <a href="/wiki/Poemen" title="Poemen">Poemen</a>, along with Paësius. Poemen was their elder, although Anoub was the eldest by age.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:3_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is said that prior to his tonsure as a monk, Anoub was persecuted as a Christian. "He suffered much for the true Faith."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
After the first attack of <a href="/wiki/Scetis" class="mw-redirect" title="Scetis">Scetis</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Mazices" title="Mazices">Mazices</a> barbarians from 407 to 408 AD which led to the diaspora of the monks,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the seven brothers moved to the ancient city of <a href="/wiki/Terenuthis" class="mw-redirect" title="Terenuthis">Terenuthis</a> (<i>modern day Al-Tarrānah in the Western Delta</i>), <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a>, at which point Abba Anoub assumed leadership of the new monastic settlement.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> They created a <a href="/wiki/Cenobitic" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenobitic">cenobitic</a> community in Terenuthis, where they temporarily occupied a former pagan temple.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to Sister <a href="/wiki/Benedicta_Ward" title="Benedicta Ward">Benedicta Ward</a>:</p><blockquote><p>The Devastation of Scetis marks a turning point in the history of early monasticism in Egypt; the monks dispersed, and gradually the centre shifted from Egypt to Palestine. This story of Anoub and his brothers indicates a new reason for the formation of cenobitic communities, that is, protection against invaders.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:3_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>It was said that Abba Anoub and his brothers kept a strict <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascesis</a> in which they devoted all their time to either prayer or manual labor, leaving only four hours a night to sleep.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Abba Anoub reposed peacefully in the second half of the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sayings">Sayings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anoub&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sayings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Abba Anoub said, <i>"Since the day when the name of Christ was invoked upon me</i> [at <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>]<i>, no lie has come out of my mouth."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Once Abba Anoub desired to remain in silence among his brothers, saying, <i>"For love's sake do this: let each of us live in quietness, each one by himself, without meeting one another the whole week."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>"For if a man attains to the measure of this saying, such that he acquires purity and sees the sins of his brother, he succeeds, by the power of his virtue, in swallowing overlooking them. He who reproaches himself justifies his neighbor, and this righteousness conceals his neighbor’s sins."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Abba Poemen said of Abba Anoub after his death, "<i>We lived together in complete unity and unbroken peace till death broke up our association."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Abba Anoub said to another monk, <i>"If a man really affirms this saying, when he sees his brother's faults he sees that his integrity exceeds his faults."</i> The brother said, <i>"What is integrity?"</i> The old man replied, <i>"Always to accuse himself."</i> <sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Stories">Stories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anoub&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Stories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The following story was related about Abba Anoub.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When the seven brothers had fled from the invasion of Scetis, they stayed temporarily in an abandoned pagan temple.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Every morning, Abba Anoub would throw stones at a statue in the temple, and every evening he would say to the statue, "Forgive me!"<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When Abba Poemen asked him why he acted in such a way, he said, "I did this for your sake. When you saw me throwing stones at the face of the statue, did it speak, or did it become angry?" Abba Poemen said, "No." Thus Abba Anoub continued, "Or again, when I bent down in penitence, was it moved, and did it say, 'I will not forgive you?'" Again Abba Poemen answered "No." Then the old man resumed, "Now we are seven brethren; if you wish us to live together, let us be like this statue, which is not moved whether one beats it or whether one flatters it. If you do not wish to become like this, there are four doors here in the temple, let each one go where he will." The seven brothers decided to remain together and to follow Abba Anoub's words.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another time, Abba Anoub's brother, Abba Paësius, said to him, "You know how austerely Abba Poemen talks; come, let us establish a monastery somewhere else, and let the two of us remain there, without cares."<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Abba Paësius showed him a small chest of gold with which they could start a monastery. Anoub knew that his brother's greed could lead him to destruction, but rather than admonish him harshly, Anoub pretended to agree with him and go with him, offering to hold the chest of gold during their journey. Yet when they crossed the river, Anoub feigned that he had lost his balance and dropped the gold in the river, losing all of it.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Then Paësius said to him: "Do not be distressed, Abba; for, since the coins have been lost, we can return to our brother."<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They returned and lived in peace. Thus, Abba Anoub worked to hide the sins of others and save them.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before Abba Anoub's death, three aged hermits visited him. He, being a clairvoyant,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> revealed to them the secrets of their hearts.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
