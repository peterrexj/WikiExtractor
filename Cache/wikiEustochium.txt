<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Eustochium</b> (c. 368 – September 28, 419 or 420), born <i>Eustochium Julia</i> at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, was a high ranking member of the community, specifically the Julian clan.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Eustochium was a <a href="/wiki/4th_century" title="4th century">fourth-century</a> noblewoman and an early <a href="/wiki/Desert_Mothers" title="Desert Mothers">Desert Mother</a>, venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>. Guided by the teachings of <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eustochium practiced asceticism and committed her life to perpetual celibacy.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eustochium&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eustochium was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Paula_of_Rome" title="Paula of Rome">Paula of Rome</a> and the third of four daughters of the Roman Senator Toxotius, for whom <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Saint Jerome</a> made a number of disputable claims of ancestry.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Jerome was a church father in early Christian history who is well known for translating the Holy Bible into Latin and encouraging the practice of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After the death of her husband around 380 Paula and her daughter Eustochium lived in Rome as austere a life as the fathers of the desert. Eustochium had three sisters,  <a href="/wiki/Blaesilla" title="Blaesilla">Blaesilla</a>, Paulina, and Rufina, and a brother, Toxotius.<sup id="cite_ref-icon_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-icon-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg/200px-Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="287" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg/300px-Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg/400px-Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n_043.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2850" data-file-height="4096" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Jerome, Saint Paula, and Saint Eustochium</i>, at National Gallery of Art in Washington.</figcaption></figure>
<p>When Jerome came to Rome from <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> in 382, they put themselves under his spiritual and educational guidance. Hymettius, an uncle of Eustochium, and his wife Praetextata tried to persuade the youthful Eustochium to give up her austere life and enjoy the pleasures of the world, but all their attempts were futile. About the year 384 she made a <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">vow of perpetual virginity</a>, on which occasion Jerome addressed to her his celebrated letter <i>De custodia virginitatis</i> (Ep. xxii in <i>P.L.,</i> XXII, 394–425). A year later Jerome returned to Palestine and soon after was followed by Paula and Eustochium.<sup id="cite_ref-icon_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-icon-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eustochium and Paula knew Jerome ever since his arrival in Rome in 382.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 386 they accompanied Jerome on his journey to <a href="/wiki/Aegyptus_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Aegyptus (Roman province)">Egypt</a>, where they visited the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Nitrian_Desert" title="Nitrian Desert">Nitrian Desert</a> in order to study and afterward imitate their mode of life. In the fall of the same year they returned to Palestine and settled permanently at <a href="/wiki/Bethlehem" title="Bethlehem">Bethlehem</a>. Paula and Eustochium at once began to erect four monasteries and a <a href="/wiki/Hospice" title="Hospice">hospice</a> near the spot where <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> was born. While the erection of the monasteries was in process (386–9) they lived in a small building in the neighborhood. One of the monasteries was occupied by monks and put under the direction of Jerome. The three other monasteries were taken by Paula and Eustochium and the numerous virgins that flocked around them. The monastic community encompassed separate quarters for men and women.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The three <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convents</a>, which were under the supervision of Paula, had only one <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a>, where all the virgins met several times daily for prayer and the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_hours" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgy of the hours">liturgy of the hours</a>. Jerome testifies (Ep. 308) that Eustochium and Paula performed the most menial services. Much of their time they spent in the study of <a href="/wiki/Scripture" class="mw-redirect" title="Scripture">scripture</a> under the direction of Jerome.
</p><p>Eustochium spoke <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Classical Greek</a> with equal ease and was able to read the scriptures in the <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a> text. Many of Jerome's Biblical commentaries owe their existence to her influence and to her he dedicated his commentaries on the prophets <a href="/wiki/Isaias" class="mw-redirect" title="Isaias">Isaias</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ezekiel" title="Ezekiel">Ezekiel</a>.
</p><p>The letters which Jerome wrote for her instruction and spiritual advancement are, according to his own testimony,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> very numerous. Jerome wrote Epitaph on Saint Paula, or Letter 108, honoring a friend and comrade in the form of a letter to Eustochium.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Within Letter 108, Jerome briefly states that he's writing the letter to comfort Eustochium after her mother's death.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Jerome's letter to Eustochium could be considered slander towards women. One modern scholar pointed to Jerome's opinions on ideal sexuality and body restrictions for women.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> However, his letters to Eustochium did not direct slander towards her, but rather praised her for her perpetual virginity.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Another scholar<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> considers his letter to be the finest expression of his ascetic doctrine.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> After the death of Paula in 404, Eustochium assumed the direction of the nunneries.<sup id="cite_ref-icon_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-icon-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her task was a difficult one on account of the impoverished condition of the temporal affairs which was brought about by the lavish almsgiving of Paula. Jerome provided his encouragement and advice.
</p><p>In 417, a crowd of ruffians attacked and pillaged the monasteries of Bethlehem, destroyed one of them by fire, besides killing and maltreating some of the residents. It is alleged<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> that this was instigated by <a href="/wiki/Bishop_John_II_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop John II of Jerusalem">John II</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Jerusalem">Patriarch of Jerusalem</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagians</a> against whom Jerome had written what were considered sharp polemics. Both Jerome and Eustochium informed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a> by letter of the occurrence, who severely reproved the patriarch for having permitted the outrage. Eustochium died shortly after and was succeeded in the supervision of the convents by her niece, the younger Paula. <a href="/wiki/Eustochius" title="Eustochius">Eustochius</a> of Tour might have been her nephew, and further lateral descendants may include <a href="/wiki/Saint_Perpetuus" title="Saint Perpetuus">Perpetuus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Volusianus_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Volusianus of Tours">Volusianus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic Church celebrates her feast on 28 September.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eustochium&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 2022, Saint Eustochium was officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day on 28 September, along with her mother, <a href="/wiki/Paula_of_Rome" title="Paula of Rome">Saint Paula of Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
