<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>

<p><b>Senán mac Geircinn</b> (<i>fl.</i> 6th century) is a prominent <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> saint in Irish tradition, founder of <a href="/wiki/Inis_Cathaigh" title="Inis Cathaigh">Inis Cathaigh</a> (Scattery Island, Iniscathy) and patron of the <a href="/wiki/Corcu_Baiscind" title="Corcu Baiscind">Corco Baiscinn</a> and the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Fidgenti" title="Uí Fidgenti">Uí Fhidgeinte</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is listed among the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Flood_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flood-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sen%C3%A1n_mac_Geirrcinn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in 488 in a place once known as Moylougha, about four miles east of present-day <a href="/wiki/Kilrush" title="Kilrush">Kilrush</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Clare" title="County Clare">County Clare</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. According to the prose life, his mother entered labour while walking through the woods; when she grasped a tree branch for support, it is said to have blossomed to foretell the virtues of the saint. The translation of "Senan" from old Gaelic means "little old wise man." It is thought that Senan may have gotten his name from an earlier river god whose name gave rise to the river Shannon.<sup id="cite_ref-hedderman_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hedderman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was a stepbrother of <a href="/wiki/Conaire_(saint)" title="Conaire (saint)">St. Cainnear</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and St <a href="/wiki/Conainne" title="Conainne">Conainne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While still only a child, Senan began to practice and preach self-denial, once even reproving his mother for gathering blackberries. God, he reminded her, made time for abstinence as well as for eating. The boy promised his life to God after the miracle at the estuary, where a path opened for him and the cattle he was driving at high tide.<sup id="cite_ref-hedderman_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hedderman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He studied under a monk, named Cassidus from whom he received the habit and tonsure of a monk. From him he learned sacred scriptures and the practices of the religious life. Cassidus sent him on to St Natalis at <a href="/wiki/Kilnamanagh" title="Kilnamanagh">Kilnamanagh</a>, whereupon completion of his studies he was ordained a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-hedderman_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hedderman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He commenced his missionary career by founding a church near Enniscorthy, in 510 (or 512), and the parish is still known as Templeshannon (Teampul Senain).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg/220px-Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg/330px-Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg/440px-Scattery_Island_from_the_air_-_geograph.org.uk_-_594050.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="427" /></a><figcaption>Scattery Island from the air</figcaption></figure>
<p>Senan was well read and an exceptional traveller in such times, travelling to Britain (see the village of Senan in Cornwall), France (Plouzane in Brittany being translated as the town or church of Senan) and Rome. He returned to Ireland about 520. Studying in many monasteries in Ireland, including  Glendalough and Kerry, he founded the abbey on <a href="/wiki/Inishmore_(or_Deer)_Island" class="mw-redirect" title="Inishmore (or Deer) Island">Inishmore (or Deer) Island</a>, leaving St Liberius to preside over it. About 534, he founded a monastery of five churches and a round tower on Inis Cathaigh or Scattery island in the bay on the <a href="/wiki/Estuary" title="Estuary">estuary</a> of the river Shannon just 3&#160;km out from Kilrush. The rule of the monastery was extremely austere. <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Ciarán</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brendan_of_Birr" title="Brendan of Birr">Brendan</a>, and other holy men, who had heard of his sanctity and miracles, visited him for spiritual guidance. Scattery Island became not only a famous abbey but the seat of a bishopric with Senan as its first bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Only men were allowed on Scattery Island while Senan was there, and legend has it that when his sister, St Conainne died, she willed it that she would be buried near Senan. To stay true to his own edicts, Senan waited until low tide to bury her in the inter-tidal zone, which was not officially the "island", thus fulfilling his sister's wish, while not breaking his own rules.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This legend seems to have been wrongly associated with Conainne as it is identical to that of the more famous <a href="/wiki/Conaire_(saint)" title="Conaire (saint)">St. Cainnear</a> or Cannera of Bantry who was mentioned in the latin life of St. Senan.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It is very unlikely, though not impossible, that Conainne and Cainnear were actually the same person. A similar story has been associated with another obscure female saint named Ibie or <a href="/w/index.php?title=Imy_of_Killimer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Imy of Killimer (page does not exist)">Imy of Killimer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Perhaps these three female saints were all refused access to Scattery Island at some point by St. Senan but it is more probable that Cainnear's story has been misappropriated.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Senan is said to have died on March 8, 544. He is buried on Scattery Island, probably in Temple Senan.<sup id="cite_ref-feast_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feast-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Chief_Ollam_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Chief Ollam of Ireland">Chief Ollam of Ireland</a> <a href="/wiki/Dall%C3%A1n_Forgaill" title="Dallán Forgaill">Dallán Forgaill</a> was his friend and wrote a eulogy about Senan entitled "Amra Senáin"<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Senan_and_the_.22Cathach.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Senan_and_the_&quot;Cathach&quot;">Senan and the "Cathach"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sen%C3%A1n_mac_Geirrcinn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Senan and the &quot;Cathach&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Legend has it that he slew a huge sea creature that inhabited the island and terrorised the locals.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
From his childhood he surely would have heard about the ‘Cathach’, the beast that roamed and occupied the island, so fearful and so dangerous that neither man nor beast dared to come near the place. Since it had been revealed to the man of God that this was the place where he should work and pray, he went there, trusting in the power and protection of the Almighty.  On his arrival in the island an archangel led Senan to the highest hill from which he was able to locate the Cathach. Facing the ferocious animal, the saint made the sign of the cross and commanded him to depart.
</p><p>A local chief hired a druid to put a spell on Senan, but when the druid landed on a nearby island, a tidal wave enveloped him and swept him to his death. That smaller island is still pointed out as Carraig an Draoi or The Druid’s Rock, which can still be seen at low tide.<sup id="cite_ref-feast_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feast-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is sometimes confused with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Senan_of_Laraghbrine" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Senan of Laraghbrine">Saint Senan of Laraghbrine</a>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sen%C3%A1n_mac_Geirrcinn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Parishes are named for him in Enniscorthy,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Kilrush,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Shanagolden and in Shannon. St Senan’s Church is located in Inniscarra.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Senan's Primary School is located on Vinegar Hill, Enniscorthy.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> St. Senan's National School is located in Shannon.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His patron day on March 8 was an important day of pilgrimage to Inis Cathaig.<sup id="cite_ref-feast_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feast-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The surname 'Gilsenan' in present-day is thought to have been the name for the followers of Saint Senan.
</p>
