<p><b>Abo of Tiflis</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">أبو التفليسي</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">Abu al-Tiflisi</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Georgian_language" title="Georgian language">Georgian</a>&#58; <span title="Georgian-language text"><span lang="ka" style="font-style: normal;">აბო თბილელი</span></span>, <span style="font-size: smaller;"><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian" title="Romanization of Georgian">romanized</a>:</span> <span lang="ka-Latn" style="font-style: italic;">abo tbileli</span>; c. 756 – 6 January 786) was an early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of <a href="/wiki/Arab" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab">Arab</a> origin, who went on to practice his faith in what is now <a href="/wiki/Tbilisi" title="Tbilisi">Tbilisi</a>, the capital of present-day <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abo_of_Tiflis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Arab" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab">Arab</a> by origin, Abo initially grew up as a <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> in <a href="/wiki/Baghdad" title="Baghdad">Baghdad</a>. At the age of seventeen or eighteen, he found himself in <a href="/wiki/Tbilisi" title="Tbilisi">Tbilisi</a>, having followed <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgian</a> Prince <a href="/wiki/Nerses_of_Iberia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nerses of Iberia">Nerses</a>, the ruler of <a href="/wiki/Kartli" title="Kartli">Kartli</a>. Nerses, having been slandered before the <a href="/wiki/Caliph" class="mw-redirect" title="Caliph">Caliph</a>, spent three years in confinement; freed by a new Caliph, he took Abo with him.
</p><p>Abo's profession in Baghdad was that of a <a href="/wiki/Perfumer" title="Perfumer">perfumer</a>, in which he excelled as a maker of fine perfumes and ointments, the art evidently implying knowledge of chemistry. On his arrival to Eastern Georgia (Kartli) he became convinced of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, which didn't happen immediately, but only after a committed soul-searching that involved heated quarrels even with Christian priests and bishops over the finer religious matters; those quarrels only consolidated him in his conviction that the truth was in Christianity. However, initially Abo was afraid to convert openly as eastern <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a> was under <a href="/wiki/Emirate_of_Tbilisi" title="Emirate of Tbilisi">Arab rule</a>; he only abandoned the Muslim habit of <a href="/wiki/Salah" title="Salah">five-times prayers per-day</a> and started praying in a Christian manner. For political reasons, his prince had to seek shelter in <a href="/wiki/Khazaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Khazaria">Khazaria</a> north of the Caspian Sea, an area which was not ruled by Muslims; Abo accompanied him, and was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> there. From Khazaria, Nerses moved to <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Abkhazia" title="Kingdom of Abkhazia">Abkhazia</a>, that was also free from the Arab dominion, taking Abo with him. There in Abkhazia Abo zealously followed the Christian life of prayers and ascetic struggles, preparing himself for a future mission.  Prince Nerses and his party returned to Tbilisi in 782, and Abo, notwithstanding the warning that it was not safe for him to go to Tbilisi, followed him. For about three years, Abo openly confessed his Christian faith on the streets of Tbilisi - both fortifying by his example the Christians who attempted to escape Arab rule and trying to convert his Arab compatriots to Christianity. A series of threats and warnings failed to dampen his zeal. In 786, he was denounced as a Christian to the Arab officials in Tbilisi, and arrested. The judge attempted to persuade Abo to return to the faith of his ancestors.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He confessed his faith at trial, was imprisoned, and executed on 6 January 786.
</p><p>Ioane Sabanisdze, Georgian religious writer and Abo's contemporary, compiled the martyr's life in his <a href="/wiki/Hagiographic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographic">hagiographic</a> novel <i>The Martyrdom of Saint Abo</i>.
</p>
