<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Drostan</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">~7th century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> &amp; <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">New Aberdour</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">11 July; 15 December</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Drostan</b> (d. early 7th century), also <b>Drustan</b>, was the founder and abbot of the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Old_Deer" title="Old Deer">Old Deer</a> in <a href="/wiki/Aberdeenshire" title="Aberdeenshire">Aberdeenshire</a>.  His relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to the church at <a href="/wiki/New_Aberdour" title="New Aberdour">New Aberdour</a> and his holy well lies nearby.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Drostan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Drostan was a Scottish abbot who flourished about A.D. 600. All that is known of him is found in the "Breviarium Aberdonense" and in the "<a href="/wiki/Book_of_Deer" title="Book of Deer">Book of Deer</a>", a ninth-century <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscript</a>, now in the <a href="/wiki/Cambridge_University_Library" title="Cambridge University Library">Cambridge University Library</a>, but these two accounts do not agree in every particular. He appears to have belonged to the royal family of the Scoti, his father's name being Cosgrach. Showing signs of a religious vocation he was entrusted at an early age to the care of St. Columba, who trained him and gave him the monastic habit.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Drostan was one of the twelve companions who sailed from Ireland to Scotland around 563 with St Columba. These twelve became known as the 'Brethren of St Columba'.<sup id="cite_ref-aberdeenshire_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aberdeenshire-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He accompanied that saint when he visited <a href="/wiki/Aberdour" title="Aberdour">Aberdour</a> in <a href="/wiki/Buchan" title="Buchan">Buchan</a>, about 45 miles from Aberdeen.
</p><p>According to the Celtic legend St. Columcille, his disciple Drostan, and others, went from Iona into Buchan and established an important missionary centre at Deer on the banks of the Ugie on lands given him by the mormaer or chief of the district whose son he had by his prayers freed of a dangerous illness.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Pictish ruler of that country gave them the site of Deir, fourteen miles farther inland, where they established a monastery, and when St. <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a> returned to <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> he left St. Drostan there as abbot of the new foundation which some sources say received royal support because of its proximity to the Pictish capital of Craig Phadrig, near Inverness.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On the death of the Abbot of Dalquhongale (Holywood) some few years later, St. Drostan was chosen to succeed him. Afterwards, feeling called to a life of greater seclusion, he resigned his abbacy, went farther north, and became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at <a href="/wiki/River_North_Esk,_Angus#Glen_Esk" title="River North Esk, Angus">Glenesk</a>. Here his sanctity attracted the poor and needy, and many miracles are ascribed to him, including the restoration of sight to a priest named Symon.
</p><p>St. Drostan is credited as being the founder of the Chapel of St. Tear near <a href="/wiki/Ackergill" title="Ackergill">Ackergill</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wick,_Caithness" title="Wick, Caithness">Wick</a> parish on the east coast of <a href="/wiki/Caithness" title="Caithness">Caithness</a>. It has been speculated that the name <i>Tear</i> might be a variant of <i>Deer.</i><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When St. Drostan died at Glen Esk his remains were conveyed back to Aberdour where they were deposited in a 'tumba lapidea' or stone coffin. Here his bones were said to work miraculous cures upon the sick and afflicted.<sup id="cite_ref-aberdour_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aberdour-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Aberdeen_Breviary" title="Aberdeen Breviary">Breviary of Aberdeen</a></i> celebrates his feast on 15 December. The monastery of Old Deer, which had fallen into decay, was rebuilt for <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monks in 1213 and so continued until the Reformation.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
