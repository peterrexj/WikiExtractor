<p><b>Hyacinth</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ὑάκινθος</span>, <i>Hyakinthos</i>; died 108) was a young <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> living at the start of the second century, who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Hyacinth is sometimes called by his <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> name <b>Hyacinthus</b> (in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Hyacinthe</i>; <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Jacinto</i>; and <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Giacinto</i>).
</p><p>According to tradition, he was a native of <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Caesarea</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>, a member of a Christian family. As a boy, he was appointed to serve as an assistant to the <a href="/wiki/Chamberlain_(office)" title="Chamberlain (office)">chamberlain</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Trajan" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Trajan">Emperor Trajan</a>. His failure to participate in the ceremonial <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrifices</a> to the official <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a> soon came to be noticed by other members of the Imperial household.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When he was denounced as a Christian, Hyacinth proclaimed his faith. As a result, he was imprisoned and underwent numerous <a href="/wiki/Flagellation" title="Flagellation">scourgings</a> and tortures. He was deliberately served only food which had been blessed for sacrifice to the gods, the eating of which was banned by both <a href="/wiki/Judaism" title="Judaism">Judaism</a> and Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Thus, he starved to death in 108 AD, dying at the age of twelve. Just before his death, legend says, his jailers saw him being comforted by <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angels</a>, who bestowed a crown on him.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hyacinth died in the city of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. Later, the saint's relics were transferred to Caesarea.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A jewel-encrusted human skeleton in a gilded glass case labeled "S. HYACINTHUS M." (Saint Hyacinth, Martyr) is preserved<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and venerated<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> in a secular building that was the Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Abbey_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey church">abbey church</a> of the former <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/F%C3%BCrstenfeld_Abbey" title="Fürstenfeld Abbey">Abbey of Fürstenfeld</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>), of which the church is the only surviving structure. The skeleton arrived at Fürstenfeld Abbey at an unknown date.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Writing of Europe’s skeletons of “<a href="/wiki/Catacomb_saints" title="Catacomb saints">catacomb saints</a>”, art historian <a href="/wiki/Paul_Koudounaris" title="Paul Koudounaris">Paul Koudounaris</a> states that they “could have been anyone, but they were pulled out of the ground and raised to the heights of glory.”<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Many places, people, and other entities are named after Hyacinth (or Jacinto, the Spanish form of "Hyacinth"). See the disambiguation pages in the "See also" section, below.
</p><p>Hyacinth is not to be confused with the third-century martyr <a href="/wiki/Protus_and_Hyacinth" title="Protus and Hyacinth">Hyacinth (died c. 257)</a>  or the medieval <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> saint <a href="/wiki/Hyacinth_of_Poland" title="Hyacinth of Poland">Hyacinth of Poland</a>.
</p>
