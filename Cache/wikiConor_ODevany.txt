<p><b>Concobhar Ó Duibheannaigh</b> (c. 1532 – 1 (<a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">O.S.</a>)/11 (<a href="/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="Gregorian calendar">N.S.</a>) February 1612; <i>Conor O'Devany</i>, <i>Cornelius O'Devany</i>) is a formally beatified <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> who was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholicism)">Roman Catholic bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p><p>Conor O'Devany was born in <a href="/wiki/Malin_Head" title="Malin Head">Malin Head</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-bodevany_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bodevany-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> educated at the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Donegal_Town" class="mw-redirect" title="Donegal Town">Donegal Town</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  While in Rome, Ó Duibheannaigh was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Down_and_Connor" title="Bishop of Down and Connor">Bishop of Down and Connor</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII" title="Pope Gregory XIII">Pope Gregory XIII</a> on 27 April 1582, and consecrated by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Nicolas_de_Pellev%C3%A9" title="Nicolas de Pellevé">Nicolas de Pellevé</a> on 2 February 1583.<sup id="cite_ref-bodevany_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bodevany-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Execution_and_martyrdom">Execution and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Concobhar_%C3%93_Duibheannaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Execution and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1588, Ó Duibheannaigh was committed to <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a>. Failing to convict him of any crime punishable by death, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Deputy" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Deputy">Lord Deputy</a> <a href="/wiki/William_FitzWilliam_(Lord_Deputy)" title="William FitzWilliam (Lord Deputy)">William Fitzwilliam</a> sought authority from <a href="/wiki/William_Cecil,_1st_Baron_Burghley" title="William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley">William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley</a> to "be rid of such an obstinate enemy of God and so rank a traitor to Her Majesty as no doubt he is".<sup id="cite_ref-ceodevany_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ceodevany-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He lay in prison until November 1590, being then released ostensibly on his own petition but doubtless through policy. He was protected by <a href="/wiki/Hugh_O%27Neill,_Earl_of_Tyrone" title="Hugh O&#39;Neill, Earl of Tyrone">Hugh O'Neill, Earl of Tyrone</a> until 1607 (see the <a href="/wiki/Flight_of_the_Earls" title="Flight of the Earls">Flight of the Earls</a>), and escaped arrest until the middle of 1611, when, almost eighty years old, he was taken while administering <a href="/wiki/Confirmation" title="Confirmation">Confirmation</a> and again committed to Dublin Castle.<sup id="cite_ref-ceodevany_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ceodevany-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His execution was at the personal wish of the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Deputy_of_Ireland" title="Lord Deputy of Ireland">Lord Deputy of Ireland</a>, Sir <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Chichester" class="mw-redirect" title="Arthur Chichester">Arthur Chichester</a>, who was vehemently anti-Catholic, and seems to have been rather against the wishes of the Government as a whole.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On 28 January 1612, Ó Duibheannaigh was tried for high treason, found guilty by the majority of a packed jury, and sentenced to die on 1 February (<a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>). He was drawn on a cart from the Castle to the gallows beyond the river; the whole route was crowded with Catholics. Protestant clergymen pestered him with ministrations and urged him to confess he died for treason. "Pray let me be", he answered, "the viceroy's messenger to me here present, could tell that I might have life and revenue for going once to that temple", pointing to a tower opposite. He kissed the gallows before mounting, and then proceeding to exhort the Catholics to constancy, he was thrown off, cut down alive, and quartered.<sup id="cite_ref-ceodevany_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ceodevany-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With him suffered <a href="/wiki/Patrick_O%27Loughran" title="Patrick O&#39;Loughran">Patrick O'Loughran</a>, a priest arrested at Cork. The people, despite the guards, carried off the halter, his clothes, and even fragments of his body and chips of the gallows. They prayed all night by the remains, an infirm man was reported cured by touching them, and Mass after Mass was said there from Midnight until day. Such was the concourse that the viceroy ordered the members to be buried on the spot, but next night the Catholics exhumed them and interred them in St. James's Churchyard. A list of martyrs compiled by Ó Duibheannaigh was used by Rothe in his "Analecta".<sup id="cite_ref-ceodevany_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ceodevany-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Concobhar_%C3%93_Duibheannaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 27 September 1992, Ó Duibheannaigh, with sixteen others, the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a>, was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in Rome. The Feast Day of the Irish Martyrs is celebrated on 20 June.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
