<p><b>Paternian</b> or <b>Paternianus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Paterniano</i>) is the name of an Italian saint. A native of <a href="/wiki/Fermo" title="Fermo">Fermo</a> who escaped to the mountains during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> by <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, he was then appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Fano" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Fano">bishop of Fano</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_I" title="Pope Sylvester I">Pope Sylvester I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>(Paternian is often confused with Parthenian (Parteniano), a bishop of Bologna, also commemorated on July 12.)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paternian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Historical details about Paternian are scarce.  The <i>Vita Sancti Paterniani</i> can be found in a <a href="/wiki/Codex" title="Codex">codex</a> of the 12th century, though it dates earlier, and was written by a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> of the 10th or 11th century.  But it is legendary and not reliable.
</p><p>Paternian was born at <a href="/wiki/Fano" title="Fano">Fano</a> around 275 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  An <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> told him in a vision to escape this city and hide out in a deserted place near the <a href="/wiki/Metauro_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Metauro River">Metauro River</a>.  He became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Later, when the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a> stopped, the citizens of Fano demanded that he become their bishop.  Paternian governed the city for many years.  He died on November 13, around 360 AD.  <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">Miracles</a> were reported at his tomb and his cult spread rapidly.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Paternian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to one legend, the inhabitants of Fano competed with those of <a href="/wiki/Cervia" title="Cervia">Cervia</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">body of the saint</a>.  Cervia would be left with a finger, while Fano would possess the rest of the saint's relics.<sup id="cite_ref-comunecervia_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comunecervia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>His cult spread across <a href="/wiki/Marche" title="Marche">Marche</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romagna" title="Romagna">Romagna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a>.  In the area known as the Camminate di Fano, there is a cave known as the <i>Grotta di San Paterniano</i>, which is said to have been his refuge during the Diocletian persecution.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austrian</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Paternion" title="Paternion">Paternion</a> takes its name from him.  The name appears in documents for the first time in 1296, and its origin is derived from the fact that the area lay under the influence of the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia_(State)" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Aquileia (State)">patriarchate of Aquileia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There was an old <a href="/wiki/Proverb" title="Proverb">proverb</a> from Romagna that ran: "Par San Paternian e' trema la coda a e' can."<sup id="cite_ref-comunecervia_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comunecervia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> ("On St. Paternian's day, the dog's tail wags").  This Cervian proverb refers to the fact that the cold began to be felt around the saint's feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-comunecervia_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-comunecervia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
