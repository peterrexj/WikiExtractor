<p><b>Pietro I Orseolo</b> <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">OSBCam</a>, also named <i>Peter Urseulus</i>, (928–987) was the <a href="/wiki/Doge_of_Venice" title="Doge of Venice">Doge of Venice</a> from 976 until 978. He abdicated his office and left in the middle of the night to become a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He later entered the order of the Camaldolese Hermits of Mount Corona. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. In 1733 the Venetian librarian Giuseppe Bettinelli published an edition of a biography written by the Friar <a href="/wiki/Fulgenzio_Manfredi" title="Fulgenzio Manfredi">Fulgenzio Manfredi</a> in 1606.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pietro_I_Orseolo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Orseolo was born in 928 near <a href="/wiki/Udine" title="Udine">Udine</a> to one of the more powerful families in <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Venice</a>: the <a href="/wiki/Orseolo" title="Orseolo">Orseolo</a> were the descendants of Doge <a href="/wiki/Teodato_Ipato" title="Teodato Ipato">Teodato Ipato</a> and Doge <a href="/wiki/Orso_Ipato" title="Orso Ipato">Orso Ipato</a>. At the age of 20 he was named commander of the Venetian fleet, performing distinguished service as a soldier; he waged successful campaigns against the <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatian</a> <a href="/wiki/Pirates" class="mw-redirect" title="Pirates">pirates</a>. He was also devoted to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. He had one son named <a href="/wiki/Pietro_II_Orseolo" title="Pietro II Orseolo">Pietro II Orseolo</a>, who also became a Doge, and a daughter who married to Giovanni Morosini, a member of the <a href="/wiki/Morosini_family" title="Morosini family">House of Morosini</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign">Reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pietro_I_Orseolo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png/150px-Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png" decoding="async" width="150" height="217" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png/225px-Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png/300px-Doge_Pietro_Orseolo_I.png 2x" data-file-width="1059" data-file-height="1535" /></a><figcaption>Coat of arms.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 976, the sitting doge, <a href="/wiki/Pietro_IV_Candiano" title="Pietro IV Candiano">Pietro IV Candiano</a>, was killed in a revolution that protested his attempts to create a monarchy. According to a statement by the Camaldolese monk and <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">cardinal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peter_Damian" title="Peter Damian">Peter Damian</a>, Orseolo himself had led a conspiracy against Candiano. This statement, however, cannot be verified. Nonetheless, Orseolo was elected as his successor. His wife and consort was <a href="/wiki/Felicia_Malipiero" title="Felicia Malipiero">Felicia Malipiero</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As doge, Orseolo demonstrated a good deal of talent in restoring order to an unsettled Venice and showed remarkable generosity in the treatment of his predecessor's widow.  He built hospitals and cared for widows, orphans and pilgrims. Out of his own resources he began the reconstruction of the ducal chapel, now <a href="/wiki/St._Mark%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mark&#39;s Basilica">St. Mark's Basilica</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Doge%27s_Palace" title="Doge&#39;s Palace">Doge's Palace</a>, which had been destroyed during the revolution, along with a great part of the city. Two years later, on 1 September 978, seemingly without notifying anyone, not even his wife and children, he left Venice with Abbot Guarin and three other Venetians (one of whom was St. <a href="/wiki/Romuald" title="Romuald">Romuald</a>) to join the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a>) abbey at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Michel-de-Cuxa" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Michel-de-Cuxa">Saint-Michel-de-Cuxa</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Miquel de Cuixà</i>) in <a href="/wiki/Prades,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales" title="Prades, Pyrénées-Orientales">Prades</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Prada</i>), southern <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.
</p><p>Here Orseolo led a life of great <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>, performing the most menial tasks. There is some evidence that he had been considering such an action for some time.  His only contact with Venice was to instruct his grandson <a href="/wiki/Otto_Orseolo" title="Otto Orseolo">Otto</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (who would become doge in 1008) in the life of Christian virtue. After some years as a monk at the abbey, probably with the encouragement of Saint Romuald (who later went on to found the <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a> branch of the Benedictines), Orseolo left the monastery to become a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in the surrounding forest, a calling he followed for seven years until he died. His body is buried in the village church in <a href="/wiki/Prades,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales" title="Prades, Pyrénées-Orientales">Prades</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Prada</i>), France.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pietro_I_Orseolo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Forty years after his death, in 1027, Orseolo was officially recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blessed</a> by the local bishop.
</p><p>Orseolo is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, his cultus having been confirmed by his <a href="/wiki/Equipollent_canonization" class="mw-redirect" title="Equipollent canonization">equivalent canonization</a> in 1731 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a>, who set his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> for 14 January. The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">reform of the liturgical calendar</a> in 1969 transferred the feast to 10 January, the day of his death. The Camaldolese order celebrates his memory on 19 January.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
