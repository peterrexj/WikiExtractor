<p><b>Tadhg Mac Cárthaigh</b> (Latinised and anglicised <i>Thaddeus McCarthy</i>) c. 1455 &#8211; 25 October 1492, was an Irish ecclesiastic. He was a bishop who never ruled his see, even though he was appointed to two of them: <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ross,_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ross, Ireland">Bishop of Ross, Ireland</a> in <a href="/wiki/1482_in_Ireland" title="1482 in Ireland">1482</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cork_and_Cloyne" title="Bishop of Cork and Cloyne">Bishop of Cork and Cloyne</a> in <a href="/wiki/1490_in_Ireland" title="1490 in Ireland">1490</a>.  His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 25 October.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tadhg_Mac_C%C3%A1rthaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mac Cárthaigh was born in 1455 at Caheragh, Co. Cork. His father was the lord of <a href="/wiki/Muskerry" title="Muskerry">Muskerry</a> and his mother was reputedly the daughter of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Fitzmaurice&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Edmund Fitzmaurice (page does not exist)">Edmund Fitzmaurice</a>, ninth <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_Kerry" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of Kerry">Lord of Kerry</a>. It is probably the case that he belonged to the sept of the <a href="/wiki/MacCarthy_Reagh" class="mw-redirect" title="MacCarthy Reagh">MacCarthy Reagh</a> of <a href="/wiki/Carbery_(barony)" title="Carbery (barony)">Carbery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ocallaghan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocallaghan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Laurence Rehenan, Professor of Ecclesiastical History of Maynooth College, suggests that he was educated by the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan Friars</a> of <a href="/wiki/Timoleague" title="Timoleague">Timoleague</a>.
</p><p>Mac Cárthaigh studied for the priesthood under an uncle, Canon Thady Mac Cárthaigh and continued his studies in Paris with another relative, Professor Don Raymond, at Paris University. He was ordained in Cork by Bishop William Roche and travelled to Rome afterwards to continue his studies.<sup id="cite_ref-ocallaghan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocallaghan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It would appear that Thaddeus, prior to nomination to <a href="/wiki/Ross,_Scotland" title="Ross, Scotland">Ross</a>, occupied a position in one of the Roman tribunals.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Ross">Bishop of Ross</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tadhg_Mac_C%C3%A1rthaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop of Ross">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While in Rome, his many qualities made a deep impression and although only 27 years old, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a> appointed him as Bishop of Ross in Cork, which see had become vacant upon the death of Bishop Donald.<sup id="cite_ref-ocallaghan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocallaghan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 3 May 1482 Mac Cárthaigh was consecrated archbishop by in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Stefano_del_Cacco" title="Santo Stefano del Cacco">Santo Stefano del Cacco</a> in Rome by Stephen Teglatius (or de Taleazis), <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Antivari" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Antivari">Archbishop of Antivari</a> (the modern <a href="/wiki/Bar,_Montenegro" title="Bar, Montenegro">Bar</a>), assisted by Daniel, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rhosus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rhosus">Bishop of Rhosus</a> in Cilicia, and by Julianus de Matheis (or de Maffei de Vulterris), Bishop of Bertinori.
</p><p>When he returned to <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> Mac Cárthaigh discovered that the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> was already in the possession of Hugh O'Driscoll, who had been appointed to the see in 1473 by the same Pope Sixtus. At this stage in Irish history there was no single political entity. There were Gaelic kings and Anglo lords. It may have happened that news had reached Rome of the death of Bishop Hugh either innocently or through political intrigue.
</p><p>A lengthy dispute broke out between the Mac Cárthaigh and O'Driscoll dynasties over who was the rightful bishop of Ross, during which the O'Driscolls made many false accusations against Mac Cárthaigh. Bishop O'Driscoll assumed Thaddeus was an imposter and complained to Rome. In 1485, Henry VII became king of England, Ireland, and his party wanted to have its own representative as bishop of Ross. Mac Cárthaigh was declared an intruder and excommunicated. In 1488 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_VIII" title="Pope Innocent VIII">Pope Innocent VIII</a> confirmed the excommunication, at which time Mac Cárthaigh appealed the decision and a commission was set up.  The commission found in his favour, and the excommunication was nullified. He was then appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cork_and_Cloyne" title="Bishop of Cork and Cloyne">Bishop of Cork and Cloyne</a> on 21 April 1490.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Cork_and_Cloyne">Bishop of Cork and Cloyne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tadhg_Mac_C%C3%A1rthaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bishop of Cork and Cloyne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg/250px-Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg/375px-Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg/500px-Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="384" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cobh_Cathedral_Blessed_Thaddeus_Chapel.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Mosaic in the Chapel of Blessed Thaddeus McCarthy, Cobh Cathedral.</div></div></div>
<p>Returning to <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a>, Mac Cárthaigh found that <a href="/wiki/Gerald_M%C3%B3r_FitzGerald,_8th_Earl_of_Kildare" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerald Mór FitzGerald, 8th Earl of Kildare">Gerald FitzGerald</a> had usurped the Diocese of Cork and Cloyne with support from local rulers. Opponents of the Mac Cárthaigh dynasty included the corporation of the City of Cork, and the Earl of Desmond. Gerald Fitzgerald had the backing of leading landowners in the county.
</p><p>Armed men took possession of the cathedral preventing Mac Cárthaigh from entering. For two years, he travelled from town to village armed with the papal documents announcing his rightful appointment and absolution from any criminal charge. Having opposed any retaliation by his own lineage, they too abandoned him. Without dynastic support, status or security, Mac Cárthaigh once again set off for Rome to plead his case to the Pope. Pope Innocent XIII gave him another document dated 18 July 1492. It ordered that Gerald, Earl of Kildare, at that time, one of the most powerful figures in Ireland, should, with his army, protect and restore Mac Cárthaigh to his rightful place as Bishop of Cork and Cloyne.
</p><p>Fearing danger to his life from his enemies, Mac Cárthaigh set out for Ireland as a pilgrim.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tadhg_Mac_C%C3%A1rthaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fourteen weeks later, Mac Cárthaigh arrived at <a href="/wiki/Ivrea" title="Ivrea">Ivrea</a> having presumably walked from Rome. On the evening of 24 October 1492, a lone pilgrim arrived on foot at the hostel for pilgrims. Dressed in a coarse habit and hood and wearing the oyster shell emblem that ensured safe passage, he appeared to be very weak and worn out by fatigue. The warden of the hostel gave the exhausted traveler a hospitable welcome and a place to rest for the night. At dawn, a strange and mysterious light was observed coming from his room. On entering, the warden discovered the body of the pilgrim illuminated by a beautiful radiance of light. Tadhg Mac Cárthaigh was 37 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-ocallaghan_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocallaghan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mac Cárthaigh would have been buried in a pauper's grave but the Bishop Nicholas Garigliatti was summoned. The bishop is said to have had an extraordinary dream during the night in which he had witnessed a man dressed in bishop's robes ascending in glory to heaven. He immediately recognised the dead man as the person he had seen in the dream. The man's belongings were examined - a wallet, a water container and a pilgrim staff were his sole possessions. However, the wallet contained some papal documents and a bishop's ring. Bishop Garigliatti ordered that the body be clothed in episcopal robes and brought to Ivrea Cathedral for solemn lying in state before burial within the cathedral. When news of the death of bishop Mac Cárthaigh traveling in disguise spread, crowds flocked to the cathedral of Ivrea for his funeral.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in the Ivrea cathedral and many miracles have been attributed to him there.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tadhg_Mac_C%C3%A1rthaigh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg/250px-Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="178" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg/375px-Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg/500px-Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3990" data-file-height="2843" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ivrea_Duomo_Urna_Taddeo_McCarty.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Altar with the funeral urn of Blessed Tadhg Mac Cárthaigh, Cathedral of Ivrea</div></div></div>
<p>His tomb became a local shrine. In 1742, when his tomb was opened, the body was found to be perfectly preserved.
</p><p>During the great famine in 1847, the clergy and people of Ivrea donated a substantial sum of money for the relief of the Irish Famine victims. They also requested any information on Mac Cárthaigh, principally because of the veneration of the people of Ivrea and the many miracles attributed to his intercession. This request aroused great interest in Mac Cárthaigh and, after a long investigation into his life, it was decided to seek his beatification on behalf of the people and clergy of Ivrea and Cork.<sup id="cite_ref-cork_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cork-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1896 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> confirmed the immemorial cult of the "Blessed Thaddeus". The same year (1896) clerics in <a href="/wiki/Ivrea" title="Ivrea">Ivrea</a> agreed to donate major relics of Mac Cárthaigh to the dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Cork_and_Ross" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Cork and Ross">Cork &amp; Ross</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Cloyne" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Cloyne">Cloyne</a>. These relics can now be found in The Church of The Immaculate Conception The Lough, <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_Mary_and_St_Anne" title="Cathedral of St Mary and St Anne">Cork North Cathedral</a>, <a href="/wiki/St_Colman%E2%80%99s_cathedral,_Cobh" class="mw-redirect" title="St Colman’s cathedral, Cobh">Cobh Cathedral</a> and St Mary's Parish Church in <a href="/wiki/Youghal" title="Youghal">Youghal</a>. His feast day of 25 October is kept in the dioceses of Ross, Cork, and Cloyne, and in Ivrea, where he was buried.
</p><p>Mac Cárthaigh was honoured with the title of "White Martyr of Munster" principally because of the intense mental and physical anguish.<sup id="cite_ref-cork_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cork-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
