<p><b>John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Society_of_Antiquaries_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Fellow of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland">FSA Scot</a></span> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;b&#39; in &#39;buy&#39;">b</span><span title="/j/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;yes&#39;">j</span><span title="/uː/: &#39;oo&#39; in &#39;goose&#39;">uː</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span></span>/</a></span></span>; 25 May 1713 – 10 March 1792), styled <b>Lord Mount Stuart</b> between 1713 and 1723, was a <a href="/wiki/British_nobility" title="British nobility">British nobleman</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain" title="Kingdom of Great Britain">Great Britain</a> from 1762 to 1763 under <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">George III</a>. He was arguably the last important royal <a href="/wiki/Favourite" title="Favourite">favourite</a> in British politics. He was the first prime minister from Scotland following the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Union_1707" title="Acts of Union 1707">Acts of Union</a> in 1707. He was also elected as the first president of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Antiquaries_of_Scotland" title="Society of Antiquaries of Scotland">Society of Antiquaries of Scotland</a> when it was founded in 1780.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_rise_to_prominence">Early life and rise to prominence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and rise to prominence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was born in Parliament Close, nearby to <a href="/wiki/St_Giles_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St Giles Cathedral">St Giles Cathedral</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Mile" title="Royal Mile">Royal Mile</a> in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh" title="Edinburgh">Edinburgh</a> on 25 May 1713, the son of <a href="/wiki/James_Stuart,_2nd_Earl_of_Bute" title="James Stuart, 2nd Earl of Bute">James Stuart, 2nd Earl of Bute</a>, and his wife, Lady Anne Campbell.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He attended <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a> from 1724 to 1730.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He went on to study <a href="/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system)" title="Civil law (legal system)">civil law</a> at the Universities of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Groningen" title="University of Groningen">Groningen</a> (1730–1732) and <a href="/wiki/Leiden_University" title="Leiden University">Leiden</a> (1732–1734) in the Netherlands,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> graduating from the latter with a degree in civil law.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A close relative of the <a href="/wiki/Clan_Campbell" title="Clan Campbell">Clan Campbell</a> (his mother was a daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Archibald_Campbell,_1st_Duke_of_Argyll" title="Archibald Campbell, 1st Duke of Argyll">1st Duke of Argyll</a>), Bute succeeded to the <a href="/wiki/Marquess_of_Bute" title="Marquess of Bute">Earldom of Bute</a> (named after the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Bute" title="Isle of Bute">Isle of Bute</a>) upon the death of his father in 1723. He was brought up thereafter by his maternal uncles, the <a href="/wiki/John_Campbell,_2nd_Duke_of_Argyll" title="John Campbell, 2nd Duke of Argyll">2nd Duke of Argyll</a> and Archibald Campbell, 3rd Duke of Argyll, 1st and only Earl of Ilay. In August 1735, he eloped with <a href="/wiki/Mary_Stuart,_Countess_of_Bute" title="Mary Stuart, Countess of Bute">Mary Wortley Montagu</a>, whose parents <a href="/wiki/Sir_Edward_Wortley_Montagu" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Edward Wortley Montagu">Sir Edward</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lady_Mary_Wortley_Montagu" title="Lady Mary Wortley Montagu">Lady Mary Wortley Montagu</a> were slow to consent to the marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1737, he was elected a <a href="/wiki/Scottish_representative_peer" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish representative peer">Scottish representative peer</a>; despite being in London in December of that year, he did not participate in deliberations in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Because of his support for Argyll against <a href="/wiki/Horace_Walpole" title="Horace Walpole">Walpole</a>, he was not re-elected in 1741.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> For the next several years he retired to his estates in Scotland to manage his affairs and indulge his interest in <a href="/wiki/Botany" title="Botany">botany</a>. 
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_Stuart,_Earl_of_Bute.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/John_Stuart%2C_Earl_of_Bute.jpg/200px-John_Stuart%2C_Earl_of_Bute.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="241" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/John_Stuart%2C_Earl_of_Bute.jpg/300px-John_Stuart%2C_Earl_of_Bute.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/32/John_Stuart%2C_Earl_of_Bute.jpg 2x" data-file-width="388" data-file-height="468" /></a><figcaption>Bute (1770)</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1745, Bute moved to Twickenham, Middlesex where his family rented a house for forty-five pounds per annum.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He met <a href="/wiki/Frederick,_Prince_of_Wales" title="Frederick, Prince of Wales">Frederick, Prince of Wales</a>, in 1747 at the <a href="/wiki/Egham" title="Egham">Egham</a> Races and became a close friend.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the Prince's death in 1751, Bute was appointed tutor to <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">Prince George, the new Prince of Wales</a> (later George III).<sup id="cite_ref-auto1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bute arranged for the Prince and his brother <a href="/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_York_and_Albany" title="Prince Edward, Duke of York and Albany">Prince Edward</a> to follow a course of lectures on <a href="/wiki/Natural_philosophy" title="Natural philosophy">natural philosophy</a> by the itinerant lecturer <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Demainbray" title="Stephen Demainbray">Stephen Demainbray</a>. This led to an interest in natural philosophy on the part of the young prince and may have led to George III's collection of natural philosophical instruments.
</p><p>Bute furthermore became close to Prince Frederick's widow, <a href="/wiki/Augusta_of_Saxe-Gotha" class="mw-redirect" title="Augusta of Saxe-Gotha">Augusta of Saxe-Gotha</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Dowager" title="Dowager">Dowager</a> <a href="/wiki/Princess_of_Wales" title="Princess of Wales">Princess of Wales</a>, and it was rumoured that the couple were having an affair. Indeed, one of the Prince of Wales's associates, <a href="/wiki/John_Horne_Tooke" title="John Horne Tooke">John Horne Tooke</a>, published a scandalous pamphlet alluding to the liaison, but the rumours were almost certainly untrue, since Bute held sincere religious beliefs against adultery and, by all indications, appeared happily married.
</p><p>In 1780, Bute was elected as the first President of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Antiquaries_of_Scotland" title="Society of Antiquaries of Scotland">Society of Antiquaries of Scotland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_minister">Prime minister<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Prime minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Bute_ministry" title="Bute ministry">Bute ministry</a></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years%27_War" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years&#39; War">Great Britain in the Seven Years' War</a></div>
<p>Because of the influence he had over his pupil, Bute expected to rise quickly to political power following George's accession to the throne in 1760, but his plans were premature. It would first be necessary to remove both the incumbent prime minister (the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham-Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">Duke of Newcastle</a>) and arguably the even more powerful Secretary of State for the Southern Department (<a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt the Elder</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975271_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975271-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The Government of the day, buoyed by recent successes in the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a>, was popular, however, and did well at the general election which, as was customary at the time, took place on the accession of the new monarch.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975272–274_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975272–274-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Supported by the King, Bute manoeuvred himself into power by first allying himself with Newcastle against Pitt over the latter's desire to declare war on Spain. Once thwarted in his designs against Spain by Bute and Newcastle, Pitt resigned his post as Secretary of State for the Southern Department. Next, Bute forced Newcastle's resignation as prime minister when he found himself in a small minority within the government over the level of funding and direction of the Seven Years' War.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975275–288_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrowning1975275–288-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Re-elected as a Scottish representative peer in 1760, Bute was appointed the <i>de facto</i> prime minister after the resignations of Pitt and Newcastle, thus ending a long period of <a href="/wiki/British_Whig_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig Party">Whig</a> dominance.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Bute's premiership was notable for the negotiation of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)" title="Treaty of Paris (1763)">Treaty of Paris (1763)</a> which concluded the Seven Years' War. In so doing, Bute had to soften his previous stance in relation to concessions given to France in that he agreed that the important fisheries in Newfoundland be returned to France without Britain's possession of Guadeloupe in return.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESchweizer198827–29_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESchweizer198827–29-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After peace was concluded, Bute and the King decided that Britain's military expenditure should not exceed its prewar levels, but they thought a large presence was necessary in America to deal with the French and Spanish threat. They therefore charged the colonists for the increased military levels, thus catalysing the resistance to taxes which led to the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolution" title="American Revolution">American Revolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESchweizer198817–35_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESchweizer198817–35-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Bute also introduced a <a href="/wiki/Cider_tax" class="mw-redirect" title="Cider tax">cider tax</a> of four shillings per hogshead in 1763 to help finance the Seven Years' War.<sup id="cite_ref-auto1_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The journalist <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a> published a newspaper, <i><a href="/wiki/The_North_Briton" title="The North Briton">The North Briton</a></i>, in which both Bute and the Dowager Princess of Wales were savagely satirised. Bute resigned as prime minister shortly afterwards, although he remained in the House of Lords as a Scottish representative peer until 1780. He remained friendly with the Dowager Princess of Wales, but her attempts to reconcile him with George III proved futile.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-premiership">Post-premiership</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Post-premiership">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>For the remainder of his life, Bute remained at his estate in <a href="/wiki/Hampshire" title="Hampshire">Hampshire</a>, where he built himself a mansion called High Cliff near <a href="/wiki/Christchurch,_Dorset" title="Christchurch, Dorset">Christchurch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ButeODNB_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButeODNB-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> From there he continued his pursuit of <a href="/wiki/Botany" title="Botany">botany</a> and became a major literary and artistic patron. Among his beneficiaries were <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Johnson" title="Samuel Johnson">Samuel Johnson</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tobias_Smollett" title="Tobias Smollett">Tobias Smollett</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Adam" title="Robert Adam">Robert Adam</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_Robertson_(historian)" title="William Robertson (historian)">William Robertson</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Hill_(author)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Hill (author)">John Hill</a>. He also gave considerably to the Scottish universities.
</p><p>He financed <a href="/wiki/Alberto_Fortis" title="Alberto Fortis">Alberto Fortis</a>'s travels into <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a>. His botanical work culminated in the publication of <i>Botanical Tables Containing the Families of British Plants</i> in 1785. Even after his retirement, Bute was accused by many Americans in the years leading up to the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War" title="American Revolutionary War">American Revolutionary War</a> as having an undue corrupting influence over the British government.<sup id="cite_ref-Bailyn_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bailyn-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died at his home in South Audley Street, <a href="/wiki/Grosvenor_Square" title="Grosvenor Square">Grosvenor Square</a>, <a href="/wiki/Westminster" title="Westminster">Westminster</a>, from complications of a fall suffered while staying at <a href="/wiki/Highcliffe" title="Highcliffe">Highcliffe</a>, and was buried at <a href="/wiki/Rothesay,_Argyll_and_Bute" class="mw-redirect" title="Rothesay, Argyll and Bute">Rothesay</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Bute" title="Isle of Bute">Isle of Bute</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ButeODNB_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButeODNB-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Flowering_plant" title="Flowering plant">flowering plant</a> genera <i><a href="/wiki/Butea" title="Butea">Butea</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Stewartia" title="Stewartia">Stewartia</a></i> are named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1761, Bute was appointed Ranger of <a href="/wiki/Richmond_Park" title="Richmond Park">Richmond Park</a> by King George III, a post he held until his death; Bute Avenue in <a href="/wiki/Petersham,_London" title="Petersham, London">Petersham</a> near the park is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to historian John Naish, the 18th-century expression "Jack Boot" meaning a stupid person originated as disparagement of Stuart's performance as prime minister.<sup id="cite_ref-naish_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naish-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Luton_Hoo">Luton Hoo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Luton Hoo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg/220px-Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg/330px-Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg/440px-Luton_Hoo_mansion-3375179309.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2705" data-file-height="1845" /></a><figcaption>Luton Hoo mansion in 2009</figcaption></figure><p>Bute purchased <a href="/wiki/Luton_Hoo" title="Luton Hoo">Luton Hoo</a>, or Luton Park, from Francis Herne MP in 1763 for the sum of £94,700.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>  Recognising that the existing buildings were unsuitable, Bute commissioned the <a href="/wiki/Neoclassicism" title="Neoclassicism">neoclassical</a> architect <a href="/wiki/Robert_Adam" title="Robert Adam">Robert Adam</a> to oversee the redesign of the estate house.<sup id="cite_ref-Russell_2004_156–157_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Russell_2004_156–157-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Initial designs were unsatisfactory and, coupled with the sale of Bute House, Adams submitted new designs for a larger complex, which Bute further adjusted to include five book rooms and seven water closets.<sup id="cite_ref-Russell_2004_156–157_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Russell_2004_156–157-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The building also housed an extensive art collection, particularly paintings of the Dutch and Flemish schools. A fire in March 1771 "did considerable damage" according to contemporary reports.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The project was completed by 1773 but not according to the full plan, the second phase of which was abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He died on 10 March 1792, from a fall he had a year and a half prior. He fell 30 feet (9.1&#160;m) down cliffs in <a href="/wiki/Hampshire" title="Hampshire">Hampshire</a> while collecting plants.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> He died in his mansion on South Audley Street off <a href="/wiki/Grosvenor_Square" title="Grosvenor Square">Grosvenor Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
