<p><b>Bruno the Great</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Brun(o) von Sachsen</i>, "Bruno of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxony</a>"; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Bruno Magnus</i>; May 925 – 11 October 965 AD) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">Archbishop of Cologne</a><sup id="cite_ref-Forse48_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forse48-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> from 953 until his death and <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Lotharingia">Duke of Lotharingia</a> after 954. He was the brother of <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Forse48_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forse48-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bruno was the youngest son of <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler" title="Henry the Fowler">Henry the Fowler</a> and his second wife <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Ringelheim" title="Matilda of Ringelheim">Matilda</a>. While he was still a child, it was decided that he should pursue a clerical career. In the early 940s he was educated in <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a> by the leading scholar, <a href="/wiki/Israel_the_Grammarian" title="Israel the Grammarian">Israel the Grammarian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 951, Otto appointed Bruno as his archchaplain.
</p><p>Bruno soon received further advancement. In 953, the Archbishopric of Cologne fell vacant just when <a href="/wiki/Conrad_the_Red" class="mw-redirect" title="Conrad the Red">Conrad the Red</a>, Duke of Lotharingia and Otto's son-in-law, had joined a rebellion against Otto. By appointing Bruno to the vacant position, Otto provided himself with a powerful ally against Conrad (much of Lotharingia fell under the archdiocese of Cologne) just when he needed one most. By the next year, the rebellion had collapsed. Otto deposed Conrad as Duke of Lotharingia and appointed Bruno in his place.
</p><p>Bruno was to be almost the last duke of the whole of Lotharingia: in 959 two local nobles, <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_I,_Duke_of_Lower_Lorraine" title="Godfrey I, Duke of Lower Lorraine">Godfrey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Duke_of_Upper_Lorraine" title="Frederick I, Duke of Upper Lorraine">Frederick</a>, were appointed as margraves of <a href="/wiki/Lower_Lotharingia" title="Lower Lotharingia">Lower Lotharingia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Lorraine" title="Duchy of Lorraine">Upper Lotharingia</a> respectively. Both margraves were recognised as dukes after Bruno's death.  The two duchies were reunited between 1033 and 1044 under <a href="/wiki/Gothelo_I,_Duke_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Gothelo I, Duke of Lotharingia">Gothelo I, Duke of Lotharingia</a>.
</p><p>The combined positions of archbishop and duke &#8212; or <i><a href="/wiki/Archduke" title="Archduke">archduke</a></i>, as his biographer Ruotger called him &#8212; made Bruno the most powerful man after Otto not just in Germany but also beyond its borders. After the deaths of <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV_of_France" title="Louis IV of France">Louis IV of West Francia</a> in 954 and <a href="/wiki/Hugh_the_Great" title="Hugh the Great">Hugh the Great</a>, his most powerful feudatory, in 956, Bruno, as brother-in-law to both of them and maternal uncle to their heirs <a href="/wiki/Lothair_of_France" title="Lothair of France">Lothair</a>, the new king, and <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Capet" title="Hugh Capet">Hugh Capet</a>, acted as regent of west Francia.
</p><p>From 962 onwards, Bruno was also appointed as Otto's regent in Germany while Otto was absent in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Forse48_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forse48-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno died in <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> in 965 and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Pantaleon" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Pantaleon">monastery of St Pantaleon</a>, which he had founded, just outside Cologne.
</p><p>Bruno's position in Cologne was little short of royal. Indeed, Otto delegated to Bruno and his successors as archbishop a number of normally royal privileges &#8212; the right to build fortifications and set up markets, to strike coins and collect (and keep) such taxes as the special ones on <a href="/wiki/Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Jew">Jews</a> in return for royal protection, those on market trading and tolls from traffic along the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>. Even though Bruno's successors as archbishops would not be dukes as well, they would be the secular as well as the ecclesiastical rulers of Cologne until the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Worringen" title="Battle of Worringen">battle of Worringen</a> three centuries later.
</p><p>Bruno's court in Cologne was the main intellectual and artistic centre of its period in Germany &#8212; far more so than that of his brother Otto, which was far more peripatetic and militarily oriented. Among others, <a href="/wiki/Ratherius_of_Verona" class="mw-redirect" title="Ratherius of Verona">Ratherius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand of Cremona</a> spent time at the court. Many of the next generation of German ecclesiastical leaders were educated at Bruno's court, like <a href="/w/index.php?title=Everaclus_of_Li%C3%A8ge&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Everaclus of Liège (page does not exist)">Everaclus of Liège</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gerard_of_Toul" title="Gerard of Toul">Gerard</a> bishop of Toul, Wikfrid, bishop of Verdun, and <a href="/wiki/Dietrich_I_of_Metz" title="Dietrich I of Metz">Theoderic</a>, bishop of Metz.
</p><p>Bruno's effect on medieval Cologne was immense. Apart from building a palace, he extended the <a href="/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral" title="Cologne Cathedral">cathedral</a> to the point where it was regarded as rivalling <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St Peter's</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> (this cathedral burned down in 1248 and was replaced by the current one). He brought the area between the old Roman walls and the Rhine within the city fortifications; and built new churches to Saint <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a> within this area and to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Saint Andrew</a> just outside the northern city wall and a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine monastery</a> dedicated to St Pantaleon to the south-west of the city.
</p><p>Bruno translated <a href="/wiki/Patroclus_of_Troyes" title="Patroclus of Troyes">St. Patroclus'</a> <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> from <a href="/wiki/Troyes" title="Troyes">Troyes</a> and buried them in 964 at St Patrokli Dom in <a href="/wiki/Soest,_Germany" title="Soest, Germany">Soest</a>, where Patroclus is still today venerated.
</p>
