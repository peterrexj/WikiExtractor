<p><b>Angelo Carletti di Chivasso</b> was a noted moral theologian of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>; born at <a href="/wiki/Chivasso" title="Chivasso">Chivasso</a> in <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>, in 1411; and died at <a href="/wiki/Cuneo" title="Cuneo">Coni</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>, in 1495.
</p><p>His name in Latin is usually given as Angelus de Clavasio (Clavasium being the Latin name of his birthplace).  This form is preserved in bibliographic usage.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelo_Carletti_di_Chivasso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Antonio Carletti was born in 1411 to a noble family of Chivasso, Italy, near Turin.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He attended the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">University of Bologna</a>, where he received the degree of Doctor of Civil and <a href="/wiki/Canon_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon Law">Canon Law</a>, and served as a magistrate in the Court of Chiavasso. He was appointed to the Senate by the Marquis of Monferrato Gian Giacomo. It was probably at the age of thirty that he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at Santa Maria del Monte in Genoa, taking the name <b>Angelo</b>.  There he met Francesco della Rovere, who was later to become <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a>.
</p><p>In 1467 he accompanied Fra Pietro da Napoli, who had been charged by the Vicar General to reorder the Franciscan province of Austria.<sup id="cite_ref-Treccani_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Treccani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1472 he was chosen to fill the office of Vicar-General of that branch of the Order then known as the Cismontane Observance, founded by <a href="/wiki/Bernadine_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernadine of Siena">Bernadine of Siena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He held that office again in 1478, in 1485 and in 1490. He founded the monasteries of <a href="/wiki/Saluzzo" title="Saluzzo">Saluzzo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mondov%C3%AC" title="Mondovì">Mondovì</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pinerolo" title="Pinerolo">Pinerolo</a>; and preached in <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a>, <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cuneo" title="Cuneo">Cuneo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Susa" title="Susa">Susa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Monferrato" class="mw-redirect" title="Monferrato">Monferrato</a> and <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> at the court of <a href="/wiki/Charles_I,_Duke_of_Savoy" title="Charles I, Duke of Savoy">Charles I, Duke of Savoy</a>. He also served as a spiritual counselor for <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Genoa" title="Catherine of Genoa">Catherine of Genoa</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paola_Gambara&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paola Gambara (page does not exist)">Paola Gambara</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Apostolic_Nuncio">Apostolic Nuncio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelo_Carletti_di_Chivasso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Apostolic Nuncio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1480 the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> under <a href="/wiki/Mehmed_II" title="Mehmed II">Mehmed II</a> took possession of <a href="/wiki/Otranto" title="Otranto">Otranto</a>, and threatened to overrun and lay waste the area. Angelo was appointed <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nuncio" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Nuncio">Apostolic Nuncio</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Sixtus_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Sixtus IV">Sixtus IV</a>, and commissioned to preach a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> against the invaders. While the residents of Otranto held out under siege, Mehmed II died and the Turkish forces retired from the Italian peninsula.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Again, in 1491, he was appointed Apostolic Nuncio and Commissary by <a href="/wiki/Innocent_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent VIII">Innocent VIII</a>, conjointly with the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bishop_of_Mauriana&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bishop of Mauriana (page does not exist)">Bishop of Mauriana</a>, and reached a peaceful agreement between Catholics and <a href="/wiki/Waldensians" title="Waldensians">Waldensians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angelo Carletti di Chivasso died on April 11, 1495 at the convent of St. Anthony at Cuneo.<sup id="cite_ref-Treccani_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Treccani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelo_Carletti_di_Chivasso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Angelo_da_Chivasso,_Summa_Angelica,_1511_-_009.tif" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif/lossy-page1-220px-Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="312" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif/lossy-page1-330px-Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif/lossy-page1-440px-Angelo_da_Chivasso%2C_Summa_Angelica%2C_1511_-_009.tif.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2160" data-file-height="3064" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Angelo_da_Chivasso,_Summa_Angelica,_1511_-_009.tif" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Summa Angelica</i>, 1511 (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Fondazione_Mansutti&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fondazione Mansutti (page does not exist)">Fondazione Mansutti</a>, Milano).</div></div></div>
<p>In theology he is considered a major adherent of <a href="/wiki/Scotism" title="Scotism">Scotism</a>. His works are given by Wadding in the latter's <i>"Scriptores Ordinis Minorum"</i>. The most noted of these is the <i>"Summa de Casibus Conscientiae"</i>, called after him the <i>"Summa Angelica"</i>. The basis of this work was a "Summa Confessorum" by John Rumsik, O. P., Lector of <a href="/wiki/Freiburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Freiburg">Freiburg</a> (d. 1314), which was then arranged alphabetically by <a href="/wiki/Bartholomew_of_San_Concordio" title="Bartholomew of San Concordio">Bartholomew of San Concordio</a> who also added material on <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>   The first edition of di Chivasso's "Summa Angelica" appeared in the year 1486,<sup id="cite_ref-ISTC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ISTC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and from that year to the year 1520 it went through 31 editions, 25 of which are preserved in the Royal Library at <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>.
</p><p>The "Summa" is divided into 659 articles arranged in alphabetical order and forming what would now be called a dictionary of moral theology. The most important of these articles is the one entitled <i>"Interrogationes in Confessione"</i>. It serves, in a way, as a dictionary of moral theology and was found very useful for confessors.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Judging the character of the work of Bl. Angelo as a theologian from this, his most important contribution to moral theology, one is impressed with the gravity and fairness that characterized his opinions throughout. The "Summa" is a valuable guide in matters of conscience and approaches closely, in the treatment of the various articles, to <a href="/wiki/Casuistry" title="Casuistry">casuistic theology</a> as this science is now understood, hence the title of the work, <i>"Summa de Casibus Conscientiae"</i>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Martin_Luther" title="Martin Luther">Martin Luther</a> considered it a symbol of Catholic orthodoxy and had it <a href="/wiki/Book_burning" title="Book burning">publicly burned</a> in the public square outside <a href="/wiki/Wittenberg" title="Wittenberg">Wittenberg</a>'s Elster Gate on December 10, 1520 together with the Bull of Excommunication <a href="/wiki/Exsurge_Domine" title="Exsurge Domine">Exsurge Domine</a>, the Corpus of Canon Law, and <a href="/wiki/Johann_Eck" title="Johann Eck">Johann Eck</a>'s <i>"Chrysopassus"</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolognini_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolognini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelo_Carletti_di_Chivasso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On April 14, 1753, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> beatified Angelo Carletti,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> giving official  approval to the cult that had for long been paid to Angelo, especially by the people of <a href="/wiki/Chivasso" title="Chivasso">Chivasso</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cuneo" title="Cuneo">Coni</a>. The latter chose him as their special patron. His feast is kept on 12 April. He is celebrated in his native Chivasso, with an old country fair each year at the end of August.
</p>
