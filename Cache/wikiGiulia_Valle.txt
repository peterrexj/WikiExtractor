<p><b>Giulia Valle</b> (26 June 1847 – 18 December 1916) was an <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and a professed member of the Sisters of Charity of Saint Joan Antida Thouret; she later assumed the religious name of "<b>Nemesia</b>" upon becoming a professed nun. Following her profession, she became an educator in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> and the surrounding areas and was known for her careful attention to people's individual educational needs.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valle died of <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> in 1916 and was beatified on 25 April 2004.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giulia_Valle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Giulia Valle was born in <a href="/wiki/Aosta" title="Aosta">Aosta</a> on 26 June 1847 to Anselmo Valle and Cristina Dalbar; she was the oldest sibling of Vincenzo however her parents had lost two sons before her birth.<sup id="cite_ref-SOC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Valle was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> hours after her birth in the Church of Saint Orso in the names of Maddalena Teresa Giulia. She worked in her parents' milliner shop during her childhood.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her mother died when she was four in 1851 and she was sent to live with paternal relatives in Aosta (her grandfather and an unmarried aunt) and then with maternal relations in <a href="/wiki/Donnas,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Donnas, Italy">Donnas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SOC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Valle prepared for the sacraments at the home of a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who was a close friend of her relatives. She was sent to France at the age of eleven in 1858 where she studied in <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a> with the Sisters of Charity of Saint Joan Antida Thouret where she learnt to master the French language and the <a href="/wiki/Piano" title="Piano">piano</a>. Valle also studied the texts of <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">Francis de Sales</a>; she finished and left in 1863 to return to her father.<sup id="cite_ref-SOC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her father later remarried and moved to <a href="/wiki/Pont-Saint-Martin,_Aosta_Valley" title="Pont-Saint-Martin, Aosta Valley">Pont-Saint-Martin</a> and Valle – not welcome in their home due to issues with her father's new wife – went back to her relations. However relations with them became strained over time and even led to the estrangement between Valle and her brother.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valle felt a strong call to religious life and when her father had begun to arrange a marriage for her it forced her hand. As a result of this she decided once and for all that she would go down the path of a religious and so began her novitiate at the Convent of Santa Margherita in <a href="/wiki/Vercelli" title="Vercelli">Vercelli</a> on 8 September 1866; her father accompanied her there.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Valle obtained her teaching qualification on 29 September 1867 and made her vows at that same time; she received her master's degree in November.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She assumed the religious name of "Nemesia" upon her solemn profession on 15 October 1873. After her profession, she was sent to the Institute of Saint Vincent in 1866 at <a href="/wiki/Tortona" title="Tortona">Tortona</a> and taught general studies to children and the French language at the high school level.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She also worked in the local <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a> and was made the Superior of her house in 1887. As a nun she continued to remind herself: "Concentrate on the one goal: God alone!"<sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She aided <a href="/wiki/Luigi_Orione" title="Luigi Orione">Luigi Orione</a> and also provided shelter to <a href="/wiki/Teresa_Grillo_Michel" title="Teresa Grillo Michel">Teresa Grillo Michel</a> and was later assigned as the Mistress of Novices at <a href="/wiki/Borgaro_Torinese" title="Borgaro Torinese">Borgaro Torinese</a> and spent the remainder of her life there where she led 500 new sisters into religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SOC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> epidemic of 1890 she opened the doors of the institute in order to tend to the ill; in 1901 she recovered from a slight illness.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On the morning of 10 May 1903 she left a message prior to her departure from Tortona: "I am leaving happily ... I entrust to <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Our Lady</a> ... I shall follow you in every moment of the day". She left at 4:00 am for Borgaro Torinese for her new task.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BC_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the autumn of 1916 her health took a sharp pitfall and declined to the point where on 11 December she contracted severe <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a>. She died a week later on 18 December 1916 at 9:10 pm; the room filled with a sweet perfume of roses and violets when she died.<sup id="cite_ref-SOC_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was buried in the church of the institute in Borgario Torinese.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giulia_Valle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process commenced with an archdiocesan process in Turin after the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> granted their approval to the initiation of the cause on 31 July 1981. The beginning of the process granted her the posthumous title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> – the first stage in the process – and saw the completion of two processes that were tasked with investigating her life and works.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Upon the completion of both processes the postulation compiled the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> – a large dossier consisting of biographical details attesting to the pros of her cause – in 1994 and submitted it to Rome for their own investigation. A historical commission was called in to ensure that there would be no barriers to the cause proceeding and granted the cause its approval. This culminated on 5 July 2002 when <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed her to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> upon acknowledging that Valle had indeed lived a model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The miracle needed for her beatification was investigated in its diocese of origin and received full ratification from Rome in 1997 before proceeding to a set of other stages needed for the investigation. The pope himself approved the healing as a miracle on 20 December 2003 and beatified Valle on 25 April 2004.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> assigned to the cause is Sister Anna Antida Casolino.
</p>
