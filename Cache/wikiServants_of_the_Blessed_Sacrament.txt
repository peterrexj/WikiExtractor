<p>The <b>Servants of the Blessed Sacrament</b> (in latin:  <i>Societatis Ancillarum a Sanctissimo Sacramento</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Contemplative" class="mw-redirect" title="Contemplative">contemplative</a>, but not <a href="/wiki/Cloistered" class="mw-redirect" title="Cloistered">cloistered</a>, congregation of sisters with a focus on <a href="/wiki/Eucharistic_adoration" title="Eucharistic adoration">Eucharistic adoration</a>.

<p>The Servants' religious congregation is the women's branch of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament">Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament</a>, founded by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Julian_Eymard" title="Peter Julian Eymard">Peter Julian Eymard</a> over one hundred forty years ago.<sup id="cite_ref-connors_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-connors-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marguerite Guillot was born December 4, 1815 in <a href="/wiki/Chasselay,_Rh%C3%B4ne" title="Chasselay, Rhône">Chasselay, Rhône</a>, the youngest child of Jean and Jeanne Boin Guillot. In February 1840, she recovered from a bout of typhoid fever and the family made a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Notre-Dame_de_Fourvi%C3%A8re" title="Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière">Our Lady of Fourviere</a> in Lyons in thanksgiving. In December 1845 she joined the Third Order of Mary, and in 1853 became the directress. Eymard was the group's spiritual director.<sup id="cite_ref-phil_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-phil-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Guillot collaborated with Eymard and became the first <a href="/wiki/Superior_general" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior general">superior general</a> for the congregation, which was approved by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IX">Pius IX</a> in 1871.<sup id="cite_ref-maine_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maine-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Congregation of the Servants of the Blessed Sacrament marks July 31, 1859 as the date of foundation. Guillot founded houses in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>. She died July 7, 1885.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Congregation">Congregation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servants_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Congregation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>From France, the Congregation spread throughout the world. In 1903 a house was opened in Canada, in Brazil in 1912, and in Australia in 1950.<sup id="cite_ref-sherbrooke_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sherbrooke-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The first house in the United States was established in <a href="/wiki/Waterville,_Maine" title="Waterville, Maine">Waterville, Maine</a>, in April 1947, by six sisters from the Canadian foundation. In June 1956 a second American foundation was established in Pueblo, Colorado.<sup id="cite_ref-connors_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-connors-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The sisters maintain continuous adoration of the Blessed Sacrament in their communities.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 2009, due to lack of vocations, the American sisters asked the help of the sisters in the Philippines so the Congregation could continue its Eucharistic presence and mission in Pueblo. Four sisters came.<sup id="cite_ref-todays_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-todays-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In January 1996, a tragedy occurred at the Sisters' convent, located in <a href="/wiki/Waterville,_Maine" title="Waterville, Maine">Waterville, Maine</a>. A mentally ill man broke into the convent in the middle of a winter storm, and attacked four of the nine Sisters who lived there. Two, including the Mother Superior were killed, while another was so severely injured that she had to be placed in a nursing home. The nuns who survived the attack have since died. The individual was committed to what was then Augusta Mental Health Institute, now Riverview Psychiatric Hospital, but in June 2013 was granted unsupervised time in the community, despite objections from prosecutors.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Australian foundation was established in 1950, when six Canadian sisters and one Australian who had taken her noviciate in Quebec arrived in <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a>. This community made its income by producing the altar breads for the parishes of Melbourne and parts of regional Victoria. These sisters practiced continuous adoration of the Blessed Sacrament in their chapel at <a href="/wiki/Armadale,_Victoria" title="Armadale, Victoria">Armadale</a>, a suburb of Melbourne, and lived by a strict regimen of silence, prayer and work. They were allowed only half an hour of reading per day, and education was not emphasised. All of this changed soon after <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Vatican II</a>, when Catholic religious were asked to review and update their Rule of Life and adapt it to the contemporary world. A group of sisters used hammers and saws to remove the grille that separated them from their visitors in the convent's parlour, dismantling the enclosure that had separated them from the broader community for decades. The nuns pursued education in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and other disciplines, and practiced a renewed <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a> at their chapel. In 1980, three of the sisters moved to <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a> to establish a convent in Newtown, where they worked closely with <a href="/wiki/Fr_Ted_Kennedy" class="mw-redirect" title="Fr Ted Kennedy">Fr Ted Kennedy</a> and the local Indigenous community of <a href="/wiki/Redfern,_New_South_Wales" title="Redfern, New South Wales">Redfern</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There are now just two sisters remaining in the Australian community. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Apostolate">Apostolate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servants_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Apostolate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg/220px-Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg/330px-Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg/440px-Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5152" data-file-height="3864" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Servantes_du_Tr%C3%A8s-Saint-Sacrement_Paris.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sisters in their chapel of Paris.</div></div></div>
<p>Their houses are located in France, Canada, Brazil, Holland, the United States, Australia, Italy, the Philippines, Vietnam and Congo. The congregation's generalate is in Sherbrooke, Quebec.<sup id="cite_ref-maine_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maine-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The congregation is centered on the person of Christ in the Eucharistic Mystery and dedicated to his love and glory. The celebration of the Eucharist, the Liturgy of the Hours and Eucharistic adoration give form to their mission of prayer. Eucharistic Adoration has the priority of their time and attention.<sup id="cite_ref-todays_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-todays-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, they also engage in other ministries, such as serving as extraordinary Eucharistic ministers to the sick and elderly.<sup id="cite_ref-phil_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-phil-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The patroness of the congregation is Mary, under the title of Our Lady of the Blessed Sacrament.<sup id="cite_ref-sherbrooke_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sherbrooke-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
