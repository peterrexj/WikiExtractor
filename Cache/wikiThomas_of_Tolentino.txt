<p><b>Thomas of Tolentino</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Tommaso di</i><sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or <i><span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">da Tolentino</i></span></i>;<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1255</span><span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>– 8 April 1321) was a <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">medieval</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_missionary" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian missionary">missionary</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyred</a> with his three companions in <a href="/wiki/Thane" title="Thane">Thane</a>, <a href="/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate" title="Delhi Sultanate">India</a>, for "<a href="/wiki/Blasphemy_in_Islam" class="mw-redirect" title="Blasphemy in Islam">blaspheming</a>" <a href="/wiki/Muhammad" title="Muhammad">Muhammad</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were removed to <a href="/wiki/Quanzhou" title="Quanzhou">Quanzhou</a>, <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Tolentino" title="Tolentino">Tolentino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Odoric_of_Pordenone" title="Odoric of Pordenone">Odoric of Pordenone</a>. He is now venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">saint</a><sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, with his <a href="/wiki/Saint%27s_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint&#39;s day">feast day</a> on April 9.
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span><span class="anchor" id="History"></span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_of_Tolentino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas was born in <a href="/wiki/Tolentino" title="Tolentino">Tolentino</a><sup id="cite_ref-CatholicOrg_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CatholicOrg-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a><sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> within the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> around 1250 to 1260.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Becoming a <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> early in life, he developed a reputation for his strict adherence to its <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_the_Franciscan_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of the Franciscan Order">rule</a>, particularly concerning his <a href="/wiki/Vow_of_poverty" class="mw-redirect" title="Vow of poverty">vow of poverty</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A fellow of St<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span><a href="/wiki/Nicholas_of_Tolentino" title="Nicholas of Tolentino">Nicholas of Tolentino</a><sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and one of <a href="/wiki/Angelo_da_Clareno" title="Angelo da Clareno">Angelo da Clareno</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Franciscans" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Franciscans">Spiritual Franciscans</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Thomas was jailed twice for his excessive condemnation of luxury.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After being released through the intervention of <a href="/wiki/Raymond_Godefroy" class="mw-redirect" title="Raymond Godefroy">Raymond Godefroy</a>, a new <a href="/wiki/Minister_General_(Franciscan)" title="Minister General (Franciscan)">minister general</a> who sympathized with the Spiritualists, Thomas traveled with <a href="/wiki/Angelo_da_Clareno" title="Angelo da Clareno">Angelo da Clareno</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marco_da_Montelupone" class="mw-redirect" title="Marco da Montelupone">Marco da Montelupone</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pietro_da_Macerata" title="Pietro da Macerata">Pietro da Macerata</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Angelo_da_Tolentino" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelo da Tolentino">Angelo da Tolentino</a><sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> to <a href="/wiki/Christian_mission" title="Christian mission">mission</a> in <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia" title="Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia">Lesser Armenia</a> in 1289. In 1291, its <a href="/wiki/King_Haython_II" class="mw-redirect" title="King Haython II">King Haython&#160;II</a> directed him to return to the courts of <a href="/wiki/Papal_Court" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Court">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Paris</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">London</a> to seek help against his <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Muslim</a> foes.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His efforts to raise a new <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">crusade</a> were unsuccessful and he returned east,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> departing a second time to gather more missionaries. Returning with twelve companions in 1302,<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he worked in Armenia and <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a>. He debated Armenian Christians he considered heretics at <a href="/wiki/Sis_(ancient_city)" title="Sis (ancient city)">Sis</a> in 1305<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and participated in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Sis&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Sis (page does not exist)">Council of Sis</a> that notionally reunited the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Church">Armenian</a> and Roman Catholic churches in 1307.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While in Persia, two letters dated 1305 and 1306 arrived from <a href="/wiki/John_of_Montecorvino" title="John of Montecorvino">John of Montecorvino</a>, the Franciscan missionary to <a href="/wiki/Yuan_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Yuan Empire">China</a>, and Thomas again traveled to Europe, delivering the correspondence to Rome in 1307. While there, he addressed a public <a href="/wiki/Papal_consistory" title="Papal consistory">consistory</a> of the pope and cardinals, praising John's work in China and asking for assistance in developing his mission.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He also discussed the matter with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_V" title="Pope Clement V">Clement&#160;V</a> at <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> in 1308, after which an ecclesiastical hierarchy was established for the Roman Catholics in China.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The pontiff named John <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Beijing" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Beijing">archbishop of Khanbaliq</a> (now within modern <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>) and seven Franciscan bishops and many friars were sent to join him. Only three of the bishops and a few friars, however, successfully completed the journey. Thomas seems to have then travelled a fourth time to Armenia and Persia.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a gap until 1320, during which Thomas may have labored in India or China.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1320, Thomas left from <a href="/wiki/Hormuz,_Iran" title="Hormuz, Iran">Hormuz</a> with his fellow Franciscans Bl. <a href="/wiki/James_of_Padua" title="James of Padua">James of Padua</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Siena_(died_1321)" title="Peter of Siena (died 1321)">Peter of Siena</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Jordan_of_Severac" class="mw-redirect" title="Jordan of Severac">Jordan of Severac</a>, and the layman <a href="/wiki/Demetrio_da_Tifliz" class="mw-redirect" title="Demetrio da Tifliz">Demetrio da Tifliz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Georgians" title="Georgians">Georgian</a> or <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenian</a>, Demetrius was proficient at languages and served as the group's interpreter.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A storm forced the party to land at <a href="/wiki/Thane,_India" class="mw-redirect" title="Thane, India">Thane</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Salsette_Island" title="Salsette Island">Salsette Island</a><sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> near <a href="/wiki/Mumbai" title="Mumbai">Mumbai</a> in <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> en route and were greeted by local Christians of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_East" title="Church of the East">Church of the East</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> Jordan left them to preach at <a href="/wiki/Bharuch" title="Bharuch">Bharuch</a>, reaching <a href="/wiki/Nala_Sopara" class="mw-redirect" title="Nala Sopara">Sopara</a> (see <a href="/wiki/Sopara" class="mw-redirect" title="Sopara">Sopara in history</a>) before he heard Demetrius and the Franciscans had been arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-Cunha174_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunha174-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The family with whom they were staying had fallen into a quarrel and the husband had beaten his wife. When she went to the <a href="/wiki/Qadi" title="Qadi">qadi</a> to report this abuse, she had mentioned the four clerics as witnesses and they were called before him. Thomas, James, and Demetrius had gone to the court while Peter remained behind to look after their things. Having begun a discussion of religion, the qadi had asked them their opinion of <a href="/wiki/Muhammad" title="Muhammad">Muhammad</a> and Thomas replied bluntly that he was "the <a href="/wiki/Son_of_perdition" title="Son of perdition">son of perdition</a> and had his place in <a href="/wiki/Christian_Hell" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian Hell">Hell</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">Devil</a> his father". At this, the Muslims around the court called for their death for <a href="/wiki/Blasphemy" title="Blasphemy">blasphemy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cunha176_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunha176-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Some accounts claim they were <a href="/wiki/Scourged" class="mw-redirect" title="Scourged">scourged</a> and <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> before their execution<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheading</a> on April 8, 1321.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> Peter was killed three days later.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_of_Tolentino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The local Christians may have buried Thomas and his companions<sup id="cite_ref-Cunha176_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunha176-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but <a href="/wiki/Jordanus_Catalani" class="mw-redirect" title="Jordanus Catalani">Jordanus Catalani</a>, arriving too late to save them, removed their bodies to the church at Supera with the help of a <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa" title="Republic of Genoa">Genovese</a> youth.<sup id="cite_ref-Cunha174_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunha174-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1323<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> or 1326,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Odoric_of_Pordenone" title="Odoric of Pordenone">Odoric of Pordenone</a> passed through the region. Having learned about Thomas and his companions, he took their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> with him to <a href="/wiki/Quanzhou" title="Quanzhou">Quanzhou</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fujian" title="Fujian">Fujian</a>. Thomas's <a href="/wiki/Skull" title="Skull">skull</a> he took with him to <a href="/wiki/Khanbaliq" title="Khanbaliq">Khanbaliq</a> and thence back to Europe, where he bestowed it on the Franciscan chapter in Tolentino<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in 1330.<sup id="cite_ref-CatholicSaints_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CatholicSaints-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It was later moved to <a href="/wiki/Tolentino_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Tolentino Cathedral">the town's cathedral</a><sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> by a <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisan</a> merchant in the late 14th century, who erected a chapel there in the martyr's honor with the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Boniface&#160;IX</a>. It is now kept in the central cathedral in a silver bust.<sup id="cite_ref-Nardi_2-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nardi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thomas and his companions have been unofficially reckoned <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> since the 14th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Jordan claimed to have <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miraculously</a> healed the <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a> of his Genovese companion with one of Thomas's teeth.<sup id="cite_ref-Cunha174_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunha174-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Thomas's cult was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pius&#160;VII</a> in 1809 and again by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Leo&#160;XIII</a> in 1894.<sup id="cite_ref-CatholicSaints_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CatholicSaints-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/List_of_saints" title="List of saints">saint</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> sometimes together with his companions<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_4-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> as the <b>Four<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>Martyrs<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>of<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>Thane</b>, on April 9.<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
