<p><b>Sir Arthur William Fadden</b> <span style="font-size:85%;"><span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_Grand_Cross_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George">GCMG</a></span></span> (13 April 1894&#160;&#8211;&#32;21 April 1973) was an Australian politician who was the 13th <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Australia">prime minister of Australia</a>, from 29 August to 7 October 1941. He was the leader of the <a href="/wiki/National_Party_of_Australia" title="National Party of Australia">Country Party</a> from 1940 to 1958 and also was <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_Australia" title="Treasurer of Australia">federal treasurer</a> for nearly ten years (1940–1941, 1949–1958).
</p><p>Fadden was born in <a href="/wiki/Ingham,_Queensland" title="Ingham, Queensland">Ingham, Queensland</a>, to Irish immigrant parents. He was raised in <a href="/wiki/Walkerston,_Queensland" title="Walkerston, Queensland">Walkerston</a>, and left school at the age of 15. He was appointed town clerk of <a href="/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland" title="Mackay, Queensland">Mackay</a> in 1916, but following the <a href="/wiki/1918_Mackay_cyclone" class="mw-redirect" title="1918 Mackay cyclone">1918 cyclone</a> moved to <a href="/wiki/Townsville" title="Townsville">Townsville</a> and opened an accountancy firm. He was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Townsville_City_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Townsville City Council">Townsville City Council</a> in 1930, and <a href="/wiki/1932_Queensland_state_election" title="1932 Queensland state election">in 1932</a> was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Assembly" class="mw-redirect" title="Queensland Legislative Assembly">Queensland Legislative Assembly</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Country_and_Progressive_National_Party" title="Country and Progressive National Party">Country and Progressive National Party</a>. Fadden lost his seat <a href="/wiki/1935_Queensland_state_election" title="1935 Queensland state election">in 1935</a>, but the following year won <a href="/wiki/1936_Darling_Downs_by-election" title="1936 Darling Downs by-election">a by-election</a> to the federal <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Darling_Downs" title="Division of Darling Downs">Division of Darling Downs</a>.
</p><p>In March 1940, Fadden was named a <a href="/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio" title="Minister without portfolio">minister without portfolio</a> in the government of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Menzies" title="Robert Menzies">Robert Menzies</a>, who led the <a href="/wiki/United_Australia_Party" title="United Australia Party">United Australia Party</a> in <a href="/wiki/Coalition_(Australia)" title="Coalition (Australia)">a coalition</a> with the Country Party. A few months later, following the deaths of three senior ministers in <a href="/wiki/1940_Canberra_air_disaster" title="1940 Canberra air disaster">an air crash</a>, he took over as <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Air_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Air (Australia)">Minister for Air</a> and <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Infrastructure_and_Regional_Development_(Australia)#Ministers_for_Aviation" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Infrastructure and Regional Development (Australia)">Minister for Civil Aviation</a>. In October 1940, Fadden was elected acting leader of the Country Party as a compromise candidate, following a deadlocked leadership vote between <a href="/wiki/Earle_Page" title="Earle Page">Earle Page</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_McEwen" title="John McEwen">John McEwen</a>. He became the <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">deputy prime minister</a> and was promoted by Menzies to treasurer.
</p><p>Fadden was <a href="/wiki/Acting_prime_minister" title="Acting prime minister">acting prime minister</a> for four months early in 1941 (while Menzies was away in Europe), and became popular for his conciliatory manner. He became the official leader of the Country Party following a ballot in March 1941. In August 1941, Menzies resigned as prime minister after losing the confidence of his ministry. Fadden was elected leader of the UAP–Country coalition in his place, and consequently became prime minister. However, he held office for just 39 days before being replaced by <a href="/wiki/John_Curtin" title="John Curtin">John Curtin</a>, whose <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party" title="Australian Labor Party">Labor Party</a> had successfully moved a <a href="/wiki/Motion_of_no_confidence" title="Motion of no confidence">motion of no confidence</a>. After losing the prime ministership, Fadden continued on as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">leader of the opposition</a> for two more years. In that capacity, he eventually resigned in favour of Menzies following the coalition's massive defeat at the <a href="/wiki/1943_Australian_federal_election" title="1943 Australian federal election">1943 election</a>.
</p><p>When Menzies returned as prime minister in 1949, Fadden became treasurer for a second time, holding office until his retirement from politics in 1958. Only <a href="/wiki/Peter_Costello" title="Peter Costello">Peter Costello</a> has served in the position for longer. Fadden enjoyed one of the most rapid rises in Australian political history, moving from private citizen to the prime ministership in just 11 years. He was the first prime minister born in Queensland, and the first and only member of the Country Party to become prime minister with his own mandate (rather than just serving as a caretaker after the death of a predecessor).
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood">Childhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Fadden was born in <a href="/wiki/Ingham,_Queensland" title="Ingham, Queensland">Ingham</a>, <a href="/wiki/Queensland" title="Queensland">Queensland</a>, on 13 April 1894. He was the eldest of ten children – seven sons and three daughters – born to Annie (née Moorhead) and Richard John Fadden. His parents were both born in Ireland, his mother in <a href="/wiki/County_Tyrone" title="County Tyrone">County Tyrone</a> and his father in <a href="/wiki/County_Galway" title="County Galway">County Galway</a>. Fadden moved to <a href="/wiki/Walkerston,_Queensland" title="Walkerston, Queensland">Walkerston</a> at a young age, where his father was officer-in-charge of the local police station.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He had a "typical country childhood", but suffered the deaths of three of his younger siblings in separate accidents. Fadden received his only formal education at the Walkerston State School, except for a brief period at <a href="/wiki/Te_Kowai,_Queensland" title="Te Kowai, Queensland">Te Kowai</a> while his usual school was being renovated. His first jobs included collecting <a href="/wiki/Dermolepida_albohirtum" title="Dermolepida albohirtum">cane beetles</a> and performing <a href="/wiki/Sound_effects" class="mw-redirect" title="Sound effects">sound effects</a> at the local cinema. Fadden left school at the age of 15 and began working as a "billy boy" (odd-job man) on a cane-cutting gang at <a href="/wiki/Pleystowe,_Queensland" title="Pleystowe, Queensland">Pleystowe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He later secured an indoor job as an office boy at the <a href="/wiki/Pleystowe_Sugar_Mill" title="Pleystowe Sugar Mill">Pleystowe Sugar Mill</a>, where his colleagues include two future <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party" title="Australian Labor Party">Labor</a> MPs – <a href="/wiki/Maurice_Hynes" title="Maurice Hynes">Maurice Hynes</a> and <a href="/wiki/George_Martens" title="George Martens">George Martens</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In his spare time, he developed an interest in the theatre, both as a performer and treasurer of the local company.<sup id="cite_ref-p4_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p4-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Professional_career">Professional career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Professional career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In April 1913, Fadden moved to <a href="/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland" title="Mackay, Queensland">Mackay</a> to become assistant <a href="/wiki/Town_clerk" class="mw-redirect" title="Town clerk">town clerk</a> of the <a href="/wiki/City_of_Mackay" title="City of Mackay">Mackay Town Council</a>. He defeated 56 other applicants for the position.<sup id="cite_ref-p4_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p4-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1916, his superior, Frederick Morley, was dismissed as town clerk over allegations of theft, which Fadden himself had uncovered. Morley eventually received a two-year jail term, and Fadden was promoted in his place, again defeating more than 50 other applicants; he was reputedly the "youngest town clerk in Australia". He had attempted to enlist in the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Army" title="Australian Army">Australian Army</a> the previous year, but was rejected on health grounds. In 1918, Fadden served on the committee of the relief fund for the <a href="/wiki/1918_Mackay_cyclone" class="mw-redirect" title="1918 Mackay cyclone">Mackay cyclone</a>, which devastated the town and killed thirty people.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, he resigned as town clerk in September of that year and moved to <a href="/wiki/Townsville" title="Townsville">Townsville</a> (the largest settlement in <a href="/wiki/North_Queensland" title="North Queensland">North Queensland</a>), where he established his own accountancy firm.<sup id="cite_ref-p6_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p6-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He had qualified as an accountant through a correspondence course from a school in <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-p4_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p4-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to his memoirs, Fadden initially struggled to make ends meet as an accountant, and considered relocating to <a href="/wiki/Brisbane" title="Brisbane">Brisbane</a>. However, he eventually found an unused loophole in the tax code that allowed him to gain a competitive advantage. His business prospered thereafter, and he was able to take on partners and opened a second office in Brisbane.<sup id="cite_ref-p6_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-p6-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1930, Fadden was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Townsville_City_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Townsville City Council">Townsville City Council</a> as part of a non-partisan grouping calling themselves the "serviceable six". He developed a feud with the city's chief engineer, Sidney Roberts, whom he publicly criticised for using coal from <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales" title="New South Wales">New South Wales</a> instead of from the local <a href="/wiki/Bowen_Basin" title="Bowen Basin">Bowen Basin</a> mines. He was once again able to use his auditing skills to his advantage, getting Roberts fired for inconsistencies in his balance sheets. The resulting publicity was a springboard for his political career.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career">Early political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="State_politics">State politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: State politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the <a href="/wiki/1932_Queensland_state_election" title="1932 Queensland state election">1932 Queensland state election</a>, Fadden was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Assembly" class="mw-redirect" title="Queensland Legislative Assembly">Queensland Legislative Assembly</a> as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Country_and_Progressive_National_Party" title="Country and Progressive National Party">Country and Progressive National Party</a> (CPNP). Aided by a favourable redistribution, he won the seat of <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Kennedy" title="Electoral district of Kennedy">Kennedy</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party" title="Australian Labor Party">Australian Labor Party</a> by just 62 votes. He was the only candidate from his party to win a seat from Labor, which won <a href="/wiki/Majority_government" title="Majority government">majority government</a> on a swing of almost 10 points.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In parliament, Fadden came to notice as a critic of the new government's financial operations. He accused the government of lacking transparency and accountability, particularly in its use of trust funds which he said had been used to cover up revenue deficiencies. His speeches impressed both his party and political correspondents, and he was asked to write a series of articles for <i><a href="/wiki/The_Courier-Mail" title="The Courier-Mail">The Courier-Mail</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fadden was the CPNP's lead speaker in the 1934 budget debates, effectively making him the chief financial spokesman for the opposition.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In the lead-up to the <a href="/wiki/1935_Queensland_state_election" title="1935 Queensland state election">1935 state election</a>, a redistribution turned Kennedy into a safe Labor seat. It was alleged by the opposition that the government had specifically targeted Fadden's seat in order to remove him as a political threat. Faced with certain defeat, he chose to run in the neighbouring seat of <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Mirani" title="Electoral district of Mirani">Mirani</a>. However, he lost to Labor's <a href="/wiki/Ted_Walsh_(politician)" title="Ted Walsh (politician)">Ted Walsh</a> by 224 votes, as the government won a landslide victory. In his memoirs, he accused Labor of having <a href="/wiki/Pork_barrel" title="Pork barrel">pork-barreled</a> Mirani in the lead-up to the election to ensure his defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Move_to_federal_politics">Move to federal politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Move to federal politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg/170px-Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="238" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg/255px-Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg/340px-Arthur_Fadden_1938.jpg 2x" data-file-width="753" data-file-height="1055" /></a><figcaption>Fadden in 1938</figcaption></figure>
<p>After losing his seat in state parliament, Fadden moved to Brisbane,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and initially returned to his accounting practice.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In early 1936, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Country_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Queensland Country Party">Queensland Country Party</a>, which had split from the CPNP in order to align with the federal <a href="/wiki/National_Party_of_Australia" title="National Party of Australia">Country Party</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Later that year, he was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives" title="Australian House of Representatives">Australian House of Representatives</a> at <a href="/wiki/1936_Darling_Downs_by-election" title="1936 Darling Downs by-election">a by-election</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Darling_Downs" title="Division of Darling Downs">Division of Darling Downs</a>. It was caused by the death of the previous member, <a href="/wiki/Littleton_Groom" title="Littleton Groom">Littleton Groom</a>, who was a member of the <a href="/wiki/United_Australia_Party" title="United Australia Party">United Australia Party</a> (UAP). Fadden, who had no previous connection with the area, was the first member of his party to contest the seat; the UAP suffered a negative swing of over 40 points.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He consolidated his hold on the seat at the <a href="/wiki/1937_Australian_federal_election" title="1937 Australian federal election">1937 federal election</a>, held less than a year later.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Menzies_Government">Menzies Government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Menzies Government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In April 1939, Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Lyons" title="Joseph Lyons">Joseph Lyons</a> died in office and was replaced by Country Party leader <a href="/wiki/Earle_Page" title="Earle Page">Earle Page</a> on an interim basis. When <a href="/wiki/Robert_Menzies" title="Robert Menzies">Robert Menzies</a> was elected by the <a href="/wiki/United_Australia_Party" title="United Australia Party">United Australia Party</a> (UAP) as Lyons' replacement, Page gave a speech accusing Menzies of disloyalty and questioning his record of military service. As a result of Page's attacks, which they deemed unfair, Fadden and <a href="/wiki/Bernard_Corser" title="Bernard Corser">Bernard Corser</a> resigned from the parliamentary Country Party to sit as "Independent Country" members; they were soon followed by <a href="/wiki/Oliver_Badman" title="Oliver Badman">Oliver Badman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Collins_(Australian_politician)" title="Thomas Collins (Australian politician)">Thomas Collins</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Fadden was generally seen as the leader of the group. When Page was forced out of the leadership in September, they attempted to rejoin the party, but the remaining members opted – by a single vote – not to re-admit them. <a href="/wiki/Archie_Cameron" title="Archie Cameron">Archie Cameron</a>, generally seen as Page's ally, was elected as the party's new leader. Fadden did not regard Cameron favourably, in one debate stating: "I take this opportunity to declare without the slightest degree of reservation that the honourable gentleman is not my leader".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> When asked in another debate if he were still a member of the Country Party, he replied "no, I am not, thank goodness, as it is now constituted and under its present leadership". In early November, however, Cameron invited the four breakaways to rejoin the party in the interests of unity, which they accepted.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Country Party's <a href="/wiki/Coalition_(Australia)" title="Coalition (Australia)">coalition</a> with the UAP had lapsed following Menzies' elevation to the prime ministership. In March 1940, Menzies and Cameron agreed to resume the coalition, providing an opportunity for five Country Party members to be added to the ministry. Cameron somewhat unexpectedly nominated Fadden as a Country Party representative, and he was appointed as an <a href="/wiki/Assistant_minister" class="mw-redirect" title="Assistant minister">assistant minister</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_Australia" title="Treasurer of Australia">Treasurer of Australia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Percy_Spender" title="Percy Spender">Percy Spender</a>) and the <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Supply_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Supply (Australia)">Minister for Supply and Development</a> (<a href="/wiki/Frederick_Stewart_(Australian_politician)" title="Frederick Stewart (Australian politician)">Frederick Stewart</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In August 1940, Fadden narrowly escaped being killed in the <a href="/wiki/1940_Canberra_air_disaster" title="1940 Canberra air disaster">Canberra air disaster</a>, which claimed the lives of three government ministers and the <a href="/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chief of the General Staff (Australia)">Chief of the General Staff</a>. He was scheduled to be aboard the flight, which was transporting the ministers back to Canberra after a cabinet meeting in Melbourne, but instead took an overnight train. He traded places with Richard Elford (<a href="/wiki/James_Fairbairn" title="James Fairbairn">James Fairbairn</a>'s private secretary), who had wanted to stay in Melbourne to celebrate a wedding anniversary; both Elford and Fairbairn were among those killed. After the crash, Fadden replaced Fairbairn as <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Air_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Air (Australia)">Minister for Air</a> and <a href="/wiki/Department_of_Civil_Aviation_(Australia)" title="Department of Civil Aviation (Australia)">Minister for Civil Aviation</a>; he also continued as assistant minister to the Treasurer.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/1940_Australian_federal_election" title="1940 Australian federal election">1940 Australian federal election</a> resulted in a <a href="/wiki/Hung_parliament" title="Hung parliament">hung parliament</a>. The UAP–Country coalition was able to remain in power with the support of two independent MPs, <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Wilson_(Australian_politician)" title="Alexander Wilson (Australian politician)">Alexander Wilson</a> and <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Coles" title="Arthur Coles">Arthur Coles</a>. The Country Party lost three seats to the Labor Party, and on 16 October the parliamentary party voted to remove Archie Cameron as leader. <a href="/wiki/John_McEwen" title="John McEwen">John McEwen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Earle_Page" title="Earle Page">Earle Page</a> both nominated for the leadership, and Fadden intended to nominate for the deputy leadership. McEwen and Page were tied at eight votes each after three separate ballots. During a break for dinner, Fadden was asked to become interim leader as a compromise between the two candidates, with the intention that another leadership ballot would be held in a few months. He was then elected unopposed as deputy leader and thus acting leader of the party. According to his biographer Tracey Arklay, "Fadden was selected because the majority in the party room considered that he was the man most likely to be able to broker deals and negotiate with Menzies and the UAP".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Edgar_George_Holt" title="Edgar George Holt">Edgar Holt</a> believed Fadden's personality was a major factor – "he had no obvious ambitions and he suffered from no delusions of grandeur [...] he was amiable and gregarious" – but also thought that Page had allowed Fadden to assume the leadership in order to deny it to McEwen.<sup id="cite_ref-Arklay_2010,_p._107_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arklay_2010,_p._107-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As the acting leader of his party, Fadden became the <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">deputy prime minister</a> and joined the <a href="/wiki/Advisory_War_Council" title="Advisory War Council">Advisory War Council</a>. He was sworn in as Treasurer on 28 October 1940, succeeding <a href="/wiki/Percy_Spender" title="Percy Spender">Percy Spender</a>, and presented his first budget less than a month later on 21 November.<sup id="cite_ref-Arklay_2010,_p._107_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arklay_2010,_p._107-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The budget featured increased levels of spending due to the ongoing war, offset by significant increases in taxation – including a reduction in the tax-free threshold, increased company taxes, and a tax on undistributed profits. In presenting the budget, Fadden noted that it brought about "the heaviest financial imposts ever placed upon the people of Australia".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> It was highly unpopular among the general public, which up until that point had perceived the war to be still quite distant. The independent MPs contemplated voting with the opposition to reject the budget, but after negotiations and some amendments it was able to be passed, allowing the government to continue in power.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister_of_Australia">Prime Minister of Australia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Prime Minister of Australia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In August 1941 <a href="/wiki/Robert_Menzies" title="Robert Menzies">Robert Menzies</a> resigned as Prime Minister.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>  Although the non-Labor <a href="/wiki/Coalition_(Australia)" title="Coalition (Australia)">Coalition</a> had been in power for a decade, the UAP was so bereft of leadership that on 28 August a joint UAP-Country meeting chose Fadden as Coalition leader even though the Country Party was the smaller of the two non-Labor parties. Fadden was duly sworn in as Prime Minister the next day, and also remained Treasurer. He was the only member of the Country/National Party to serve as Prime Minister without an expectation of a short tenure (the other two Country/National Prime Ministers, Page and McEwen, were caretakers).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg/220px-Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="266" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg/330px-Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg/440px-Official_Portrait_of_Arthur_Fadden_by_William_Dargie.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2054" data-file-height="2480" /></a><figcaption>Parliament House portrait of Fadden by <a href="/wiki/William_Dargie" title="William Dargie">William Dargie</a>, 1947</figcaption></figure>
<p>Nevertheless, Fadden's term of office was troubled from the start. Even parliamentarians in his own party feared the worst. It was later reported that Fadden decided against moving into <a href="/wiki/The_Lodge_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="The Lodge (Australia)">The Lodge</a>, the official Prime Minister's residence in Canberra, after his predecessor as  Country Party leader, Cameron, crudely told him that he would "scarcely have enough time to wear a track from the backdoor to the shithouse before you’ll be out".<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 3 October, the two independents who had been keeping the Coalition in office for the last year, Coles and Wilson, voted against Fadden's budget. Coles and Wilson had been so disgusted with how Menzies had been treated that they refused to support the Coalition any longer. Due to this <a href="/wiki/Loss_of_supply" title="Loss of supply">loss of supply</a>, Fadden submitted his government's resignation to the Governor-General <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Hore-Ruthven,_1st_Earl_of_Gowrie" title="Alexander Hore-Ruthven, 1st Earl of Gowrie">Lord Gowrie</a> later the same day. This was the last occasion to date on which an Australian government was forced to resign after being defeated on the floor of the House of Representatives.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Fadden joked that he was like the Flood: he had "reigned for 40 days and 40 nights".
</p><p>The Governor-General was reluctant to call an election for a House that was barely a year old, especially considering that the war had recently been brought to the nation's doorstep with Japan's advances.  However, he would have been left with no other option if Labor leader <a href="/wiki/John_Curtin" title="John Curtin">John Curtin</a> did not have enough support to govern.  With this in mind, Gowrie summoned Coles and Wilson and obtained their assurances that they would support Curtin as Prime Minister and end the instability that had plagued the government since 1940. Coles and Wilson agreed to this, and Curtin was sworn in on 7 October.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Opposition">Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Leader_of_the_Opposition">Leader of the Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Leader of the Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="358" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg/440px-Australia_Needs_A_National_Government_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3722" data-file-height="6065" /></a><figcaption>Election advertisement used by the Coalition at the <a href="/wiki/1943_Australian_federal_election" title="1943 Australian federal election">1943 Australian federal election</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Following the fall of his ministry, a joint UAP-Country Party meeting endorsed Fadden as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">Leader of the Opposition</a>, even though the UAP was nominally the senior coalition partner. As a result, Menzies resigned as UAP leader. The UAP was so bereft of leadership that it was forced to elect 79-year-old former Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a> as its new leader. The Coalition sank into near-paralysis in opposition. Even allowing for Curtin's personal popularity, as well as the significant advantages which an incumbent government in a Westminster system has in wartime, Fadden proved a disappointment as Leader of the Opposition; he was unable to get the better of Curtin.  The Coalition suffered a crushing loss in the <a href="/wiki/1943_Australian_federal_election" title="1943 Australian federal election">1943 election</a>. It was reduced to 19 seats, including a mere seven for Fadden's Country Party. Accepting responsibility for this severe defeat, Fadden then handed the Opposition leadership back to Menzies, who had resumed the UAP leadership.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Rebuilding_the_Coalition">Rebuilding the Coalition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Rebuilding the Coalition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden,_November_1949.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg/170px-StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="263" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg/255px-StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg/340px-StateLibQld_1_196899_Sir_Arthur_William_Fadden%2C_November_1949.jpg 2x" data-file-width="647" data-file-height="1000" /></a><figcaption>Fadden in 1949</figcaption></figure>
<p>After the Coalition was again vanquished at the <a href="/wiki/1946_Australian_federal_election" title="1946 Australian federal election">1946 election</a>, Fadden resumed his political partnership with Menzies. Two years earlier, Menzies had folded the UAP into the new <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Australia" title="Liberal Party of Australia">Liberal Party of Australia</a>. There was some speculation that the Country Party would be included in the merger (as had already happened in several states), but Fadden was keen to assert the independence of his party.
</p><p>Always an outspoken conservative, in the late 1940s he became a strong <a href="/wiki/Anti-communism" title="Anti-communism">anti-communist</a>, urging Menzies to ban the <a href="/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Australia" title="Communist Party of Australia">Communist Party of Australia</a> if he ever came to power. Indeed, in the lead up to the <a href="/wiki/1949_Australian_federal_election" title="1949 Australian federal election">1949 election</a>, Fadden often made inflammatory claims about the "socialist" nature of the Labor Party which Menzies could then "clarify" or repudiate as he saw fit, thus appearing more "moderate". His often extreme views were concealed behind a jolly and jovial public manner and he enjoyed his nickname of "Artie."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Fadden was a friend of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_Frederick_Bird_Wake&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Robert Frederick Bird Wake (page does not exist)">Robert Frederick Bird Wake</a>, one of the country's leading security experts at the time. It was an odd relationship, based on their mutual love of Queensland and Queenslanders. Wake, who was a founder director with the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation" title="Australian Security Intelligence Organisation">Australian Security Intelligence Organisation</a> (1949), was supplied with inside information by Fadden about security leaks in the UK. Although Wake was more inclined to support the Labor Party, the relationship stood the test of time and proved mutually beneficial.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Treasurer">Treasurer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Treasurer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arthur_Fadden.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Arthur_Fadden.jpg/170px-Arthur_Fadden.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="245" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Arthur_Fadden.jpg/255px-Arthur_Fadden.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Arthur_Fadden.jpg/340px-Arthur_Fadden.jpg 2x" data-file-width="643" data-file-height="927" /></a><figcaption>Fadden c. 1950</figcaption></figure>
<p>The Coalition won a massive victory in the <a href="/wiki/1949_Australian_federal_election" title="1949 Australian federal election">1949 election</a>, and Fadden, who transferred to the newly created seat of the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_McPherson" title="Division of McPherson">Division of McPherson</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland" title="Gold Coast, Queensland">Gold Coast</a>, became Treasurer in the second Menzies government. Although <a href="/wiki/Inflation" title="Inflation">inflation</a> was high in the early 1950s, forcing him to impose several "horror budgets", he generally presided over a booming economy, with times especially good for farmers.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On the night before the <a href="/wiki/1954_Australian_federal_election" title="1954 Australian federal election">1954 federal election</a>, Fadden was seriously injured in a car accident while travelling back to Brisbane from <a href="/wiki/Dalby,_Queensland" title="Dalby, Queensland">Dalby</a>. Near <a href="/wiki/Grantham,_Queensland" title="Grantham, Queensland">Grantham</a>, the car in which he was travelling failed to negotiate a curve on a slippery road, and rolled three times. Fadden, who had been sitting next to the driver, was pulled from the car unconscious and spent election day in hospital, unable to cast his vote. He was left with injuries to his face, head, and legs, and required five separate operations.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010240_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010240-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fadden resigned as leader of the Country Party on 26 March 1958, with John McEwen elected unopposed as his successor. At the same time, he announced that he would retire from politics at the <a href="/wiki/1958_Australian_federal_election" title="1958 Australian federal election">1958 election</a>. He delivered his eleventh and final budget on 5 August.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010254_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010254-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Although the election was held on 22 November, he did not resign from the ministry until 9 December, remaining in office as treasurer for over two weeks while not being a member of parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010255_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010255-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Having continued as Treasurer after his resignation as Country Party leader it was unusual that the Treasurer was a member of the junior party of the Coalition who was not also the current party leader.
</p><p>Fadden and fellow Country Party leader Earle Page are the only Country Party members to have been Treasurers in non-Labor Coalition governments and Fadden is the only non-Liberal Treasurer in a Liberal-led Coalition government. Since Fadden's retirement, every Treasurer in  a Coalition government has been a Liberal.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_years">Final years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Final years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After leaving parliament, Fadden pursued business interests across a wide range of industries. He visited Japan twice in 1959, and was appointed as the representative of a Japanese company hoping to acquire a licence to export iron ore from Western Australia. The deal fell through, which he attributed to political interference.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010259,_262_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010259,_262-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> Fadden was chairman of Centenary Estates Limited, which built the housing development that became known as the <a href="/wiki/Centenary_Suburbs" title="Centenary Suburbs">Centenary Suburbs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> He took up several company directorships, including with the real estate firm <a href="/wiki/LJ_Hooker" title="LJ Hooker">Hooker Finance</a> and the ice-cream manufacturer Toppa. He also worked as a consultant for a sugar mill in <a href="/wiki/Tully,_Queensland" title="Tully, Queensland">Tully, Queensland</a>, and invested in an iron ore deposit at <a href="/wiki/Mourilyan,_Queensland" title="Mourilyan, Queensland">Mourilyan, Queensland</a>. In 1960, he was commissioned by the state government to produce a report into the viability of <a href="/wiki/Central_Queensland" title="Central Queensland">Central Queensland</a> ports.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010266_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010266-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1969, Fadden published a memoir titled <i>They Called Me Artie</i>. Reviewing the book for <i><a href="/wiki/The_Canberra_Times" title="The Canberra Times">The Canberra Times</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Don_Aitkin" title="Don Aitkin">Don Aitkin</a> observed that Fadden had "the reputation of a political buffoon, a man of earthy wit and hail-fellow-well-metness who survived in politics because he knew more and better stories than the next man [...] his autobiography does little to destroy that picture".<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> Shortly before his death in 1973 he also published a nine-page account of his prime ministership in <i><a href="/wiki/Australian_Outlook" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Outlook">Australian Outlook</a></i>, titled "Forty days and forty nights".<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fadden suffered from ill health during his retirement, including a bout of hepatitis and a vision defect that left him blind in one eye and required an operation to correct.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010266_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010266-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> He died of leukaemia on 21 April 1973, aged 79, at <a href="/wiki/St_Andrew%27s_War_Memorial_Hospital" class="mw-redirect" title="St Andrew&#39;s War Memorial Hospital">St Andrew's War Memorial Hospital</a> in Brisbane. A <a href="/wiki/State_funeral" title="State funeral">state funeral</a> was held at the Toowong Presbyterian Church on 27 April, followed by cremation at <a href="/wiki/Mt_Thompson_Crematorium" class="mw-redirect" title="Mt Thompson Crematorium">Mt Thompson Crematorium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-heaven_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heaven-40">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg/220px-Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="164" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg/330px-Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg/440px-Arthur_Fadden_and_family.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="747" /></a><figcaption>Fadden with his wife Ilma and daughter Betty at a ball in 1951</figcaption></figure>
<p>On 27 December 1916, Fadden married Ilma Nita Thornber (2 April 1895 – 14 May 1987), who worked as a milliner.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Her father James Thornber and brother-in-law George Fay both served periods as <a href="/wiki/City_of_Mackay#List_of_mayors" title="City of Mackay">mayor of Mackay</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> The couple had four children together – Gordon, John, Mavis, and Betty.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> His oldest son Gordon predeceased him, dying in 1956 at the age of 34.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010251_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010251-45">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Arthur Fadden was initiated as a Freemason in Lodge Caledonia, No. 737 S.C. (Mackay, Qld), on 29 July 1915. After being elected to the Australian Parliament on 19 December 1936, he joined Lamington Lodge, No. 110 U.G.L.Q.(Brisbane, Qld), on 4 February 1937.  Bro. Sir Arthur Fadden remained a member of Lamington Lodge, No. 110 U.G.L.Q., until he resigned in March 1973, a month before his passing.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Arthur Fadden was once a member of an acting group in Mackay called the "Nigger Minstrel Troupe".<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Honours">Honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arthur_Fadden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Honours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg/170px-Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg/255px-Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg/340px-Arthur_Fadden_bust.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>Bust of Arthur Fadden by sculptor Wallace Anderson located in the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister%27s_Avenue" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime Minister&#39;s Avenue">Prime Minister's Avenue</a> in the Ballarat Botanical Gardens</figcaption></figure>
<p>Fadden was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a> in 1942, which granted him the style "<a href="/wiki/Right_Honourable" class="mw-redirect" title="Right Honourable">Right Honourable</a>", and was made a Knight Commander of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" title="Order of St Michael and St George">Order of St Michael and St George</a> (KCMG) in 1951.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> He was knighted in person by King George VI in London on 31 January 1952, only a week before the King's death, and formally sworn of the Privy Council the following day.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> In his memoirs he recalled that the King had accidentally knighted him as "Sir William" (his middle name). He corrected the King who knighted him again as "Sir Arthur".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010221_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010221-52">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> Fadden was raised to Knight Grand Cross (GCMG) of the order in 1958, to mark his retirement from politics.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> In 1972, he was made a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Laws" class="mw-redirect" title="Doctor of Laws">Doctor of Laws</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Honoris_causa" class="mw-redirect" title="Honoris causa">honoris causa</a></i> by the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Queensland" title="University of Queensland">University of Queensland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArklay2010263_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArklay2010263-54">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Fadden's death, the <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a> suburb of <a href="/wiki/Fadden,_Australian_Capital_Territory" title="Fadden, Australian Capital Territory">Fadden</a> and the federal electoral <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Fadden" title="Division of Fadden">Division of Fadden</a> were named in his honour, as is traditional for Australian prime ministers. In 1975 he was honoured on a <a href="/wiki/Postage_stamp" title="Postage stamp">postage stamp</a> bearing his portrait issued by <a href="/wiki/Australia_Post" title="Australia Post">Australia Post</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> In 1976, the Sir Arthur Fadden Memorial Garden was established in the Brisbane suburb of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Ommaney,_Queensland" title="Mount Ommaney, Queensland">Mount Ommaney</a>, consisting of 3,000 trees.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
