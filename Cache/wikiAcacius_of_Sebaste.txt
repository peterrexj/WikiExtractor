<p><b>Saint Acacius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἅγιος Ἀκάκιος Σεβαστείας</span>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 304) was a 4th-century <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who lived in <a href="/wiki/Sivas" title="Sivas">Sebaste</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Armenia</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Under the governor Maximus (284–305) seven women and two children were brought to justice in Sebaste.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The women were accused of having tempted their husbands to become Christians. They did not lose their dignity even under the harsh torture. One of the executioners, Irenarchus, was so impressed by their attitude to their faith that he joined them. It was the priest Acacius who administered the baptism of Irenarchus. All of them suffered of torture and were killed by the sword or the stake.<sup id="cite_ref-Book_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Book-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They are venerated in the Orthodox church and their feast day is on November 27.<sup id="cite_ref-Book_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Book-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This Acacius should not be confused with the soldier Acacius, one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste" title="Forty Martyrs of Sebaste">Forty Martyrs of Sebaste</a>.
</p>
