					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th-century Roman general and last ruler of the Kingdom of Soissons</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/Syagrius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Syagrius (disambiguation)">Syagrius (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Reame_di_Siagrio_(486).png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png/300px-Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png" decoding="async" width="300" height="280" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png/450px-Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png/600px-Reame_di_Siagrio_%28486%29.png 2x" data-file-width="1440" data-file-height="1344" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Reame_di_Siagrio_(486).png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Possible extent of Syagrius's "kingdom", most likely exaggerated</div></div></div>
<p><b>Syagrius</b> (430 – 486<sup id="cite_ref-webcite_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webcite-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or 487 or 493–4<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHughes2015207,_234_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHughes2015207,_234-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) was a Roman general and the last ruler of a Roman <a href="/wiki/Rump_state" title="Rump state">rump state</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, now called the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Soissons" title="Kingdom of Soissons">Kingdom of Soissons</a>. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> referred to him as <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Romans" title="King of the Romans">King of the Romans</a>. Syagrius's defeat by king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a> of the Franks is considered the end of <a href="/wiki/Western_Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Roman">Western Roman</a> rule outside of Italy. He inherited his position from his father, <a href="/wiki/Aegidius" title="Aegidius">Aegidius</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.18_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.18-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.27_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.27-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> the last Roman <i><a href="/wiki/Magister_militum" title="Magister militum">magister militum</a> per <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gallias</a></i>.  Syagrius preserved his father's territory between the <a href="/wiki/Somme_(river)" title="Somme (river)">Somme</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a> around <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire" title="Fall of the Western Roman Empire">collapse of central rule in the Western Empire</a>, a domain <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> called the "Kingdom" of Soissons. Syagrius governed this <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> enclave from the death of his father in 464 until 486, when he was defeated in battle by <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a>.
</p><p>Historians have mistrusted the title "Rex Romanorum" that Gregory of Tours gave him, at least as early as <a href="/wiki/Godefroid_Kurth" title="Godefroid Kurth">Godefroid Kurth</a>, who dismissed it as a gross error in 1893. The common consensus has been to follow Kurth, based on the historical <a href="/wiki/Truism" title="Truism">truism</a> that Romans hated kingship from the days of the expulsion of <a href="/wiki/Tarquin_the_Proud" class="mw-redirect" title="Tarquin the Proud">Tarquin the Proud</a>; for example, Syagrius' article in the <i><a href="/wiki/Prosopography_of_the_Later_Roman_Empire" title="Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire">Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire</a></i> omits this title, preferring to refer to him as a "Roman ruler (in North Gaul)". However, Steven Fanning has assembled a number of examples of <i>rex</i> being used in a neutral, if not favorable, context, and argues that "the phrase <i>Romanorum rex</i> is not peculiar to Gregory of Tours or to Frankish sources", and that Gregory's usage may indeed show "that they were, or were seen to be, claiming to be Roman emperors."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFanning1992_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFanning1992-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="End_of_Roman_Gaul">End of Roman Gaul</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Syagrius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: End of Roman Gaul">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg/220px-Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="295" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg/330px-Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="356" data-file-height="477" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Syagrius_brought_before_Clovis.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The captured Syagrius is brought before Alaric II who orders him sent to Clovis I</div></div></div>
<p>Despite being isolated from the surviving portions of the Roman Empire, Syagrius managed to maintain a degree of Roman authority in northern Gaul for twenty years, and his state outlived the end of the Western Empire itself, the last Emperors being overthrown or killed in 476 and 480. Syagrius managed to hold off the neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Salian_Franks" title="Salian Franks">Salian Franks</a>, who were internally divided under kings including <a href="/wiki/Childeric_I" title="Childeric I">Childeric</a>. However, it is known that Childeric had previously come to the aid of the Gallo-Romans, joining a certain officer named <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_(Gallo-Roman)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paul (Gallo-Roman) (page does not exist)">Paul</a> in operations against <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a> who at one point seized <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.18_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.18-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II,19_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II,19-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon Childeric's death in 481 his son Clovis succeeded him. While Childeric had seen no need to overthrow the last Roman foothold in the west, Clovis assembled an army, issued a challenge, and met Syagrius's forces. Few details are known of the subsequent clash, the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Soissons_(486)" title="Battle of Soissons (486)">Battle of Soissons</a>, but Syagrius was decisively defeated and fled. His domain passed to the Franks.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.27_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.27-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As <a href="/wiki/Edward_Gibbon" title="Edward Gibbon">Edward Gibbon</a> later wrote, "It would be ungenerous, without some more accurate knowledge of his strength and resources, to condemn the rapid flight of Syagrius, who escaped after the loss of a battle to the distant court of <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Toulouse was the capital of <a href="/wiki/Alaric_II" title="Alaric II">Alaric II</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Visigoths</a>. Intimidated by the victorious Franks, the Visigoths imprisoned Syagrius, then surrendered him to Clovis. He died not long after, stabbed in secret according to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.37_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGregory_of_Tours1916II.37-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Descendants">Descendants</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Syagrius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Descendants">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Despite the assassination of Syagrius, the family evidently prospered under Frankish rule. King <a href="/wiki/Guntram" title="Guntram">Guntram</a> sent a Count Syagrius on a diplomatic mission to the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> in 585. A member of the family, Syagria, made a large donation of land to the monks of <a href="/wiki/Novalesa_Abbey" title="Novalesa Abbey">Novalesa Abbey</a> in 739. "The last known member of the <a href="/wiki/Syagrii" title="Syagrii">Syagrii</a> was an abbot of <a href="/wiki/Nantua" title="Nantua">Nantua</a> who was mentioned in 757."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMusset1965127_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMusset1965127-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
