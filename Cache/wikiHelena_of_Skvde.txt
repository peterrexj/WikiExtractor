<p><b>Helena of Sköfde</b> (d. 1164, also called <b>Helen</b> or <b>Elin</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-touristcentre_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-touristcentre-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a Swedish saint and patron to what is now <a href="/wiki/V%C3%A4sterg%C3%B6tland" title="Västergötland">Västergötland</a>, in southwest <a href="/wiki/Sweden" title="Sweden">Sweden</a>, where she was born. Her father might have been a duke (or jarl) named Guthorm.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Helena was widowed at a young age, but instead of remarrying, she "devoted herself to works of charity and piety, keeping her gates open to the poor, and clothing them".<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She also built, at her expense, most of the church in <a href="/wiki/Sk%C3%B6vde" title="Skövde">Sköfde</a> in southcentral Sweden.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographer</a> Agnes B. C. Dunbar, she built a portico between the church and its tower, and when asked about its purpose, replied, "God will give us some saint whose body and relics can be suitably placed there".<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eventually, she was interred there. The church, which was named for her since the Middle Ages, was burned down during the city's fire in 1759, was rebuilt on its original foundations, and is still used today. Helena's burial chamber is located in the oldest part of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-touristcentre_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-touristcentre-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Helena had a dream, while visiting <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B6tene_Municipality" title="Götene Municipality">Götene</a> in western Sweden, that the village's church, with her in it, flew away to Sköfde. She interpreted the dream as a prediction that she would die in Gotene and be buried at Sköfde, which, as Dunbar reported, "eventually happened".<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In about 1164, her daughter's husband, while Helena was on a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, was killed by his servants, who admitted to it but claimed that Helena had incited them to do it.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His family became her enemies and persecutors; one of them, while she was visiting Gotene "for indulgences to the consecration of the church of Gotene",<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> stabbed and killed her. 
</p><p>Many miracles occurred after her death. A blind man was healed the day of Helena's murder, while passing by where it happened with a boy who was leading him. The boy saw "a light like a burning candle in the bushes",<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> told the man what he saw, and after a search, found Helena's finger wearing a ring she had brought from Jerusalem. The blind man touched his eyes with the blood from the finger and was immediately healed. While transporting Helena's body to Sköfde, a fountain, later called <i>Lene Kild (</i>"St. Helen's Fountain") sprung up where they rested. At the cemetery where she was buried in Sköfde, her body was washed on a large stone, which was cut in two parts. The part of the stone stained with her blood was propped up so no one would step on it; according to Dunbar, many miracles occurred there, which became a place of veneration for Helena, even after the <a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In the late 16th century, according to Catholic historian Francis Mershman, <a href="/wiki/Abraham_Angermannus" title="Abraham Angermannus">Abraham Angermannus</a>, the fourth <a href="/wiki/Lutheran" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran">Lutheran</a> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Uppsala" title="Archbishop of Uppsala">Archbishop of Uppsala</a>, had Helena's fountain, along with other springs with religious significance, filled up with stones and rubbish, but pilgrims traveled to Helena's grave every summer, where they were healed after staying there all night; they took little bags of earth from under her tombstone, leaving their crutches behind, and made "<a href="/wiki/Votive_offering" title="Votive offering">votive offerings</a> in token of gratitude".<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Another legend states that devotion to the coffin containing Helena's body floated to <a href="/wiki/Tisvilde" title="Tisvilde">Tiisvilde</a>, a small town in the nearby Danish island <a href="/wiki/Zealand" title="Zealand">Zealand</a>, where a spring "broke forth where the coffin touched land". Mershman stated that it might have been a reason for Helena's veneration at Tiisvilde, although it is possible that she visited there or that some of her relics had been brought there.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Helena was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>, on the recommendation of <a href="/wiki/Stefan_(archbishop_of_Uppsala)" title="Stefan (archbishop of Uppsala)">Stefan, the first Archbishop of Uppsala</a>. She was the first Swedish nun to be canonized. Her feast day is 31 July.<sup id="cite_ref-touristcentre_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-touristcentre-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-373_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-mershman_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
