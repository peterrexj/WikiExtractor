<p><b>Jan Mayen</b> (<small>Urban East Norwegian:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="no-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Norwegian" title="Help:IPA/Norwegian">[jɑn ˈmɑ̀ɪən]</a></span>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is a <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norwegian</a> <a href="/wiki/Volcanic_island" title="Volcanic island">volcanic island</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Arctic_Ocean" title="Arctic Ocean">Arctic Ocean</a> with no permanent population. It is 55&#160;km (34&#160;mi) long (southwest-northeast) and 373&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (144&#160;sq&#160;mi) in area, partly covered by <a href="/wiki/Glacier" title="Glacier">glaciers</a> (an area of 114.2&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (44.1&#160;sq&#160;mi) around the <a href="/wiki/Beerenberg" title="Beerenberg">Beerenberg</a> <a href="/wiki/Volcano" title="Volcano">volcano</a>). It has two parts: larger northeast Nord-Jan and smaller Sør-Jan, linked by a 2.5&#160;km (1.6&#160;mi) wide <a href="/wiki/Isthmus" title="Isthmus">isthmus</a>. It lies 600&#160;km (370&#160;mi) northeast of <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Iceland</a> (495&#160;km [305&#160;mi] NE of <a href="/wiki/Kolbeinsey" title="Kolbeinsey">Kolbeinsey</a>), 500&#160;km (310&#160;mi) east of central <a href="/wiki/Greenland" title="Greenland">Greenland</a>, and 900&#160;km (560&#160;mi) northwest of <a href="/wiki/Vester%C3%A5len" title="Vesterålen">Vesterålen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>. The island is mountainous, the highest summit being the Beerenberg volcano in the north. The isthmus is the location of the two largest lakes of the island, <a href="/wiki/S%C3%B8rlaguna" title="Sørlaguna">Sørlaguna</a> (South Lagoon) and <a href="/wiki/Nordlaguna" title="Nordlaguna">Nordlaguna</a> (North Lagoon). A third lake is called Ullerenglaguna (Ullereng Lagoon). Jan Mayen was formed by the <a href="/wiki/Jan_Mayen_hotspot" title="Jan Mayen hotspot">Jan Mayen hotspot</a> and is defined by geologists as a microcontinent.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although administered separately, in the <a href="/wiki/ISO_3166-1" title="ISO 3166-1">ISO 3166-1</a> standard, Jan Mayen and <a href="/wiki/Svalbard" title="Svalbard">Svalbard</a> are collectively designated as <i><a href="/wiki/Svalbard_and_Jan_Mayen" title="Svalbard and Jan Mayen">Svalbard and Jan Mayen</a></i>, with the two-letter country code "SJ".
</p><p>Jan Mayen is home to Beerenberg, which is the northernmost active volcano in the world.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Natural_resources">Natural resources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Natural resources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg/200px-Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="335" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg/300px-Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg/400px-Jan_Mayen_Von_Karman_Vortex_street_Apr_5_2012.jpg 2x" data-file-width="547" data-file-height="916" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/K%C3%A1rm%C3%A1n_vortex_street" title="Kármán vortex street">Kármán vortex street</a> created by <a href="/wiki/Beerenberg" title="Beerenberg">Beerenberg</a> volcano in the westerly winds.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Jan Mayen Island has one exploitable natural resource, <a href="/wiki/Gravel" title="Gravel">gravel</a>, from a site located at Trongskaret. Other than this, economic activity is limited to providing services for employees of <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Radio_communications" class="mw-redirect" title="Radio communications">radio communications</a> and <a href="/wiki/Meteorological" class="mw-redirect" title="Meteorological">meteorological</a> stations located on the island. Jan Mayen has one unpaved <a href="/wiki/Airstrip" class="mw-redirect" title="Airstrip">airstrip</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jan_Mayensfield" title="Jan Mayensfield">Jan Mayensfield</a>, which is about 1,585&#160;m (5,200&#160;ft) long. The 124.1&#160;km (77.1&#160;mi) coast has no <a href="/wiki/Port" title="Port">ports</a> or <a href="/wiki/Harbour" class="mw-redirect" title="Harbour">harbours</a>, only offshore anchorages.
</p><p>There are important <a href="/wiki/Fishing" title="Fishing">fishing</a> resources, and the existence of Jan Mayen establishes a large <a href="/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone" title="Exclusive economic zone">exclusive economic zone</a> (EEZ) around it. Norway has asserted a 200-nautical-mile (370-kilometre) EEZ around the island since 1980 encompassing more than a quarter of a million square kilometers. The <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_Coast_Guard" title="Norwegian Coast Guard">Norwegian Coast Guard</a> is responsible for conducting fishery and other maritime surveillance and enforcement in these waters.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Norway has found large deposits of <a href="/wiki/Minerals" class="mw-redirect" title="Minerals">minerals</a> along the <a href="/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_Ridge" title="Mid-Atlantic Ridge">Mid-Atlantic Ridge</a> between Jan Mayen and southern Svalbard/Bear Island,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> including <a href="/wiki/Copper" title="Copper">copper</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zinc" title="Zinc">zinc</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cobalt" title="Cobalt">cobalt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gold" title="Gold">gold</a> and <a href="/wiki/Silver" title="Silver">silver</a>. The expeditions have also discovered high concentrations of <a href="/wiki/Lithium" title="Lithium">lithium</a> and rare earth metal <a href="/wiki/Scandium" title="Scandium">scandium</a>. In total, it is estimated that the amount of copper could amount to 21.7 million tonnes, but other estimates are around 7 million tonnes. License for deep sea mining is now under consideration.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A dispute between Norway and <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a> regarding the fishing exclusion zone between Jan Mayen and Greenland was settled in 1988 granting Denmark the greater area of sovereignty. Geologists suspect significant deposits of petroleum and natural gas lie below Jan Mayen's surrounding <a href="/wiki/Seabed" title="Seabed">seafloors</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Status">Status</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Status">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG/220px-Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG/330px-Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG/440px-Beerenberg_JanMayen.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>The snow-covered <a href="/wiki/Beerenberg" title="Beerenberg">Beerenberg</a> volcano beyond the coastal hills</figcaption></figure>
<p>Jan Mayen Island is <a href="/wiki/List_of_possessions_of_Norway" title="List of possessions of Norway">an integral part of the Kingdom of Norway</a>. Since 1995, Jan Mayen has been administered by the <a href="/wiki/County_governor_(Norway)" title="County governor (Norway)">County Governor</a> (<i>statsforvalter</i>) of the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Norway" title="Northern Norway">northern Norwegian</a> county of <a href="/wiki/Nordland" title="Nordland">Nordland</a>, to which it is closest. However, some authority over Jan Mayen has been assigned to the station commander of the Norwegian Defence Logistics Organisation, a branch of the <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_Armed_Forces" title="Norwegian Armed Forces">Norwegian Armed Forces</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Society">Society</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Society">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Demography">Demography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Demography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg/220px-Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="122" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg/330px-Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg/440px-Jan_mayen-station_hg.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1416" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Olonkinbyen" title="Olonkinbyen">Olonkinbyen</a> in August</figcaption></figure>
<p>The only inhabitants on the island are personnel working for the Norwegian Armed Forces and the <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_Meteorological_Institute" title="Norwegian Meteorological Institute">Norwegian Meteorological Institute</a>. Eighteen people spend the winter on the island, but the <a href="/wiki/Population" title="Population">population</a> may roughly double (35) during the summer, when heavy maintenance is performed. Personnel serve either six months or one year and are exchanged twice a year in April and October. The support crew, including mechanics, cooks, and a nurse, are among the military personnel. The military personnel operated a <a href="/wiki/Loran-C" title="Loran-C">Loran-C</a> <a href="/wiki/LORAN-C_transmitter_Jan_Mayen" title="LORAN-C transmitter Jan Mayen">base</a> until it closed at the end of 2015.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Both the LORAN transmitter and the <a href="/wiki/Meteorological_station" class="mw-redirect" title="Meteorological station">meteorological station</a> are located a few kilometres away from the settlement <a href="/wiki/Olonkinbyen" title="Olonkinbyen">Olonkinbyen</a> (Olonkin Town), where all personnel live.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Transport">Transport</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Transport">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Transport to the island is provided by <a href="/wiki/C-130_Hercules" class="mw-redirect" title="C-130 Hercules">C-130 Hercules</a> military transport planes operated by the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Air_Force" title="Royal Norwegian Air Force">Royal Norwegian Air Force</a> that land at <a href="/wiki/Jan_Mayensfield" title="Jan Mayensfield">Jan Mayensfield</a>'s gravel runway. The planes fly in from <a href="/wiki/Bod%C3%B8_Main_Air_Station" title="Bodø Main Air Station">Bodø Main Air Station</a> eight times a year. Since the airport does not have any instrument landing capabilities, good visibility is required, and it is not uncommon for the planes to have to return to <a href="/wiki/Bod%C3%B8" title="Bodø">Bodø</a>, two hours away, without landing. For heavy goods, freight ships visit during the summer, but since there are no harbours, the ships must anchor.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Communication">Communication</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Communication">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The island has no indigenous population but is assigned the <a href="/wiki/ISO_3166-1_alpha-2" title="ISO 3166-1 alpha-2">ISO 3166-1 alpha-2</a> <a href="/wiki/Country_code" title="Country code">country code</a> SJ (together with <a href="/wiki/Svalbard" title="Svalbard">Svalbard</a>). It uses the <a href="/wiki/Internet" title="Internet">Internet</a> country code <a href="/wiki/Top-level_domain" title="Top-level domain">top-level domain</a> (<a href="/wiki/CcTLD" class="mw-redirect" title="CcTLD">ccTLD</a>) <a href="/wiki/.no" title=".no">.no</a> (<a href="/wiki/.sj" title=".sj">.sj</a> is allocated but not used)<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and data code JN. Jan Mayen has telephone and internet connection over satellite, using Norwegian telephone numbers (country code 47). Its <a href="/wiki/Amateur_radio" title="Amateur radio">amateur radio</a> <a href="/wiki/Call_sign" title="Call sign">call sign</a> prefix is JX. It has a postal code, NO-8099 JAN MAYEN, but delivery time varies, especially during the winter.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Business">Business</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Business">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There are no <a href="/wiki/Exploitable_resources" class="mw-redirect" title="Exploitable resources">exploitable resources</a> on Jan Mayen, except fish in the surrounding waters of the Island. The <a href="/wiki/Economic_activity" class="mw-redirect" title="Economic activity">economic activity</a> is limited to the operation of the station that is staffed by the Norwegian <b>Cyberdefence</b> and the <b>Metereological Agency of Norway</b>. There has also been established a reference station for <a href="/wiki/European_Geostationary_Navigation_Overlay_Service" title="European Geostationary Navigation Overlay Service">EGNOS</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> There is also a reference station for the satellite navigation system <a href="/wiki/Galileo_(satellite_navigation)" title="Galileo (satellite navigation)">Galileo</a> on Jan Mayen.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> There was also an earlier Jan Mayen <a href="/wiki/Loran-C" title="Loran-C">LORAN-C</a> <a href="/wiki/Transmitter" title="Transmitter">Transmitter</a>, but the <a href="/wiki/Transmitter" title="Transmitter">transmitter</a> is now decommissioned and demolished.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jan_Mayen_Radio">Jan Mayen Radio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Jan Mayen Radio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jan Mayen Radio was a Norwegian coastal radio station on Jan Mayen. The first <a href="/wiki/Radiostation" class="mw-redirect" title="Radiostation">radiostation</a> was built in 1921 on a part of the island called "Eldsmetten - Norwegian" on the eastern side of the Island. The radiostation consisted of a 3&#160;kW <a href="/wiki/Telefunken" title="Telefunken">Telefunken</a> <a href="/wiki/Spark-gap_transmitter" title="Spark-gap transmitter">spark-gap transmitter</a> and a 55&#160;m (180&#160;ft) wooden radiomast. The station was destroyed by Norwegian forces in September 1940, and the crew was sent to <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Iceland</a>.
</p><p>In 1941 a new <a href="/wiki/Radiostation" class="mw-redirect" title="Radiostation">radiostation</a> was constructed on the western side of the island, it was moved to a <a href="/wiki/Plateau" title="Plateau">plateau</a> above. In 1962 this station was again moved to "Helenesanden - Norwegian" about 3&#160;km (1.9&#160;mi) north from the <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_Armed_Forces" title="Norwegian Armed Forces">Norwegian army</a>'s <a href="/wiki/LORAN" title="LORAN">LORAN</a>-station.
</p><p>In 1984 the station was moved to the Norwegian army's station. In 1989 there was an <a href="/wiki/VHF" class="mw-redirect" title="VHF">VHF</a>-receiver installed, and later in 1994 was a MF-Digital-<a href="/wiki/Selcall" title="Selcall">Selcall</a>-receiver installed and is now controlled remotely from Bodø-Radio. The station is now controlled remotely via <a href="/wiki/Satellite" title="Satellite">satellite</a>, but can be taken in local control by a disconnection against Eik Satellite Earth Station.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arctic.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Arctic.svg/290px-Arctic.svg.png" decoding="async" width="290" height="364" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Arctic.svg/435px-Arctic.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Arctic.svg/580px-Arctic.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="505" data-file-height="633" /></a><figcaption>In the <a href="/wiki/Category:Dutch_exploration_in_the_Age_of_Discovery" title="Category:Dutch exploration in the Age of Discovery">Golden Age of Dutch exploration and discovery</a> (c. 1590s–1720s), Dutch navigators were the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Dutch_explorations" title="List of Dutch explorations">first non-natives to undisputedly explore</a> and <a href="/wiki/Early_modern_Netherlandish_cartography" class="mw-redirect" title="Early modern Netherlandish cartography">map many largely unknown isolated areas of the world</a>, including Jan Mayen and the <a href="/wiki/Svalbard_archipelago" class="mw-redirect" title="Svalbard archipelago">Svalbard archipelago</a> in the Arctic Ocean.</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span id="Unverified_.22discoveries.22_of_a_terra_nullius"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Unverified_&quot;discoveries&quot;_of_a_terra_nullius">Unverified "discoveries" of a <i>terra nullius</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Unverified &quot;discoveries&quot; of a terra nullius">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Terra_nullius" title="Terra nullius">Terra nullius</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg/290px-Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="218" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg/435px-Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg/580px-Jan_mayen_egg-oeja_hg.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>A beach on Jan Mayen</figcaption></figure>
<p>Between the fifth and ninth centuries (400–900 AD), numerous communities of monks originating in Ireland (<a href="/wiki/Papar" title="Papar">Papar</a>) navigated throughout the north Atlantic in leather boats, exploring and sometimes settling in distant islands where their monastic communities could be separated from close contact with others. Strong indicators exist of their presence in the <a href="/wiki/Faroe_Islands" title="Faroe Islands">Faroe Islands</a> and <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Iceland</a> before the arrival of the <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Vikings</a>, and medieval Gaelic chronicles such as the famous <i><a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator#The_Voyage_of_Saint_Brendan_the_Abbot" title="Brendan the Navigator">Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot</a></i> testify to the extensive interest in exploration at the time. A modern-day trans-Atlantic journey proved the ability of the early navigators to reach all lands of the north Atlantic even further from Ireland than Jan Mayen – and, given favourable winds, at a speed roughly equal to that of modern yachts.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Though quite feasible, there is nevertheless no direct physical trace of medieval landings or settlement on Jan Mayen.
</p><p>The land named <i>Svalbarð</i> ("cold coast") by the Vikings in the early medieval book <a href="/wiki/Landn%C3%A1mab%C3%B3k" title="Landnámabók">Landnámabók</a> may have been Jan Mayen (instead of <a href="/wiki/Spitsbergen" title="Spitsbergen">Spitsbergen</a>, renamed <a href="/wiki/Svalbard" title="Svalbard">Svalbard</a> by the Norwegians in modern times); the distance from Iceland to <i>Svalbarð</i> mentioned in this book is two days' sailing (with favorable winds), consistent with the approximate 550&#160;km (340&#160;mi) to Jan Mayen and not with the minimum 1,550&#160;km (960&#160;mi) to Spitsbergen.<sup id="cite_ref-Wordie_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wordie-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> However much Jan Mayen may have been known in Europe at that time, it was subsequently forgotten for some centuries.
</p><p>In the 17th century, many claims of the island's rediscovery were made, spurred by the rivalry on the Arctic whaling grounds, and the island received many names. According to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Edge" title="Thomas Edge">Thomas Edge</a>, an early 17th-century whaling captain who was often inaccurate, "William &#32;&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Sic" title="Sic">sic</a></i>&#93; Hudson" discovered the island in 1608 and named it "Hudson's Touches" (or "Tutches"). However, the well-known explorer <a href="/wiki/Henry_Hudson" title="Henry Hudson">Henry Hudson</a> could only have come by on his voyage in 1607 (if he had made an illogical detour) and he made no mention of it in his journal.<sup id="cite_ref-Wordie_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wordie-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/William_Scoresby" title="William Scoresby">William Scoresby</a> (1820: p.&#160;154), referring to the mistaken belief that the Dutch had discovered the island in 1611, <a href="/wiki/Kingston_upon_Hull" title="Kingston upon Hull">Hull</a> whalers discovered the island "about the same time" and named it "Trinity Island". Muller (1874: pp.&#160;190–191) took this to mean they had come upon Jan Mayen in 1611 or 1612, which was repeated by many subsequent authors. There were, in fact, no Hull whalers in either of these years, the first Hull whaling expedition having been sent to the island only in 1616 (see below). As with the previous claim made by Edge, there is no cartographical or written proof for this supposed discovery.<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="During_the_Golden_Age_of_Dutch_exploration_and_discovery_.28c._1590s.E2.80.931720s.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="During_the_Golden_Age_of_Dutch_exploration_and_discovery_(c._1590s–1720s)">During the Golden Age of Dutch exploration and discovery (c. 1590s–1720s)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: During the Golden Age of Dutch exploration and discovery (c. 1590s–1720s)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="First_verified_discoveries:_mapping_and_naming">First verified discoveries: mapping and naming</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: First verified discoveries: mapping and naming">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg/290px-C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="229" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg/435px-C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg/580px-C.G._Zorgdragers_Bloeyende_opkomst_der_aloude_en_hedendaagsche_Groenlandsche_visschery_-_no-nb_digibok_2014010724007-V5.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3872" data-file-height="3059" /></a><figcaption>A map of Jan Mayen during the Golden Age of Dutch exploration and discovery (c. 1590s–1720s). <p>The first verified discoveries of Jan Mayen, by three separate expeditions, occurred in the summer of 1614, probably within one month of each other. The Dutchman Fopp Gerritsz, whilst in command of a whaling expedition sent out by the Englishman John Clarke, of <a href="/wiki/Dunkirk" title="Dunkirk">Dunkirk</a>, claimed (in 1631) to have discovered the island on 28 June and named it "Isabella".<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Holland_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holland-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In January the <i><a href="/wiki/Noordsche_Compagnie" title="Noordsche Compagnie">Noordsche Compagnie</a></i> (Northern Company), modelled on the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company" title="Dutch East India Company">Dutch East India Company</a>, had been established to support Dutch whaling in the Arctic. Two of its ships, financed by merchants from <a href="/wiki/Amsterdam" title="Amsterdam">Amsterdam</a> and <a href="/wiki/Enkhuizen" title="Enkhuizen">Enkhuizen</a>, reached Jan Mayen in July 1614. The captains of these ships—<a href="/wiki/Jan_Jacobszoon_May_van_Schellinkhout" title="Jan Jacobszoon May van Schellinkhout">Jan Jacobszoon May van Schellinkhout</a> (after whom the island was ultimately named) on the <i>Gouden Cath</i> (Golden Cat), and Jacob de Gouwenaer on the <i>Orangienboom</i> (Orange Tree)—named it <i>Mr. Joris Eylant</i> after the Dutch cartographer <a href="/wiki/Joris_Carolus" title="Joris Carolus">Joris Carolus</a> who was on board and mapped the island. The captains acknowledged that a third Dutch ship, the <i>Cleyn Swaentgen</i> (Little Swan) captained by Jan Jansz Kerckhoff and financed by <i>Noordsche Compagnie</i> shareholders from <a href="/wiki/Delft" title="Delft">Delft</a>, had already been at the island when they arrived. They had assumed the latter, who named the island <i>Maurits Eylandt</i> (or Mauritius) after <a href="/wiki/Maurice_of_Nassau,_Prince_of_Orange" class="mw-redirect" title="Maurice of Nassau, Prince of Orange">Maurice of Nassau, Prince of Orange</a>, would report their discovery to the <a href="/wiki/States_General_of_the_Netherlands" title="States General of the Netherlands">States General</a>. However, the Delft merchants had decided to keep the discovery secret and returned in 1615 to hunt for their own profit. The ensuing dispute was only settled in 1617, though both companies were allowed to whale at Jan Mayen in the meantime.<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1615, the English whaler <a href="/wiki/Robert_Fotherby" title="Robert Fotherby">Robert Fotherby</a> went ashore. Apparently thinking he had made a new discovery, he named the island "Sir Thomas Smith's Island" and the volcano "Mount Hakluyt".<sup id="cite_ref-Wordie_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wordie-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> On a map of c. 1634, <a href="/wiki/Jean_Vrolicq" title="Jean Vrolicq">Jean Vrolicq</a> renamed the island <i>Île de Richelieu</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jan Mayen first appeared on <a href="/wiki/Willem_Jansz_Blaeu" class="mw-redirect" title="Willem Jansz Blaeu">Willem Jansz Blaeu</a>'s 1620 edition map of Europe, originally published by Cornelis Doedz in 1606. Blaeu, who lived in Amsterdam, named it "Jan Mayen" after captain <a href="/wiki/Jan_Jacobszoon_May_van_Schellinkhout" title="Jan Jacobszoon May van Schellinkhout">Jan Jacobszoon May van Schellinkhout</a> of the Amsterdam-financed <i>Gouden Cath</i>. Blaeu made the first detailed map of the island in his famous "Zeespiegel" atlas of 1623, establishing its current name.<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Dutch_whaling_base">Dutch whaling base</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Dutch whaling base">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg/290px-Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="177" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg/435px-Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg/580px-Jan_mayen_coast_hg.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2547" data-file-height="1557" /></a><figcaption>Road along the west coast, about 500 metres (1,600&#160;ft) off the station.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png/290px-Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png" decoding="async" width="290" height="218" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png/435px-Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png/580px-Altes_Grabkreuz_f%C3%BCr_einen_tapferen_Holl%C3%A4nder.png 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>"HOLLENDERHAUGEN. HER HVILER TAPRE HOLLANDSKE MENN." Old cross on the grave of seven Dutchmen, reading "The Dutch men's hill. Here rest brave Dutch men".</figcaption></figure>
<p>From 1615 to 1638, Jan Mayen was used as a whaling base by the Dutch <i>Noordsche Compagnie</i>, which had been given a monopoly on whaling in the Arctic regions by the States General in 1614. Only two ships, one from the <i>Noordsche Compagnie</i>, and the other from the Delft merchants, were off Jan Mayen in 1615. The following year a score of vessels were sent to the island. The <i>Noordsche Compagnie</i> sent eight ships escorted by three warships under Jan Jacobsz. Schrobop; while the Delft merchants sent up five ships under Adriaen Dircksz. Leversteyn, son of one of the above merchants.<sup id="cite_ref-Muller_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muller-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> There were also two ships from Dunkirk sent by John Clarke, as well as a ship each from London and Hull.
</p><p>Heertje Jansz, master of the <i>Hope</i>, of Enkhuizen, wrote a day-by-day account of the season. The ships took two weeks to reach Jan Mayen, arriving early in June. On 15 June they met the two English ships, which Schrobop allowed to remain, on condition they gave half their catch to the Dutch.<sup id="cite_ref-Dalgård_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalgård-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The ships from Dunkirk were given the same conditions. By late July the first ship had left with a full cargo of <a href="/wiki/Whale_oil" title="Whale oil">whale oil</a>; the rest left early in August, several filled with oil.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That year 200 men were seasonally living and working on the island at six temporary whaling stations (spread along the northwest coast). During the first decade of whaling, more than ten ships visited Jan Mayen each year, while in the second period (1624 and later) five to ten ships were sent. With the exception of a few ships from Dunkirk, which came to the island in 1617 and were either driven away or forced to give a third of their catch to the Dutch,<sup id="cite_ref-Dalgård_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalgård-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> only the Dutch and merchants from Hull<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> sent up ships to Jan Mayen from 1616 onward. In 1624 ten wooden houses were built in <a href="/wiki/Titeltbukta" title="Titeltbukta">South Bay</a>. About this time the Dutch appear to have abandoned the temporary stations consisting of tents of sail and crude furnaces, replacing them with two semi-permanent stations with wooden storehouses and dwellings and large brick furnaces, one in the above-mentioned South Bay and the other in the <a href="/wiki/Engelskbukta_(Jan_Mayen)" title="Engelskbukta (Jan Mayen)">North Bay</a>. In 1628 two forts were built to protect the stations.<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Among the sailors active at Jan Mayen was the later admiral <a href="/wiki/Michiel_Adriaensz_de_Ruyter" class="mw-redirect" title="Michiel Adriaensz de Ruyter">Michiel Adriaensz de Ruyter</a>. In 1633, at the age of 26, he was for the first time listed as an officer aboard <i>de Groene Leeuw</i> (The Green Lion). He again went to Jan Mayen in 1635, aboard the same ship.
</p><p>In 1632 the <i>Noordsche Compagnie</i> expelled the Danish-employed Basque whalers from Spitsbergen. In revenge, the latter sailed to Jan Mayen, where the Dutch had left for the winter, to plunder the Dutch equipment and burn down the settlements and factories. Captain Outger Jacobsz of <a href="/wiki/Grootebroek" title="Grootebroek">Grootebroek</a> was asked to stay the next winter (1633/34) on Jan Mayen with six shipmates to defend the island. While a group with the same task survived the winter on Spitsbergen, all seven on Jan Mayen died of <a href="/wiki/Scurvy" title="Scurvy">scurvy</a> or <a href="/wiki/Trichinosis" title="Trichinosis">trichinosis</a> (from eating raw polar bear meat) combined with the harsh conditions.
</p><p>During the first phase of whaling the hauls were generally good, some exceptional. For example, Mathijs Jansz. Hoepstock caught 44 whales in <a href="/wiki/Hoepstockbukta" title="Hoepstockbukta">Hoepstockbukta</a> in 1619, which produced 2,300 casks of whale oil. During the second phase the hauls were much lower. While 1631 turned out to be a very good season, the following year, due to the weather and ice, only eight whales were caught. In 1633 eleven ships managed to catch just 47 whales; while a meager 42 were caught by the same number in 1635.<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Bowhead_whale" title="Bowhead whale">bowhead whale</a> was locally hunted to near-extinction around 1640 (approximately 1000 had been killed and processed on the island),<sup id="cite_ref-Hacq_19-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hacq-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> at which time Jan Mayen was abandoned and stayed uninhabited for two and a half centuries.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="19th_and_20th_expeditions">19th and 20th expeditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: 19th and 20th expeditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg/290px-Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg.png" decoding="async" width="290" height="312" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg/435px-Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg/580px-Jan_Mayen-Stations-en1_1.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="354" data-file-height="381" /></a><figcaption>Map of settlements on Jan Mayen</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg/290px-Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="218" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg/435px-Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg/580px-Jan_mayen_sign_hg.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>Traditional signpost with directions to civilization on Jan Mayen station</figcaption></figure>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/International_Polar_Year" title="International Polar Year">International Polar Year</a> 1882&#8211;1883 the <a href="/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_North_Pole_Expedition" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Hungarian North Pole Expedition">Austro-Hungarian North Pole Expedition</a> stayed one year at Jan Mayen. The expedition performed extensive mapping of the area, their maps being of such quality that they were used until the 1950s. The <a href="/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Hungarian Empire">Austrian</a> polar station on Jan Mayen Island was built and equipped in 1882 fully at <a href="/wiki/Count_Johann_Nepomuk_Wilczek" class="mw-redirect" title="Count Johann Nepomuk Wilczek">Count Wilczek's</a> own expense.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Polar_bear" title="Polar bear">Polar bears</a> appear on Jan Mayen,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> although in diminished numbers compared with earlier times. Between 1900 and 1920, there were a number of Norwegian trappers spending winters on Jan Mayen, hunting <a href="/wiki/Arctic_fox" title="Arctic fox">Arctic foxes</a> in addition to some polar bears. But the exploitation soon made the profits decline, and the hunting ended. Polar bears in this region of the Arctic are genetically distinguishable from those living elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/League_of_Nations" title="League of Nations">League of Nations</a> gave Norway jurisdiction over the island, and in 1921 Norway opened the first meteorological station.<sup id="cite_ref-Rigge_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rigge-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The Norwegian Meteorological Institute annexed the middle part of the island for Norway in 1922 and the whole island in 1926 when <a href="/wiki/Hallvard_Devold" title="Hallvard Devold">Hallvard Devold</a> was head of the weather observations base on the island. On 27 February 1930, the island was made <i><a href="/wiki/De_jure" title="De jure">de jure</a></i> a part of the Kingdom of Norway.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_World_War">Second World War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Second World War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, continental Norway was <a href="/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung" title="Operation Weserübung">invaded and occupied by Germany</a> in spring 1940. The four-man team on Jan Mayen stayed at their posts and in an act of defiance began sending their weather reports to the United Kingdom instead of Norway. The British codenamed Jan Mayen 'Island X' and attempted to reinforce it with troops to counteract any <a href="/wiki/Nazi_Germany" title="Nazi Germany">German</a> attack. The Norwegian patrol boat <a href="/wiki/HNoMS_Fridtjof_Nansen_(1930)" title="HNoMS Fridtjof Nansen (1930)">HNoMS&#160;<i>Fridtjof Nansen</i></a> ran aground on <a href="/wiki/Nansenflua" title="Nansenflua">Nansenflua</a>, one of the islands' many uncharted lava reefs and the 68-man crew abandoned ship and joined the Norwegian team on shore. The British expedition commander, prompted by the loss of the gunboat, decided to abandon Jan Mayen until the following spring and radioed for a rescue ship. Within a few days a ship arrived and evacuated the four Norwegians and their would-be reinforcements, after demolishing the weather station to prevent it from falling into German hands. The Germans attempted to land a weather team on the island on 16 November 1940; the German <a href="/wiki/Naval_trawler" title="Naval trawler">naval trawler</a> carrying the team crashed on the rocks just off Jan Mayen after a patrolling British <a href="/wiki/Destroyer" title="Destroyer">destroyer</a> had picked them up on radar. The detection was not by chance, as the German plan had been compromised from the beginning with British wireless interceptors of the Radio Security Service following the communications of the <a href="/wiki/Abwehr" title="Abwehr">Abwehr</a> (the German Intelligence service) concerning the operation, and the destroyer had been waiting. Most of the crew struggled ashore and were taken prisoner by a landing party from the destroyer.<sup id="cite_ref-Rigge_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rigge-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II" title="Allies of World War II">The Allies</a> returned to the island on 10 March 1941, when the Norwegian ship <i>Veslekari</i>, escorted by the patrol boat <i>Honningsvaag</i>, dropped 12 Norwegian weathermen on the island. The team's radio transmissions soon betrayed its presence to the <a href="/wiki/Axis_Powers" class="mw-redirect" title="Axis Powers">Axis</a>, and German planes from Norway began to bomb and strafe Jan Mayen whenever weather permitted, but did little damage. Soon supplies and reinforcements arrived, and even some anti-aircraft guns, giving the island a garrison of a few dozen weathermen and soldiers. By 1941, Germany had given up hope of evicting the Allies from the island and the constant air raids stopped.
</p><p>On 7 August 1942, a German <a href="/wiki/Focke-Wulf_Fw_200" class="mw-redirect" title="Focke-Wulf Fw 200">Focke-Wulf Fw 200</a> "Condor", probably on a mission to bomb the station, crashed into the nearby mountainside of Danielssenkrateret in fog, killing its crew of nine.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> In 1950, the wreck of another German plane with four crew members was discovered on the southwest side of the island.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> In 1943, the Americans established a radio locating station named Atlantic City in the north to try to locate <a href="/wiki/Greenland_in_World_War_II" title="Greenland in World War II">German radio bases in Greenland</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cold_War">Cold War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Cold War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the war, the meteorological station was located at Atlantic City, but moved in 1949 to a new location. Radio Jan Mayen also served as an important radio station for ship traffic in the <a href="/wiki/Arctic_Ocean" title="Arctic Ocean">Arctic Ocean</a>. In 1959 <a href="/wiki/NATO" title="NATO">NATO</a> started building the <a href="/wiki/LORAN-C" class="mw-redirect" title="LORAN-C">LORAN-C</a> network in sites on the Atlantic Ocean; one of the transmitters was to be on Jan Mayen. By 1961 the new military installations, including a new airfield, were operational.
</p><p>For some time scientists doubted that the Beerenberg volcano would become active, but in 1970 it erupted for about three weeks, adding another 3&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (1.2&#160;sq&#160;mi) of land mass to the island. It also erupted in 1973 and 1985. During an eruption, the sea temperature around the island may increase from just above freezing to about 30&#160;°C (86&#160;°F).
</p><p>Historic stations and huts on the island are Hoyberg, Vera, Olsbu, <a href="/wiki/Puppebu" title="Puppebu">Puppebu</a> (cabin), Gamlemetten or Gamlestasjonen (the old weather station), Jan Mayen Radio, Helenehytta, Margarethhytta, and Ulla (a cabin at the foot of the Beerenberg).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Environment">Environment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Environment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Nature_reserve">Nature reserve</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Nature reserve">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A regulation dating from 2010 renders the island a nature reserve under Norwegian jurisdiction.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The aim of this regulation is to ensure the preservation of a pristine Arctic island and the marine life nearby, including the ocean floor. Landings at Jan Mayen can be done by boat. However, this is permitted only at a small part of the island, named Båtvika (Boat Bay). As there is no commercial airline operating at the island, one cannot get there by plane except by <a href="/wiki/Air_charter" title="Air charter">chartering one</a>. Permission for landings by a charter plane has to be obtained in advance. Permission to stay on the island has to be obtained in advance, and is generally limited to a few days (or even hours). Putting up a tent or setting up camp is prohibited. There is a separate regulation for the stay of foreigners.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Geography_and_geology">Geography and geology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=19" title="Edit section: Geography and geology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg/220px-R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="201" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg/330px-R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg/440px-R-29-IX-X-XI_200-K_1967_Jan_Mayen.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2924" data-file-height="2675" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> topographic map</figcaption></figure>
<p>Jan Mayen consists of two geographically distinct parts. Nord-Jan has a round shape and is dominated by the 2,277&#160;m (7,470&#160;ft) high <a href="/wiki/Beerenberg" title="Beerenberg">Beerenberg</a> volcano with its large <a href="/wiki/Ice_cap" title="Ice cap">ice cap</a> (114.2&#160;km<sup>2</sup> or 44&#160;sq&#160;mi), which can be divided into twenty individual outlet glaciers. The largest of those is <a href="/w/index.php?title=S%C3%B8rbreen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sørbreen (page does not exist)">Sørbreen</a>, with an area of 15&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (5.8&#160;sq&#160;mi) and a length of 8.7&#160;km (5.41&#160;mi). South-Jan is narrow, comparatively flat and unglaciated. Its highest elevation is <a href="/wiki/Rudolftoppen" title="Rudolftoppen">Rudolftoppen</a> at 769&#160;m (2,523&#160;ft). The station and living quarters are located on South-Jan. The island lies at the northern end of the <a href="/wiki/Jan_Mayen_Microcontinent" title="Jan Mayen Microcontinent">Jan Mayen Microcontinent</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Continental_fragment" title="Continental fragment">microcontinent</a> was originally part of the <a href="/wiki/Greenland_Plate" title="Greenland Plate">Greenland Plate</a>, but now forms part of the <a href="/wiki/Eurasian_Plate" title="Eurasian Plate">Eurasian Plate</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Important_Bird_Area">Important Bird Area</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=20" title="Edit section: Important Bird Area">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The island was identified as an <a href="/wiki/Important_Bird_Area" title="Important Bird Area">Important Bird Area</a> (IBA) by <a href="/wiki/BirdLife_International" title="BirdLife International">BirdLife International</a> because it is a breeding site for large numbers of <a href="/wiki/Seabird" title="Seabird">seabirds</a>, supporting populations of <a href="/wiki/Northern_fulmar" title="Northern fulmar">northern fulmars</a> (78,000–160,000 pairs), <a href="/wiki/Little_auk" title="Little auk">little auks</a> (10,000–100,000 pairs), <a href="/wiki/Thick-billed_guillemot" class="mw-redirect" title="Thick-billed guillemot">thick-billed guillemot</a> (74,000–147,000 pairs) and <a href="/wiki/Black_guillemot" title="Black guillemot">black guillemots</a> (100–1,000 pairs).<sup id="cite_ref-bli_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bli-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Climate">Climate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Climate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jan Mayen has an oceanic <a href="/wiki/Climate_of_the_Arctic" title="Climate of the Arctic">polar</a> climate with a <a href="/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification" title="Köppen climate classification">Köppen classification</a> of <i>ET</i>, sometimes reckoned as <i>EM</i> (maritime polar). Jan Mayen is situated in between the cold <a href="/wiki/East_Greenland_Current" title="East Greenland Current">East Greenland Current</a> to the west and the warm <a href="/wiki/Gulf_Stream" title="Gulf Stream">Gulf Stream</a> to the east of the island, and is the only landmass in the northern hemisphere where warm and cold ocean currents meet.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> The surrounding seas makes seasonal temperature variations very small considering the latitude of the island, with ranges from around 6&#160;°C (43&#160;°F) in August to −4&#160;°C (25&#160;°F) in March, but also makes the island extremely cloudy with little sunshine even during the continuous polar day. The deep snow cover prevents any <a href="/wiki/Permafrost" title="Permafrost">permafrost</a> from developing. As a result of warming, the 1991-2020 temperature normal shows a mean annual temperature 1.9&#160;°C (3.4&#160;°F) warmer than during 1961-1990, pushing the annual temperature above freezing.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Mayen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=22" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jan Mayen is featured as an <a href="/wiki/Easter_egg_(media)" title="Easter egg (media)">easter egg</a> in several grand strategy video games published by <a href="/wiki/Paradox_Interactive" title="Paradox Interactive">Paradox Interactive</a>, such as <i><a href="/wiki/Europa_Universalis_IV" title="Europa Universalis IV">Europa Universalis IV</a></i>. In <i>Europa Universalis IV</i>, typing "bearhaslanded" into the command console will spawn Jan Mayen as a country in a random location. Players can also specify where Jan Mayen will spawn by including a province ID in the command.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
