<p><b>Goeric of Metz</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Goericus</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Goëri</i>; died September 19 643 AD), also known as <b>Abbo I of Metz</b>, <b>Goericus of Metz</b>, and <b>Gury of Metz</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Metz">bishop of Metz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> Churches.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Goeric_of_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was a married man with two daughters. He recovered his eyesight at St. Stephen's in Metz. Shortly thereafter, he joined the clergy and was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> by <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">Arnulf of Metz</a>. In 627, he succeeded Arnulf as bishop of Metz.
</p><p>As bishop, he transferred the relics of his predecessor Arnulf to the Church of the Apostles. He also built the church of Great St. Peter's and the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Epinal" class="mw-redirect" title="Epinal">Epinal</a> for his two daughters, Precia and Victorina.  He was also a personal friend of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>.
</p><p>He died in 643. He has the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of September 19. In the 10th century, his relics were brought from Saint-Symphorien to Epinal. This event is commemorated in the local <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">Calendar of Saints</a> on April 15.
</p>
