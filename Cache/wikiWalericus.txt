<p>Saint <b>Walaric</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> modern French <b>Valery</b> (died 620), was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> monk turned hermit who founded the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Saint-Valery-sur-Somme (page does not exist)">abbey of Saint-Valery-sur-Somme</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/abbey_of_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme" class="extiw" title="fr:abbey of Saint-Valery-sur-Somme">fr</a>&#93;</span>. His cult was recognized in <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy" title="Duchy of Normandy">Normandy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Medieval_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval England">England</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Walaric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walaric was born in the <a href="/wiki/Auvergne" title="Auvergne">Auvergne</a> to a peasant family. Taught to read at a young age, he abandoned the occupation of tending sheep to join the abbey of Autumo. He later moved on to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Germain_d%27Auxerre" title="Abbey of Saint-Germain d&#39;Auxerre">abbey of Saint-Germain d'Auxerre</a> and finally the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Luxeuil" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Luxeuil">abbey of Luxeuil</a> under the famous abbot <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>. At Luxeuil he was renowned for his horticultural skills. His ability to protect his vegetables from insects was regarded as miraculous.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_II" title="Theuderic II">Theuderic II</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy" title="Kingdom of Burgundy">Burgundy</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;595–613</span>), expelled Columbanus from his domains, Walaric and a fellow monk named Waldolanus left the kingdom to preach the gospel in <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> and, according to tradition, the <a href="/wiki/Pas-de-Calais" title="Pas-de-Calais">Pas-de-Calais</a>. He eventually settled down as a hermit at a place called Leuconay near the mouth of the <a href="/wiki/Somme_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Somme River">Somme River</a>. A community of disciples grew up around him. After his death, his successor Blitmund (Blimont) built a monastery for the community, which came to bear Walaric's name. The village that developed around the monastery still does: <a href="/wiki/Saint-Valery-sur-Somme" title="Saint-Valery-sur-Somme">Saint-Valery-sur-Somme</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Memory">Memory</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Walaric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Memory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/220px-Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="160" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/330px-Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/440px-Abbaye_de_Saint-Valery-sur-Somme_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4328" data-file-height="3140" /></a><figcaption>Walaric's abbey in the 17th century</figcaption></figure>
<p>A biography (<a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">saint's life</a>) of Walaric was composed in the 11th century. It was wrongly attributed to a certain Raginbertus.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The so-called "Valerian prophecy" was a legend originating in Walaric's abbey and the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Riquier" title="Saint-Riquier">Saint-Riquier</a> intended to refute the claims of the early 11th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Francorum_Senonensis" title="Historia Francorum Senonensis">Historia Francorum Senonensis</a></i> that the <a href="/wiki/Capetian_dynasty" title="Capetian dynasty">Capetian dynasty</a> were illegitimate usurpers. According to the legend, Walaric appeared in a vision to <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Capet" title="Hugh Capet">Hugh Capet</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;987–996</span>), the first Capetian, and thanked him for rescuing his body from the <a href="/wiki/Carolingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingians">Carolingians</a>. He prophesied that the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">kingdom of France</a> would belong to Hugh's heirs "until the seventh generation". Interpreted figuratively, the number seven signified perfection and thus eternity; interpreted literally, it meant that the <a href="/wiki/Philip_Augustus" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Augustus">Philip Augustus</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;1180–1223</span>) would be the last Capetian.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cures were claimed from an early date at Walaric's tomb. Duke <a href="/wiki/William_II_of_Normandy" class="mw-redirect" title="William II of Normandy">William II of Normandy</a> had Walaric's relics put on public display and invoked his name in a prayer for a favourable wind for his <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">invasion of England</a>. The invasion fleet sailed from Saint-Valery-sur-Somme in 1066.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walaric's cult thus spread to England, where a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Alnmouth" title="Alnmouth">Alnmouth</a> was dedicated to him in the 12th century. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was celebrated on 1 April in <a href="/wiki/Chester_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Chester Abbey">Chester Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Croyland_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Croyland Abbey">Croyland Abbey</a>. King <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_England" title="Richard I of England">Richard I of England</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;1189–1199</span>) transferred his relics from Saint-Valery-sur-Somme to <a href="/wiki/Saint-Valery-en-Caux" title="Saint-Valery-en-Caux">Saint-Valery-en-Caux</a>. His translation (transfer of relics) was celebrated in Chester and Croyland on 12 December. His abbey in Saint-Valery-sur-Somme, however, later recovered his relics.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The English village of <a href="/wiki/Hinton_Waldrist" title="Hinton Waldrist">Hinton Waldrist</a> is named after its 12th-century lord, Thomas de Saint-Valery.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
