<p>

<b>Thomas Palasor</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (c. 1570 - 9 August 1600) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1987. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Palasor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Palasor was born at <a href="/wiki/Ellerton-on-Swale" title="Ellerton-on-Swale">Ellerton-on-Swale</a>, parish of <a href="/wiki/Catterick,_North_Yorkshire" title="Catterick, North Yorkshire">Catterick</a>, North Riding of Yorkshire.  He arrived at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> on 24 July 1592, and set out for the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Valladolid" title="English College, Valladolid">English College, Valladolid</a> on 24 August 1592. There, he was ordained priest in 1596.
</p><p>He was arrested in the house of <b>John Norton</b>, of Ravensworth, nearly <a href="/wiki/Lamesley" title="Lamesley">Lamesley</a>, County Durham. Norton seems to have been the second son of Richard Norton, of <a href="/wiki/Norton_Conyers" title="Norton Conyers">Norton Conyers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Attainted" class="mw-redirect" title="Attainted">attainted</a> for his share in the <a href="/wiki/Rising_of_the_North" title="Rising of the North">Rising of the North</a> in 1569. Norton and his wife (if the identification is correct, she was his second wife, Margaret, daughter of Christopher Redshaw of Owston) were arrested at the same time, with a noble English gentleman, the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_John_Talbot" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed John Talbot">Blessed John Talbot</a>, one of the Talbots of <a href="/wiki/Thornton-le-Street" title="Thornton-le-Street">Thornton-le-Street</a>, North Riding of Yorkshire.
</p><p>All four were tried at <a href="/wiki/Durham,_England" title="Durham, England">Durham</a> and condemned to death, Palasor for being a priest, and the others for assisting him. Another gentleman was condemned at the same time but saved his life by conforming to the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>, as the others might have done. Mrs. Norton, being supposed to be with child, was reprieved. The three others were executed together.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Richard_Challoner" title="Richard Challoner">Richard Challoner</a> tells how an attempt to poison Palasor and his companions made by the jailer's wife resulted in the conversion of her maid-servant Mary Day. Thomas Palasor, John Norton and John Talbot were beatified as members of the <a href="/wiki/Eighty-five_martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Eighty-five martyrs of England and Wales">Eighty-five martyrs of England and Wales</a> by Pope John Paul II at the Vatican on 22 November, 1987.
</p>
