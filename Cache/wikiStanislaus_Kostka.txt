<p><b>Stanisław Kostka</b> S.J. (28 October 1550&#160;– 15 August 1568) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>. He is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> as <b>Saint Stanislaus Kostka</b> (as distinct from his namesake, the 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" title="Archbishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a> <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w" title="Stanislaus of Szczepanów">Stanislaus the Martyr</a>).
</p><p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Rostkowo,_Przasnysz_County" title="Rostkowo, Przasnysz County">Rostkowo, Przasnysz County</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, on 28 October 1550, and died in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during the night of 14–15 August 1568. He entered the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on his 17th birthday (28 October 1567), and is said to have foretold his death a few days before it occurred.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His father was a senator of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_(1385%E2%80%931569)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)">Kingdom of Poland</a> and castellan of <a href="/wiki/Zakroczym" title="Zakroczym">Zakroczym</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-global_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-global-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> his mother was Małgorzata Kryska from Drobni (Margaret de Drobniy Kryska), the sister and niece of the <a href="/wiki/Masovian_Voivodeship_(1526%E2%80%931795)" title="Masovian Voivodeship (1526–1795)">voivodes of Masovia</a> and the aunt of the celebrated <a href="/wiki/Kanclerz" class="mw-redirect" title="Kanclerz">Chancellor of Poland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Feliks_Kryski" title="Feliks Kryski">Feliks Kryski</a> (Felix Kryski)(Szczęsny Kryski). He was the second of seven children. His older brother Paweł (Paul) survived to be present at the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> ceremony of Stanislaus in 1605. At home, the two brothers were taught with firmness, even severity; its results were their piety, modesty, and temperance.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="School_life">School life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: School life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 25 July 1564, they arrived at <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> with their tutor to attend the Jesuit college that had been opened four years before. Stanislaus was soon conspicuous among his classmates during his three years of schooling, not only for his amiability and cheerfulness of expression, but also for his growing religious fervour and piety.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His brother Paul said during the process of beatification that "He devoted himself so completely to spiritual things that he frequently became unconscious, especially in the church of the Jesuit Fathers at Vienna." One of the practices of devotion which he joined while at Vienna was the Congregation of <a href="/wiki/St._Barbara" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Barbara">St. Barbara</a> and Our Lady, "of which he, with numbers of the pupils of the Society of Jesus" also belonged.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Stanislaus alleged to a fellow-member of the Society at Rome that Saint Barbara brought two <a href="/wiki/Angels" class="mw-redirect" title="Angels">angels</a> to him during the course of a serious illness, in order to give him the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>. His tutor, John Bilinski, witnessed the miracle, and though he himself did not see what Stanislaus claimed to see, he "was certain that Stanislaus was not at all out of his mind through the violence of his sickness.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>"
</p><p>Exasperated by his younger brother's piety, Paul began to mistreat Stanislaus. Stanislaus suffered the unjust treatment with remarkable stoicism and patience, but "one night after Stanislaus  had again suffered the harsh comments and blows of his brother, he turned on Paul with the words: 'Your rough treatment will end in my going away never to return, and you will have to explain my leaving to our father and mother.' Paul's sole reply was to swear violently at him."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Entry_into_the_Society_of_Jesus">Entry into the Society of Jesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Entry into the Society of Jesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The thought of joining the Society of Jesus had already entered the mind of the saintly young man. It was six months, however, before he ventured to speak of this to the superiors of the Society. At Vienna they hesitated to receive him, fearing the tempest that would probably be raised by his father against the Society, which had just quieted a storm unleashed by other admissions to the Company. Another Jesuit suggested he go to <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a>, Germany where <a href="/wiki/Peter_Canisius" title="Peter Canisius">Peter Canisius</a> was provincial. The distance was over four hundred miles, which had to be made on foot, without equipment or guide or any other resources but that did not deter him.<sup id="cite_ref-global_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-global-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Andrea_pozzo,_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG/225px-Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG" decoding="async" width="225" height="279" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG/338px-Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG/450px-Andrea_pozzo%2C_bozzetti_a_tempera_per_le_storie_di_san_stanislao_kostka_02_stanislao_perseguitato_dal_fratello_paolo_a_vienna.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2208" data-file-height="2740" /></a><figcaption>Stanislaus Kostka beaten by his brother, painting by <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Pozzo" title="Andrea Pozzo">Andrea Pozzo</a></figcaption></figure>
<blockquote><p>On the morning of the day on which he was to carry out his project he called his servant to him early and told him to notify his brother Paul and his tutor in the course of the morning that he would not be back that day to dinner. Then he started, exchanging the dress of gentleman for that of a <a href="/wiki/Mendicant" title="Mendicant">mendicant</a>, which was the only way to escape the curiosity of those he met. By nightfall Paul and the tutor comprehended that Stanislaus had fled, as he had threatened. They were seized with a fierce anger, and as the day was ended the fugitive had gained a day over them. They started to follow him, but were not able to overtake him; either their exhausted horses refused to go further, or a wheel of their carriage would break, or, as the tutor frankly declared, they had mistaken the route, having left the city by a different road from the one which Stanislaus had taken. It is noticeable that in his testimony Paul gives no explanation of his ill-luck.</p></blockquote>
<blockquote><p>Stanislaus stayed for a month at <a href="/wiki/Dillingen_an_der_Donau" title="Dillingen an der Donau">Dillingen</a>, where the provincial of that time, Saint <a href="/wiki/Peter_Canisius" title="Peter Canisius">Peter Canisius</a>, put the young aspirant's vocation to the test by employing him in the boarding-school. He arrived 25 October 1567 in Rome. As he was greatly exhausted by the journey, the general of the order, Saint <a href="/wiki/Francis_Borgia" title="Francis Borgia">Francis Borgia</a>, would not permit him to enter the novitiate of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale" title="Sant&#39;Andrea al Quirinale">Saint Andrew</a> until several days later. During the ten remaining months of his life, according to the testimony of the master of novices, Father Giulio Fazio, "he was a model and mirror of religious perfection. Notwithstanding his very delicate constitution he did not spare himself the slightest penance".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had such a burning fever in his chest that he was often obliged to apply cold compresses."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On the evening of the feast of <a href="/wiki/Lawrence_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Lawrence of Rome">Saint Lawrence</a> (10 August), Stanislaus fell ill with a high fever, and clearly saw that his last hour had come. He wrote a letter to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a> begging her to call him to the skies there to celebrate with her the glorious anniversary of her Assumption (15 August).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His confidence in the Blessed Virgin, which had already brought him many favours, was this time again rewarded; on 15 August 1568, towards 4:00 in the morning, while he prayed he died. Many in the city proclaimed him a saint and people hastened from all parts to venerate his remains and to obtain, if possible, some relics.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_Kostka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> ratified his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> in 1605; he was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1726. St. Stanislaus is a popular saint of Poland, and many religious institutions have chosen him as the protector of their novitiates. The representations of him in art are quite varied; he is sometimes depicted receiving <a href="/wiki/Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Communion">Holy Communion</a> from the hands of angels, or receiving the <a href="/wiki/Infant_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Infant Jesus">Infant Jesus</a> from the hands of the Virgin, or in the midst of a battle putting to flight the enemies of his country. At times he is depicted near a fountain putting a wet <a href="/wiki/Linen" title="Linen">linen</a> cloth on his breast. He is invoked for <a href="/wiki/Heart_palpitation" class="mw-redirect" title="Heart palpitation">palpitations of the heart</a> and for dangerous cases of illness.
</p><p>On 15 August 2018 Pope Francis wrote to the Bishop of <a href="/wiki/P%C5%82ock" title="Płock">Płock</a> in honor of the 450th anniversary of Stanislaus's death. In his message, Pope Francis cites a maxim of Stanislaus's: “<b>Ad maiora natus sum</b> – 'I was born for greater things'".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Heiliger-Stanislaus-Kostka.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/Heiliger-Stanislaus-Kostka.jpg" decoding="async" width="201" height="355" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="201" data-file-height="355" /></a><figcaption>Portrait in stained glass, Church <a href="/wiki/Liesing" title="Liesing">Liesing</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>St. <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_of_Lisieux" title="Thérèse of Lisieux">Thérèse of Lisieux</a> wrote a “play” or “pious recreation” about Saint Stanislaus Kostka. Her sister Pauline had asked Thérèse to write verses and theatrical entertainment for liturgical and community festivals. "Like Stanislaus, [Therese] thought she was going to die young having accomplished nothing, 'with empty hands.' From then on, her desire to do good after her death intensified."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Saint_Stanislaus_College" title="Saint Stanislaus College">Saint Stanislaus College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bay_Saint_Louis,_Mississippi" class="mw-redirect" title="Bay Saint Louis, Mississippi">Bay Saint Louis, Mississippi</a>, a Catholic day and residential school for boys in grades 7 to 12, founded in 1854 and chartered in 1870 as Saint Stanislaus College</li></ul>
<ul><li>The novitiate of the Central and Southern Province of the Society of Jesus –Saint Stanislaus Kostka at Saint Charles College – located in Grand Coteau, Louisiana.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. In 2008, <a href="/wiki/St._John%27s_Jesuit_High_School_and_Academy" title="St. John&#39;s Jesuit High School and Academy">St. John's Jesuit High School and Academy</a> of Toledo, Ohio, is one of the schools that uses his name in their house system.</li></ul>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Kostka_Church_(Pittsburgh)" title="St. Stanislaus Kostka Church (Pittsburgh)">St. Stanislaus Kostka Church</a> is a parish in <a href="/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania">Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania</a>, US.</li></ul>
<ul><li><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.diocesemontreal.org/en/lets-connect/churches-parishes/saint-stanislas-de-kostka">Église Saint-Stanislas-de-Kostka</a> is a parish in Montreal located at 1350 Boulevard Saint-Joseph Est.</li></ul>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Stanislas-de-Kostka" title="Saint-Stanislas-de-Kostka">Saint-Stanislas-de-Kostka</a>, Quebec, Canada, a municipality southwest of <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>.</li></ul>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Forane_Church,_Mala" title="St. Stanislaus Forane Church, Mala">St. Stanislaus Forane Church, Mala</a> situated at <a href="/wiki/Mala,_Kerala" title="Mala, Kerala">Mala, Kerala</a>, is the only parish in <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> having St. Stanislaus as the patron saint.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. <ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_High_School" title="St. Stanislaus High School">St. Stanislaus High School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bandra" title="Bandra">Bandra</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mumbai" title="Mumbai">Mumbai</a>, is a Jesuit founded in 1863.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<ul><li>The <a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Institute_(Slovenia)" title="St. Stanislaus Institute (Slovenia)">St. Stanislaus Institute</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ljubljana" title="Ljubljana">Ljubljana</a>, <a href="/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia">Slovenia</a>, an educational institution founded in 1901, is named for Stanislaus Kostka.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> <figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG/220px-St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG/330px-St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG/440px-St._Stanislaus_Kostka.JPG 2x" data-file-width="683" data-file-height="1024" /></a><figcaption>St. Stanislaus Kostka. <ul><li>The high school campus of the <a href="/wiki/Ateneo_de_Manila_University" title="Ateneo de Manila University">Ateneo de Manila University</a> has a Saint Stanislaus Kostka chapel, noticeably at the center of the compound.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Saint Kostka is also the patron saint of the Ateneo de Manila High School. At the <a href="/wiki/Ateneo_de_Davao_University" title="Ateneo de Davao University">Ateneo de Davao University</a>, the grade school chapel, adorned with stained glass depictions of the life of Jesus Christ, was named after him.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup></li></ul>
<ul><li>Saint Stanislaus Kostka is the patron saint of the attendees of Oblates of Saint Joseph Minor Seminary (a high school-level seminary in San Jose, Batangas, Philippines), as well as Manukan, Zamboanga del Norte Philippines.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup></li></ul>
<ul><li>One of the junior campuses of the Jesuit school <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.xavier.vic.edu.au/">Xavier College</a> in Melbourne, Australia, is named <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070301093017/http://www.xavier.vic.edu.au/AboutXavier/kostkaoverview.htm">Kostka Hall</a>; <a href="/wiki/Boston_College" title="Boston College">Boston College</a> also has named one of their freshman dorms Kostka Hall. He is also a patron of the minor seminarians of the Oblates of St. Joseph Minor Seminary.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. In Belgium, there is <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Saint-Stanislas" title="Collège Saint-Stanislas">Collège Saint-Stanislas</a> in Mons.</li></ul>

<ul><li>Also, a public school in <a href="/wiki/Guyana" title="Guyana">Guyana</a> is named after him, <a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_College_(Guyana)" title="St. Stanislaus College (Guyana)">St. Stanislaus College</a>.</li></ul>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Kostka_Church_(Chicago)" title="St. Stanislaus Kostka Church (Chicago)">St. Stanislaus Kostka Church (Chicago)</a> opened in 1867. By 1897, St. Stanislaus Kostka Parish was the largest parish in the United States with 8,000 families, totaling 40,000 people. There were twelve Masses each Sunday: six Masses in the upper church and another six Masses in the lower church. St. Stanislaus Kostka Parish is considered the mother church of the many Polish parishes.</li></ul>
<ul><li>On November 10, 2011, the <a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Kostka_Church_(Winona,_Minnesota)" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stanislaus Kostka Church (Winona, Minnesota)">Church of Saint Stanislaus Kostka</a> in Winona, Minnesota, was elevated by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> to the status of <a href="/wiki/Minor_basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Minor basilica">Minor Basilica</a>, making it the first ever Basilica of Saint Stanislaus Kostka.</li></ul>

<ul><li>The first Catholic secondary school in <a href="/wiki/Tonga" title="Tonga">Tonga</a>, founded in 1865, was named after St Stanislaus. This same school became known as <a href="/wiki/Apifo%27ou_College" title="Apifo&#39;ou College">Apifo'ou College</a> since 1987.</li></ul>
