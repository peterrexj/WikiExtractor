<p><b>Elizabeth of Aragon</b>, more commonly known as <b>Saint Elizabeth of Portugal</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">T.O.S.F.</a> (1271 – 4 July 1336; <i>Elisabet</i> in <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>, <i>Isabel</i> in <a href="/wiki/Aragonese_language" title="Aragonese language">Aragonese</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>), was <a href="/wiki/Queen_consort" title="Queen consort">queen consort</a> of <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">tertiary</a> of the Franciscan Order and is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_and_early_life">Family and early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family and early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Isabel_Dinis_de_Portugal.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Isabel_Dinis_de_Portugal.jpg/200px-Isabel_Dinis_de_Portugal.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="360" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/34/Isabel_Dinis_de_Portugal.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="250" data-file-height="450" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Isabel_Dinis_de_Portugal.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>King <a href="/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal" title="Denis of Portugal">Denis of Portugal</a>, the <i>Rei Lavrador</i>, and Queen Elizabeth of Portugal</div></div></div>
<p>Born in 1271 into the royal house of Aragon,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Elizabeth was the daughter of <i><a href="/wiki/Infante" title="Infante">Infante</a></i> Peter (later King <a href="/wiki/Peter_III_of_Arag%C3%B3n" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter III of Aragón">Peter III</a>) and his wife <a href="/wiki/Constance_of_Sicily,_Queen_of_Aragon" title="Constance of Sicily, Queen of Aragon">Constance of Sicily</a> and the sister of three kings: <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Aragon" title="Alfonso III of Aragon">Alfonso II</a> and <a href="/wiki/James_II_of_Aragon" title="James II of Aragon">James II of Aragon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Frederick_III_of_Sicily" title="Frederick III of Sicily">Frederick III of Sicily</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Great niece and namesake of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a>, she was the original source of the <a href="/wiki/Miracle_of_the_roses" title="Miracle of the roses">bread to roses miracle</a> often depicted in art of her better known great aunt. The kingly suspicion of generosity to poor people is far more characteristic of her warlike and abusive husband than Elizabeth of Hungary's loving and pious spouse, <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia" title="Louis IV, Landgrave of Thuringia">Louis</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Elizabeth was educated very piously, and led a life of strict regularity from her childhood: she said the full Divine Office daily, fasted and did other penances.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her marriage to King <a href="/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal" title="Denis of Portugal">Denis of Portugal</a> was arranged in 1281 when she was 10 years old, receiving the towns of <a href="/wiki/%C3%93bidos,_Portugal" title="Óbidos, Portugal">Óbidos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abrantes" title="Abrantes">Abrantes</a> and <a href="/wiki/Porto_de_M%C3%B3s" title="Porto de Mós">Porto de Mós</a> as part of her <a href="/wiki/Dowry" title="Dowry">dowry</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Romcambolescas_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Romcambolescas-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was only in 1288 that the wedding was celebrated, when Denis was 26 years old, while Elizabeth was 17.<sup id="cite_ref-Romcambolescas_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Romcambolescas-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal" title="Denis of Portugal">Denis</a>, a poet and statesman, was known as the <i>Rei Lavrador</i> (English: <span lang="en">Farmer King</span>), because he planted a large pine forest near <a href="/wiki/Leiria" title="Leiria">Leiria</a> to prevent the <a href="/wiki/Soil_degradation" class="mw-redirect" title="Soil degradation">soil degradation</a> that threatened the region.<sup id="cite_ref-Livermore2004_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Livermore2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Elizabeth quietly pursued the regular religious practices of her youth and was devoted to the poor and sick. Such a life was taken as a reproach to many around her and caused ill will in some quarters.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Eventually, her prayer and patience succeeded in converting her husband, who had been leading a sinful life of spousal abuse and  adultery.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Elizabeth took an active interest in Portuguese politics and was a decisive conciliator during the negotiations concerning the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Alca%C3%B1ices_(1297)" class="mw-redirect" title="Treaty of Alcañices (1297)">Treaty of Alcañices</a>, signed by Denis and Fernando IV of Castile in 1297 (which fixed the borders between the two countries). In 1304, the Queen and Denis returned to Spain to arbitrate between <a href="/wiki/Fernando_IV_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Fernando IV of Castile">Fernando IV of Castile</a> and James II of Aragon, brother of Elizabeth.<sup id="cite_ref-Romcambolescas_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Romcambolescas-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
She had two children, a daughter named <a href="/wiki/Constance_of_Portugal" title="Constance of Portugal">Constance</a>, who married King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_IV_of_Castile" title="Ferdinand IV of Castile">Ferdinand IV of Castile</a> and also a son Afonso (who later became King <a href="/wiki/Afonso_IV_of_Portugal" title="Afonso IV of Portugal">Afonso IV of Portugal</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FarmerFarmer2011_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FarmerFarmer2011-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:A_Rainha_Santa_em_Alvalade_(Roque_Gameiro,_Quadros_da_Hist%C3%B3ria_de_Portugal,_1917).png" class="image"><img alt="" civil war in 1323, on the field of Alvalade</div></div></div><p>Elizabeth would serve as intermediary between her husband and Afonso, during the Civil War between 1322 and 1324. The <a href="/wiki/Infante" title="Infante">Infante</a> greatly resented the king, whom he accused of favoring the king's illegitimate son, Afonso Sanches. Repulsed to <a href="/wiki/Alenquer_Municipality,_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Alenquer Municipality, Portugal">Alenquer</a>, which supported the Infante, Denis was prevented from killing his son through the intervention of the Queen, when she, in 1323, mounted on a mule, positioned herself between both opposing armies on the field of Alvalade in order to prevent the combat. Peace returned in 1324, once the illegitimate son was sent into exile, and the Infante swore loyalty to the king.<sup id="cite_ref-Romcambolescas_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Romcambolescas-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Dowager_Queen">Dowager Queen</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Dowager Queen">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Denis' death in 1325, Elizabeth retired to the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare_nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare nuns">Poor Clare nuns</a>, now known as the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Santa_Clara-a-Velha" title="Monastery of Santa Clara-a-Velha">Monastery of Santa Clara-a-Velha</a> (which she had founded in 1314) in <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a>. She joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, devoting the rest of her life to the poor and sick in obscurity.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During the great <a href="/wiki/Famine" title="Famine">famine</a> in 1293, she donated flour from her cellars to the starving in Coimbra. She was also known for being modest in her dress and humble in conversation, for providing lodging for pilgrims, distributing small gifts, paying the dowries of poor girls, and educating the children of poor nobles. She was a benefactor of various hospitals (Coimbra, Santarém and Leiria) and of religious projects (such as the Trinity Convent in Lisbon, chapels in Leiria and Óbidos, and the <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alcoba%C3%A7a,_Portugal" title="Alcobaça, Portugal">Alcobaça</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Romcambolescas2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Romcambolescas2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was called to act once more as a peacemaker in 1336, when Afonso IV marched his troops against King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_XI_of_Castile" title="Alfonso XI of Castile">Alfonso XI of Castile</a>, to whom he had married his daughter Maria, and who had neglected and ill-treated her. In spite of age and weakness, the Queen-dowager insisted on hurrying to <a href="/wiki/Estremoz" title="Estremoz">Estremoz</a>, where the two kings' armies were drawn up. She again stopped the fighting and caused terms of peace to be arranged. But the exertion brought on her final illness.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As soon as her mission was completed, she took to her bed with a fever from which she died on 4 July, in the castle of Estremoz. She earned the title of <i>Peacemaker</i> on account of her efficacy in solving disputes.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although Denis' tomb was located in <a href="/wiki/Odivelas" title="Odivelas">Odivelas</a>, Elizabeth was buried in the Convent of Santa Clara in Coimbra,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in a magnificent <a href="/wiki/Gothic_art" title="Gothic art">Gothic</a> sarcophagus. After frequent flooding by the <a href="/wiki/Mondego_River" title="Mondego River">Mondego River</a> in the 17th century, the Poor Clares moved her mortal remains to the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Santa_Clara-a-Nova" title="Monastery of Santa Clara-a-Nova">Monastery of Santa Clara-a-Nova</a> (also in Coimbra). Her body was transferred to the main chapel, where it was buried in a sarcophagus of silver and crystal.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg/170px-Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="261" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg/255px-Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="313" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro_isabel_portugal.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Portrait by <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Gil_de_Castro" title="José Gil de Castro">José Gil de Castro</a></div></div></div>
<p>She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1526 and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> on 24 June 1626.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Her feast was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 4 July. In the year 1694 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> moved her feast to 8 July, so it would not conflict with the celebration of the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">Octave</a> of Saints Peter and Paul, Apostles.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1955, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> abolished this octave.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The 1962 Roman Missal changed the rank of the feast from "Double" to "Third-Class Feast".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the Calendar classified the celebration as an optional <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> and restored it to 4 July. Her feast is also kept on the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">Calendar of Saints</a>. Since the establishment in 1819 of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of San Cristóbal de La Laguna">Diocese of San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a> (<a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>),  Saint Elizabeth is the co-patron of the diocese and of its <a href="/wiki/La_Laguna_Cathedral" title="La Laguna Cathedral">cathedral</a> pursuant to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> issued by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
In the United States her memorial has been transferred to 5 July since 4 July is the date of the independence of that nation, a national holiday.
</p><p>Elizabeth is usually depicted in royal garb with a dove or an olive branch.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Popular_culture">Popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was the subject of a 1947 Portuguese-Spanish film, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Holy_Queen" class="mw-redirect" title="The Holy Queen">The Holy Queen</a></i>, in which she was played by <a href="/wiki/Maruchi_Fresno" title="Maruchi Fresno">Maruchi Fresno</a>. In Portuguese popular culture, she is commonly associated with a "<a href="/wiki/Miracle_of_the_roses" title="Miracle of the roses">miracle of the roses</a>".
</p><p>The young adult <a href="/wiki/Historical_fantasy" title="Historical fantasy">historical fantasy</a> novel <i>A Curse of Roses</i> by Portuguese author Diana Pinguicha retells her story as a princess who can turn food into flowers and falls for an <a href="/wiki/Enchanted_Moura" title="Enchanted Moura">Enchanted Moura</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
