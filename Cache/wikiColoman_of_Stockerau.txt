<p><b>Coloman of Stockerau</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Colmán</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Colomannus</i>; died 18 October 1012) was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. While on pilgrimage to the Holy Land, he was mistaken for a spy and hanged near Vienna.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Coloman_of_Stockerau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Originally known as Colmán (variously rendered Koloman, Kálmán, Colman, and Colomannus), he was an Irish pilgrim en route to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> and was mistaken for a <a href="/wiki/Spy" class="mw-redirect" title="Spy">spy</a> because of his strange appearance. He was tortured and hanged at <a href="/wiki/Stockerau" title="Stockerau">Stockerau</a>, near Vienna, Austria, on 16 July 1012.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Later tradition has it that he was "a son of <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1el_Sechnaill_mac_Domnaill" title="Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill">Máel-Sechnaill</a> (d. 1022), high king of Ireland." (Breen, 2009)
</p><p>At the time of his death, there were continual skirmishes among <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>. Coloman spoke no <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>, so he could not give an understandable account of himself. He was <a href="/wiki/Hanging" title="Hanging">hanged</a> alongside several robbers.
</p><p>According to Aidan Breen, "He was made a saint by the local people, possibly out of remorse for the deed and because of his endurance under torture and the many miracles reported from where his body was buried." (Breen, 2009)
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Coloman_of_Stockerau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Melk71.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Melk71.jpg/150px-Melk71.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Melk71.jpg/225px-Melk71.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Melk71.jpg/300px-Melk71.jpg 2x" data-file-width="960" data-file-height="1280" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Melk71.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sarcophagus of Coloman. Melk Abbey, Austria.</div></div></div> 
<p>On 13 October 1014, his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were transferred to the Abbey of Melk by Bishop Megingard at the request of <a href="/wiki/Henry_I,_Margrave_of_Austria" title="Henry I, Margrave of Austria">Marquis Henry of Austria</a>. Decades later, they were taken to Hungary.  Coleman became the object of a popular cult, and many churches and chapels in Austria, the <a href="/wiki/Electorate_of_the_Palatinate" class="mw-redirect" title="Electorate of the Palatinate">Electorate of the Palatinate</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> were dedicated to him.  He is also venerated in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.
</p><p>A legend states that Coloman's body remained incorruptible for eighteen months, remaining undisturbed by birds and beasts.  The <a href="/wiki/Scaffolding" title="Scaffolding">scaffolding</a> itself is said to have taken root and to have blossomed with green branches, one of which is preserved under the high altar of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> church at Stockerau.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/G%C3%A9za_I_of_Hungary" title="Géza I of Hungary">Géza I of Hungary</a> named one of his sons, King <a href="/wiki/Coloman_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Coloman of Hungary">Coleman of Hungary</a>, in his honor. In the 13th century, the younger brother of King <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary" title="Béla IV of Hungary">Bela IV of Hungary</a> was named <a href="/wiki/Coloman_of_Galicia-Lodomeria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coloman of Galicia-Lodomeria">Coloman of Galicia-Lodomeria</a> in honor of Coloman.
</p><p>Eventually, the relics of Coloman were taken back from the Cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r" title="Székesfehérvár">Székesfehérvár</a> to Melk Abbey in Austria, where they are still kept. Many Austrian rulers made modifications to the tomb of Coloman, and the actual reliquary was made in the <a href="/wiki/Baroque_style" class="mw-redirect" title="Baroque style">Baroque style</a>.
</p>
