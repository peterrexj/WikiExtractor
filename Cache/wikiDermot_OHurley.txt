<p><b>Dermot O'Hurley</b> (c. 1530 – 19 or 20 June 1584)—also <i>Dermod or Dermond O'Hurley</i>: <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Diarmaid Ó hUrthuile</i>—was the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cashel,_Tipperary" class="mw-redirect" title="Cashel, Tipperary">Cashel</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Tudor_conquest_of_Ireland" title="Tudor conquest of Ireland">Tudor conquest of Ireland</a>. After being held and tortured in <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a>, Archbishop O'Hurley was put to death, officially for <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a>, but in reality as part of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_persecution" title="Religious persecution">religious persecution</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland" title="Catholic Church in Ireland">Catholic Church in Ireland</a> by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a> and her officials. He is one of the most celebrated of the 24 formally recognized <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a>, and was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 September 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-bohurl_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bohurl-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>O'Hurley was born into the <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_nobility_of_Ireland" title="Gaelic nobility of Ireland">Gaelic nobility of Ireland</a>, either in or near <a href="/wiki/Emly" title="Emly">Emly</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>, around the year 1530. His father, William, was the <a href="/wiki/O%27Hurley" title="O&#39;Hurley">O'Hurley</a> <a href="/wiki/Clan" title="Clan">clan</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Chief_of_the_Name" title="Chief of the Name">Chief of the Name</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bailiff" title="Bailiff">Bailiff</a> of Emly, with duties similar to a <a href="/wiki/Tacksman" title="Tacksman">Tacksman</a>, for <a href="/wiki/James_FitzGerald,_14th_Earl_of_Desmond" class="mw-redirect" title="James FitzGerald, 14th Earl of Desmond">James FitzGerald, 14th Earl of Desmond</a>. Dermot's mother was Honora Ni Brien, a descendant of the <a href="/wiki/O%27Brien_dynasty" title="O&#39;Brien dynasty">O'Brien dynasty</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thomond" title="Thomond">Thomond</a>. Both sides of the Archbishop's family claimed descent from the royal <a href="/wiki/Derbhfine" class="mw-redirect" title="Derbhfine">derbhfine</a> of the  <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_gCais" class="mw-redirect" title="Dál gCais">Dál gCais</a>, one of the most powerful <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> in the history of <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> or of <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland" title="Gaelic Ireland">Gaelic Ireland</a>. The future Archbishop had one sister named Honora Ni Hurley. The "William Oge O'Hurley" listed among the Desmond rebels to be pardoned is believed to have been the Archbishop's brother. He also had much younger brother named Andrew O'Hurley, whom, as of 1642, was over 80 years old, blind, paralyzed, and living in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The future Archbishop is believed to have received his early education at a Cathedral school overseen by his kinsman, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Thomas_O%27Herlahy" title="Thomas O&#39;Herlahy">Thomas O'Hurley</a>, in the monastery founded by Saint <a href="/wiki/Ailbe_of_Emly" title="Ailbe of Emly">Ailbe of Emly</a>. The O'Hurley family later moved to Lickadoon Castle, <a href="/wiki/Ballyneety" title="Ballyneety">Ballyneety</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Limerick" title="County Limerick">County Limerick</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where O'Hurley was educated by tutors and then sent to <a href="/wiki/Flemish_Brabant" title="Flemish Brabant">Flemish Brabant</a> to study at the <a href="/wiki/Old_University_of_Leuven" title="Old University of Leuven">University of Leuven</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBoylan1998_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoylan1998-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1551 he graduated with a <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts" title="Master of Arts">Master of Arts</a> degree, then a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate_of_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Doctorate of Law">doctorate of Law</a> and was appointed a professor of philosophy in one of that university's greater colleges, where he remained for 15 years and acquired a reputation for his commentaries on <a href="/wiki/Aristotle" title="Aristotle">Aristotle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200566_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200566-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1574 he was appointed a professor of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon</a> and civil law in the <a href="/wiki/Rheims_Faculty_of_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims Faculty of Law">Faculty of Law</a> of <a href="/wiki/Reims_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Reims University">Reims University</a> by <a href="/wiki/Louis_de_Guise" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis de Guise">Louis de Guise</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200567_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200567-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> at which he spent 4 years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Fugitive_archbishop">Fugitive archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Fugitive archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1570 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> excommunicated Queen <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I of England</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Regnans_in_Excelsis" title="Regnans in Excelsis">Regnans in Excelsis</a></i>. This led to the <a href="/wiki/Second_Desmond_Rebellion" title="Second Desmond Rebellion">Second Desmond Rebellion</a> in 1579–83, which was still in progress when O'Hurley was required to travel to Ireland. On 11 September 1581, while still a <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">layman</a>, he was appointed Archbishop of Cashel by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII" title="Pope Gregory XIII">Pope Gregory XIII</a>. He was ordained and consecrated and set out on his mission in 1583.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Through its elaborate spy system, the government in <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a> had immediate knowledge of Dermot's appointment to the See of Cashel, and Elizabeth's spies and <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a> were soon on his tracks.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Although it was later claimed by Lord Justices <a href="/wiki/Adam_Loftus_(bishop)" title="Adam Loftus (bishop)">Adam Loftus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wallop" title="Henry Wallop">Henry Wallop</a> in their letters to Sir <a href="/wiki/Francis_Walsingham" title="Francis Walsingham">Francis Walsingham</a> that Archbishop O'Hurley had been employed by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Inquisition" title="Roman Inquisition">Roman Inquisition</a>, this is not sustainable by other evidence. For example, a 33-line work of <a href="/wiki/Praise_poetry" class="mw-redirect" title="Praise poetry">praise poetry</a> in <a href="/wiki/Renaissance_Latin" title="Renaissance Latin">Renaissance Latin</a>, which was composed to celebrate Dermot O'Hurley's promotion to the Episcopate, confirms that he had always been merely a theology professor.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>O'Hurley's voyage was fraught with danger because of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Desmond_Rebellion#1580_Spanish_and_Papal_landing" title="Second Desmond Rebellion">state of war</a> between the Pope and England, but he accepted the risks involved and arranged for a sea captain from <a href="/wiki/Drogheda" title="Drogheda">Drogheda</a> to smuggle him from the French port of <a href="/wiki/Le_Croisic" title="Le Croisic">Le Croisic</a> into <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. Archbishop O'Hurley disembarked upon Holmpatrick Strand in what is now <a href="/wiki/Skerries,_Dublin" title="Skerries, Dublin">Skerries</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Dublin" title="County Dublin">County Dublin</a> in the autumn of 1583<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and was met by a priest named Fr. John Dillon, who accompanied him to <a href="/wiki/Drogheda" title="Drogheda">Drogheda</a>, where they lodged in a <a href="/wiki/Hostelry" class="mw-redirect" title="Hostelry">hostelry</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Archbishop O'Hurley's letters, which had been sent via a different ship, were intercepted by <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_Sea_Dogs" title="Elizabethan Sea Dogs">English pirates</a>, who handed the letters over to the Lord Justices in Dublin.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg/220px-Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg/330px-Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg/440px-Cavan_Towne_Map_1591.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>Map of Cavan town from 1591 showing its market square and the Clan O'Reilly castle and stronghold upon Tullymongan Hill</figcaption></figure>
<p>After being advised by a resident of Drogheda that Lord Justices Loftus and Wallop already knew their location, Archbishop O'Hurley and Fr. Dillon decided to leave for <a href="/wiki/Slane_Castle" title="Slane Castle">Slane Castle</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> where they were concealed by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Fleming,_10th_Baron_Slane" title="Thomas Fleming, 10th Baron Slane">Thomas Fleming, 10th Baron Slane</a>, at one point inside a <a href="/wiki/Priest_hole" title="Priest hole">priest hole</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Archbishop O'Hurley also covertly travelled from Slane Castle to <a href="/wiki/Cavan" title="Cavan">Cavan</a> to visit with some fellow priests whom he had known while living in <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Europe" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Europe">Catholic Europe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> At the time, Cavan town and <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a> was the territory of the <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Clan</a> <a href="/wiki/O%27Reilly" title="O&#39;Reilly">O'Reilly</a>. However, O'Hurley was recognised during a visit to <a href="/wiki/Slane_Castle" title="Slane Castle">Slane Castle</a> by the Baron's first cousin, <a href="/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Common_Pleas_for_Ireland" title="Chief Justice of the Common Pleas for Ireland">Lord Chief Justice</a> Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Dillon_(died_1597)" title="Robert Dillon (died 1597)">Robert Dillon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Philip_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Philip O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Philip O'Sullivan Beare</a>, "A grave question was started at dinner, in the presence of the squint-eyed Robert Dillon, one of the Queen's judges. The heretics, giving each his own opinion, freely proceeded to such extreme folly, that Dermot, who was present, and long kept silent lest he should betray himself, could not any longer stand their rashness, and so, to the great astonishment of all, he easily refuted the silly doctrines of the heretics, with an air of authority, and great eloquence and learning. Hereupon Dillon was led to surmise that this was some distinguished person who might greatly obstruct heresy."<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Once his suspicions were aroused, Sir Robert Dillon made inquiries, eventually uncovered the real identity of his cousin's houseguest,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and immediately informed <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a>. Baron Slane was immediately summoned by Lord Justices Loftus and Wallop and, under pain of severe penalties, agreed to arrest Archbishop Dermot O'Hurley.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg/220px-Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg/330px-Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg/440px-Ormonde_Castle_-_Carrick-on-Suir.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>The Elizabethan Manor House at <a href="/wiki/Ormonde_Castle" title="Ormonde Castle">Ormonde Castle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Carrick-on-Suir" title="Carrick-on-Suir">Carrick-on-Suir</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Meanwhile, the Archbishop had already left <a href="/wiki/Slane_Castle" title="Slane Castle">Slane Castle</a> and was staying with <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Butler,_10th_Earl_of_Ormond" title="Thomas Butler, 10th Earl of Ormond">Thomas Butler, 10th Earl of Ormond</a>, a Protestant, referred to as "Black Thomas" (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Tomás dubh</i>) (lit. "Thomas the Black", fig. "Thomas the <a href="/wiki/Puritanism" class="mw-redirect" title="Puritanism">Puritan</a>"), who was then the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Treasurer_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Treasurer of Ireland">Lord Treasurer of Ireland</a>.
</p><p>As his nickname suggests, the Earl was a Protestant and had played a role in ending the <a href="/wiki/Second_Desmond_Rebellion" title="Second Desmond Rebellion">Second Desmond Rebellion</a> through <a href="/wiki/Scorched_earth" title="Scorched earth">scorched earth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Total_war" title="Total war">total war</a> that triggered a State-imposed famine, which killed an estimated third of <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a>'s population. At the same time, however, the Earl's entire family, his <a href="/wiki/Old_English_(Ireland)" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English (Ireland)">Old English</a> tenants, and all the <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> subject to his leadership were Catholic <a href="/wiki/Recusancy_in_Ireland" title="Recusancy in Ireland">Recusants</a>, who were covertly being allowed <a href="/wiki/Religious_toleration" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious toleration">religious toleration</a>. Meanwhile, Loftus, Wallop, and many other officials in <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a>, greatly envied the Earl's favour with the Queen and kept him accordingly under constant surveillance in the hopes of implicating him in illegally tolerating Catholicism or in anything else that that might construe as <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a>. Despite the extremely high risk, however, the Earl of Ormond seems to have agreed to conceal and protect Archbishop Dermot O'Hurley, as long as he avoided matters other than his religious ministry and remained within the confines of <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While a guest at the Earl's still extant <a href="/wiki/Ormonde_Castle" title="Ormonde Castle">Elizabethan Manor House</a> at <a href="/wiki/Carrick-on-Suir" title="Carrick-on-Suir">Carrick-on-Suir</a>, the Archbishop wrote a letter to Dr. <a href="/wiki/Miler_Magrath" title="Miler Magrath">Miler Magrath</a>, the former <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a> who had become the Protestant <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a>'s Archbishop of Cashel. In the letter, Archbishop O'Hurley both requested a meeting<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and suggested nonviolent mutual <a href="/wiki/Religious_toleration" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious toleration">religious toleration</a> of one another's competing apostolates. The Earl of Ormond, to whom O'Hurley had assigned the delivery, did not trust Rev. Dr. Magrath and secretly kept the letter, which still survives at the <a href="/wiki/Bodleian_Library" title="Bodleian Library">Bodleian Library</a> at <a href="/wiki/Oxford_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford University">Oxford University</a> (MS Carte 55, fol. 546).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg/220px-IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg/330px-IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg/440px-IMGHolyCross_4780w.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey" title="Holy Cross Abbey">Holy Cross Abbey</a>, Thurles, <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Immediately after making a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey" title="Holy Cross Abbey">Holy Cross Abbey</a> near <a href="/wiki/Thurles" title="Thurles">Thurles</a><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> in September 1583, Archbishop O'Hurley was met at <a href="/wiki/Ormonde_Castle" title="Ormonde Castle">Ormonde Castle</a> by Baron Slane. The Baron explained the imminent danger to both himself and his family and in return, the Archbishop voluntarily agreed to travel with him and surrender at Dublin Castle.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During their journey, the conversation turned to the recent conversion to <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglicanism</a> by Rt.-Rev. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Power_(bishop)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter Power (bishop) (page does not exist)">Peter Power</a>, the Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ferns" title="Bishop of Ferns">Bishop of Ferns</a>. Archbishop O'Hurley commented about his former colleague, "Many who are lions before the battle are timid stags when the hour of trial comes. Lest this prove true of me, I daily pray to our good Lord for strength; for 'let him that thinketh himself to stand look lest he fall.'"<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The unwritten laws governing hospitality were considered sacred among the <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_nobility_of_Ireland" title="Gaelic nobility of Ireland">Gaelic nobility of Ireland</a> and were just as important to <a href="/wiki/Gaelicisation" title="Gaelicisation">Gaelicised</a> <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Normans" class="mw-redirect" title="Hiberno-Normans">Hiberno-Normans</a> like the Earl of Ormond. The Earl is alleged to have felt insulted by the arrest of a guest in his house who was under his protection. Later emigre chronicler <a href="/wiki/Philip_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Philip O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Philip O'Sullivan Beare</a> considered the Archbishop's arrest to be so heinous of an insult under the traditional <a href="/wiki/Code_of_conduct" title="Code of conduct">code of conduct</a> that the Earl should have raised the clans subject to him and taken up arms against Baron Slane and the Lord Justices. Even though the Earl chose not to take up arms, O'Sullivan Beare does credit him with doing everything he possibly could to save Archbishop O'Hurley from the executioners.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Other more recent historians, however, believe that the Earl of Ormond may secretly have colluded with Baron Slane and played just as central of a role in arranging the Archbishop's arrest.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_torture">Imprisonment and torture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and torture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bermingham_Tower,_Dublin_Castle.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg/220px-Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="366" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg/330px-Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg/440px-Bermingham_Tower%2C_Dublin_Castle.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1493" data-file-height="2482" /></a><figcaption>The Bermingham Tower, where Archbishop O'Hurley was held, as was typical for all other State prisoners at the time, is one of the sole parts of <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a> that still survives from the <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_era" title="Elizabethan era">Elizabethan era</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>On 8 October 1583 Archbishop O'Hurley was handed over by Baron Slane to Lord Justices Wallop and Loftus and imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a>. Under orders from both Loftus and Wallop, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Waterhouse" title="Edward Waterhouse">Edward Waterhouse</a>, a senior member of the Irish Privy Council, conducted a series of interrogations of the Archbishop between 8 and 20 October 1583.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The Archbishop admitted that he had been asked by the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Protector" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Protector">Cardinal Protector</a> of Ireland to deliver sealed letters to the <a href="/wiki/Gerald_FitzGerald,_14th_Earl_of_Desmond" title="Gerald FitzGerald, 14th Earl of Desmond">Rebel Earl</a> and the other leaders of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Desmond_Rebellion" title="Second Desmond Rebellion">Second Desmond Rebellion</a>. O'Hurley explained, however, that he had chosen to leave those letters in France, as, "he would not meddle", in matters other than his religious mission,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200574_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200574-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> which, as he repeated insisted, was purely a peaceful one.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Finding these results unsatisfactory, Loftus and Wallop wrote to Sir <a href="/wiki/Francis_Walsingham" title="Francis Walsingham">Francis Walsingham</a> on 20 October 1583, seeking further instructions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In his response, Walsingham ordered both Justices to use, "torture or any other severe manner of proceeding" against the Archbishop, "to gain his knowledge of all foreign practices against Her Majesty's States."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In response, Loftus and Wallop wrote back upon 10 December that they lacked the necessary instruments of torture and recommended instead that Archbishop O'Hurley be transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>. Instead, Walsingham ordered them to extract information from O'Hurley, "not only of any practice or disturbance pretended against the Land in particular, but also of any foreign conspiracy against Her Majesty in England or any other part of her dominions". Loftus and Wallop were ordered to try gentle persuasion at first, but if that failed, to "put him to the torture... which was to toast his feet against the fire with hot boots."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Philip_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Philip O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Philip O'Sullivan Beare</a>, "First he was brought before Adam, the Chancellor, and Henry, the Treasurer, and civilly and kindly invited to follow the tenets of the heretics, and promised large rewards on condition of abjuring his sacred character; relinquishing the office received from the Pope, and (O villainy!) entering upon the Archbishopric <a href="/wiki/Caesaropapism" title="Caesaropapism">under the Queen's authority</a>. He told them that he was bound and resolved never to desert the Church, Faith, or Vicar of Christ Jesus for any consideration. Then the Chancellor and Treasurer endeavoured to deceive him by cunning arguments, straining every nerve to establish the truth of their falsehoods. Dermot, not relishing this, especially as he was not allowed to reply to their nonsense, bade them, stupid and ignorant men (such was his high spirit), not to offer ridiculous and false doctrines to him, an Archbishop, and Doctor of celebrated academies. Then the heretics, filled with anger, exclaimed: 'If we cannot convince you by argument, we will make you quit this your false law and embrace our religion, or feel our power.'"<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 7 March 1584,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Archbishop O'Hurley was accordingly subjected to the boiling boot by <a href="/wiki/Edward_Waterhouse" title="Edward Waterhouse">Edward Waterhouse</a>. His bare feet were imprisoned in iron boots, filled with oil and water, that were slowly heated over a fire until the water boiled away both skin and flesh left the Archbishop's bones partially exposed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> To the even greater fury of Loftus and Wallop, however, O'Hurley refused to embrace <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestantism</a>, "confess" to any political offenses, and still stubbornly declined to "cooperate" in <a href="/wiki/Frame-up" class="mw-redirect" title="Frame-up">framing</a> the Earl of Ormond for high treason against the Queen.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Instead, Archbishop O'Hurley was heard to repeatedly cry out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy upon me"!<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>After the torture was finally ceased on fear that the executioner might be punished for accidentally killing the Archbishop without orders, O'Hurley was returned to his cell inside <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a> and received medical treatment from a fellow priest named Fr. Charles MacMorris.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>When the Archbishop had recovered enough to sit up and to limp a little, the Queen's officials sent visitors into Dublin Castle, including <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Jones_(bishop)" title="Thomas Jones (bishop)">Thomas Jones</a>, the Anglican Bishop of Meath,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200577_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200577-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> offering O'Hurley a high position within the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> hierarchy in return for taking the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a>. Even his only sister, Honora Ni Hurley, according to <a href="/wiki/David_Rothe" title="David Rothe">David Rothe</a> "was induced to go in and tempt him to apostatise and she urgently besought him to yield; but he, frowning on her, ordered her to fall at his knees and humbly beg pardon of God and absolution for so grave a crime against God, so hurtful to her own soul, and so abhorred by her brother."<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to surviving correspondence between <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Whitehall" title="Whitehall">Whitehall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a> was reluctant to dispense with a fair trial under <a href="/wiki/English_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="English Law">English Law</a>, but her mind was changed by Sir <a href="/wiki/Francis_Walsingham" title="Francis Walsingham">Francis Walsingham</a> at the urging of Loftus and Wallop, who alleged that a public trial would allow the Archbishop to make an "impudent and clamorous denial" of the charges against him. Loftus and Wallop also feared that a public <a href="/wiki/Trial_by_jury" class="mw-redirect" title="Trial by jury">trial by jury</a> might result in an acquittal for "treasons committed in foreign parts against Her Majesty" owing to the lack of evidence that a crime had been committed and to the widespread Catholic sympathies within <a href="/wiki/The_Pale" title="The Pale">the Pale</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200571_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200571-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 28 April 1584, a direct order was sent from Walsingham on behalf of the Queen, expressly forbidding the further use of torture,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570_28-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200570-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> while also adding that it was the Queen's pleasure that Archbishop O'Hurley, "being so notorious and ill a Subject", should be executed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200576_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200576-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stkevins.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Stkevins.jpg/220px-Stkevins.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="153" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Stkevins.jpg/330px-Stkevins.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Stkevins.jpg/440px-Stkevins.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="348" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/St._Kevin%27s_Church,_Camden_Row,_Dublin" title="St. Kevin&#39;s Church, Camden Row, Dublin">St. Kevin's in Camden Row</a>, burial place of O'Hurley</figcaption></figure>
<p>Upon learning that the Earl of Ormonde, by whose influence and power they feared Dermot O'Hurley's life would be saved, was coming to <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a> to congratulate new <a href="/wiki/Lord_Deputy_of_Ireland" title="Lord Deputy of Ireland">Lord Deputy</a> Sir <a href="/wiki/John_Perrot" title="John Perrot">John Perrot</a>, Loftus and Wallop decided to put the Archbishop to death as soon as possible.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903_26-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Sullivan-Beare1903-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> On 19 June 1584, Loftus and Wallop, with Perrot's permission, tried the Archbishop before a drumhead <a href="/wiki/Military_tribunal" class="mw-redirect" title="Military tribunal">military tribunal</a>, where the <a href="/wiki/Papal_Bull" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Bull">Papal Bull</a> documenting his consecration to the Episcopate, his letters of introduction, and other documents were presented as evidence against him.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Loftus and Wallop then issued a <a href="/wiki/Death_warrant" class="mw-redirect" title="Death warrant">death warrant</a> to the Knight Marshal, with orders to, "do execution", upon Archbishop Dermot O'Hurley.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200571-72_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200571-72-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> In the early morning of 19,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrady187621_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrady187621-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrydeGreenwayPorterRoy1986417_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrydeGreenwayPorterRoy1986417-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcNeill1911_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcNeill1911-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> or 20,<sup id="cite_ref-bohurl_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bohurl-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoodyMartinByrne1984354_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoodyMartinByrne1984354-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> June 1584, O'Hurley was taken out of the Bermingham Tower in Dublin Castle at dawn. Despite efforts at both silence and secrecy, the Archbishop's fellow Catholic prisoners took notice and called out that O'Hurley was innocent.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Rev. Power, the former <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ferns" title="Bishop of Ferns">Bishop of Ferns</a>, who, after learning of O'Hurley's constancy, had returned to Catholicism and once again had again been imprisoned, too,<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> "called out aloud that he rather deserved that fate for the scandal he had formerly given, but that Hurley was an innocent and holy man. Upon which the jailer severely flogged him and the others, and so reduced them to silence."<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Lord Justice <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wallop" title="Henry Wallop">Henry Wallop</a> and three or four guards went before him, the Archbishop was drawn on a hurdle through the Garden Gate, or Postern Gate, in the city walls of Dublin to be hanged in a <a href="/wiki/Willow" title="Willow">willow</a> forest,<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> either in <a href="/wiki/St_Stephen%27s_Green" title="St Stephen&#39;s Green">St Stephen's Green</a><sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> or at <a href="/wiki/College_Green,_Dublin" title="College Green, Dublin">Hoggen Green</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kev_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kev-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> The usual location of the gallows in <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_era" title="Elizabethan era">Elizabethan era</a> Dublin was at Lower Baggot Street (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Sráid Bhagóid Íochtarach</i>), formerly called Gallows Road; where Fitzwilliam Street and <a href="/wiki/Baggot_Street" title="Baggot Street">Baggot Street</a> now intersect and between Fitzwilliam Street and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pembroke_Street,_Dublin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pembroke Street, Dublin (page does not exist)">Pembroke Street</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
The execution party was taken by surprise by the arrival of a group of Dublin city worthies, who had come to Hoggen Green to shoot an <a href="/wiki/Archery" title="Archery">archery</a> match.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200572_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200572-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> In his speech from the gallows, the Archbishop proclaimed,<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Be it therefore known unto you...that I am a priest anointed and also a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a>, although unworthy of soe sacred dignitites, and noe cause could they find against me that might in the least deserve the paines of death, but merely for my funcon of <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> wherein they have proceeded against me in all pointes cruelly contrarie to their own lawes ...and I doe injoin you (Deere Christian Brethren) to manifest the same to the world and also to beare witness on the <a href="/wiki/Day_of_Judgment" class="mw-redirect" title="Day of Judgment">Day of Judgment</a> of my Innocent death, which I indure for my function and profession of the most holy Catholick Faith.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200576–77_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200576–77-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>After also "forgiving his torturers with all his heart",<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> Archbishop O'Hurley was hanged, allegedly from a noose woven out of willow branches in order to further prolong his sufferings by causing a slow death from <a href="/wiki/Strangulation" class="mw-redirect" title="Strangulation">strangulation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup> Afterwards, like other Elizabethan era execution victims, Archbishop O'Hurley was cast into a <a href="/wiki/Mass_grave" title="Mass grave">mass grave</a> located in a nearby field. His body was secretly exhumed, placed in a wooden urn by <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>-born <a href="/wiki/Recusancy_in_Ireland" title="Recusancy in Ireland">Recusant</a> William Fitzsimon, and reburied under cover of darkness in consecrated ground at <a href="/wiki/St._Kevin%27s_Church,_Camden_Row,_Dublin" title="St. Kevin&#39;s Church, Camden Row, Dublin">St. Kevin's Church, Camden Row, Dublin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>  Many miracles are said to have been wrought at his gravesite,<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> which has remained a site of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> ever since.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As word of his execution spread, O'Hurley was immediately revered as a martyr by Catholics throughout Europe. Several accounts of his life and death were printed and reached a wide audience.
</p><p>Exiled English Recusant poet <a href="/wiki/Richard_Verstegan" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard Verstegan">Richard Verstegan</a> wrote a detailed <a href="/wiki/Renaissance_Latin" title="Renaissance Latin">Renaissance Latin</a> account of Archbishop O'Hurley's martyrdom in the volume <i>Theatrum crudelitatum Hæreticorum nostri temporis</i> ("Theatre of the Cruelties of the Heretics of our Time"), which was published at <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands" title="Spanish Netherlands">Spanish Netherlands</a> in 1587. Historian J.J. Meagher has written of Verstegan, "He enhanced his account with an engraving which was a composite representation of the three <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish martyrs</a>, Dermot O'Hurley, <a href="/wiki/Patrick_O%27Healy" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrick O&#39;Healy">Patrick O'Healy</a>, and Conn O'Rourke. The printed word helped considerably to propagate and preserve the reputation of martyrdom. There were at least eight editions of Verstegan's <i>Theatrum</i> up to 1607, and these contributed in no small way to maintaining the <i>fama martyrii</i> overseas. Moreover, <a href="/wiki/John_Bridgewater" title="John Bridgewater">John Bridgewater</a>, an English priest, reprinted word for word Verstegan's account of the martyrdom in 1588."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200578_57-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200578-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Detailed accounts of Archbishop O'Hurley's life and death were also written and published by <a href="/wiki/Philip_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Philip O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Philip O'Sullivan Beare</a>, <a href="/wiki/David_Rothe" title="David Rothe">David Rothe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Luke_Wadding" title="Luke Wadding">Luke Wadding</a>, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Stanihurst" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard Stanihurst">Richard Stanihurst</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Bruodin" title="Anthony Bruodin">Anthony Bruodin</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Lynch_(Gratianus_Lucius)" title="John Lynch (Gratianus Lucius)">John Lynch</a>, John Coppinger, and John Mullin.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579_58-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In one of the great ironies of <a href="/wiki/Irish_history" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish history">Irish history</a>, Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Adam_Loftus_(bishop)" title="Adam Loftus (bishop)">Adam Loftus</a>, through his daughter Anne Loftus, who married <a href="/wiki/Henry_Colley_(died_1584)" title="Henry Colley (died 1584)">Sir Henry Colley</a>, was the ancestor of <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington</a>, who was baptized in 1767 by a <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> <a href="/wiki/Anglican_clergyman" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican clergyman">clergyman</a> at <a href="/wiki/St._Kevin%27s_Church,_Camden_Row,_Dublin" title="St. Kevin&#39;s Church, Camden Row, Dublin">St. Kevin's Church in Camden Row</a>. It was ultimately the Duke of Wellington who, as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a> in 1829, forced a bill granting <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Emancipation">Catholic Emancipation</a> in the <a href="/wiki/British_Isles" title="British Isles">British Isles</a> through Parliament and persuaded a highly reluctant <a href="/wiki/George_IV_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="George IV of England">King George IV</a> to grudgingly grant the bill <a href="/wiki/Royal_Assent" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal Assent">Royal Assent</a>. Sir <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wallop" title="Henry Wallop">Henry Wallop</a>, on the other hand, is the ancestor of every subsequent <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Portsmouth" title="Earl of Portsmouth">Earl of Portsmouth</a>. On 20 June 1584, the same day as O'Hurley's execution, the Archbishop's torturer, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Waterhouse" title="Edward Waterhouse">Edward Waterhouse</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Knighted" class="mw-redirect" title="Knighted">knighted</a> by Sir John Perrot inside <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church_Cathedral,_Dublin" title="Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin">Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin</a>, officially because Waterhouse, "dispended yearly more than a thousand marks."
</p><p>Also following Catholic Emancipation in 1829, interest in Archbishop O'Hurley was rekindled by the republication of Bishop <a href="/wiki/David_Rothe" title="David Rothe">David Rothe</a>'s <i>Analecta Sancta</i> and <a href="/wiki/Philip_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Philip O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Philip O'Sullivan Beare</a>'s <i>Historiae Catholicae Iberniae Compendium</i>. According to J.J. Meagher, "The reawakened reputation of martyrdom was further strengthened in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries by the historical works and critical editions of manuscripts published by <a href="/wiki/Patrick_F._Moran" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrick F. Moran">Patrick F. Moran</a> and others."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579_58-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1904, the hierarchy of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland" title="Catholic Church in Ireland">Catholic Church in Ireland</a> began an investigation into Archbishop O'Hurley's life and death, as well as those of the other <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a>. One of the most valuable resources was found to be the documents and letters written by the men, like Wallop and Loftus, who tortured and executed him and which had been examined in the <a href="/wiki/Public_Record_Office" title="Public Record Office">Public Record Office</a> in London. In response, Archbishop O'Hurley, whose life and martyrdom had been found to be one of the most meticulously documented and was therefore seen as one of the most promising Causes for Roman Catholic Sainthood, was declared a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200578-79_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200578-79-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 12 February 1915 Apostolic decree <i>In Hibernia, heroum nutrice</i>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> formally authorized the introduction of Archbishop O'Hurley's Cause for Roman Catholic Sainthood. During a further Apostolic Process held at Dublin between 1917 and 1930, the evidence surrounding 260 alleged cases for martyrdom were further investigated, after which the findings were again submitted to the Holy See.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579_58-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECorishMillet200579-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 27 September 1992, O'Hurley was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, alongside 16 other <a href="/wiki/Irish_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish martyrs">Irish martyrs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESQPN_60-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESQPN-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup> Following his Beatification, a memorial plaque was installed by the <a href="/wiki/Dublin_City_Council" title="Dublin City Council">Dublin City Council</a> at St Kevin's Churchyard, at the insistence of local activist James O'Doherty.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Doherty2005-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Folklore">Folklore</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Folklore">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>According to Burke's 1879 <i>History of the Irish Lord Chancellors</i>, "Multitudes of pilgrims for three centuries flocked to his tomb, which the fancy, perhaps the superstition, of the people clothed with many legends."<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> For example, in local <a href="/wiki/Irish_folklore" title="Irish folklore">Irish folklore</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ghost_stories" class="mw-redirect" title="Ghost stories">ghost stories</a> about passersby on dark and stormy nights seeing Archbishop Dermot O'Hurley offering the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Mass" title="Tridentine Mass">Tridentine Mass</a> in black Requiem vestments upon a makeshift altar over his grave in St. Kevin's churchyard are commonly told. It is said, when the Archbishop reaches the moment of <a href="/wiki/Transubstantiation" title="Transubstantiation">Transubstantiation</a> and the Raising of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Host</a>, that both O'Hurley and the altar disappear and only darkness remains.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dermot_O%27Hurley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>The <a href="/wiki/Recusant" class="mw-redirect" title="Recusant">Recusant</a> poet <a href="/wiki/Richard_Rowlands" title="Richard Rowlands">Richard Verstegan</a> composed an <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_English" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabethan English">Elizabethan English</a> <a href="/wiki/Elegy" title="Elegy">elegy</a> about the Archbishop's martyrdom, entitled "Slane's Treason; or The Fall of the Baron of Slane." The poem was set to music by celebrated Richard Cruise, the official harpist to the Earl of Ormond, and became widely popular, both among <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-English" title="Hiberno-English">Hiberno-English</a>-speaking Recusants throughout <a href="/wiki/The_Pale" title="The Pale">The Pale</a> and even among the <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> throughout <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland" title="Gaelic Ireland">Gaelic Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
