<p><b>Christina the Astonishing</b> (c.1150 &#8211; 24 July 1224), also known as <b>Christina Mirabilis</b>, was a Christian holy woman born in Brustem (near <a href="/wiki/Sint-Truiden" title="Sint-Truiden">Sint-Truiden</a>), <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. She was considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in her own time, and for centuries following her death, as noted by her appearance in the Fasti Mariani Calendar of Saints of 1630,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and Butler's <i>Lives of the Saints - Concise Edition</i>, published in the 18th century.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;Literary 1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her notability began when she was 21 years old. About to be buried and already in the church resting in an open coffin, according to the custom of the time, during the <a href="/wiki/Agnus_Dei" title="Agnus Dei">Agnus Dei</a> of her <a href="/wiki/Requiem" title="Requiem">funeral Mass</a>, she arose, stupefying the whole city of <a href="/wiki/Sint-Truiden" title="Sint-Truiden">St. Trond</a>. She subsequently lived a long life, dying at the age of 74.
</p><p>She is recognized as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and commemorated in the current edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> on 24 July, the day of her death.
</p><p>Some think that Christina receives attention today as much for the curious descriptions of her miracles as for her faith. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Christina_the_Astonishing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Christina was born to a religious family, the youngest of three daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-Sweetman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sweetman-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After being orphaned at the age of fifteen, she worked taking the herds to pasture.<sup id="cite_ref-Dickens_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dickens-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She suffered a massive <a href="/wiki/Seizure" title="Seizure">seizure</a> when she was in her early 20's. Her condition was so severe that witnesses assumed she had died. A funeral was held, but during the service, "she arose full of vigor, stupefying with amazement the whole city of Sint-Truiden, which had witnessed this wonder. She levitated up to the rafters, later explaining that she could not bear the smell of the sinful people there."<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-forum_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-forum-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She related that she had witnessed Heaven, Hell and Purgatory. She said that as soon as her soul was separated from her body, angels conducted it to a very gloomy place, entirely filled with souls enduring such torments that it was impossible to describe them. She claimed that she had been offered a choice of either  remaining in heaven or returning to earth to perform penance in order to deliver souls from the flames of Purgatory.<sup id="cite_ref-Dickens_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dickens-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Christina agreed to return to life and in that instant stood up. She told those around her that she had returned to life for the sole purpose of bringing relief to the departed and conversion to sinners. 
Christina renounced all of life's comforts, reduced herself to extreme destitution, dressed in rags, lived without home or hearth, and not content with these privations eagerly sought out all that could cause her suffering. At first, she fled human contact and, suspected of being possessed, was jailed. Upon her release, she took up the practice of extreme penance.<sup id="cite_ref-Sweetman_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sweetman-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p><a href="/wiki/Thomas_of_Cantimpr%C3%A9" title="Thomas of Cantimpré">Thomas of Cantimpré</a>, then a <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a> who was a professor of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>, wrote a report eight years after her death, based on the accounts of those who knew her. 
</p><p>"We have", wrote St <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>, "reason for believing his testimony, since he has for guarantee another grave author, Jacques de Vitry, Bishop and Cardinal, and because he relates what happened in his own time, and even in the province where he lived. Besides, the sufferings of this admirable virgin were not hidden. Everyone could see that she was in the midst of the flames without being consumed, and covered with wounds, every trace of which disappeared a few moments afterwards. But more than this was the marvellous life she led for forty-two years after she was raised from the dead, God clearly showing that the wonders wrought in her by virtue from on high. The striking conversions which she effected, and the evident miracles which occurred after her death, manifestly proved the finger of God, and the truth of that which, after her resurrection, she had revealed concerning the other life."
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg/220px-St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="236" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg/330px-St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg/440px-St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1356" data-file-height="1452" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Christina_the_Astonishing_church_photo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Christina the Astonishing (Mirabilis) church photo with a caption reading "In pestilence, famine, and war, deliver us Lord - Saint Christina for your community intercede".</div></div></div>
<p>Thus, argues Bellarmine, "God willed to silence those libertines who make open profession of believing in nothing, and who have the audacity to ask in scorn, Who has returned from the other world? Who has ever seen the torments of Hell or Purgatory? Behold two witnesses. They assure us that they have seen them and that they are dreadful. What follows, then, if not that the incredulous is inexcusable, and that those who believe and nevertheless neglect to do penance are still more to be condemned?"
</p><p>The reference was to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Jacques_de_Vitry" title="Jacques de Vitry">Jacques de Vitry</a>, who met her and recounted that she would throw herself into burning furnaces and there suffer great tortures for extended times, uttering frightful cries, yet coming forth with no sign of burns upon her. In winter she would plunge into the frozen <a href="/wiki/Meuse_(river)" class="mw-redirect" title="Meuse (river)">Meuse River</a> for hours and even days and weeks at a time, all the while praying to God and imploring his mercy. She sometimes allowed herself to be carried by the currents downriver to a mill where the wheel "whirled her round in a manner frightful to behold", yet she never suffered any dislocations or broken bones. She was chased by dogs which bit her. 
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png/220px-Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="399" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png/330px-Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png/440px-Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png 2x" data-file-width="761" data-file-height="1381" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Christina_the_Astonishing_back_of_1630_prayer_card_Fasti_Mariani_01.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Christina the Astonishing appearing in the 1630 Fasti Mariani calendar of saints - feast day July 24th back of card</div></div></div>
<p>After being jailed a second time, upon her release she moderated her approach somewhat.<sup id="cite_ref-Sweetman_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sweetman-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Christina died at the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> Convent  of Saint Catherine in Sint-Truiden, of natural causes, aged 74. The <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">prioress</a> there later testified that, despite her behaviour, Christina would humbly and fully obey any command given her by the prioress.
</p><p>In his commentaries to a new edition of the Latin text, the French historian <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sylvain_Piron&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sylvain Piron (page does not exist)">Sylvain Piron</a> suggests that she was only about 12 at the time of her apparent death. Her birth should rather be placed around 1170 than 1150.<sup id="cite_ref-Piron_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Piron-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_interpretations">Modern interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Christina_the_Astonishing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Modern interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While for some Christina's <i>Vita</i> is an example of credulous medieval superstition,<sup id="cite_ref-Dickens_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dickens-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> historian <a href="/wiki/Barbara_Newman" title="Barbara Newman">Barbara Newman</a> finds that there is reason to understand Christina's behavior in terms of <a href="/wiki/Hysteria" title="Hysteria">hysteria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Many people interpret her story (as Professor Q. Dingle explains) as a recover story.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Christina_the_Astonishing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St Christina the Astonishing has been recognized as a saint since the 12th century. She was placed in the calendar of the saints by at least two bishops of the Catholic Church in two different centuries (17th &amp; 19th) that also recognized her life in a religious order and preservation of her relics. The Catholic Church allows and recognizes veneration of saints upheld by the laity; canonization is understood as a re-affirming of the more notable examples of Christian life as mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church" title="Catechism of the Catholic Church">Catechism of the Catholic Church</a>, and Saint Christina the Astonishing, having early Church recognition, is due her title of Saint as stated by the Church's <a href="/wiki/Magisterium" title="Magisterium">Magisterium</a> and Sacred Tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;Literary 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">Veneration</a> of Christina the Astonishing has never been formally approved by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, but there remains a strong devotion to her in her native region of <a href="/wiki/Limburg_(Netherlands)" title="Limburg (Netherlands)">Limburg</a>. Prayers are traditionally said to Christina to seek her intercession for millers, for those suffering from mental illness, and for mental health workers.
</p>
