<p><b>Vincent de Paul</b>, <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">CM</a> (24 April 1581 – 27 September 1660), commonly known as <b>Saint Vincent de Paul</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Occitan_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Occitan people">Occitan French</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who dedicated himself to serving the <a href="/wiki/Poverty" title="Poverty">poor</a>.
</p><p>In 1622 Vincent was appointed a chaplain to the galleys. After working for some time in Paris among imprisoned galley slaves, he returned to be the superior of what is now known as the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a>, or the "Vincentians" (in France known as "Lazaristes").
</p><p>These priests, with vows of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity,_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity, obedience">poverty, chastity, obedience</a>, and stability, were to devote themselves entirely to the people in smaller towns and villages. Vincent was zealous in conducting retreats for clergy at a time when the local clergy's morals were flagging. He was a pioneer in seminary education and founded the Congregation of the Mission and the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul</a>.
</p><p>He was renowned for his compassion, humility, and generosity. Vincent was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1737 and is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Attwater,_Donald_p_337_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater,_Donald_p_337-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ranquines_11.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Ranquines_11.JPG/220px-Ranquines_11.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Ranquines_11.JPG/330px-Ranquines_11.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Ranquines_11.JPG/440px-Ranquines_11.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ranquines_11.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ranquines, birthplace of Vincent</div></div></div>
<p>Vincent de Paul was born in 1581 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Vincent-de-Paul,_Landes" title="Saint-Vincent-de-Paul, Landes">Pouy</a>, in the Province of <a href="/wiki/Guyenne" title="Guyenne">Guyenne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Kingdom of France</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 18">&#58;&#8202;18&#8202;</span></sup> to peasant farmers; his father was Jean and his mother Bertrande de Moras de Paul. There was a stream named the "Paul" in the vicinity and it is believed that this might have been the derivation of the family name. He wrote the name as one word – Depaul, possibly to avoid the inference that he was of noble birth, but none of his correspondents did so.<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 21–22">&#58;&#8202;21–22&#8202;</span></sup> He had three brothers – Jean, Bernard, and Gayon – and two sisters, Marie and Marie-Claudine.<sup id="cite_ref-Michael_Walsh_1991_p_304_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Michael_Walsh_1991_p_304-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was the third child. He demonstrated a talent for literacy early in life, but also worked as a child, herding his family's livestock.<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At 15, his father sent him to seminary, which he paid for by selling the family's oxen.<sup id="cite_ref-seton_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-seton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For three years, Vincent studied at a college in <a href="/wiki/Dax,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Dax, France">Dax, France</a>. It adjoined a monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Friars Minor</a>, where he resided. In 1597, he enrolled in theology at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Toulouse" title="University of Toulouse">University of Toulouse</a>. The atmosphere at the university was rough. Fights broke out between various factions of students which escalated into armed battles. An official was murdered by two students. Nevertheless, he continued his studies, financing them by tutoring others. He was ordained on 23 September 1600, at the age of nineteen, in <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau-l%27%C3%89v%C3%AAque" title="Château-l&#39;Évêque">Château-l'Évêque</a>, near <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigueux" title="Périgueux">Périgueux</a>. This was against the regulations established by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a> which required a minimum of 24 years of age for ordination, so when he was appointed parish priest in <a href="/wiki/Tilh" title="Tilh">Tilh</a>, the appointment was appealed in the <a href="/wiki/Court_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Court of Rome">Court of Rome</a>. Rather than respond to a lawsuit in which he would probably not have prevailed, he resigned from the position and continued his studies. On 12 October 1604, he received his <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Theology" title="Bachelor of Theology">Bachelor of Theology</a> from the University of Toulouse. Later he received a Licentiate in <a href="/wiki/Canon_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon Law">Canon Law</a> from the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 31">&#58;&#8202;31&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abduction_and_enslavement">Abduction and enslavement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Abduction and enslavement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Traditional biographies of Vincent describe his capture and enslavement during two years away from France, from 1605 to 1607. More recent biographies, starting with <a href="/wiki/Antoine_R%C3%A9dier" title="Antoine Rédier">Antoine Rédier</a>'s <i>La vraie vie de Saint Vincent de Paul,</i> doubt this narrative.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The biographer Pierre Coste, who wrote <i>Monsieur Vincent</i>, a comprehensive biography based on correspondence, interviews, and documents, publicly confirmed the accuracy of Vincent's captivity and enslavement. According to Rédier, Coste held the opposing view in private, and questioned the reliability of the two letters supporting the account of Vincent's enslavement.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> To avoid scandal and possible backlash, Coste kept his doubt of the slavery narrative private.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Skeptics agree that the letters themselves were written by Vincent, but question Vincent's account of the events of 1605–1607. There is no alternative account of Vincent's life during these two years, but Pierre Grandchamps and Paul Debongnie argue that the captivity narrative is implausible.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the letters, in 1605, Vincent sailed from <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a> on his way back from <a href="/wiki/Castres" title="Castres">Castres</a> where he had gone to sell property he had inherited from a wealthy patron in <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>. He was taken captive by <a href="/wiki/Barbary_pirates" title="Barbary pirates">Barbary pirates</a>, who took him to <a href="/wiki/Tunis" title="Tunis">Tunis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> De Paul was auctioned off as a <a href="/wiki/Slave" class="mw-redirect" title="Slave">slave</a>, and spent two years in bondage.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His first master was a fisherman, but Vincent was unsuitable for this line of work due to sea-sickness and was soon sold. His next master was a <a href="/wiki/Spagyric" class="mw-redirect" title="Spagyric">spagyrical</a> physician, <a href="/wiki/Alchemist" class="mw-redirect" title="Alchemist">alchemist</a> and inventor. He became fascinated by his arts and was taught how to prepare and administer his master's spagyric remedies.<sup id="cite_ref-Pormann_Edinburgh_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pormann_Edinburgh-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The fame of Vincent's master became so great that it attracted the attention of men who summoned him to <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>. During the passage, the old man died and Vincent was sold once again.<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 50">&#58;&#8202;50&#8202;</span></sup> His new master was a former <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> from <a href="/wiki/Nice,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Nice, France">Nice</a>, Guillaume Gautier. He, Gautier, had converted to <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a> in order to gain his freedom from <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">slavery</a> and was living in the mountains with three wives. The second wife, a Muslim by birth, was drawn to and visited Vincent in the fields to question him about his faith. She became convinced that his faith was true and admonished her husband for renouncing his Christianity. Her husband became remorseful and decided to escape back to France with his slave. They had to wait ten months, but finally they secretly boarded a small boat and crossed the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean" class="mw-redirect" title="Mediterranean">Mediterranean</a>, landing in <a href="/wiki/Aigues-Mortes" title="Aigues-Mortes">Aigues-Mortes</a> on 29 June 1607.<sup id="cite_ref-coste_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coste-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 52">&#58;&#8202;52&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Europe">Return to Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Return to Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After returning to France, Vincent went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. There he continued his studies until 1609 when he was sent back to France on a mission to King <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France" title="Henry IV of France">Henry IV</a>. Once in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, he made the acquaintance of <a href="/wiki/Pierre_de_B%C3%A9rulle" title="Pierre de Bérulle">Pierre de Bérulle</a>, whom he took as his spiritual advisor. André Duval, of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Sorbonne</a> introduced him to <a href="/wiki/Benet_Canfield" title="Benet Canfield">Canfield</a>'s "Rule of Perfection".<sup id="cite_ref-odonnell_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odonnell-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Vincent was by nature a rather irascible person, but he slowly learned to become more sensitive to the needs of others.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1612 he was sent as a parish priest to the Church of Saint-Medard in <a href="/wiki/Clichy,_Hauts-de-Seine" title="Clichy, Hauts-de-Seine">Clichy</a>. In less than a year, Bérulle recalled him to Paris to serve as a chaplain and tutor to the <a href="/wiki/Gondi_family" title="Gondi family">Gondi family</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> "Although Vincent had initially begun his priesthood with the intention of securing a life of leisure for himself, he underwent a change of heart after hearing the confession of a dying peasant."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It was the Countess de Gondi who persuaded her husband to endow and support a group of able and zealous missionaries who would work among poor tenant farmers and country people in general.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 13 May 1643, with Louis XIII dead, Queen Anne had her husband's will annulled by the Parlement de Paris (a judicial body comprising mostly nobles and high clergymen), making her the sole Regent of France. Anne nominated Vincent de Paul as her spiritual adviser; he helped her deal with religious policy and the <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenism</a> question.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_religious_congregations">Foundation of religious congregations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundation of religious congregations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1617, Vincent contacted the Daughters of Charity and they then introduced him to poor families. Vincent then brought them food and comfort. He organized these wealthy women of Paris to collect funds for missionary projects, founded hospitals, and gather relief funds for the victims of war and to ransom 1,200 <a href="/wiki/Galley" title="Galley">galley</a> slaves from North Africa. This participation of women would eventually result in, with the help of <a href="/wiki/Louise_de_Marillac" title="Louise de Marillac">Louise de Marillac</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul</a> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Filles de la Charité</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-Attwater,_Donald_p_337_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater,_Donald_p_337-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> a <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Apostolic_Life" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Apostolic Life">Society of Apostolic Life</a> for women within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>In 1622 Vincent was appointed a chaplain to the galleys.<sup id="cite_ref-Michael_Walsh_1991_p_304_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Michael_Walsh_1991_p_304-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After working for some time in Paris among imprisoned galley slaves, he returned to be the superior of what is now known as the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a>, or the "Vincentians" (in France known as "Lazaristes"). These priests, with vows of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity,_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity, obedience">poverty, chastity, obedience</a>, and stability, were to devote themselves entirely to the people in smaller towns and villages.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent was zealous in conducting retreats for clergy at a time when there was great laxity, abuse, and ignorance among them. He was a pioneer in clerical training and was instrumental in establishing seminaries.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He spent 28 years serving as the spiritual director of the <a href="/wiki/Temple_du_Marais" title="Temple du Marais">Convent of St. Mary of Angels</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent died in Paris on 27 September 1660.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Society_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul">The Society of Saint Vincent de Paul</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: The Society of Saint Vincent de Paul">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent is the patron of all works of charity. A number of organizations specifically inspired by his work and teaching and which claim Vincent as their founder or patron saint are grouped in a loose federation known as the <a href="/wiki/Vincentian_Family" title="Vincentian Family">Vincentian Family</a>. The 1996 publication <i>The Vincentian Family Tree</i> presents an overview of related communities from a genealogical perspective.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Among these organizations is the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Society of Saint Vincent de Paul">Society of Saint Vincent de Paul</a>, a charitable organization dedicated to the service of the poor, established in <a href="/wiki/1833" title="1833">1833</a> by French university students, led by the <a href="/wiki/Frederic_Ozanam" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederic Ozanam">Frederic Ozanam</a>. The society is today present in 153 countries.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>In 1705, the Superior General of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a> requested that the holy process of Vincent's <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> be instituted. On 13 August 1729 he was declared <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blessed</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> nearly eight years later by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> on 16 June 1737.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent's body was exhumed in 1712, 53 years after his death. The written account of an eyewitness states that "the eyes and nose alone showed some decay". However, when it was exhumed again during the canonization in 1737, it was found to have decomposed due to an underground flood. His bones have been encased in a waxen figure which is displayed in a glass <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in the chapel of the headquarters of the Vincentian fathers in Paris, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_de_Paul_Chapel" title="Saint Vincent de Paul Chapel">Saint Vincent de Paul Chapel</a>, rue de Sèvres. His heart is still <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>, and is displayed in a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in the chapel of the motherhouse of the Daughters of Charity in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_(relikwie).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg/200px-Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg/300px-Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg/400px-Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_%28relikwie%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2702" data-file-height="2026" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wincenty_%C3%A0_Paulo_(relikwie).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The waxen mask and hands encasing Vincent's bones</div></div></div>
<p>In 1737, Vincent's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was included in the Roman Calendar for celebration on 19 July, this date being chosen because his day of death was already used for the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian" title="Saints Cosmas and Damian">Saints Cosmas and Damian</a>. The new celebration was given the <a href="/wiki/Ranking_of_liturgical_days_in_the_Roman_Rite" title="Ranking of liturgical days in the Roman Rite">rank</a> of "Double", and was changed to the rank of "Third-Class Feast" in 1960.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> transferred his <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> to 27 September, moving Cosmas and Damian to 26 September to make way for him, as he is now better known in the West than them.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent is <a href="/wiki/List_of_Anglican_Church_calendars" title="List of Anglican Church calendars">honored</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/September_27" title="September 27">27 September</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> honors him together with <a href="/wiki/Louise_de_Marillac" title="Louise de Marillac">Louise de Marillac</a> on 15 March.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of the feasts celebrated by the French Deist Church of the <a href="/wiki/Theophilanthropy" title="Theophilanthropy">Theophilanthropy</a> was dedicated to Vincent.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_de_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Niagara_University" title="Niagara University">Niagara University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lewiston_(town),_New_York" title="Lewiston (town), New York">Lewiston, NY</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._John%27s_University_(New_York_City)" title="St. John&#39;s University (New York City)">St. John's University</a> in <a href="/wiki/New_York,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="New York, New York">New York, New York</a>, and <a href="/wiki/DePaul_University" title="DePaul University">DePaul University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicago, Illinois">Chicago, Illinois</a> were founded in 1856,<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> 1870<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> and 1898,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> respectively, by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a> in the United States.
</p><p>Parishes are dedicated to Vincent in Los Angeles;<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Washington, DC;<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Syracuse, New York; Chicago, Illinois;<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska" title="Omaha, Nebraska">Omaha, Nebraska</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mays_Landing,_New_Jersey" title="Mays Landing, New Jersey">Mays Landing, New Jersey</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mt._Vernon,_Ohio" class="mw-redirect" title="Mt. Vernon, Ohio">Mt. Vernon, Ohio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Houston,_Texas" class="mw-redirect" title="Houston, Texas">Houston, Texas</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida" title="Delray Beach, Florida">Delray Beach, Florida</a>; <a href="/wiki/Wheeling,_West_Virginia" title="Wheeling, West Virginia">Wheeling, West Virginia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Coventry,_Rhode_Island" title="Coventry, Rhode Island">Coventry, Rhode Island</a>, <a href="/wiki/Churchville,_New_York" title="Churchville, New York">Churchville, New York</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Peryville, Missouri,<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Lenox_Dale,_Massachusetts" title="Lenox Dale, Massachusetts">Lenox Dale, Massachusetts</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Girardville,_Pennsylvania" title="Girardville, Pennsylvania">Girardville, Pennsylvania</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Arlington,_Texas" title="Arlington, Texas">Arlington, Texas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Denver,_Colorado" class="mw-redirect" title="Denver, Colorado">Denver, Colorado</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> and elsewhere.
</p><p>Schools are also dedicated in Vincent's name. High schools, <a href="/wiki/DePaul_College_Prep" title="DePaul College Prep">DePaul College Prep</a>, affiliated with DePaul University, in Chicago, Illinois and DePaul Catholic High School, in <a href="/wiki/Wayne,_New_Jersey" title="Wayne, New Jersey">Wayne, New Jersey</a>; <a href="/wiki/Los_Angeles" title="Los Angeles">Los Angeles</a><sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Vincent de Paul Village, a mission-style campus of affordable and supportive apartments is dedicated in Vincent's name in San Diego, CA.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In New Zealand, the Society of St Vincent de Paul was founded in July 1867 by Father Jean Baptiste Chataigner, a Marist priest (Society of Mary). It is a lay organisation working towards a "more just and compassionate society".<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A long-term care 1,200 bed facility for elderly people is dedicated to St. Vincent de Paul in <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a>. In the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, a church is dedicated to him located in Ermita, <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adamson_University" title="Adamson University">Adamson University</a> and <a href="/wiki/San_Juan_de_Dios_Educational_Foundation" title="San Juan de Dios Educational Foundation">San Juan de Dios College</a> adopted him as their Patron Saint after the Vincentian Priests took over the University and College.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The example of Vincent de Paul and the Lazarists inspired <a href="/wiki/Charles_Lowder" title="Charles Lowder">Charles Fuge Lowder</a> to found the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Holy_Cross" title="Society of the Holy Cross">Society of the Holy Cross</a> in the Church of England.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
