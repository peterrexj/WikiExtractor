class="mw-redirect" title="Prague, Czech Republic">Prague, Czech Republic</a>; <a href="/wiki/Rheumatic_chorea" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheumatic chorea">rheumatic chorea</a> (Saint Vitus Dance); <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>; <a href="/wiki/Snake_bite" class="mw-redirect" title="Snake bite">snake bites</a>; <a href="/wiki/Storm" title="Storm">storms</a>; <a <p><b>Vitus</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;v&#39; in &#39;vie&#39;">v</span><span title="/aɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;tide&#39;">aɪ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span>), whose name is sometimes rendered <b>Guy</b> or <b>Guido</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a> from <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>. His surviving <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> is pure legend. The dates of his actual life are unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-BW_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BW-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DA_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He has for long been tied to the Sicilian martyrs Modestus and Crescentia but in the earliest sources it is clear that these were originally different traditions that later became combined.<sup id="cite_ref-DHF_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DHF-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The figures of Modestus and Crescentia are probably fictitious.<sup id="cite_ref-BW_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BW-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to his legend, he died during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> in AD 303. In the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, he was counted as one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, his feast was celebrated with dancing before his statue. This dancing became popular and the name "Saint Vitus Dance" was given to the neurological disorder <a href="/wiki/Sydenham%27s_chorea" title="Sydenham&#39;s chorea">Sydenham's chorea</a>. It also led to Vitus being considered the patron saint of dancers and of entertainers in general.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is also said to protect against lightning strikes, animal attacks and oversleeping. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 15 June. In places where the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a> is used, this date coincides, in the 20th and 21st centuries, with 28 June on the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Vitus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg/220px-Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="221" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg/330px-Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg/440px-Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="503" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitusmodestuscrescentia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The martyrdom of Vitus, Modestus, and Crescentia, from a fourteenth-century manuscript</div></div></div>
<p>According to the legend, Vitus, Modestus and Crescentia were martyrs under <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. The earliest testimony for their veneration is offered by the "<a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a>" (ed. <a href="/wiki/G._B._de_Rossi" class="mw-redirect" title="G. B. de Rossi">G. B. de Rossi</a>-<a href="/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" title="Louis Duchesne">Louis Duchesne</a>, 78: "In Sicilia, Viti, Modesti et Crescentiae"). The fact that the note is in the three most important manuscripts indicates that it was also in the common exemplar of these, which appeared in the fifth century. The same <i>Martyrologium</i> has under the same day another mention of a Vitus at the head of a list of nine martyrs, with the statement of the place, in <a href="/wiki/Eboli" title="Eboli">Eboli</a>, "In <a href="/wiki/Lucania" title="Lucania">Lucania</a>", that is, in the Roman province of that name in <a href="/wiki/Southern_Italy" title="Southern Italy">southern Italy</a> between the Tuscan Sea and the <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Taranto" title="Gulf of Taranto">Gulf of Taranto</a>. It is easily possible that it is the same martyr Vitus in both cases. 
</p><p>According to J. P. Kirsch,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> the testimony to the public veneration of the three saints in the fifth century proves that they are historical martyrs. There are, nevertheless, no historical accounts of them, nor of the time or the details of their martyrdom.
</p><p>During the sixth and seventh centuries a purely legendary <a href="/wiki/Narrative" title="Narrative">narrative</a> of their martyrdom appeared which appears to be based upon other <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legends</a>, especially on the legend of <a href="/wiki/Potitus" title="Potitus">Potitus</a>, and ornamented with accounts of fantastic miracles. According to this legend, which has no apparent historical value, Vitus was a 7-year-old son of a senator of Lucania (some versions make him 12 years old). He resisted his father's attempts, which included various forms of torture, to make him turn away from his faith. He fled with his tutor Modestus and Modestus's wife Crescentia, who was Vitus's nanny, to Lucania. He was taken from there to Rome to <a href="/wiki/Exorcism" title="Exorcism">drive out a demon</a> which had taken possession of a son of the Emperor Diocletian. He successfully performed the exorcism, but, because he stayed faithful to Christianity, he and his tutors were <a href="/wiki/Tortured" class="mw-redirect" title="Tortured">tortured</a>. By a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> brought back the three to Lucania, where they died from the tortures they had endured. Three days later, Vitus appeared to a distinguished matron named Florentia, who then found the bodies and buried them where they lay. 
</p>
<div style="clear:both;"></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Vitus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg/170px-St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg/255px-St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg/340px-St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Vitus_Cathedral_from_south.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">St. Vitus Cathedral</a> is the main church of the former imperial capital, <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The veneration of the martyrs spread rapidly in Southern Italy and Sicily, as is shown by the note in the "Martyrologium Hieronymianum". Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> mentions a monastery dedicated to Vitus in Sicily ("Epist.", I, xlviii, P.L., LXXXVII, 511).
</p><p>The veneration of Vitus, the chief saint of the group, also appeared very early at Rome. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius I</a> (492–496) mentions a shrine dedicated to him (Jaffé, "Reg. Rom. Pont.", 2nd ed., I, 6 79), and at Rome in the seventh century the chapel of a deaconry was dedicated to him ("Liber Pont.", ed. Duchesne, I, 470 sq.).
</p><p>In AD 756, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fulrad" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Fulrad">Abbot Fulrad</a> is said to have brought the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of St. Vitus to the <a href="/wiki/Saint-Denis_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Denis Abbey">monastery of St-Denis</a>. They were later presented to Abbot Warin of <a href="/wiki/Corvey" class="mw-redirect" title="Corvey">Corvey</a> in Germany, who solemnly transferred some of them to this abbey in AD 836. From Corvey the veneration of St Vitus spread throughout Westphalia and in the districts of eastern and northern Germany. His popularity grew in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, Bohemia when, in AD 925, king <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I of Germany">Henry I of Germany</a> presented as a gift the bones of one hand of St. Vitus to <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" title="Wenceslaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Wenceslaus</a>, Duke of Bohemia. Since then, this relic has been a sacred treasure in the <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">St. Vitus Cathedral</a> in Prague. Other relics of Saint Vitus were taken in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> (they were kept in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Marino,_Pavia" title="San Marino, Pavia">San Marino</a>) by the emperor <a href="/wiki/Charles_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles IV</a> in 1355 and were brought to Prague.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The veneration of St. Vitus became very popular in <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavic</a> lands, where his name (Sveti Vid) may have replaced more ancient worship of the god of light <a href="/wiki/Svetovid" class="mw-redirect" title="Svetovid">Svetovid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a> his feast day, known as <a href="/wiki/Vidovdan" title="Vidovdan">Vidovdan</a>, is of particular historical importance.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The day is part of the <a href="/wiki/Kosovo_Myth" title="Kosovo Myth">Kosovo Myth</a> — the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Kosovo" title="Battle of Kosovo">Battle of Kosovo</a> occurred on that day; several events have symbolically occurred on that day, such as the 1914 <a href="/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria">assassination of the Austrian royal couple</a>; Vitus was the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia" title="Kingdom of Serbia">Kingdom of Serbia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CoveWestwell2002_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CoveWestwell2002-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> he has been venerated as <i>Szent Vid</i> since the early Middle Ages. In <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>, it is called Vidovden (Видовден) or Vidov Den (Видов ден) and is particularly well known among the <a href="/wiki/Shopi" title="Shopi">Shopi</a>, in the western part of the country.  In <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, 123 churches are dedicated to St. Vitus.
</p><p>In the Netherlands, Vitus is the patron saint of Winschoten, as well as of the region of the Gooi, where in each of the three largest towns (Hilversum, Bussum and Naarden), the main Catholic Church is dedicated to St Vitus.
</p><p>Vitus is one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourteen Martyrs">Fourteen Martyrs</a> who give aid in times of trouble. He is specifically invoked against <a href="/wiki/Sydenham%27s_chorea" title="Sydenham&#39;s chorea">chorea</a>, which is called <i>St. Vitus Dance</i>. 
</p><p>He is represented as a young man with a palm-leaf, in a cauldron, sometimes with a raven and a lion, his <a href="/wiki/Saint_symbology" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint symbology">iconographic attribute</a> because according to the legend he was thrown into a cauldron of boiling tar and molten lead, but miraculously escaped unscathed.
</p><p>The names of Saints Modestus and Crescentia were added in the 11th century to the Roman Calendar,<sup id="cite_ref-CR_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> so that from then on all three names were celebrated together until 1969, when their feast was removed from the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>. Vitus is still recognized as a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, being included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> under 15 June,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> may be celebrated in his honor on that day wherever the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> is celebrated,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> while Modestus and Crescentia, who are associated with Vitus in legend, have been omitted, because they appear to be merely fictitious personages.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vitus is the patron saint of the city of <a href="/wiki/Rijeka" title="Rijeka">Rijeka</a> in <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>; the towns of <a href="/wiki/Ciminna" title="Ciminna">Ciminna</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vita,_Sicily" title="Vita, Sicily">Vita</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>; <a href="/wiki/Forio" title="Forio">Forio</a> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Ischia" title="Ischia">Ischia</a>, the town of Sapri in <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>; the <i>contrada</i> of San Vito, in <a href="/wiki/Torella_dei_Lombardi" title="Torella dei Lombardi">Torella dei Lombardi</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Avellino" title="Avellino">Avellino</a>; the town of <a href="/wiki/Rapone" title="Rapone">Rapone</a>, Italy; the Gooi region in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>; the Italian colony of <a href="/wiki/San_Vito_(Costa_Rica)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Vito (Costa Rica)">San Vito</a> in Costa Rica; and the town of <a href="/wiki/St._Vith" title="St. Vith">St. Vith</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. Various places in <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> are named <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Veit_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Sankt Veit (disambiguation)">Sankt Veit</a> in his honour.
</p><p>The saint's feast day is also the subject of a popular weather rhyme: "If St. Vitus' Day be rainy weather, it shall rain for thirty days together". This rhyme often appears in such publications as <a href="/wiki/Almanac" title="Almanac">almanacs</a>; its origin is uncertain.
</p><p>Michael J. Towsend writes that "the phrase 'The patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Methodism" title="Methodism">Methodism</a> is St Vitus' summed up with reasonable accuracy many people's impressions of the Methodist Church. Methodists, surely, are supremely busy people, always rushing around organizing things and setting up committees to do good works. They can generally be relied upon to play their part in running <a href="/wiki/Christian_Aid_Week" title="Christian Aid Week">Christian Aid Week</a>, the sponsored walk for the local hospice or the group protesting about homelessness, and they are known, even now, to be activists in trades unions and political parties."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
