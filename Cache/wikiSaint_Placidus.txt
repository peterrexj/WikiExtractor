<p><b>Placidus</b> (also known as <b>Placid</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciple</a> of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Benedict of Nursia</a>.  He was the son of the <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(post-Roman_Europe)" title="Patrician (post-Roman Europe)">patrician</a> Tertullus, was brought as a child to Benedict at Sublaqueum (<a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Subiaco, Italy">Subiaco</a>) and dedicated to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> as provided for in chapter 69 of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Rule of St. Benedict</a> (<a href="/wiki/Oblate" title="Oblate">oblate</a>).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Placidus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Placidus was the eldest son of the patrician Tertullus.  When he was about eight years old, his father placed him under the care of Benedict at Subiaco, to be educated. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> (<i>Dialogues</i>, II, vii) relates an account of Placidus being rescued from <a href="/wiki/Drowning" title="Drowning">drowning</a> by his fellow monk, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurus" title="Saint Maurus">Maurus</a>, who, at Benedict's order, ran across the surface of the <a href="/wiki/Lake" title="Lake">lake</a> below the monastery and drew Placidus safely to shore. It appears certain that he accompanied Benedict when, about 529, he removed to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>, which was said to have been made over to him by the father of Placidus.<sup id="cite_ref-Huddleston_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Huddleston-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Of his later life nothing is known, but in an ancient <i>psalterium</i> at <a href="/wiki/Vallombrosa" title="Vallombrosa">Vallombrosa</a> his name is found in the <a href="/wiki/Litany_of_the_Saints" title="Litany of the Saints">Litany of the Saints</a> placed among the confessors immediately after those of Benedict and Maurus; the same occurs in <a href="/wiki/Codex" title="Codex">Codex</a> CLV at Subiaco, attributed to the ninth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Huddleston_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Huddleston-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Placidus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Placid is venerated on October 5 in the 2001 <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and on the same date along with Placid in the <i>Proper Masses for the Use of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Confederation" title="Benedictine Confederation">Benedictine Confederation</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Placidus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is the co-patron of <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a> along with the Madonna of the Letter, and is the official patron of <a href="/wiki/Biancavilla" title="Biancavilla">Biancavilla</a>, <a href="/wiki/Castel_di_Lucio" title="Castel di Lucio">Castel di Lucio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Montecarotto" title="Montecarotto">Montecarotto</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Poggio_Imperiale" title="Poggio Imperiale">Poggio Imperiale</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Because a large portion of <a href="/wiki/Easton,_Pennsylvania" title="Easton, Pennsylvania">Easton, Pennsylvania</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Italian_American" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian American">Italian</a> community originally came from Castel di Lucio, Placidus is given particular veneration with an annual parade through South Side on the Sunday before <a href="/wiki/Labor_Day" title="Labor Day">Labor Day</a>.  The Sunday after is the Feast of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross#Veneration" title="True Cross">Holy Cross</a>, celebrated by immigrants from the neighboring town of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Stefano_di_Camastra" title="Santo Stefano di Camastra">Santo Stefano di Camastra</a>.
</p>
