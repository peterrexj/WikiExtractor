<p><b>Elizabeth Ann Bayley Seton</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_Federation_in_the_Vincentian-Setonian_Tradition" title="Sisters of Charity Federation in the Vincentian-Setonian Tradition">SC</a></span> (August 28, 1774 – January 4, 1821) was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> religious <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sister (Catholic)">sister</a> in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> and an educator, known as a founder of the country's <a href="/wiki/Parochial_school" title="Parochial school">parochial school</a> system. After her death, she became the first person born in what would become the United States to be <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> (September 14, 1975).<sup id="cite_ref-PaulVI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PaulVI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She also established the first Catholic girls' school in the nation in <a href="/wiki/Emmitsburg,_Maryland" title="Emmitsburg, Maryland">Emmitsburg, Maryland</a>, where she likewise founded the first American <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> of religious sisters, the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_Federation_in_the_Vincentian-Setonian_Tradition" title="Sisters of Charity Federation in the Vincentian-Setonian Tradition">Sisters of Charity</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Elizabeth Ann Bayley was born on August 28, 1774, the second child of a socially prominent couple, a surgeon, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Bayley" title="Richard Bayley">Richard Bayley</a> and Catherine Charlton of <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-shrine_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrine-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Bayley and Charlton families were among the earliest European settlers in the New York area. Her father's parents were of <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Huguenot" class="mw-redirect" title="Huguenot">Huguenot</a> and <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> descent and lived in <a href="/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York" title="New Rochelle, New York">New Rochelle, New York</a>. As Chief <a href="/wiki/Health_Officers" class="mw-redirect" title="Health Officers">Health Officer</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Port_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey" title="Port of New York and New Jersey">Port of New York</a>, Bayley attended to immigrants disembarking from ships onto Staten Island and cared for New Yorkers when <a href="/wiki/Yellow_fever" title="Yellow fever">yellow fever</a> swept through the city (for example, killing 700 in four months).<sup id="cite_ref-Barkley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barkley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Bayley later served as the first professor of <a href="/wiki/Anatomy" title="Anatomy">anatomy</a> at <a href="/wiki/Columbia_University" title="Columbia University">Columbia College</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-randolph_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randolph-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Elizabeth's mother was the daughter of a <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Rector_(ecclesiastical)" title="Rector (ecclesiastical)">rector</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Andrew%27s_Church_(Staten_Island,_New_York)" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Andrew&#39;s Church (Staten Island, New York)">St. Andrew's Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/Staten_Island" title="Staten Island">Staten Island</a> for 30 years. Elizabeth was raised in what would eventually become (in the years after the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolution" title="American Revolution">American Revolution</a>) the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church</a>.
</p><p>Her mother, Catherine, died in 1777 when Elizabeth was three years old, possibly due to complications from the birth of her namesake Catherine, who died early the following year. Elizabeth's father then married Charlotte Amelia Barclay, a member of the Jacobus James <a href="/wiki/Roosevelt_family" title="Roosevelt family">Roosevelt family</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-shrine_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrine-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> to provide a mother for his two surviving daughters. The new Mrs. Bayley participated in her church's social ministry and often took young Elizabeth with her on charitable rounds. They visited the poor in their homes to distribute food and needed items.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg/210px-Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg" decoding="async" width="210" height="351" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg/315px-Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Appletons%27_Seton_Elizabeth_Ann.jpg 2x" data-file-width="350" data-file-height="585" /></a><figcaption>Elizabeth Ann Seton, from <i>Appleton's Cyclopaedia</i></figcaption></figure>
<p>The couple had five children, but the marriage ended in separation. During the breakup, their stepmother rejected Elizabeth and her older sister. Their father then traveled to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> for further medical studies, so the sisters lived temporarily in New Rochelle with their paternal uncle, William Bayley, and his wife, Sarah Pell Bayley. Elizabeth endured a time of darkness, grieving the absence of a second mother, as she later reflected in her journals. In these journals, Elizabeth showed her love for nature, poetry, and music, especially the piano. Other entries expressed her religious aspirations and favorite passages from her reading, showing her introspection and natural bent toward contemplation. Elizabeth was fluent in French, a fine musician, and an accomplished horsewoman.<sup id="cite_ref-crystal_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crystal-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_and_motherhood">Marriage and motherhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Marriage and motherhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On January 25, 1794, at age 19, Elizabeth married William Magee Seton, aged 25, a wealthy businessman in the <a href="/wiki/Import" title="Import">import</a> trade. <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Provoost" title="Samuel Provoost">Samuel Provoost</a>, the first Episcopal <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of New York, presided at their wedding.<sup id="cite_ref-ehs_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ehs-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Her husband's father, William Seton (1746–1798), belonged to an impoverished noble Scottish family, emigrated to New York in 1758, and became superintendent and part-owner of the iron-works of <a href="/wiki/Ringwood,_New_Jersey" title="Ringwood, New Jersey">Ringwood, New Jersey</a>. A <a href="/wiki/Loyalist_(American_Revolution)" title="Loyalist (American Revolution)">loyalist</a>, the senior William Seton was the last royal <a href="/wiki/Notary_public" title="Notary public">public notary</a> for the city and province of New York. He brought his sons William (Elizabeth's husband) and James into the import-export mercantile firm, the William Seton Company, which became Seton, Maitland, and Company in 1793. The younger William had visited important <a href="/wiki/Counting_house" title="Counting house">counting houses</a> in Europe in 1788, was a friend of Filippo Filicchi (a renowned merchant in <a href="/wiki/Livorno" title="Livorno">Leghorn</a>, Italy, with whom his firm traded), and brought the first <a href="/wiki/Stradivarius" title="Stradivarius">Stradivarius</a> violin to America.<sup id="cite_ref-Barkley_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barkley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after they married, Elizabeth and William moved into a fashionable residence on Wall Street. Socially prominent in New York society, the Setons belonged to <a href="/wiki/Trinity_Episcopal_Church,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinity Episcopal Church, New York">Trinity Episcopal Church</a>, near Broadway and Wall Streets. A devout communicant, Elizabeth took <a href="/wiki/John_Henry_Hobart" title="John Henry Hobart">John Henry Hobart</a> (later a bishop) as her spiritual director. Along with her sister-in-law Rebecca Mary Seton (1780–1804) (her soul-friend and dearest confidante), Elizabeth continued her former stepmother's social ministry—nursing the sick and dying among family, friends, and needy neighbors. Influenced by her father, she became a charter member of The Society for the Relief of Poor Widows with Small Children (1797) and served as its treasurer.<sup id="cite_ref-archb_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-archb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the elder William Seton died, the Seton family fortunes waned during the volatile economic climate preceding the <a href="/wiki/War_of_1812" title="War of 1812">War of 1812</a>. The couple took in William's six younger siblings, ages seven to seventeen, in addition to their own five children: Anna Maria (Annina) (1795–1812), William II (1796–1868), Richard Seton (1798–1823), <a href="/wiki/Catherine_Seton" title="Catherine Seton">Catherine</a> (1800–1891) (who was to become the first American to join the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Mercy" title="Sisters of Mercy">Sisters of Mercy</a>) and Rebecca Mary (1802–1816). This necessitated a move to the larger Seton family residence.<sup id="cite_ref-Barkley_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barkley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Widowhood_and_conversion_to_Catholicism">Widowhood and conversion to Catholicism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Widowhood and conversion to Catholicism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jwatsonhouse.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Jwatsonhouse.JPG/220px-Jwatsonhouse.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Jwatsonhouse.JPG/330px-Jwatsonhouse.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Jwatsonhouse.JPG/440px-Jwatsonhouse.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1862" data-file-height="1396" /></a><figcaption>The Seton home in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> was located at the site on which a <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Holy_Rosary_Church_(New_York_City)" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of the Holy Rosary Church (New York City)">church</a> now stands in her honor, with the adjacent <a href="/wiki/James_Watson_House" title="James Watson House">James Watson House</a> serving as the rectory.</figcaption></figure>
<p>A <a href="/wiki/Quasi-War" title="Quasi-War">dispute between the United States of America and the French Republic</a> from 1798 to 1800 led to a series of attacks on American shipping. The <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>'s blockade of France, and the loss of several of his ships at sea, led William Seton into bankruptcy, and the Setons lost their home at 61 Stone Street in lower Manhattan.<sup id="cite_ref-ehs_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ehs-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The following summer, Elizabeth and the children stayed with her father, who was still the health officer for the Port of New York on Staten Island.<sup id="cite_ref-crystal_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crystal-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> From 1801 to 1803, they lived in a house at 8 State Street, on the site of the present Church of Our Lady of the Most Holy Rosary (built in 1964). Through most of their married life, William Seton suffered from tuberculosis. The stress worsened his illness; his doctors sent him to Italy for the warmer climate, with Elizabeth and their eldest daughter as his companions. Upon landing at the port of Leghorn, they were held in <a href="/wiki/Quarantine" title="Quarantine">quarantine</a> for a month, for authorities feared they might have brought yellow fever from New York. William died on December 27, 1803,<sup id="cite_ref-randolph_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randolph-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in Italy's <a href="/wiki/Old_English_Cemetery,_Livorno" title="Old English Cemetery, Livorno">Old English Cemetery</a>. Elizabeth and Anna Maria were received by the families of her late husband's Italian business partners, Filippo and Antonio Filicchi, who introduced her to <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church,_Manhattan,_New_York.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg/220px-Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="359" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg/330px-Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg/440px-Old_Saint_Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church%2C_Manhattan%2C_New_York.jpg 2x" data-file-width="880" data-file-height="1436" /></a><figcaption>St. Peter's, Barclay Street, 1785</figcaption></figure>
<p>Returning to New York, the widow Seton was received into the Catholic Church on March 14, 1805, by Father Matthew O'Brien, <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Roman_Catholic_Church,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter&#39;s Roman Catholic Church, New York">St. Peter's Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-archb_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-archb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> then the city's only Catholic church. (Anti-Catholic laws had been lifted just a few years before.) A year later, she received the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">sacrament</a> of <a href="/wiki/Confirmation" title="Confirmation">confirmation</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Baltimore" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Baltimore">Bishop of Baltimore</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Carroll_(bishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Carroll (bishop)">John Carroll</a>, the only Catholic bishop in the nation.
</p><p>To support herself and her children, Seton had started an <a href="/wiki/Academy" title="Academy">academy</a> for young ladies, as was common for widows of social standing in that period. After news of her conversion to Catholicism spread, most parents withdrew their daughters from her school. In 1807, students attending a local Protestant Academy were boarded at her house on Stuyvesant Lane in the Bowery, near St. Mark's Church.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Seton was about to move to Canada when she met a visiting priest, Father <a href="/wiki/Louis_William_Valentine_Dubourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis William Valentine Dubourg">Louis William Valentine Dubourg</a>, who was a member of the French <a href="/wiki/%C3%89migr%C3%A9" title="Émigré">émigré</a> community of <a href="/wiki/Society_of_St._Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of St. Sulpice">Sulpician Fathers</a> and then president of <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_College,_Baltimore" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mary&#39;s College, Baltimore">St. Mary's College, Baltimore</a>. The Sulpicians had taken refuge in the United States from the religious persecution of the <a href="/wiki/Reign_of_Terror" title="Reign of Terror">Reign of Terror</a> in France. They were in the process of establishing the first Catholic <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> for the United States, in keeping with the goals of their society. For several years, Dubourg had envisioned a religious school to meet the educational needs of the new nation's small Catholic community.<sup id="cite_ref-archb_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-archb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After living through many difficulties in life, in 1809, Seton accepted the Sulpicians' invitation and moved to Emmitsburg, Maryland. A year later, she established the <a href="/wiki/St._Joseph%27s_College_and_Mother_Seton_Shrine" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Joseph&#39;s College and Mother Seton Shrine">Saint Joseph's Academy and Free School</a>, a school dedicated to Catholic girls' education. This was possible due to the financial support of Samuel Sutherland Cooper,<sup id="cite_ref-randolph_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randolph-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> a wealthy convert and seminarian at the newly established <a href="/wiki/Mount_Saint_Mary%27s_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Mount Saint Mary&#39;s University">Mount Saint Mary's University</a>, begun by <a href="/wiki/John_Dubois" title="John Dubois">John Dubois</a>, S.S., and the Sulpicians.
</p><p>On July 31, Seton established a religious community in Emmitsburg dedicated to the care of the children of the poor. This was the first congregation of religious sisters founded in the United States, and its school was the first free Catholic school in America. This modest beginning marked the start of the Catholic parochial school system in the United States.<sup id="cite_ref-emmitsburg_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-emmitsburg-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The congregation was initially called the Sisters of Charity of St. Joseph's. From that point on, she became known as "Mother Seton." In 1811, the sisters adopted the rules of the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity</a>, co-founded in France by <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a> and <a href="/wiki/Louise_de_Marillac" title="Louise de Marillac">Louise de Marillac</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life_and_death">Later life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Later life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg/220px-Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg/330px-Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg/440px-Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_St._Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_interior_14.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>Mother Seton's tomb in Emmitsburg</figcaption></figure>
<p>The remainder of Seton's life was spent leading and developing the new congregation. Seton was described as a charming and cultured lady. Her connections to New York society and the accompanying social pressures to leave the new life she had created for herself did not deter her from embracing her religious vocation and charitable mission. The most significant difficulties she faced were internal, stemming from misunderstandings, interpersonal conflicts, and the deaths of two daughters, other loved ones, and young sisters in the community.
</p><p>Elizabeth Ann Seton died on January 4, 1821, at the age of 46. Today, her remains are interred in the <a href="/wiki/National_Shrine_of_Saint_Elizabeth_Ann_Seton" class="mw-redirect" title="National Shrine of Saint Elizabeth Ann Seton">National Shrine of Saint Elizabeth Ann Seton</a> in Emmitsburg, Maryland.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG/220px-Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG/330px-Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG/440px-Elizabeth_Ann_Seton_Sign.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3008" data-file-height="2000" /></a><figcaption>Sign of the New York shrine</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG/220px-Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="451" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG/330px-Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG/440px-Seton_Raymond_cem_jeh.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1156" data-file-height="2372" /></a><figcaption>Statue in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Raymond%27s_Cemetery,_Bronx" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Raymond&#39;s Cemetery, Bronx">St. Raymond's Cemetery</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bronx,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bronx, New York">Bronx, New York</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>By 1830, the Sisters of Charity of St. Joseph's were running orphanages and schools as far west as Cincinnati and New Orleans and had established the first hospital west of the Mississippi in St. Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-emmitsburg_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-emmitsburg-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It had been Seton's original intention to join the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_St._Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Daughters of Charity of St. Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of St. Vincent de Paul</a>, but the <a href="/wiki/Embargo" class="mw-redirect" title="Embargo">embargo</a> of <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> due to the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a> prevented this connection. It was only decades later, in 1850, that the Emmitsburg community took steps to merge with the Daughters and become their first American branch, as their foundress had envisioned.<sup id="cite_ref-emmitsburg_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-emmitsburg-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today, six separate religious congregations trace their roots to the beginnings of the Sisters of Charity in Emmitsburg. In addition to the original community of Sisters at Emmitsburg (now part of the Vincentian order), they are based in <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_New_York" title="Sisters of Charity of New York">New York City</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Cincinnati" title="Sisters of Charity of Cincinnati">Cincinnati, Ohio</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Sisters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Halifax, Nova Scotia</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Convent_Station,_New_Jersey" title="Convent Station, New Jersey">Convent Station, New Jersey</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Seton_Hill" title="Sisters of Charity of Seton Hill">Greensburg, Pennsylvania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The community at Convent Station established the <a href="/wiki/Academy_of_Saint_Elizabeth" title="Academy of Saint Elizabeth">Academy of Saint Elizabeth</a> in 1860 and the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Saint_Elizabeth" class="mw-redirect" title="College of Saint Elizabeth">College of Saint Elizabeth</a> in 1899. Additionally, a church in Bothell, Washington, has been named after her.
</p><p>Elizabeth Ann Seton has also been inducted into the <a href="/wiki/National_Women%27s_Hall_of_Fame" title="National Women&#39;s Hall of Fame">National Women's Hall of Fame</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1952, a miracle involving the healing of 4 year old Ann O'Neil from <a href="/wiki/Leukemia" title="Leukemia">leukemia</a> was attributed to the intercession of Seton after a nun prayed for the girl to Seton. The miracle was a factor in the beatification of Seton and Seton was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> on March 17, 1963. The pope said on the occasion, "In a house that was very small, but with ample space for charity, she sowed a seed in America which by Divine Grace grew into a large tree."<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Seton on September 14, 1975, in a ceremony in St. Peter's Square. In his words, "Elizabeth Ann Seton is a saint. St. Elizabeth Ann Seton is an American. All of us say this with special joy and with the intention of honoring the land and the nation from which she sprang forth as the first flower in the calendar of the saints. Elizabeth Ann Seton was wholly American! Rejoice for your glorious daughter. Be proud of her. And know how to preserve her fruitful heritage."<sup id="cite_ref-PaulVI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PaulVI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The miracle which led to the canonization of Seton involved the healing of a man in 1963, who was given hours to live after contracting <a href="/wiki/Meningitis" title="Meningitis">meningitis</a> and having <a href="/wiki/Encephalitis" title="Encephalitis">encephalitis</a> in his brain.
</p><p>Seton's feast day is January 4, the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Days_of_Christmas" title="Twelve Days of Christmas">eleventh day</a> of <a href="/wiki/Christmastide" title="Christmastide">Christmastide</a> and the anniversary of her death.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Seton is the patron saint of seafarers<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> and widows.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Elizabeth Ann Seton is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">honored</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">liturgical calendar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church in the United States of America</a> on <a href="/wiki/January_4" title="January 4">January 4</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Eponymous_institutions">Eponymous institutions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Eponymous institutions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hospital">Hospital</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Hospital">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul#United_States" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity</a> Health Network established <a href="/wiki/Bayley_Seton_Hospital" title="Bayley Seton Hospital">Bayley Seton Hospital</a> in 1980 on the site of the former <a href="/wiki/Marine_Hospital_Service" title="Marine Hospital Service">Marine Hospital Service</a> hospital in <a href="/wiki/Stapleton,_Staten_Island" title="Stapleton, Staten Island">Stapleton, Staten Island</a>, New York.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Most of the property is now the Bayley Seton campus of <a href="/wiki/Richmond_University_Medical_Center" title="Richmond University Medical Center">Richmond University Medical Center</a>, while a portion is used by <a href="/wiki/New_York_Foundling" title="New York Foundling">New York Foundling</a>, a Catholic social services organization.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Schools">Schools</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Schools">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg/220px-Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg/330px-Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg/440px-Seton_HS_from_northeast.jpg 2x" data-file-width="6016" data-file-height="4000" /></a><figcaption>Seton High School, Cincinnati, Ohio</figcaption></figure>
<p>Seton High School is a parochial all-female, college-preparatory high school in the Price Hill neighborhood of Cincinnati, Ohio. Seton was founded as Mount St. Vincent Academy in 1854. It was also known as Cedar Grove Academy. Cedar Grove was renamed in honor of Elizabeth Ann Seton on September 12, 1927.
</p><p>Mother Seton School in <a href="/wiki/Emmitsburg,_Maryland" title="Emmitsburg, Maryland">Emmitsburg, Maryland</a>, is a direct descendant of the Saint Joseph's Academy and Free School.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> It is located less than a mile from the site of the original school and is sponsored by the Daughters of Charity.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Mother Seton School is a private elementary school located in Emmitsburg and enrolls 306 students from pre-kindergarten through 8th grade. Mother Seton School is the 84th-largest private school in Maryland and the 3,381st-largest nationally. It has 15 students to every teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, the Elizabeth Seton School in BF Resort Village, <a href="/wiki/Las_Pi%C3%B1as" title="Las Piñas">Las Piñas</a> was established in 1975, the year of Seton's canonization. It is the largest Catholic school in the city in terms of population.<sup id="cite_ref-SetonPH_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SetonPH-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Seton_College" title="Elizabeth Seton College">Elizabeth Seton College</a>, located in <a href="/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York" title="Yonkers, New York">Yonkers, New York</a>, was a college opened to assist young struggling women and men in need of furthering their education, offering Associate of Science or Associate of Occupational Science degrees. It merged with <a href="/wiki/Iona_College_(New_York)" class="mw-redirect" title="Iona College (New York)">Iona College</a> in 1989.
</p><p>Seton Hall College (now known as <a href="/wiki/Seton_Hall_University" title="Seton Hall University">Seton Hall University</a>) in <a href="/wiki/South_Orange,_New_Jersey" title="South Orange, New Jersey">South Orange, New Jersey</a>, was founded in 1856 by Seton's nephew Bishop <a href="/wiki/James_Roosevelt_Bayley" title="James Roosevelt Bayley">James Roosevelt Bayley</a> and named after his aunt.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> As was <a href="/wiki/Seton_Hall_Preparatory_School" title="Seton Hall Preparatory School">Seton Hall Preparatory School</a>, an all-boys High School in <a href="/wiki/West_Orange,_New_Jersey" title="West Orange, New Jersey">West Orange, New Jersey</a>, that was formerly associated with the University, but is now independent.
</p><p>The Seton Hill Schools (now part of <a href="/wiki/Seton_Hill_University" title="Seton Hill University">Seton Hill University</a>), named for Seton, were founded by the Sisters of Charity in 1885. The university continues to operate in <a href="/wiki/Greensburg,_Pennsylvania" title="Greensburg, Pennsylvania">Greensburg, Pennsylvania</a>, under the auspices of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Seton_Hill" title="Sisters of Charity of Seton Hill">Sisters of Charity of Seton Hill</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Seton_High_School" title="Elizabeth Seton High School">Elizabeth Seton High School</a>, an all-girls Catholic school in <a href="/wiki/Bladensburg,_Maryland" title="Bladensburg, Maryland">Bladensburg, Maryland</a>, sponsored by the Daughters of Charity, is named in honor of Seton.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Seton_Academy_(South_Holland,_Illinois)" title="Seton Academy (South Holland, Illinois)">Seton Academy</a> (1963–2016) was a high school in <a href="/wiki/South_Holland,_Illinois" title="South Holland, Illinois">South Holland, Illinois</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Seton_Catholic_High_School_(Pittston,_Pennsylvania)" title="Seton Catholic High School (Pittston, Pennsylvania)">Seton Catholic High School</a> (1900–2007) was a high school in <a href="/wiki/Pittston,_Pennsylvania" title="Pittston, Pennsylvania">Pittston, Pennsylvania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pittston_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pittston-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Seton_School_(Manassas,_Virginia)" title="Seton School (Manassas, Virginia)">Seton School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Manassas,_Virginia" title="Manassas, Virginia">Manassas, Virginia</a> is also named for Mother Seton.
</p><p>Seton Catholic School (K-8) Hudson, Ohio
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/College_of_Mount_Saint_Vincent" title="College of Mount Saint Vincent">College of Mount Saint Vincent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bronx,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bronx, New York">Bronx, New York</a>, was founded by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_New_York" title="Sisters of Charity of New York">Sisters of Charity of New York</a> and traces its lineage to Seton.  It features the Elizabeth Seton Library and the Italian Renaissance-style Seton Hall, with a cornerstone set by John Cardinal Farley in November 1911.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Niagara_University" title="Niagara University">Niagara University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lewiston,_New_York" title="Lewiston, New York">Lewiston, New York</a>, near Niagara Falls, also has a dormitory building named after her, called Seton Hall.
</p><p>St. Elizabeth Seton is a K-9 school in Calgary, Alberta
</p><p>Seton Catholic College, a high school in Perth, Western Australia, was founded in 1990 upon the merging of De Vialar College and St Brendan's College. With the merge, the school was placed under the patronage of Elizabeth Ann Seton.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches">Churches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Churches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Several Catholic churches are named for Seton. The first parish named in her honor, Blessed Elizabeth Ann Seton, was established in 1963 in <a href="/wiki/Shrub_Oak,_New_York" title="Shrub Oak, New York">Shrub Oak, New York</a>, with a school opening in 1966, staffed by the Sisters of Charity. 
</p><p>Upon her canonization in 1975, St. Elizabeth Ann Seton Catholic Church was established in <a href="/wiki/Crofton,_Maryland" title="Crofton, Maryland">Crofton, Maryland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> in the same <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Baltimore" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Baltimore">Archdiocese of Baltimore</a> where she had founded Saint Joseph's Academy and Free School. 
</p><p>As of 2018<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup>, there are churches in her name in more than 40 states of the United States, plus Canada and Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Buildings">Buildings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Buildings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/College_of_Mount_Saint_Vincent" title="College of Mount Saint Vincent">College of Mount Saint Vincent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bronx,_New_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bronx, New York">Bronx, New York</a>, was founded by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_New_York" title="Sisters of Charity of New York">Sisters of Charity of New York</a> and traces its lineage to Seton.  It features the Elizabeth Seton Library and the Italian Renaissance-style Seton Hall, with a cornerstone set by John Cardinal Farley in November 1911.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Niagara_University" title="Niagara University">Niagara University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lewiston,_New_York" title="Lewiston, New York">Lewiston, New York</a>, near Niagara Falls, also has a dormitory building named after her, called Seton Hall.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_University" title="Sacred Heart University">Sacred Heart University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fairfield,_Connecticut" title="Fairfield, Connecticut">Fairfield, Connecticut</a>, has a residence hall named after her, called Elizabeth Ann Seton Hall.
</p><p>The parish hall at St. James Catholic Church in Gadsden, AL is named Seton Hall in honor of St. Elizabeth Ann Seton.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Charities">Charities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Ann_Seton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Charities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Seton Villa, a small charity for providing accommodation for women with disabilities, was established in 1966 by the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_St_Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Daughters of Charity of St Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of St Vincent de Paul</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a>, Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-Seton_Villa_Charity_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seton_Villa_Charity-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
