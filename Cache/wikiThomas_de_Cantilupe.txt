<p><b>Thomas de Cantilupe</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1218</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;25 August 1282; also spelled <i>Cantelow, Cantelou, Canteloupe</i>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_Latinised_names" title="List of Latinised names">Latinised</a> to <i>de Cantilupo</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> was <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor" title="Lord Chancellor">Lord Chancellor of England</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hereford" title="Bishop of Hereford">Bishop of Hereford</a>. He was canonised in 1320 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins">Origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_de_Cantilupe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas was the third son of <a href="/wiki/William_de_Cantilupe_(died_1251)" title="William de Cantilupe (died 1251)">William II de Cantilupe</a> (died 1251) (anciently <i>Cantelow, Cantelou, Canteloupe, etc</i>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_Latinised_names" title="List of Latinised names">Latinised</a> to <i>de Cantilupo</i>), 2nd <a href="/wiki/Feudal_barony_of_Eaton_Bray" title="Feudal barony of Eaton Bray">feudal baron of Eaton Bray</a> in Bedfordshire,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who was steward of the household to King Henry III (as his father <a href="/wiki/William_de_Cantilupe_(died_1239)" title="William de Cantilupe (died 1239)">William I de Cantilupe</a> (died 1239) had been to Henry's father King John). Thomas's mother was Millicent (or Maud) de Gournai (died 1260), a daughter of Hugh de Gournai and widow of <a href="/wiki/Amaury_VI_of_Montfort-%C3%89vreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Amaury VI of Montfort-Évreux">Amaury VI of Montfort-Évreux</a> (died 1213), <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Gloucester" title="Earl of Gloucester">Earl of Gloucester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was born at <a href="/wiki/Hambleden" title="Hambleden">Hambleden</a> in <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a>, a manor belonging to his mother's first husband but awarded to her during her lifetime as her dowry.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Thomas's uncle was <a href="/wiki/Walter_de_Cantilupe" title="Walter de Cantilupe">Walter de Cantilupe</a> (died 1266), <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_de_Cantilupe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cantilupe was educated at <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>, Paris and <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>, and was a teacher of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a>, where he became <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_(education)" title="Chancellor (education)">Chancellor</a> in 1261.<sup id="cite_ref-WalshP598_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WalshP598-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Second_Barons%27_War" title="Second Barons&#39; War">Second Barons' War</a>, Cantilupe favoured <a href="/wiki/Simon_de_Montfort,_6th_Earl_of_Leicester" title="Simon de Montfort, 6th Earl of Leicester">Simon de Montfort</a> and the baronial party. He represented the <a href="/wiki/Baron" title="Baron">barons</a> before King <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a> at <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> in 1264.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911217–218_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911217–218-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 25 February 1264, when he was <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Stafford" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdeacon of Stafford">Archdeacon of Stafford</a>, Cantilupe was made <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor" title="Lord Chancellor">Lord Chancellor</a> of England,<sup id="cite_ref-HandbookP85_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HandbookP85-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but was deprived of the office after de Montfort's death at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Evesham" title="Battle of Evesham">Battle of Evesham</a>, and lived abroad for a while. Following his return to England, he was again appointed Chancellor of Oxford University, where he lectured on theology and held several ecclesiastical appointments.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WalshP598_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WalshP598-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Hereford">Bishop of Hereford</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_de_Cantilupe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bishop of Hereford">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StThomasDeCantilupeSeal.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/3/38/StThomasDeCantilupeSeal.jpg/170px-StThomasDeCantilupeSeal.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="262" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/3/38/StThomasDeCantilupeSeal.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="254" data-file-height="391" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Mandorla" title="Mandorla">Mandorla</a>-shaped seal of Bishop Thomas de Cantilupe. Legend: <i>TOMAS DEI GRATIA HEREFORDENSIS EP(ISCOPU)S</i> (Thomas by the grace of God Bishop of Hereford). The arms of Cantilupe <a href="/wiki/Ancient_and_modern_arms" title="Ancient and modern arms">ancient</a> are displayed on each side of the bishop: <i>three <a href="/wiki/Fleur-de-lis" title="Fleur-de-lis">fleurs-de-lys</a></i>. Hereford Cathedral Archives 6460. He stands on a wolf (Latin <i>lupus</i>), a <a href="/wiki/Canting_arms" title="Canting arms">canting</a> charge seen on pre-heraldic seals of the Cantilupe family<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1274 Cantilupe attended the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Lyons">Second Council of Lyons</a><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and on 14 June 1275 he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hereford" title="Bishop of Hereford">Bishop of Hereford</a>, being consecrated on 8&#160;September 1275.<sup id="cite_ref-HandbookP250_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HandbookP250-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cantilupe was now a trusted adviser of King <a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">Edward I</a> and when attending royal councils at <a href="/wiki/Windsor_Castle" title="Windsor Castle">Windsor Castle</a> or at <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster" title="Palace of Westminster">Westminster</a> he lived at <a href="/wiki/Whiteknights_Park" title="Whiteknights Park">Earley</a> in <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>. Even when differing from the king's opinions, he did not forfeit his favour.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cantilupe had a "great conflict" in 1290 with the "Red Earl", <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_de_Clare,_7th_Earl_of_Gloucester" title="Gilbert de Clare, 7th Earl of Gloucester">Gilbert de Clare, 7th Earl of Gloucester, 6th Earl of Hertford</a>, concerning hunting rights in <a href="/wiki/Malvern,_Worcestershire" title="Malvern, Worcestershire">Malvern</a>, Worcestershire, and a ditch dug by de Clare. The issue was settled by costly litigation.<sup id="cite_ref-Nott1885_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nott1885-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death in 1279 of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Kilwardby" title="Robert Kilwardby">Robert Kilwardby</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, a friend of Cantilupe's, and formerly his confessor, a series of disputes arose between him and <a href="/wiki/John_Peckham" title="John Peckham">John Peckham</a>, the new archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-WalshP598_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WalshP598-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The disagreements culminated in Peckham excommunicating Cantilupe, who proceeded to Rome to pursue the matter with the pope.<sup id="cite_ref-BartlettP23_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BartlettP23-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Death.2C_burial.2C_and_canonization"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Death,_burial,_and_canonization">Death, burial, and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_de_Cantilupe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death, burial, and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hereford_Cathedral_(7823179108).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg/220px-Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg/330px-Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg/440px-Hereford_Cathedral_%287823179108%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="2736" /></a><figcaption>The restored tomb of Thomas de Cantilupe in <a href="/wiki/Hereford_Cathedral" title="Hereford Cathedral">Hereford Cathedral</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Cantilupe died at <a href="/wiki/Ferento" class="mw-redirect" title="Ferento">Ferento</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Orvieto" title="Orvieto">Orvieto</a>, in Italy, on 25 August 1282.<sup id="cite_ref-WalshP598_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WalshP598-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HandbookP250_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HandbookP250-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He is buried in <a href="/wiki/Hereford_Cathedral" title="Hereford Cathedral">Hereford Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-WalshP598_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WalshP598-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Part of the evidence used in his cause of canonization was the supposed raising from the dead of <a href="/wiki/William_Cragh" title="William Cragh">William Cragh</a>, a Welsh rebel who was hanged in 1290, eight years after Cantilupe's death. A papal inquiry was convened in London on 20 April 1307 to determine whether or not Cantilupe had died excommunicate, since this would have precluded his being canonized. Forty-four witnesses were called and various letters produced, before the commissioners of the inquiry concluded that Cantilupe had been absolved in Rome before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-BartlettP23_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BartlettP23-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It was difficult for his cause of death to be determined as much of his body had disintegrated.
</p><p>After a papal investigation lasting almost 13 years, Cantilupe was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a> on 17 April 1320.<sup id="cite_ref-BartlettP123_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BartlettP123-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> His feast day was fixed on 2 October.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> became a popular place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>, but only its base survived the <a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a> until a new upper section (a <a href="/wiki/Feretory" class="mw-redirect" title="Feretory">feretory</a>) was recently<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span title="The time period mentioned near this tag is ambiguous. (April 2020)">when?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> recreated under the guidance of architect <a href="/wiki/Robert_Chitham" title="Robert Chitham">Robert Chitham</a>.  The new section is in vivid colours with a painted scene of the Virgin and Child holding the <a href="/wiki/Mappa_Mundi" class="mw-redirect" title="Mappa Mundi">Mappa Mundi</a>. A reliquary containing his skull has been held at <a href="/wiki/Downside_Abbey" title="Downside Abbey">Downside Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a> since 1881.
</p><p>In the current Latin edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> (2004 edition), Cantilupe is listed under 25 August as follows: "At Montefiascone in Tuscia, the passing of Saint Thomas Cantelupe, Bishop of Hereford in England, who, resplendent with learning, severe toward himself, to the poor however showed himself a generous benefactor".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_de_Cantilupe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cantilupe appears to have been an exemplary bishop in both spiritual and secular affairs. His charities were large and his private life blameless. He was constantly visiting his diocese, correcting offenders and discharging other episcopal duties, and he compelled neighbouring landholders to restore estates which rightly belonged to the see of Hereford.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Cantilupe has been lauded as the "Father of Modern Charity," and is cited as an inspiration by <a href="/wiki/Mother_Teresa" title="Mother Teresa">Mother Teresa</a> and <a href="/wiki/Melinda_Gates" class="mw-redirect" title="Melinda Gates">Melinda Gates</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Cantilupe Society was a <a href="/wiki/Text_publication_society" title="Text publication society">text publication society</a> founded in 1905 to publish the episcopal registers of the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Hereford" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Hereford">See of Hereford</a>, of which Cantilupe's is the earliest to survive,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911218-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and other records relating to the cathedral and diocese. It fell into abeyance after 1932.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cantilupe is referenced in <a href="/wiki/Graham_Greene" title="Graham Greene">Graham Greene</a>'s novel <i><a href="/wiki/Travels_With_My_Aunt" class="mw-redirect" title="Travels With My Aunt">Travels With My Aunt</a></i> (1969), when the narrator's sharp-tongued aunt opines "I would have thought he was very lucky to die in Orvieto rather than in Hereford. A small civilized place even today with a far, far better climate and an excellent restaurant in the Via Garibaldi."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
