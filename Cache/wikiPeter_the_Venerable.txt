<p><b>Peter the Venerable</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1092</span>&#160;– 25 December 1156), also known as <b>Peter of Montboissier</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Cluny" title="Abbot of Cluny">abbot</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Cluny" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Cluny">abbey of Cluny</a>. He has been honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, though he was never <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in the Middle Ages. Since in 1862 Pope Pius IX confirmed his historical cult, and the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Romanum" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologium Romanum">Martyrologium Romanum</a></i>, issued by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> in 2004, regards him as a <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Venerable&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Blessed_Raingarde&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Blessed Raingarde (page does not exist)">Blessed Raingarde</a> in <a href="/wiki/Auvergne_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Auvergne (province)">Auvergne</a>, Peter was "dedicated to God" at birth and given to the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Sauxillanges" title="Sauxillanges">Sauxillanges</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Cluny" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Cluny">Congregation of Cluny</a> where he took his vows at age seventeen. By the age of twenty he gained a professorship and was appointed <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of the monastery of <a href="/wiki/V%C3%A9zelay" title="Vézelay">Vézelay</a>, transferring later to the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Dom%C3%A8ne" title="Domène">Domène</a>. Success at Vézelay and Domène led to his election as abbot general of the order, aged thirty. After his predecessor, the abbot <a href="/wiki/Pons_of_Melgueil" title="Pons of Melgueil">Pontius</a>, had been deposed by the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, Peter became a tireless reformer of the Cluniac order, in the face of criticism from other orders and prominent monks and theologians, including the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> <a href="/wiki/St._Bernard_of_Clairvaux" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Bernard of Clairvaux">St. Bernard of Clairvaux</a>. His defence of his order against critics and his introduction of radical reforms, earned him the appellation of "venerable".<sup id="cite_ref-Fournet_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fournet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter, as an associate of national and religious leaders, attended many of the international religious councils. At the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Pisa" title="Council of Pisa">Council of Pisa</a> in 1134 he supported the cause of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II" title="Pope Innocent II">Pope Innocent II</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Reims_(1148)" title="Council of Reims (1148)">Council of Reims</a> in 1147 and helped avert a Church schism. He defended the <a href="/wiki/Rationalism" title="Rationalism">rationalistic</a> <a href="/wiki/Trinitarianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarianism">Trinitarianism</a> of the French <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a> <a href="/wiki/Peter_Abelard" title="Peter Abelard">Peter Abelard</a> against the sentence of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Sens" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Sens">Council of Sens</a>, granting Abelard hospitality at Cluny<sup id="cite_ref-Fournet_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fournet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and working towards the eventual reconciliation of Abelard and his principal accuser, <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>. Peter granted Abelard a posthumous absolution at the request of <a href="/wiki/Heloise_(student_of_Abelard)" class="mw-redirect" title="Heloise (student of Abelard)">Heloise</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Peter collected sources on, and writings about, Islam (see below) and spent a long sabbatical in Spain with Islamic scholars of all ranks. His vast correspondence reflects an almost encyclopedic theological knowledge. He produced some of the most important documents of the 12th century, and published the first Latin translation of the Qu'ran which became the standard Benedictine text used by preachers of the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>. His Talmudic contributions are tenuous and still under scrutiny.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His friendship and correspondence with Bishop <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Blois" title="Henry of Blois">Henry of Blois</a> of Winchester and Glastonbury, between 1138 and 1142, together with his debating skills, brought wider recognition of his scholarship. The internecine truce between Peter and Bernard of Clairvaux must be seen as superficial in light of recent scholarship detailing the repressiveness of Bernard's <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a> toward the <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluniac</a> orders.<sup id="cite_ref-those_terrible_middle_ages_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-those_terrible_middle_ages-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter the Venerable died at Cluny on 25 December 1156.<sup id="cite_ref-Fournet_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fournet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His works are edited in <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologia Latina</a></i> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://archive.org/details/patrologiaecurs11unkngoog/page/n7/mode/2up">vol. 189</a>.
</p>
<h2><span id="Contribution_to_Muslim.E2.80.93Christian_relations"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Contribution_to_Muslim–Christian_relations">Contribution to Muslim–Christian relations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Venerable&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Contribution to Muslim–Christian relations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Cluniacense" title="Corpus Cluniacense">Corpus Cluniacense</a></div>
<p>Despite his active life and important role in European history, Peter's greatest achievement is his contribution to the reappraisal of the Church's relations with the religion of <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a>. A proponent of studying Islam based upon its own sources, he commissioned a comprehensive translation of Islamic source material, and in 1142 he traveled to <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> where he met his translators. One scholar has described this as a "momentous event in the intellectual history of Europe."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Arabic manuscripts which Peter had translated may have been obtained in <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a>, which was an important centre for translation from the Arabic. However, Peter appears to have met his team of translators further north, possibly in <a href="/wiki/La_Rioja_(autonomous_community)" class="mw-redirect" title="La Rioja (autonomous community)">La Rioja</a>, where he is known to have visited the Cluniac monastery of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Real_of_N%C3%A1jera" title="Santa María la Real of Nájera">Santa María la Real of Nájera</a>. The project translated a number of texts relating to Islam (known collectively as the "corpus toletanum"). They include the <a href="/wiki/Apology_of_al-Kindy_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="Apology of al-Kindy (book)"><i>Apology of al-Kindi</i></a>; and most importantly the first-ever translation into <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a> <a href="/wiki/Qur%27an" class="mw-redirect" title="Qur&#39;an">Qur'an</a> (the "<i><a href="/wiki/Lex_Mahumet_pseudoprophete" title="Lex Mahumet pseudoprophete">Lex Mahumet pseudoprophete</a></i>") for which <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Ketton" title="Robert of Ketton">Robert of Ketton</a> was the main translator. <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Toledo" title="Peter of Toledo">Peter of Toledo</a> is credited for planning and annotating the collection, and <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Poitiers_(secretary)" title="Peter of Poitiers (secretary)">Peter of Poitiers</a> (Peter the Venerable's secretary) helped to polish the final Latin version. The team also included Robert of Ketton's friend <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Carinthia" title="Herman of Carinthia">Herman of Carinthia</a> and a Muslim called Mohammed. The translation was completed in either June or July 1143, in what has been described as "a landmark in <a href="/wiki/Islamic_Studies" class="mw-redirect" title="Islamic Studies">Islamic Studies</a>. With this translation, the West had for the first time an instrument for the serious study of Islam."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter used the newly translated material in his own writings on Islam, of which the most important are the <i>Summa totius heresis Saracenorum</i> (The Summary of the Entire Heresy of the Saracens) and the <i>Liber contra sectam sive heresim Saracenorum</i> (The Refutation of the Sect or Heresy of the Saracens). In these works Peter portrays Islam as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> that approaches <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">paganism</a>, and he explains to St. Bernard that his goal is "<i>ut morem illum patrum sequerer, quo nullam unquam suorum temporum vel levissimam (ut sic dicam) haeresim silendo praeterirent, quin ei totis fidei viribus resisterent et scriptis ac disputationibus esse detestandam ac damnabilem demonstrarent.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> That is, "that I may follow the custom of those Fathers, who passed over no heresy in silence ever, even the lightest (as I will thus call it), but rather resisted it with all the strength of their faith, and showed it, through writings and arguments, to be detestable and damnable."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>While his interpretation of Islam was basically negative, it did manage in "setting out a more reasoned approach to Islam…through using its own sources rather than those produced by the hyperactive imagination of some earlier Western Christian writers."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Although this alternative approach was not widely accepted or emulated by other Christian scholars of the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, it did achieve some influence among a limited number of Church figures, including <a href="/wiki/Roger_Bacon" title="Roger Bacon">Roger Bacon</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>At his weekly general audience in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 14 October 2009, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> used Peter as an example of compassion and understanding, citing Peter's governance of Cluny, diplomacy, and study of <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
