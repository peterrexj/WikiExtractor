<p><b>Frederick John Robinson, 1st Earl of Ripon</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (1 November 1782 – 28 January 1859), styled <b>The Honourable F. J. Robinson</b> until 1827 and known between 1827 and 1833 as <b>The Viscount Goderich</b> (pronounced <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/ɡ/: &#39;g&#39; in &#39;guy&#39;">ɡ</span><span title="/oʊ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;code&#39;">oʊ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="&#39;r&#39; in &#39;rye&#39;">r</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="/tʃ/: &#39;ch&#39; in &#39;China&#39;">tʃ</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling">GOHdrich</i></a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), the name by which he is best known to history, was a British politician who served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a> from 1827 to 1828.
</p><p>A member of the rural landowning aristocracy, Robinson entered politics through family connections. In the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="House of Commons of the United Kingdom">House of Commons</a>, he rose through junior ministerial ranks, achieving cabinet office in 1818 as <a href="/wiki/President_of_the_Board_of_Trade" title="President of the Board of Trade">President of the Board of Trade</a>. In 1823, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>, a post he held for four years. In 1827, he was raised to the peerage, and in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> was <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Lords" title="Leader of the House of Lords">Leader of the House</a> and <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies" title="Secretary of State for War and the Colonies">Secretary of State for War and the Colonies</a>.
</p><p>In 1827, Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a> died after only 119 days in office, and Goderich succeeded him. However, he was unable to hold together Canning's fragile coalition of moderate <a href="/wiki/Tory_(British_political_party)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tory (British political party)">Tories</a> and <a href="/wiki/Whig_(British_political_party)" class="mw-redirect" title="Whig (British political party)">Whigs</a>, and he himself resigned after only 144 days. Canning and Goderich were the two <a href="/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_the_United_Kingdom_by_length_of_tenure" title="List of prime ministers of the United Kingdom by length of tenure">shortest-ruling Prime Ministers in British history</a>, until <a href="/wiki/Liz_Truss" title="Liz Truss">Liz Truss</a> in 2022.
</p><p>After leaving the premiership Goderich served in the cabinets of two of his successors, <a href="/wiki/The_Earl_Grey" class="mw-redirect" title="The Earl Grey">the Earl Grey</a> and Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a>.
</p><p><br />
</p>

<h2><span id="Early_years:_1782.E2.80.931804"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years:_1782–1804">Early years: 1782–1804</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years: 1782–1804">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robinson was born at <a href="/wiki/Newby_Hall" title="Newby Hall">Newby Hall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, the second son of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Robinson,_2nd_Baron_Grantham" title="Thomas Robinson, 2nd Baron Grantham">Thomas Robinson, 2nd Baron Grantham</a>, by his wife Lady <a href="/wiki/Mary_Robinson,_Baroness_Grantham" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary Robinson, Baroness Grantham">Mary Yorke</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Philip_Yorke,_2nd_Earl_of_Hardwicke" title="Philip Yorke, 2nd Earl of Hardwicke">Philip Yorke, 2nd Earl of Hardwicke</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was educated at a preparatory school at <a href="/wiki/Sunbury-on-Thames" title="Sunbury-on-Thames">Sunbury-on-Thames</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> then attended <a href="/wiki/Harrow_School" title="Harrow School">Harrow School</a> from 1796 to 1799, followed by <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Cambridge" title="St John&#39;s College, Cambridge">St John's College, Cambridge</a>, from 1799 to 1802.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a> was <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom)" title="Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)">Member of Parliament</a> for Cambridge University, to which, as <i><a href="/wiki/The_Times" title="The Times">The Times</a></i> said, "accordingly most of the budding <a href="/wiki/Tory_(British_political_party)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tory (British political party)">Tory</a> statesmen of the day resorted".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> Robinson was an accomplished <a href="/wiki/Classicist" class="mw-redirect" title="Classicist">classicist</a>, winning <a href="/wiki/Browne_medal" class="mw-redirect" title="Browne medal">Sir William Browne's Medal</a> for the best Latin ode in 1801.<sup id="cite_ref-times_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-times-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After graduating in 1802 he was admitted to <a href="/wiki/Lincoln%27s_Inn" title="Lincoln&#39;s Inn">Lincoln's Inn</a>. He remained a member there until 1809, but did not pursue a legal career and was not <a href="/wiki/Call_to_the_bar" title="Call to the bar">called to the bar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Against the background of the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a> Robinson did part-time military service at home as captain (1803), ultimately major (1814–1817) in the <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire_Hussars" title="Yorkshire Hussars">Northern Regiment of West Riding Yeomanry</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hop_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hop-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Early_political_career:_1804.E2.80.931807"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career:_1804–1807">Early political career: 1804–1807</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early political career: 1804–1807">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Member_of_Parliament.2C_1804.E2.80.931812"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Member_of_Parliament,_1804–1812">Member of Parliament, 1804–1812</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Member of Parliament, 1804–1812">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Robinson entered politics through a family connection. His mother's cousin, the <a href="/wiki/Philip_Yorke,_3rd_Earl_of_Hardwicke" title="Philip Yorke, 3rd Earl of Hardwicke">third Earl of Hardwicke</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland" title="Lord Lieutenant of Ireland">Lord Lieutenant of Ireland</a>, appointed him as his private secretary in 1804.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Two years later Hardwicke secured for him the parliamentary seat of <a href="/wiki/Carlow_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Carlow (UK Parliament constituency)">Carlow</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Pocket_borough" class="mw-redirect" title="Pocket borough">pocket borough</a> near Dublin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In 1807 Robinson gave up the seat and was elected as MP for <a href="/wiki/Ripon_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Ripon (UK Parliament constituency)">Ripon</a>, close to his family home in Yorkshire.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="First_political_appointments:_1807.E2.80.931812"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="First_political_appointments:_1807–1812">First political appointments: 1807–1812</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: First political appointments: 1807–1812">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his first years in Parliament Robinson declined offers of junior ministerial posts, out of deference to his patron Hardwicke, who was an opponent of the Prime Minister, the <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland">Duke of Portland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> However, the <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs">Foreign Secretary</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a>, chose him as the secretary of <a href="/wiki/George_Herbert,_11th_Earl_of_Pembroke" title="George Herbert, 11th Earl of Pembroke">Lord Pembroke</a>'s mission to Vienna, aimed at securing a new treaty of alliance between Britain and Austria.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The mission was unsuccessful, but Robinson's reputation was not damaged, and, as his biographer E Royston Pike puts it, "as a good Tory [he was] given several small appointments in successive ministries."<sup id="cite_ref-pike176_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pike176-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His political thinking was greatly influenced by Canning, but he became the protégé of Canning's rival <a href="/wiki/Robert_Stewart,_Viscount_Castlereagh" title="Robert Stewart, Viscount Castlereagh">Lord Castlereagh</a>, who appointed him his under-secretary at the <a href="/wiki/War_Office" title="War Office">War Office</a> in May 1809. When Castlereagh resigned from the government in October, unwilling to serve under the new Prime Minister, <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Perceval" title="Spencer Perceval">Spencer Perceval</a>, Robinson resigned with him.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In June 1810 he accepted office as a member of the Admiralty board.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the time of <a href="/wiki/Assassination_of_Spencer_Perceval" title="Assassination of Spencer Perceval">Perceval's assassination</a> early in 1812, he was absent from parliament ostensibly on militia duties in Yorkshire.<sup id="cite_ref-hop_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hop-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was made a <a href="/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Most_Honourable_Privy_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Her Majesty&#39;s Most Honourable Privy Council">Privy Counsellor</a> in August 1812,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1814 Robinson married Lady Sarah Albinia Louisa Hobart (1793–1867), daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Robert_Hobart,_4th_Earl_of_Buckinghamshire" title="Robert Hobart, 4th Earl of Buckinghamshire">4th Earl of Buckinghamshire</a>, and first cousin to <a href="/wiki/Amelia_Stewart,_Viscountess_Castlereagh" title="Amelia Stewart, Viscountess Castlereagh">Castlereagh's wife</a>. There were three children of the marriage, only one of whom survived to adulthood:<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li>Hobart Frederick Robinson (September 1816)</li>
<li>Eleanor Henrietta Robinson (31 October 1826)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/George_Robinson,_1st_Marquess_of_Ripon" title="George Robinson, 1st Marquess of Ripon">George Frederick Samuel Robinson, 1st Marquess of Ripon</a> (24 October 1827 – 9 July 1909)</li></ul>
<h2><span id="Senior_Minister:_1812.E2.80.931822"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Senior_Minister:_1812–1822">Senior Minister: 1812–1822</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Senior Minister: 1812–1822">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robinson served under <a href="/wiki/Robert_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Liverpool</a> as <a href="/wiki/Vice-President_of_the_Board_of_Trade" class="mw-redirect" title="Vice-President of the Board of Trade">Vice-President of the Board of Trade</a> between 1812 and 1818,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and as joint-<a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a> between 1813 and 1817, from which position he sponsored the <a href="/wiki/Corn_Laws" title="Corn Laws">Corn Laws</a> of 1815.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Robinson's Corn Importation Bill, successfully presented to Parliament in February 1815, was a <a href="/wiki/Protectionism" title="Protectionism">protectionist</a> measure, imposing minimum prices for imported wheat and other grains.<sup id="cite_ref-dallas304_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dallas304-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The historian Gregor Dallas writes:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Robinson's Bill began a debate on free trade and protection that would last for thirty years and would change the political landscape of Britain. Battle lines were drawn up in February and March, 1815, and the first shots fired in what would become one of the most furious political struggles of the century.<sup id="cite_ref-dallas304_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dallas304-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The Corn Laws made the price of wheat artificially high, to the benefit of the landed classes and the detriment of the working classes. While the Bill was going through Parliament Robinson's London house in <a href="/wiki/Old_Burlington_Street" title="Old Burlington Street">Old Burlington Street</a><sup id="cite_ref-Wheatley_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wheatley-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> was frequently attacked by angry citizens; in one such attack the railings outside the house were ripped out, the front door smashed open, paintings ripped, and furniture thrown out of the window.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In another attack two people were shot, one of them fatally.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Describing the incident to the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="House of Commons of the United Kingdom">House of Commons</a> Robinson was moved to tears,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> showing, as the biographer P.&#160;J. Jupp put it, "a propensity under stress which was to earn him the first of several nicknames, in this case the Blubberer".<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Prime_Ministers_of_the_19th_century.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/Prime_Ministers_of_the_19th_century.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="200" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="200" data-file-height="200" /></a><figcaption>PMs in whose cabinets Robinson served: clockwise from top l., <a href="/wiki/Robert_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Liverpool</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a>, <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey">Earl Grey</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1818 Robinson entered the cabinet as <a href="/wiki/President_of_the_Board_of_Trade" title="President of the Board of Trade">President of the Board of Trade</a> and <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_Navy" title="Treasurer of the Navy">Treasurer of the Navy</a>, under the premiership of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Liverpool</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1823 he succeeded <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Vansittart,_1st_Baron_Bexley" title="Nicholas Vansittart, 1st Baron Bexley">Nicholas Vansittart</a> as <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>. The historian Richard Helmstadter writes:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Robinson was a first-rate administrator, a superb head of a department. He had a good mind, a great capacity for work, and an appetite for precision. He was a great fusser, but he fussed in a gentle way, and no one disliked him for it. His very lack of strong partisan convictions enabled him to serve, almost as a neutral civil servant, a long succession of political leaders.<sup id="cite_ref-Helmstadter_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helmstadter-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer:_1822.E2.80.931827"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer:_1822–1827">Chancellor of the Exchequer: 1822–1827</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Chancellor of the Exchequer: 1822–1827">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robinson served as Chancellor for four years, and was regarded as a success in the post.<sup id="cite_ref-pike176_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pike176-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The public finances were in good order, with a revenue surplus for the first three years of his chancellorship.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> He cut taxes and made grants to house the Royal Library in the <a href="/wiki/British_Museum" title="British Museum">British Museum</a> and to buy the <a href="/wiki/Angerstein_Collection" title="Angerstein Collection">Angerstein Collection</a> for the <a href="/wiki/National_Gallery" title="National Gallery">National Gallery</a>. Jupp writes, "These achievements, together with his support for Catholic relief and the abolition of slavery, led to his being regarded as one of the most liberal members of the government and to two more nicknames – 'Prosperity Robinson' and 'Goody'."<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Robinson's last year at the Treasury was overshadowed by a run on the banks, caused by the collapse of the City of London bankers Pole Thornton and Co.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Robinson was not blamed for the collapse, but his measures to mitigate the crisis were widely seen as half-hearted.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;n 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Under strain from the financial crisis, Robinson asked Liverpool for a change of post. In January 1827 he was given a <a href="/wiki/Peerage" title="Peerage">peerage</a> as <b>Viscount Goderich,</b><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;n 3&#93;</a></sup> but Liverpool had no time to reshuffle his cabinet, being taken ill in February 1827 and resigning the premiership.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by Canning, whose appointment caused a major realignment in the political factions of the day. The Tories split into four groups, distinguished by their view of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Emancipation">Catholic Emancipation</a>. Canning and his followers were liberal on the matter; Robinson belonged to a moderate group that was willing to support Canning; the faction led by the <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a> opposed emancipation; and an ultra-Tory group resisted any kind of liberalising measure.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>To the anger of the King, <a href="/wiki/George_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George IV of the United Kingdom">George IV</a>, who regarded it as a betrayal, Wellington and Peel refused to serve under Canning. With half the Tories ranged against him, Canning was obliged to seek support from the <a href="/wiki/Whig_(British_political_party)" class="mw-redirect" title="Whig (British political party)">Whigs</a>. Goderich, appointed by Canning as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Lords" title="Leader of the House of Lords">Leader of the House of Lords</a> as well as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies" title="Secretary of State for War and the Colonies">Secretary of State for War and the Colonies</a>, found the upper house no less stressful than the Commons. He was the target for the anger of the anti-Canning Tories in the Lords, suffering many personal verbal assaults; when he attempted to get a new Corn Law enacted it was defeated by an alliance of peers led by Wellington.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Prime_Minister:_1827.E2.80.931828"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister:_1827–1828">Prime Minister: 1827–1828<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Prime Minister: 1827–1828">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Goderich_ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Goderich ministry">Goderich ministry</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg/170px-George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="215" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg/255px-George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg/340px-George_IV._of_the_United_Kingdom.jpg 2x" data-file-width="427" data-file-height="540" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/George_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George IV of the United Kingdom">George IV</a></figcaption></figure>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment">Appointment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Appointment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Canning's health had been declining since the beginning of 1827, and on 8 August he died.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> A prominent Whig commented, "God has declared against us. He is manifestly for the tories, and I fear the king also, which is much worse."<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> The King, however, though he had long inclined to favour Tories over Whigs, was still angry at the refusal of Wellington and Peel to serve in Canning's cabinet. A widespread expectation (possibly shared by Wellington himself) that the King would send for Wellington was confounded.<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> On the day of Canning's death Goderich and the <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_Sturges_Bourne" title="William Sturges Bourne">William Sturges Bourne</a>, were summoned to <a href="/wiki/Windsor_Castle" title="Windsor Castle">Windsor Castle</a>, where the King announced his intention of appointing Goderich to the premiership.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;n 4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Government_crisis">Government crisis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Government crisis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Goderich immediately encountered difficulty in balancing the conflicting demands of the King and the Whigs about the composition of his cabinet. George considered that the three ministerial posts held by Whigs were quite enough; the Whigs pressed hard for the inclusion of a fourth, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Vassall-Fox,_3rd_Baron_Holland" title="Henry Vassall-Fox, 3rd Baron Holland">Lord Holland</a>, as Foreign Secretary.<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Goderich satisfied nobody with his inability to resolve matters. A leading Whig, <a href="/wiki/George_Tierney" title="George Tierney">George Tierney</a>, spoke of his party's dissatisfaction with Goderich: "[T]hey think Goderich has behaved so ill in this affair that they can have no confidence in him. They believe so much in the integrity of his character that they do not suspect him of any duplicity in what has passed, but his conduct has been marked by such deplorable weakness as shows how unfit he is for the situation he occupies."<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>There was further discontent in the coalition cabinet at Goderich's vacillation over the appointment of a Chancellor of the Exchequer, once again caught between the demands of the King and those of his Whig allies.<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Within a month, <a href="/wiki/William_Huskisson" title="William Huskisson">William Huskisson</a>, a Tory colleague, was writing of Goderich: "The king has taken the exact measure of him, and openly says he must do all the duties of a premier himself, because Goderich has no nerves! I am using nearly his own words; and he has been acting, and still talks of acting up to this declaration."<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> George's contempt for his Prime Minister was confirmed in his description of Goderich as "a damned, snivelling, blubbering blockhead."<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/220px-Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="273" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/330px-Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/440px-Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder_-_The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2824" data-file-height="3505" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder" title="Pieter Christoffel Wonder">Pieter Christoffel Wonder</a> – <a href="/wiki/The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter" title="The Staircase of the London Residence of the Painter">The Staircase of the London Residence of the Painter</a> – <a href="/wiki/Centraal_Museum" title="Centraal Museum">Centraal Museum</a> <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a> (the figure identified as Goderich on day of his resignation as Prime Minister).</figcaption></figure>
<p>In addition to the conflicting pressures from the King and the Whigs, Goderich had to cope with the mental problems from which his wife was suffering. In December Huskisson wrote:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Poor Goderich is quite unnerved, and in a most pitiful state. Much of this misfortune is perhaps the natural effect of his character, but it is, in the present instance, greatly aggravated by the constant worry in which he has been kept by his all but crazy wife, and by the entire ascendancy which his good nature (not to say his weakness) has allowed her to assume.<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Resignation">Resignation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Resignation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Wellington was by now distancing himself from the Extreme-Tory wing of his party,<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> and by January 1828 the King had concluded that the coalition could not continue and that a Tory ministry under Wellington would be preferable. Goderich had already written a letter of resignation to the King, but had not yet sent it, when he was summoned to Windsor. He described the disintegrating state of his administration; the King asked him to send for the Lord Chancellor, who was in turn bidden to summon Wellington to receive the King's commission to form a government.<sup id="cite_ref-aspinall_30-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aspinall-30">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> According to one account, Goderich was in tears during his interview with the King, who passed him a handkerchief, but within days Goderich was rejoicing in his release from office: "quite another man [who] sleeps at nights now, and laughs and talks as usual."<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His premiership had lasted 144 days, which remains one of the shortest in British history, twenty-five days longer than that of his immediate predecessor, Canning.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Goderich is 'the man with the hat' in the painting "<a href="/wiki/The_Staircase_of_the_London_Residence_of_the_Painter" title="The Staircase of the London Residence of the Painter">The Staircase of the London Residence of the Painter</a>" by the Dutch painter <a href="/wiki/Pieter_Christoffel_Wonder" title="Pieter Christoffel Wonder">Pieter Christoffel Wonder</a>. In 2014 a Dutch art student did research on the painting and discovered that it depicts the resignation of Prime Minister Frederick John Robinson in January 1828.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Later_years:_1828.E2.80.931859"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years:_1828–1859">Later years: 1828–1859</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Later years: 1828–1859">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Later_cabinet_posts.2C_1830.E2.80.931846"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_cabinet_posts,_1830–1846">Later cabinet posts, 1830–1846</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Later cabinet posts, 1830–1846">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg/170px-Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="135" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg/255px-Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg/340px-Microcosm_of_London_Plate_052_-_House_of_Lords.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1780" data-file-height="1410" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> in the early 19th century</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1830 Goderich moved over to the Whigs and joined <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey">Lord Grey</a>'s cabinet, as Colonial Secretary. Both on moral and on economic grounds he was strongly opposed to slavery throughout his career, and he worked hard in the 1830s for the emancipation of slaves throughout the <a href="/wiki/British_Empire" title="British Empire">British Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> His work was continued by his successor as Colonial Secretary, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby" title="Edward Smith-Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby">Lord Stanley</a>, whose abolitionist legislation Goderich piloted through the House of Lords.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1833 Goderich was created <b>Earl of Ripon</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> He had not sought the advancement in the peerage, but wished to accept the King's offer of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter" title="Order of the Garter">Garter</a>, for which, at that time, a viscountcy was considered an insufficient rank.<sup id="cite_ref-pike177_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pike177-39">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> He left the Colonial Office in the same year, and did not wish to hold any further office, but Grey insisted on his taking the senior non-departmental post of Lord Privy Seal.<sup id="cite_ref-pike177_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pike177-39">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> However, the next year Goderich and Stanley broke with the Whigs over what they saw as a threat to the <a href="/wiki/State_religion" title="State religion">established</a> status of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1841 to 1843 Ripon served in Peel's second administration as President of the Board of Trade, with the young <a href="/wiki/W._E._Gladstone" class="mw-redirect" title="W. E. Gladstone">W. E. Gladstone</a> as his deputy.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His final ministerial post was <a href="/wiki/President_of_the_Board_of_Control" title="President of the Board of Control">President of the Board of Control</a> from 1843 to 1846.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During his career, as Helmstadter observes, he had been, in succession, "a Pittite, a Tory, a Canningite, a Whig, a Stanleyite, a Conservative, and a Peelite. Between 1818 and 1846 he was a member of every government except Wellington's and <a href="/wiki/Viscount_Melbourne" title="Viscount Melbourne">Melbourne</a>'s."<sup id="cite_ref-Helmstadter_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helmstadter-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Apart from his political career Goderich served as president of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Geographical_Society" title="Royal Geographical Society">Royal Geographical Society</a> from 1830 to 1833, and of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Literature" title="Royal Society of Literature">Royal Society of Literature</a> from 1834 to 1845.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> He died in January 1859, aged 76. He outlived five of his successors in the prime ministry.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Viscount_goderich_of_nocton_tomb.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/d/d7/Viscount_goderich_of_nocton_tomb.jpg/220px-Viscount_goderich_of_nocton_tomb.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="148" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/d/d7/Viscount_goderich_of_nocton_tomb.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="301" data-file-height="202" /></a><figcaption>Ripon and his wife are buried in the memorial chapel at All Saints' Church, <a href="/wiki/Nocton" title="Nocton">Nocton</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ripon died at <a href="/wiki/Putney_Heath" class="mw-redirect" title="Putney Heath">Putney Heath</a>, London, in January 1859, aged 76.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by his only son, <a href="/wiki/George_Robinson,_1st_Marquess_of_Ripon" title="George Robinson, 1st Marquess of Ripon">George</a> who became a noted Liberal statesman and cabinet minister and was created <a href="/wiki/Marquess_of_Ripon" title="Marquess of Ripon">Marquess of Ripon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_4-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The son was unique in being conceived at No. 11 Downing Street, while Robinson was Chancellor of the Exchequer, and being born at No. 10, when his father, now Goderich, was Prime Minister.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
