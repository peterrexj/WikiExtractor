<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicetas_the_Goth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nicetas, a <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Gothic</a> soldier, lived in the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> region at the margins of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Presumably, he received his Greek name on the occasion of his baptism by the Gothic bishop <a href="/wiki/Theophilus_(bishop_of_the_Goths)" title="Theophilus (bishop of the Goths)">Theophilus</a>, a participant in the First Ecumenical Council. Pagan Goths began to oppose the spread of Christianity, which resulted in internecine strife. Nicetas fought in the Gothic civil war between the pagan <a href="/wiki/Athanaric" title="Athanaric">Athanaric</a> and the Christian <a href="/wiki/Fritigern" title="Fritigern">Fritigern</a>.
</p><p>After the defeat of Athanaric and after the invention of <a href="/wiki/Gothic_alphabet" title="Gothic alphabet">Gothic alphabets</a> by <a href="/wiki/Ulfilas" title="Ulfilas">Ulfilas</a>, Nicetas worked intensely <a href="/wiki/Gothic_Christianity" title="Gothic Christianity">among the Goths</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He was condemned to the <a href="/wiki/Death_by_burning" title="Death by burning">stake</a> in 372. According to his <i>Passio</i>, the devil, shaped as an angel, induced Nicetas to sacrifice to the pagan gods for saving his life; Nicetas, however, put him to flight by means of prayer and assisted by <a href="/wiki/Michael_(archangel)" title="Michael (archangel)">archangel Michael</a>. His body was buried in Cilicia, and later transferred to Constantinople.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a> there are several churches and monasteries named after St Nicetas. See <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Nicetas_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Church of Saint Nicetas (disambiguation)">Church of Saint Nicetas</a>.
</p><p>St Nicetas is prayed to for the preservation of children from birth defects.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The veneration of this saint in the medieval period gave rise to the Slavic forms of his name: <i><a href="/wiki/Nikita_(given_name)" title="Nikita (given name)">Nikita</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mykyta" title="Mykyta">Mykyta</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mikita" title="Mikita">Mikita</a></i>.
</p>
