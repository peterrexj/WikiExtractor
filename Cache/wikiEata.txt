<p><b>Eata</b> (died 26 October 686), also known as <b>Eata of Lindisfarne</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hexham" title="Bishop of Hexham">Bishop of Hexham</a> from 678 until 681,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook217_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook217-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and of then <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lindisfarne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lindisfarne">Bishop of Lindisfarne</a> from before 681 until 685.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook219_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook219-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He then was translated back to Hexham where he served until his death in 685 or 686.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook217_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook217-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the first native of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> to occupy the bishopric of Lindisfarne.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eata_of_Hexham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eata was originally taken to Lindisfarne as a boy under <a href="/wiki/Aidan_of_Lindisfarne" title="Aidan of Lindisfarne">Aidan</a> and trained as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He was chosen as one of the 12 monks selected from Lindisfarne to found the new daughter <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Melrose,_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Melrose, Scotland">Melrose</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 651 he was elected <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of Melrose. Around 658 he left Melrose and founded a new monastery at <a href="/wiki/Ripon" title="Ripon">Ripon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, taking with him the young St <a href="/wiki/Cuthbert" title="Cuthbert">Cuthbert</a>, who was his guest-master. In 661 King <a href="/wiki/Alhfrith_of_deira" class="mw-redirect" title="Alhfrith of deira">Alchfrith</a> of Deira expelled Eata from Ripon, because he had appointed <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a> as the new abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh166_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh166-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Stephanus_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stephanus-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eata returned to Melrose.
</p><p>The historian <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> described Eata as a gentle and greatly revered man. As an administrator Eata applied his skills at the time of plague, civil disorders and major ecclesiastical change.<sup id="cite_ref-Atcham_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atcham-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 663 Alhfrith and Wilfrid persuaded King <a href="/wiki/Oswiu_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswiu of Northumbria">Oswiu</a> to hold the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> to decide whether the local Church, English and Irish, would come into line with the traditions of the universal Church and would practice the Roman [aka Western] Rite of that Church, or would continue to diverge from it where it clashed with Irish traditions as practised in Northumbria. Thus it would decide whether <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman</a> traditions, would take priority in Northumbria over matters such as the <a href="/wiki/Clerical_tonsure" class="mw-redirect" title="Clerical tonsure">clerical tonsure</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Easter_controversy" title="Easter controversy">date of Easter</a>; the synod decided to accept the arguments of Wilfrid and the king for the universal Church traditions using the Roman Rite, to which Eata, unlike <a href="/wiki/Colm%C3%A1n_of_Lindisfarne" title="Colmán of Lindisfarne">Colmán of Lindisfarne</a>, acquiesced.<sup id="cite_ref-Bede25_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede25-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before Whitby, the abbot of Lindisfarne was also the Bishop of Lindisfarne, after Whitby these two roles were divided. The old abbot, Colman, left Lindisfarne to go back to <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> with 30 English monks. <a href="/wiki/Tuda_of_Lindisfarne" title="Tuda of Lindisfarne">Tuda</a> was selected as the next <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lindisfarne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lindisfarne">Bishop of Lindisfarne</a> and Eata moved from Melrose to become abbot of Lindisfarne. He appointed Cuthbert as <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> at Lindisfarne.<sup id="cite_ref-Bede26_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede26-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 678, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore</a> split the diocese of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> into two new bishoprics.  Eata became bishop of <a href="/wiki/Bernicia" title="Bernicia">Bernicia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Bernicia had two episcopal sees, one at Hexham and the other at Lindisfarne.  Eata was the bishop of the whole of Bernicia for three years, after which the see of <a href="/wiki/Hexham" title="Hexham">Hexham</a> was assigned to <a href="/wiki/Trumbert" title="Trumbert">Trumbert</a>, and Lindisfarne to Eata.  After the death of Trumbert in 684, Cuthbert was elected Bishop of Hexham, but was reluctant to leave his hermitage on <a href="/wiki/Inner_Farne" class="mw-redirect" title="Inner Farne">Inner Farne</a>. Following his consecration at York on Easter 685, Cuthbert went to see Eata, who was at Melrose. Eata and Cuthbert exchanged sees shortly thereafter, and for the last year of his life Eata occupied Hexham.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Eata died of dysentery at Hexham in 686,<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> Abbey of Hexham.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh166_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh166-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Like most of the early saints of the English Church, St. Eata was canonized by general repute of sanctity among the faithful in the regions which he helped to Christianize.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eata_of_Hexham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eata is remembered in St. Eats' Chapel and St. Eata's Well, both in Alvie, on the south shore of Loch Alvie, in Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The only church dedicated to him in England is <a href="/wiki/St_Eata%27s_Church,_Atcham" title="St Eata&#39;s Church, Atcham">St Eata's Church</a> at <a href="/wiki/Atcham" title="Atcham">Atcham</a> in <a href="/wiki/Shropshire" title="Shropshire">Shropshire</a>, where he is depicted in one of the stained glass windows.<sup id="cite_ref-Atcham_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Atcham-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
