<p><b>Marie-Nicolas-Antoine Daveluy</b> (16 March 1818 &#8211; 30 March 1866) was a French <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is <a href="/wiki/March_30" title="March 30">March 30</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-martyrology_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrology-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and he is also venerated along with the rest of the 103 <a href="/wiki/Korean_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Korean martyrs">Korean martyrs</a> on <a href="/wiki/September_20" title="September 20">September 20</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marie-Nicolas-Antoine_Daveluy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_(14215559772).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg/220px-Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="121" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg/330px-Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg/440px-Korea_Shrine_of_Sinri_07_%2814215559772%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5400" data-file-height="2976" /></a><figcaption>Residence of Bishop Antoine Daveluy between 1845 and 1866 in the village Sin-ri (rural part of <a href="/wiki/Dangjin" title="Dangjin">Dangjin</a>).</figcaption></figure>
<p>Antoine Daveluy was born 16 March 1818 in Amiens, France. His father was a factory owner, town councilman, and government official. The members of his family were devout Catholics and two of his brothers became <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priests</a>. He entered the St. Sulpice Seminary in <a href="/wiki/Issy-les-Moulineaux" title="Issy-les-Moulineaux">Issy-les-Moulineaux</a> himself in October 1834 and was ordained a priest on 18 December 1841.
</p><p>His first assignment was as an assistant priest in <a href="/wiki/Roye,_Somme" title="Roye, Somme">Roye</a>. Despite poor health, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> on 4 October 1843. He departed for East Asia on 6 February 1844, intending to serve as a missionary in the <a href="/wiki/Ryukyu_Islands" title="Ryukyu Islands">Ryukyu Islands</a> of <a href="/wiki/Japan" title="Japan">Japan</a>. He arrived in <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a>, where he was persuaded by the newly appointed <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_vicariate" title="Apostolic vicariate">apostolic vicar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Seoul" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Seoul">Korea</a>, Jean-Joseph-Jean-Baptiste Ferréol, to accompany him there instead. The two were joined by <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Kim_Tae-g%C5%8Fn" class="mw-redirect" title="Andrew Kim Tae-gŏn">Andrew Kim Tae-gŏn</a>, a Korean <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminarian</a> who had been studying for the priesthood in Macau. They first traveled to <a href="/wiki/Shanghai" title="Shanghai">Shanghai</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> Ferréol ordained Father Kim on 17 August 1845. The three priests then made a stormy crossing by sea to Korea, arriving in <a href="/wiki/Chungcheong_Province" title="Chungcheong Province">Chungcheong Province</a> in October.
</p><p>Father Daveluy began work as a missionary in Korea, becoming fluent in the <a href="/wiki/Korean_language" title="Korean language">language</a>. He wrote a Korean-French dictionary and other books about the Catholic Church and its history in Korean. On 13 November 1855, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> appointed him titular bishop of <a href="/wiki/Acre,_Israel" title="Acre, Israel">Akka</a> and <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">coadjutor</a> to Bishop <a href="/wiki/Sim%C3%A9on-Fran%C3%A7ois_Berneux" title="Siméon-François Berneux">Siméon-François Berneux</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-HC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who had been appointed apostolic vicar in 1854<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> after the death of Bishop Ferréol in 1853.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was consecrated by Bishop Berneux on 25 March 1857.<sup id="cite_ref-HC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Bishop Berneux was executed during a campaign by the <a href="/wiki/Joseon_Dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseon Dynasty">Korean government</a> against <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christians</a>, Bishop Daveluy became apostolic vicar on 8 March 1866. He was promptly arrested on 11 March. Imprisoned and tortured, he staunchly defended his Catholic faith. Sentenced to death, he asked to be executed on <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> 30 March. He was beheaded at a Korean naval base in Galmaemot (갈매못) near present-day Boryeong along with two French priests, Pierre Aumaître and Martin-Luc Huin, and two lay <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Catechist">catechists</a>, Lucas Hwang Sŏk-tu (Bishop Daveluy's personal assistant) and Joseph Chang Chu-gi.
</p><p>All five were canonized on 6 May 1984 along with Father Kim, Bishop Berneux and 96 other <a href="/wiki/Korean_Martyrs" title="Korean Martyrs">Korean martyrs</a>.
</p>
