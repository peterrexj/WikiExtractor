<p><b>Peter of Alcántara</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Pedro de Alcántara</i>; 1499 – October 
18, 1562) was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1669.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Alc%C3%A1ntara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Alc%C3%A1ntara" title="Alcántara">Alcántara</a>, <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1ceres_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cáceres (province)">Province of Cáceres</a>, <a href="/wiki/Extremadura" title="Extremadura">Extremadura</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>. His father, Peter Garavita, was the governor of Alcántara, and his mother was of the noble family of Sanabia. He decided to join the Franciscans at the age of 16 shortly after he was sent to university in Salamanca by his stepfather.<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning home, he became a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> friar of the Stricter Observance in the friary at Manxaretes, <a href="/wiki/Extremadura" title="Extremadura">Extremadura</a>, in 1515. At the age of twenty-two he was sent to found a new community of the Stricter Observance at <a href="/wiki/Badajoz" title="Badajoz">Badajoz</a>. He was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a> in 1524, and the following year was appointed <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of the friary of St. Mary of the Angels at Robredillo, <a href="/wiki/Old_Castile" title="Old Castile">Old Castile</a>. A few years later he began preaching with much success. He preferred to preach to the poor; his <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a>, taken largely from the <a href="/wiki/Prophets_in_Judaism" title="Prophets in Judaism">Prophets</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sapiential_Books" class="mw-redirect" title="Sapiential Books">Sapiential Books</a>, breathe the tenderest human sympathy. At the time Peter entered the Order, the reform of the "Discalced Friars" consisted of the <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Custody</a> of the friaries in Spain and Santa Maria Pietatis in Portugal, all subject to the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Observants</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-reagan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reagan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1538 Peter was made minister provincial of the Franciscan province of St Gabriel of Estremadura but resigned when his plans to enforce severe rules among the friars were opposed,<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and he retired with <a href="/wiki/John_of_Avila" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Avila">John of Avila</a> into the mountains of <a href="/wiki/Arr%C3%A1bida" class="mw-redirect" title="Arrábida">Arrábida</a> in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, where he joined Friar Martim de Santa Maria in a life of <a href="/wiki/Eremitical" class="mw-redirect" title="Eremitical">eremitical</a> solitude. Soon, though, other friars came to join him, and several little communities were established. Peter was chosen guardian and <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Master of novices</a> at the friary of Palhais, <a href="/wiki/Barreiro_Municipality" class="mw-redirect" title="Barreiro Municipality">Barreiro</a>. In 1560 these communities were erected into the Province of Arrábida.<sup id="cite_ref-reagan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reagan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning to Spain in 1553 he spent two more years in solitude; then he journeyed barefoot to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and obtained permission of <a href="/wiki/Julius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Julius III">Julius III</a> to found some poor friaries in Spain under the jurisdiction of the Minister General of the <a href="/wiki/Conventuals" class="mw-redirect" title="Conventuals">Conventuals</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Friaries were established at <a href="/wiki/Pedrosa" title="Pedrosa">Pedrosa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Plasencia" title="Plasencia">Plasencia</a> and elsewhere; in 1556 they were made a <a href="/wiki/Commissariat" title="Commissariat">commissariat</a>, with Peter as <a href="/wiki/Commissary" title="Commissary">Commissary</a>, and in 1561 a religious Province under the title of St Joseph. Not discouraged by the opposition and ill-success his efforts at reform had met with in St Gabriel Province, Peter drew up the constitutions of the new province with even greater severity. The reform spread rapidly into other provinces of Spain and Portugal.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG/220px-Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="295" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG/330px-Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG/440px-Lucini_-_Alcara.JPG 2x" data-file-width="452" data-file-height="607" /></a><figcaption><i>The Miracle of Saint Peter of Alcantara</i> by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Lucini" title="Giovanni Battista Lucini">Giovanni Battista Lucini</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1562 the Province of St Joseph was put under the jurisdiction of the Minister General of the Observants, and two new custodies were formed: St. John Baptist in <a href="/wiki/Valencia_(city_in_Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia (city in Spain)">Valencia</a> and St. Simon in <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Spain)" title="Galicia (Spain)">Galicia</a> (see Friars Minor). <a href="/wiki/Francis_Borgia" title="Francis Borgia">Francis Borgia</a> once wrote to him: "Your remarkable success is a special comfort to me."<sup id="cite_ref-habig_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila" title="Teresa of Ávila">Teresa of Ávila</a>, Peter perceived a soul chosen of God for a great work, and her success in the <a href="/wiki/Reform_of_Carmel" class="mw-redirect" title="Reform of Carmel">reform of Carmel</a> (see Carmelites) was in great measure due to his counsel, encouragement and defense. It was a letter from Peter (dated April 14, 1562) that encouraged her to found her first monastery at Avila, August 24 of that year. Teresa's autobiography is the source of much of our information regarding Peter's life, work, the <a href="/wiki/Gift_of_miracles" title="Gift of miracles">gift of miracles</a> and prophecy. According to Teresa of Ávila, it was a very common thing for him to take food only once in three days, and that sometimes he would go a week without eating.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was a man of remarkable austerity and poverty who travelled throughout Spain preaching the Gospel to the poor. He wrote a <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_golden_treatise_of_mental_prayer" class="extiw" title="s:A golden treatise of mental prayer"><i>Treatise on Prayer and Meditation</i></a>, which was considered a masterpiece by Teresa, <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">Francis de Sales</a> and <a href="/wiki/Louis_of_Granada" title="Louis of Granada">Louis of Granada</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in prayer and contemplation, he was often seen in ecstasies and <a href="/wiki/Flying_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Flying saints">levitation</a>. In his deathbed, he was offered a glass of water which he refused, saying that <i>"Even my Lord Jesus Christ thirsted on the Cross..."</i> He died while on his knees in prayer on October 18, 1562 in a monastery at Arenas (now <a href="/wiki/Arenas_de_San_Pedro" title="Arenas de San Pedro">Arenas de San Pedro</a>, <a href="/wiki/Province_of_%C3%81vila" title="Province of Ávila">Province of Ávila</a>, <a href="/wiki/Old_Castile" title="Old Castile">Old Castile</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Alc%C3%A1ntara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is also said that Peter slept very little and that he always slept sitting up. Because he was awake most of the time when his brother friars were sleeping, he is the patron saint of night watchmen.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Alc%C3%A1ntara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg/220px-Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="312" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg/330px-Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg/440px-Luca_Giordano_-_The_apparition_of_St_John_of_Capistrano_to_St_Peter_of_Alcantara.jpg 2x" data-file-width="526" data-file-height="746" /></a><figcaption><i>The apparition of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_of_Capistrano" title="John of Capistrano">John of Capistrano</a> to Saint Peter of Alcantara</i> <small>by <a href="/wiki/Luca_Giordano" title="Luca Giordano">Luca Giordano</a></small></figcaption></figure>
<p>Peter of Alcantara was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in Rome by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XV" title="Pope Gregory XV">Pope Gregory XV</a> on April 18, 1622, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_IX" title="Pope Clement IX">Pope Clement IX</a> on April 28, 1669. 
</p><p>In 1670, Peter of Alcantara's feast day was set as October 19, the day after his death, because in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> October 18 was the feast of Saint <a href="/wiki/Luke_the_Evangelist" title="Luke the Evangelist">Luke the Evangelist</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of that calendar omitted his name because of what it saw as the limited importance of the feast on a worldwide level, while at the same time it expressly envisaged its inclusion in local calendars.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> His feast could thus be moved to his day of death, October 18, which is now its official date.<sup id="cite_ref-MR_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholic">Traditionalist Catholics</a> continue to observe versions of the General Roman Calendar of the 1670-1969 period, of which the 1960 version is incorporated into the 1962 <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a> that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> permitted to be used by all <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> priests for Masses without the people and, under the conditions indicated in article 5 of his motu proprio <i><a href="/wiki/Summorum_Pontificum" title="Summorum Pontificum">Summorum Pontificum</a></i>, in Masses with the people.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter of Alcantara is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of nocturnal <a href="/wiki/Adoration_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Adoration of the Blessed Sacrament">adoration of the Blessed Sacrament</a>. In 1826, he was named patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>, and in 1962 (the fourth centenary of his death), of the Spanish region of <a href="/wiki/Extremadura" title="Extremadura">Extremadura</a>. He is also venerated as patron saint of various parishes in the United States and the Philippines. The town of <a href="/wiki/San_Pedro_de_Alc%C3%A1ntara" class="mw-redirect" title="San Pedro de Alcántara">San Pedro de Alcántara</a> in the province of <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1laga" title="Málaga">Málaga</a> is named after him.
</p>
