<p><b>Torpes of Pisa</b> (<i>Torpetius, Tropesius</i>) (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Torpès, Saint Tropez</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Torpete, Torpes, Torpè</i>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">святой мученик Тропезий</span>) (died 65 AD) is venerated as an early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.  The town of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Tropez" title="Saint-Tropez">Saint-Tropez</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, is named after him.  His legend states that he was martyred during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  
Most of the accounts about him are considered unreliable.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
Nothing else is known about his life.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He is first mentioned in sources dating from the 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Torpes_of_Pisa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Elaborations of his legend state that he was a <a href="/wiki/Gladiator" title="Gladiator">gladiator</a> or <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> who was an attendant to the Emperor Nero, or head of the emperor's personal bodyguard.<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-nrj-saint-tropez.com_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nrj-saint-tropez.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  His full name was <i>Caïus Silvius Torpetius</i> and he was a native of <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Torpes became a Christian after being converted by <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul the Apostle</a>.  He professed his faith during a ceremony in which Nero declared <a href="/wiki/Diana_(mythology)" title="Diana (mythology)">Diana</a> to be the creator of the universe.<sup id="cite_ref-nrj-saint-tropez.com_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nrj-saint-tropez.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  After Torpes declared himself a Christian, Nero did not want to kill him immediately and instead asked him to renounce his faith.  When Torpes refused to do so, Nero had him <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Another variation of the legend states that Torpes left Rome and went to Pisa, but was recognized as a Christian by the local prefect, Satellicus, who had him executed.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Tropez.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St._Tropez.jpg/200px-St._Tropez.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="133" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St._Tropez.jpg/300px-St._Tropez.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St._Tropez.jpg/400px-St._Tropez.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="1024" /></a><figcaption>Saint-Tropez "le vieux port" (the old port)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Torpes’ head was tossed into the <a href="/wiki/Arno" title="Arno">Arno</a> (and was later claimed by Pisa).<sup id="cite_ref-nrj-saint-tropez.com_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nrj-saint-tropez.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  His body was placed in a rotten boat with a cock and a dog, which had been placed there in order to nourish themselves on the saint's body.  The boat floated towards <a href="/wiki/Liguria" title="Liguria">Liguria</a>.
</p><p>A holy woman named Celerina (Célèrine) had a premonition in a dream of the arrival of the saint's body, and indeed the boat reached the present-day location of Saint-Tropez, where Celerina lived.  The boat landed not far from the present-day sailors' <a href="/wiki/Cemetery" title="Cemetery">cemetery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nrj-saint-tropez.com_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nrj-saint-tropez.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  The body was untouched by both the cock and the dog.  The cock flew away towards the village later named <a href="/wiki/Cogolin" title="Cogolin">Cogolin</a> after it; the dog headed towards the village later named in its honor <a href="/wiki/Grimaud,_Var" title="Grimaud, Var">Grimaud</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nrj-saint-tropez.com_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nrj-saint-tropez.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-aben.over-blog.com_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aben.over-blog.com-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The local people named their village in honor of him.<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  The theme of the relics being transported across the sea in a small boat is a tale found in the legends of other saints of the region, such as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Reparata" title="Saint Reparata">Reparata</a> and <a href="/wiki/Devota" title="Devota">Devota</a>.  Places on the coasts of Spain and Portugal also declared themselves to be the locations where Torpes' boat had landed.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Torpes was venerated in <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.  He became the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of sailors.<sup id="cite_ref-aben.over-blog.com_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aben.over-blog.com-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Torpes_of_Pisa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_(4).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg/220px-Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg/330px-Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg/440px-Bravades_de_Saint_Tropez_2015_%284%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="960" data-file-height="1280" /></a><figcaption>Bust of Saint Tropez during the Bravades of Saint-Tropez.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The earliest church in Pisa dedicated to him dates from the eleventh century.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  The <a href="/wiki/Civil_code" title="Civil code">civil code</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa" title="Republic of Pisa">Republic of Pisa</a> of 1284 established 29 April as the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  The Pisans credit Torpes with ending a terrible <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a> that afflicted the city in 1633.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Torpete_Torpes,_Torpè-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Genoa, where the cult of Torpes was imported by Pisan merchants,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Torpete" title="San Torpete">San Torpete</a> is dedicated to the saint.
</p><p>In Saint-Tropez, his <a href="/wiki/Bust_(sculpture)" title="Bust (sculpture)">bust</a> is honored during the festival of Les Bravades in mid-May and again in mid-June in the festival of <i>Les Bravades des Espagnols</i>, a religious and military celebration commemorating the victory of the Tropezian <a href="/wiki/Militia" title="Militia">militia</a> over the <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> in 1637.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
