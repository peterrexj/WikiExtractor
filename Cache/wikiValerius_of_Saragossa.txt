<p><b>Saint Valerius of Saragossa</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Valero</i>; <a href="/wiki/Aragonese_language" title="Aragonese language">Aragonese</a>: <i lang="an">Sant Valero</i>) (d. 315 AD) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saragossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Saragossa">Saragossa</a>. He was bishop of this city from 290 until his death. He assisted at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Elvira" title="Synod of Elvira">Council of Elvira</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE1913_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE1913-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is January 29.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Valerius_of_Saragossa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Valerius (4th century) was bishop of <a href="/wiki/Caesaraugusta" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesaraugusta">Caesaraugusta</a> (Saragossa), participated in the council of Elbira (Granada), around the year 306, had Saint Vicente Mártir as deacon <a href="/wiki/Vincent_of_Saragossa" title="Vincent of Saragossa">Vincent of Saragossa</a> first [martyr] of Spain. Both Valerius and Vincent suffered imprisonment under <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>.
Valerius was held captive in Valencia during Diocletian's persecution, apparently he saved his life and perhaps he was banished to an undetermined area of the Aragonese Pyrenees. Valerius was exiled for a time to a place called Enet, near <a href="/wiki/Barbastro" title="Barbastro">Barbastro</a>.
In 1050, some remains that were considered his were transferred to Roda de Isábena and, from 1118, after the entry into Zaragoza of the Christian troops of Alfonso I the Battler, these remains were transferred to Zaragoza in successive shipments throughout several decades.
</p><p>Venerated ever since by the people of [Zaragoza], he is the patron saint of the city.
An idealised portrait of this Bishop is found in the magnificent Pantaleão, Zaragoza.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Why rosconero?
This sweet has traditionally been associated with festivities and that in Roman times, at Saturnalia, a bean was hidden around the house and the slave who found it was free until spring.
</p><p>According to the Provincial Association of Confectionery and Pastry Entrepreneurs of Zaragoza, which brings together some 60 establishments, for San Valero the Aragonese usually consume more than 150,000 roscones to sweeten the after-meal.
</p><p>An idealised portrait of this Bishop is found in the magnificent Pantaleão, Zaragoza.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
