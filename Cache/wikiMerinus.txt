<p class="mw-empty-elt">

<p>Saint Mirin was born in 565, is also known as <b>Mirren of Benchor</b> (now called <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_Northern_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Bangor, Northern Ireland">Bangor</a>), <b>Merinus</b>, <b>Merryn</b> and <b>Meadhrán</b>. The <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the town and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Paisley" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Paisley">diocese of Paisley</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, he was the founder of a religious community which grew to become <a href="/wiki/Paisley_Abbey" title="Paisley Abbey">Paisley Abbey</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> of this <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the abbey became a centre of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>.
</p><p>A contemporary of the better known St Columba of Iona and disciple of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Comgall" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Comgall">Saint Comgall</a>, he was <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Down" title="County Down">County Down</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> before making his missionary voyage to <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. He is venerated in both Ireland and Scotland and in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History_and_legend">History and legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Mirin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History and legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Much of what is known about Mirin is difficult to separate from fable, however it is believed that he was of noble birth.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While still a young boy, his mother took him to the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Down" title="County Down">County Down</a> in the north east of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, where he was placed under the care of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Comgall" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Comgall">St. Comgall</a>. St Mirin later took oversight of the monastery and thus became the prior of Bangor Abbey, where he accepted visitors and sojourners.
</p><p>Later on, St Mirin travelled to the camp of the <a href="/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland" title="High King of Ireland">High King of Ireland</a> with the purpose of spreading the <a href="/wiki/Christian_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian faith">Christian faith</a>. Having heard of Mirin's arrival, the king refused to allow the saint to enter the camp. Mirin, thus slighted, was said to have prayed to God that the king might feel his wife's labour pangs, her time being near. The legend continues that, just as St Mirin had prayed, the king fell ill and roared in pain for three days and nights. In desperation the king sought out Mirin and granted him all he wished, including the right to go out and preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> to the men of his camp. In response to these concessions St Mirin prayed on his behalf and he was freed from his pain.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thereafter he was appointed to the west of Scotland and, after a long and difficult journey, arrived where the town of Paisley now stands. The area had recently been abandoned by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Romans</a> and was in the possession of a powerful local chieftain. This chief took a liking to Mirin and the saint was allotted a small field near the river in the southern part of town. This plot was called St Mirin's Croft until it was later developed.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He founded the first church in Paisley, thought to be at Seedhill and after his death the shrine of Saint Mirin became a centre of pilgrimage.<sup id="cite_ref-paisley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paisley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In various charters and <a href="/wiki/Papal_Bull" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Bull">Papal Bulls</a> Mirin is referred to as <i>The glorious confessor, Saint Mirin</i>. His image was engraved on the seal of the Abbey, depicting him in the <a href="/wiki/Vestments" class="mw-redirect" title="Vestments">vestments</a> of a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. Around the seal was inscribed the prayer <i>O Mirin, pray for your servants</i>. In <a href="/wiki/James_IV_of_Scotland" title="James IV of Scotland">King James IV</a>'s Charter of 1488 raising Paisley to the status of <a href="/wiki/Burgh_of_barony" title="Burgh of barony">burgh of barony</a>, one of the reasons cited was "the singular respect we have for the glorious confessor, Saint Mirin".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mirin_in_modern_Scotland">Mirin in modern Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Mirin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mirin in modern Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg/220px-Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg/330px-Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg/440px-Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg 2x" data-file-width="480" data-file-height="640" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Mirin_-_geograph.org.uk_-_844430.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of St Mirin in Paisley</div></div></div>
<p>There is a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> within <a href="/wiki/Paisley_Abbey" title="Paisley Abbey">Paisley Abbey</a>, dedicated to Mirin containing a sculptured <a href="/wiki/Rock_(geology)" title="Rock (geology)">stone</a> <a href="/wiki/Frieze" title="Frieze">frieze</a> depicting the life of the saint. The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/St_Mirin%27s_Cathedral" title="St Mirin&#39;s Cathedral">St Mirin's Cathedral</a> is also named in his honour. <a href="/wiki/St_Mirren_F.C." title="St Mirren F.C.">St Mirren F.C.</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Football_(soccer)" class="mw-redirect" title="Football (soccer)">football</a> club from <a href="/wiki/Paisley,_Renfrewshire" title="Paisley, Renfrewshire">Paisley</a>, is named after him. The St Mirin Burn flows into the <a href="/wiki/River_Cart" title="River Cart">White Cart Water</a> close to the town centre. He is also commemorated by St Mirren Street which links Paisley Cross to Causeyside Street. Since the closure of <a href="/wiki/St_Mirin%27s_Academy" title="St Mirin&#39;s Academy">St Mirin's and St Margaret's High School</a> (formerly St Mirin's Academy) in 2001, there has been no school in the town dedicated to the saint. However, there is a St Mirin's Primary School in the <a href="/wiki/Simshill" title="Simshill">Simshill</a> area of the neighbouring city of <a href="/wiki/Glasgow" title="Glasgow">Glasgow</a>.
</p><p>Elsewhere in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, the island of <a href="/wiki/Inchmurrin" title="Inchmurrin">Inchmurrin</a> (i.e. Mirin's Island) in <a href="/wiki/Loch_Lomond" title="Loch Lomond">Loch Lomond</a> and a farm called Knockmurran (i.e. Mirin's Hill) near <a href="/wiki/Coylton" title="Coylton">Coylton</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ayrshire" title="Ayrshire">Ayrshire</a> are named after him. St Mirin's Well can be found near <a href="/wiki/Kilsyth" title="Kilsyth">Kilsyth</a> in <a href="/wiki/Stirlingshire" title="Stirlingshire">Stirlingshire</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Statue">Statue</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Mirin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Statue">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A pedestal of blond <a href="/wiki/Sandstone" title="Sandstone">sandstone</a>, designed by landscape architect Daniel McKendry, bearing the inscription taken from the <a href="/wiki/Aberdeen_Breviary" title="Aberdeen Breviary">Aberdeen Breviary</a> <i>At Length Full of Sanctity and Miracles, Mirin Slept in the Lord at Paisley</i>  was erected in 2003 opposite St Mirin's Cathedral at the junction of Incle Street, Gauze Street and Glasgow Road in Paisley. A <a href="/wiki/Bronze" title="Bronze">bronze</a> <a href="/wiki/Statue" title="Statue">statue</a> of the saint<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Norman_Galbraith&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Norman Galbraith (page does not exist)">Norman Galbraith</a> was mounted on the pedestal and was unveiled on the saint's day, 15 September 2007, by the <a href="/wiki/Provost_(civil)" title="Provost (civil)">Provost</a> of <a href="/wiki/Renfrewshire" title="Renfrewshire">Renfrewshire</a> Councillor <a href="/w/index.php?title=Celia_Lawson&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Celia Lawson (page does not exist)">Celia Lawson</a> in the presence of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paisley" title="Bishop of Paisley">Bishop of Paisley</a> the Rt Rev <a href="/wiki/Philip_Tartaglia" title="Philip Tartaglia">Philip Tartaglia</a>, the Minister of <a href="/wiki/Paisley_Abbey" title="Paisley Abbey">Paisley Abbey</a> the Rt Rev <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alan_Birss&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Alan Birss (page does not exist)">Alan Birss</a>, the Rt Hon <a href="/wiki/Douglas_Alexander" title="Douglas Alexander">Douglas Alexander</a> MP, <a href="/wiki/Jim_Sheridan_(politician)" title="Jim Sheridan (politician)">Jim Sheridan</a> MP, <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Henry" title="Hugh Henry">Hugh Henry</a> MSP, <a href="/wiki/Debbie_McGee" title="Debbie McGee">Debbie McGee</a>, the Deputy <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Renfrewshire" title="Lord Lieutenant of Renfrewshire">Lord Lieutenant of Renfrewshire</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Wardrop_(deputy_Lord_Lieutenant)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James Wardrop (deputy Lord Lieutenant) (page does not exist)">James Wardrop</a> and the sculptor Norman Galbraith. The erection and unveiling of the statue were co-ordinated by Daniel McKendry on behalf of the Mirin Project.
</p>
