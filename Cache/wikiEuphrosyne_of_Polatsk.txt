<p>Saint <b>Euphrosyne of Polotsk</b> (or Polatsk, Połack) (<a href="/wiki/Belarusian_language" title="Belarusian language">Belarusian</a>: <span lang="be">Еўфрасіння Полацкая</span>; 1104–1167) was the granddaughter of a <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Polotsk" title="Principality of Polotsk">prince of Polotsk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vseslav_of_Polotsk" title="Vseslav of Polotsk">Vseslav</a>, and daughter of Prince <a href="/wiki/Vseslav_of_Polotsk#His_Family" title="Vseslav of Polotsk">Svyatoslav of Polotsk</a>. She has long been a popular saint among Orthodox devotees, particularly those in Belarus, Ukraine, and Russia with a traditional feast day of May 23. In addition, since 1984, she has been one of the 15 patron <a href="/wiki/List_of_Russian_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Russian saints">saints</a> of Belarus, whose lives are celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Belarusian_Orthodox_Church" title="Belarusian Orthodox Church">Belarusian Orthodox Church</a>, on the first Sunday after <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kasaty_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kasaty-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Euphrosyne_of_Polotsk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Predslava was born between 1101 and 1104, into the <a href="/wiki/Rurik" title="Rurik">Rurik</a> noble family, members of which were the dukes of the <a href="/wiki/Principality" title="Principality">principality</a> of <a href="/wiki/Polotsk" title="Polotsk">Polotsk</a>, in what is modern day <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarus</a>. Her father was Prince Svyatoslav-Georgy Vseslavich, second son of <a href="/wiki/Vseslav_of_Polotsk" title="Vseslav of Polotsk">Vseslav the Sorcerer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Belarus_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Belarus-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She refused all proposals of marriage and, without her parents' knowledge, ran away to the convent where her aunt was the <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. She became a nun and took the name Euphrosyne.  With the blessing of the Bishop of Polotsk, she began to live near the Sophia cathedral, where she spent her time copying books.<sup id="cite_ref-church_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The money she thus earned she distributed amongst the poor.
</p><p>Around 1128 Bishop Elias of Polotsk entrusted Euphrosyne the task of setting up a convent. At the newly constructed Savior-Transfiguration convent at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Seltse,_Belarus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Seltse, Belarus (page does not exist)">Seltse</a> she taught young women to copy books, sing, sew and do other handicrafts. Through her efforts, in 1161, a cathedral was built which survives to the present day.<sup id="cite_ref-church_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
She also founded a monastery dedicated to the Mother of God, as well as two churches. The church of The Holy Saviour still stands today and is considered to be the most precious monument of early <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarusian</a> architecture.
</p><p>Towards the end of her life, she undertook a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-church_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Michael_II_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Michael II of Constantinople">Patriarch Michael II of Constantinople</a> gave her an icon of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a>, which is now called the <i>Virgin of Korsun</i>. The Crusader king, <a href="/wiki/Amalric_I_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Amalric I of Jerusalem">Amalric I of Jerusalem</a>, also received her in the Holy Land<sup id="cite_ref-ecole_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ecole-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  where she died circa 1173. Her body, after the conquest of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> by <a href="/wiki/Saladin" title="Saladin">Saladin</a> in 1187, was carried by the monks to <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> and deposited there in the <a href="/wiki/Kiev_Pechersk_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev Pechersk Monastery">Monastery of the Caves</a>. It was only in 1910 that the relics of the saint were brought back to her native town of <a href="/wiki/Polotsk" title="Polotsk">Polotsk</a>.
</p>

<p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on May 23.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Euphrosyne is the only virgin <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of East Slav origin.
</p><p>Euphrosyne (or Efrosinia) of Polotsk is a patron saint of Belarus.<sup id="cite_ref-Belarus_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Belarus-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  In Belarus there is a Convent of Saint Euphrosyne in Polotsk and a Saint Euphrosyne Orthodox Church in Minsk.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In addition, there are churches dedicated to Euphrosyne of Polotsk in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Toronto" title="Toronto">Toronto</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/South_River,_New_Jersey" title="South River, New Jersey">South River, New Jersey</a> (see: <a href="/wiki/St._Euphrosynia_Belarusian_Orthodox_Church" title="St. Euphrosynia Belarusian Orthodox Church">St. Euphrosynia Belarusian Orthodox Church</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cross_of_Saint_Euphrosyne">Cross of Saint Euphrosyne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Euphrosyne_of_Polotsk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cross of Saint Euphrosyne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Cross_of_Saint_Euphrosyne" title="Cross of Saint Euphrosyne">Cross of Saint Euphrosyne</a> was a gem-studded cross created at her behest by a local master, Lazar Bohsa (<a href="/wiki/Belarusian_language" title="Belarusian language">Belarusian</a>: <span lang="be">Лазар Богша</span>). The famous six-armed golden cross was decorated with enamels and precious stones and presented by her to the Church of the Holy Saviour in 1161. The relic survived centuries of turbulence until <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, when it disappeared during the evacuation of the museum in 1941. The cross was last seen in <a href="/wiki/Mogilev" title="Mogilev">Mogilev</a>. Despite efforts of the Belarusian government to trace its whereabouts in the early 1990s, which included searching in private collections in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>, it has not been found.
</p>
