<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:428px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Oaten-hill-martyrs.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Oaten-hill-martyrs.png/426px-Oaten-hill-martyrs.png" decoding="async" width="426" height="144" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Oaten-hill-martyrs.png/639px-Oaten-hill-martyrs.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Oaten-hill-martyrs.png/852px-Oaten-hill-martyrs.png 2x" data-file-width="1450" data-file-height="490" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Oaten-hill-martyrs.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Oaten Hill Martyrs (Blessed Robert Wilcox, Blessed Gerard Edwardes a.k.a. "Edward Campion", Blessed Christopher Buxton and Blessed Robert Widmerpool)</div></div></div>
<p>The <b>Oaten Hill Martyrs</b> were <a href="/wiki/Catholic_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyrs</a> who were executed by <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanging, drawing and quartering</a> at Oaten Hill, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a>, on 1 October 1588. The gallows had been put up in 1576. These four were <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-oaten_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oaten-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Robert_Wilcox">Robert Wilcox</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oaten_Hill_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Robert Wilcox">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Robert Wilcox</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1558<br /><a href="/wiki/Chester" title="Chester">Chester</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">1 October 1588 (aged 29 - 30)<br />Oaten Hill, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 December 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 October</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Robert Wilcox was born in <a href="/wiki/Chester,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Chester, England">Chester, England</a> in 1558 and entered the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a> when he was twenty-five years old and was ordained on 20 April 1585.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was sent to England with other priests seeking to expand the Catholic faith and deal with the country's expanding Protestantism under Queen <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>. Wilcox arrived in England on 7 June 1586 but was arrested almost immediately at <a href="/wiki/Lydd" title="Lydd">Lydd</a> in Kent, near to where he entered the country. As a captive, he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a> prison where he was examined on 15 August 1588. Here he admitted he was a Catholic priest and was sent for trial with the others to <a href="/wiki/Canterbury,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Canterbury, England">Canterbury, England</a>. Wilcox was the first of the four to be executed. It is recorded that he told his companions to be of good heart. He was going to heaven before them, where he would carry the tidings of their coming after him.<sup id="cite_ref-RW_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RW-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Wilcox was thirty years of age.
</p>
<h2><span id="Gerard_Edwards_.28.22Edward_Campion.22.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Gerard_Edwards_(&quot;Edward_Campion&quot;)">Gerard Edwards ("Edward Campion")</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oaten_Hill_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Gerard Edwards (&quot;Edward Campion&quot;)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gerard Edwards, a Catholic priest, was born at <a href="/wiki/Ludlow" title="Ludlow">Ludlow</a>, Shropshire, and studied at <a href="/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford" title="Jesus College, Oxford">Jesus College, Oxford</a>, but left without obtaining a degree, as he was unable to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oaten_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oaten-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was for some time in the service of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Fiennes,_10th_Baron_Dacre" title="Gregory Fiennes, 10th Baron Dacre">Gregory Fiennes, 10th Baron Dacre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 22 February 1586 he left England to study for the priesthood in Rheims. In accordance with the custom of many Douai priests of taking an alias for their greater safety on the English mission, he changed his name to "Edward Campion" in honour of St <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Because of his education he was ordained after just a year and returned to England at Easter 1587. He was captured in <a href="/wiki/Sittingbourne" title="Sittingbourne">Sittingbourne</a>, Kent, just a few weeks later, however, and was imprisoned at the Newgate and the Marshalsea prisons in London following questioning by order of the Privy Council on 22 April 1587. Upon a second examination on 14 August 1588, he admitted to being a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was thirty-six years of age at the time of his execution. 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Edward Campion</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Gerard Edwards
c. 1552<br /><a href="/wiki/Ludlow" title="Ludlow">Ludlow</a>, Shropshire, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">1 October 1588 (aged 35 - 36)<br />Oaten Hill, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 December 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">October 1</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Christopher_Buxton">Christopher Buxton</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oaten_Hill_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Christopher Buxton">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Christopher Buxton (1562 – 1588) was born in <a href="/wiki/Derbyshire" title="Derbyshire">Derbyshire</a> in 1562. He was a student of <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Garlick" title="Nicholas Garlick">Nicholas Garlick</a> at the Grammar School, <a href="/wiki/Tideswell" title="Tideswell">Tideswell, Peak District</a>. Garlick entered the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> at Rheims on 22 June 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Buxton arrived about a month later.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1584 he was sent to the English College in Rome where he was ordained on 26 October 1586.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He had a lengthy and difficult journey across Europe, calling in at Rheims on his way to Dieppe. Word had already reached the continent concerning the government's plans to exile or execute the priests then held in prison. A Dr. Darbishire in Paris directed Buxton to delay his departure for the time being, and in obedience complied. But having learned that Darbishire's counsel was not a directive of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Persons" title="Robert Persons">Father Persons</a> in England, Buxton crossed to Kent in early September 1587.
</p><p>He was arrested in Kent about two months later, and taken to the Marshalsea prison. On 15 August 1588, he was examined at which time he admitted he was a priest. As he was so young, it was thought that his constancy might be shaken by the sight of the deaths of his companions, and his life was offered to him if he would conform to the new religion; but he answered that he would not purchase a corruptible life at such a price, and that if he had a hundred lives he would willingly surrender them all in defence of his faith.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea_Prison" class="mw-redirect" title="Marshalsea Prison">Marshalsea Prison</a> he wrote a <i>Rituale</i>, the manuscript of which is now preserved as a relic at <a href="/wiki/Olney,_Buckinghamshire" title="Olney, Buckinghamshire">Olney, Buckinghamshire</a>. He sent this manuscript to a priest, as the last token of his friendship, the day before he was taken from prison. He was taken to Canterbury for trial and execution. Buxton died at the age of twenty-six. Christopher Buxton was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Christopher Buxton</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1562<br /><a href="/wiki/Derbyshire" title="Derbyshire">Derbyshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">1 October 1588 (aged 25 - 26)<br />Oaten Hill, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 December 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">October 1</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Robert_Widmerpool">Robert Widmerpool</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oaten_Hill_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Robert Widmerpool">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert Widmerpool, a layman, was born in <a href="/wiki/Nottinghamshire" title="Nottinghamshire">Nottinghamshire</a>. He attended <a href="/wiki/Gloucester_College,_Oxford" title="Gloucester College, Oxford">Gloucester Hall, Oxford</a>, but did not graduate. He obtained a post as tutor at the home of the <a href="/wiki/Anne_Percy,_Countess_of_Northumberland" title="Anne Percy, Countess of Northumberland">Countess of Northumberland</a>, and was arrested there for giving aid to a Catholic priest. He was imprisoned with the others at the Marshalsea.<sup id="cite_ref-oaten_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oaten-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When he had the rope round his neck, he thanked God for the glory of dying in Canterbury for the cause for which St. Thomas died.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was twenty-eight years old. 
</p>
