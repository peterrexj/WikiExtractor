					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Ethiopian Roman Catholic priest (1791–1855)</div>

<p><b>Ghébrē-Michael</b>, <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">CM</a> (1791 - 30 July 1855) was an <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ethiopia" title="Catholic Church in Ethiopia">Ethiopian Catholic</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulant</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Born in 1791, he became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church" title="Coptic Orthodox Church">Coptic Orthodox Church</a> in 1813 and later met <a href="/wiki/Giustino_de_Jacobis" title="Giustino de Jacobis">Giustino de Jacobis</a> on a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. Jacobis would later receive Ghébrē-Michael into the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ordain him as a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Coptic Orthodox bishop - the single Orthodox bishop in <a href="/wiki/Ethiopia" title="Ethiopia">Ethiopia</a> - took an intense disliking of Ghébrē-Michael and set out to eliminate both him and his patron de Jacobis. Ghébrē-Michael was soon imprisoned and tortured and later died in prison due to ill treatment.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gh%C3%A9br%C4%93-Michael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ghébrē-Michael was born in <a href="/wiki/Ethiopia" title="Ethiopia">Ethiopia</a> in Dibo in 1791.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was part of one of the three Ethiopian religious sects called the Kevats. He was educated in Dibo then aged twelve began his high school studies in one of the monastic schools where he was known for his shrewd manner of learning.<sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his childhood he lost one eye in an accident that in his culture would have rendered him unfit for most forms of work. He received an education and then began his studies in one of the <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> where he became a gifted student. He became professed as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> in 1813.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He had not prepared for <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church" title="Coptic Orthodox Church">Coptic Orthodox Church</a> and since monks did not become priests but he became fascinated instead with declining standards in monasteries and set out to find out what the causes were. His superiors helped facilitate his research and he was also allowed to travel across the nation visiting these monasteries and doing further research in their libraries. He also decided that these matters required further research and so decided to go to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> for additional studies.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In order to do this he joined a small delegation of Orthodox faithful who attempted to go to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> - and then <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a> - to ask the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Patriarch" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Patriarch">Coptic Patriarch</a> to appoint a new Orthodox bishop since the sole position had been rendered vacant. He also learnt that the bishop <a href="/wiki/Giustino_de_Jacobis" title="Giustino de Jacobis">Giustino de Jacobis</a> would become part of it due to high esteem for the latter. De Jacobis had been reluctant to accept but compromised that he would go with the delegation if it headed to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on the sojourn back to Ethiopia.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The delegation was not all too pleased with the bishop appointed since he had received part of his education from <a href="/wiki/Protestants" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestants">Protestants</a> and this bishop would go on to cause trouble for Catholicism in Ethiopia and had a particular disliking for Ghébrē-Michael and de Jacobis. The delegation then headed to Rome to meet <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> which the rector of the Irish College in Rome <a href="/wiki/Paul_Cullen_(cardinal)" title="Paul Cullen (cardinal)">Paul Cullen</a> (later a <a href="/wiki/Cardinalate" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinalate">cardinal</a>) detailed in a 19 August 1841 letter to the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Dublin" title="Archbishop of Dublin">Archbishop of Dublin</a> Daniel Murray.
</p><p>He first met de Jacobis during that <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> in September 1843 and the bishop advised him to return home via a different route since de Jacobis believed that his research would cause him to make enemies. The delegation returned to the <a href="/wiki/Red_Sea" title="Red Sea">Red Sea</a> port of <a href="/wiki/Massawa" title="Massawa">Massawa</a> and he himself returned sometime later on his own as de Jacobis advised. The two would meet on a regular basis for the next six months and the two together visited monasteries before de Jacobis himself received him into Catholicism in February 1844; this led to six other Coptic monks asking for admission too after having seen his example.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1850 he and de Jacobis were in conversation when the latter asked him if he desired becoming a priest. De Jacobis later <a href="/wiki/Ordination_in_the_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordination in the Catholic Church">ordained</a> him to the priesthood on 1 January 1851. The new Orthodox bishop instigated persecution of Catholicism and at one stage - when he was set to enter the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Mission" title="Congregation of the Mission">Congregation of the Mission</a> as a postulant - he and de Jacobis were arrested with four others. De Jacobis was kept hidden from the others while their captors hoped extreme isolation and torture would cause them to abandon Catholicism.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> But de Jacobis was later released and in late 1854 the Orthodox bishop failed through torture to get him and the others to apostatize. <a href="/wiki/Tewodros_II" title="Tewodros II">Tewodros II</a> was crowned as the king in 1855 and the king also sought to torture him to get him to apostatize to no avail. To that end the emperor kept him in chains and would have him brought with him whenever and wherever he travelled. In May 1855 the British Consul visited the emperor and the latter decided to put the priest on trial in the consul's presence. But he refused to abandon his faith and the court deemed that he should be shot dead. But the consul asked for him to be spared and the emperor agreed which left the priest back in chains and moved from place to place as the emperor travelled.<sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He later died due to ill treatment on a road under a <a href="/wiki/Cedrus" title="Cedrus">cedar tree</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His remains were never found.<sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>De Jacobis later explained in a letter to the order's Superior-General Jean-Baptiste Etienne that Ghébrē-Michael could be considered a member in the order despite his having been a postulant; in his words "he belonged in his heart and in his spirit to the congregation".<sup id="cite_ref-VC_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FV_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gh%C3%A9br%C4%93-Michael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The informative phase in investigation into potential beatification opened and closed in <a href="/wiki/Addis_Ababa" title="Addis Ababa">Addis Ababa</a> in 1904 as did a similar process in <a href="/wiki/Asmara" title="Asmara">Asmara</a> in 1909; the formal introduction to the cause came under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> on 25 January 1920 and he became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The apostolic process was held from 1921 to 1922 after which point the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> validated the previous processes on 11 December 1923. On 22 May 1926 Ghébrē-Michaels beatification received formal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> who beatified him on 3 October.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
