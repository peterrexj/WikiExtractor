<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>

<p><b>Gilbert de Moravia</b> (died 1245), later known as <b>Saint Gilbert of Dornoch</b>, or <b>Gilbert of Caithness</b>, was the most famous <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Caithness" title="Bishop of Caithness">Bishop of Caithness</a> and founder of <a href="/wiki/Dornoch_Cathedral" title="Dornoch Cathedral">Dornoch Cathedral</a>.  His name may suggest that he came from the semi-<a href="/wiki/Gaelicization" class="mw-redirect" title="Gaelicization">Gaelicized</a> family of <a href="/wiki/Flemings" class="mw-redirect" title="Flemings">Flemish</a> origin who were Lords of <a href="/wiki/Duffus" title="Duffus">Duffus</a>, and who during Gilbert's episcopate would create the <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Sutherland" title="Earl of Sutherland">Earldom of Sutherland</a> under Gilbert's possible cousin, <a href="/wiki/William_de_Moravia,_1st_Earl_of_Sutherland" title="William de Moravia, 1st Earl of Sutherland">William de Moravia, 1st Earl of Sutherland</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gilbert_de_Moravia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is known that Gilbert was the son of one Muiredach, son of Alexander <i>de Moravia</i> ("of <a href="/wiki/Moray" title="Moray">Moray</a>", thus indicating the ancestral home and not necessarily a family name). If Gilbert was of purely <a href="/wiki/Goidelic_languages" title="Goidelic languages">Gaelic</a> origin, his name may be a <a href="/wiki/Francization" title="Francization">Francization</a> of the Gaelic name <i>Gille Brigte</i> (<a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Gaelic language">modern:</a> <i>Gillebrìghde</i>). Gilbert allegedly had a younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Richard_de_Moravia" title="Richard de Moravia">Richard de Moravia</a>, who was killed fighting against Scandinavians and whose effigy-sarcophagus currently resides in the cathedral. He is also known to have had an older brother John, and a younger brother, Simon.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As his name indicates, Gilbert very likely came from Moray.  The family owned extensive lands in <a href="/wiki/Duffus" title="Duffus">Duffus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Strathbrock" class="mw-redirect" title="Strathbrock">Strabok</a>.  Gilbert was for a long time the Archdeacon of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Moray" title="Bishop of Moray">Bishopric of Moray</a>; it is probable that Gilbert was elected to the see sometime in the year 1223, in the presence of King <a href="/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Scotland" title="Alexander II of Scotland">Alexander II of Scotland</a> and his army. He was certainly bishop of Caithness by the summer of 1224. King Alexander probably decided that, after the murder of Gilbert's predecessor <a href="/wiki/Adam_of_Melrose" title="Adam of Melrose">Adam of Melrose</a>, the bishopric's seat (<i>cathedra</i>) should be moved closer to royal protection. So it was that Gilbert's episcopate saw the move of the bishopric from <a href="/wiki/Halkirk" title="Halkirk">Halkirk</a> in the far north of the diocese to <a href="/wiki/Dornoch" title="Dornoch">Dornoch</a> in the far south. It was to the new cathedral that, in 1239, Gilbert would move Bishop Adam's body. Gilbert nevertheless continued to reside for much of his episcopate in the more violent north, and maintained a palace at <a href="/wiki/Scrabster" title="Scrabster">Scrabster</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Achievements_and_death">Achievements and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gilbert_de_Moravia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Achievements and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gilbert's achievements include the building of the <a href="/wiki/Dornoch_Cathedral" title="Dornoch Cathedral">cathedral at Dornoch</a>, and the establishment of several hospices for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became known as a fine preacher, and he did much work to civilise his diocese.
</p><p>Gilbert died in his <a href="/wiki/Scrabster_Castle" title="Scrabster Castle">palace at Scrabster</a> in 1245, traditionally on 1 April. The latter day is his feast day. He was buried at Dornoch, and his relics were venerated until the <a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a>, oaths being sworn on them at least until 1545.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is the last Scottish saint to appear in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">Calendar of Saints</a>, although it is not known if he was ever formally <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>.
</p><p>Folklore surrounding St Gilbert was collected in Sutherlandshire in the late nineteenth century, including a legend of how he overcame a dragon—or salamander—that was terrorising the country.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
