<p><b>Plegmund</b> (or <b>Plegemund</b>;<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> died 2 August either 914 or 923) was a medieval English <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. He may have been a hermit before he became archbishop in 890.  As archbishop, he reorganised the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Winchester" title="Diocese of Winchester">Diocese of Winchester</a>, creating four new <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">sees</a>, and worked with other scholars in translating religious works.  He was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> after his death.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Plegmund&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of the early life of Plegmund except that he was of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercian</a> descent.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A later tradition, dating 300 years after his death,<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks153_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks153-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> stated that Plegmund lived as a hermit at <a href="/wiki/Plemstall" title="Plemstall">Plemstall</a> in Cheshire.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> His reputation as a scholar<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley215_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley215-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> attracted the attention of King <a href="/wiki/Alfred_the_Great" title="Alfred the Great">Alfred the Great</a>, who was trying to revive scholarship.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Some time before 887, Alfred summoned Plegmund to his court. There he worked with three other scholars – <a href="/wiki/W%C3%A6rferth" class="mw-redirect" title="Wærferth">Wærferth</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a>, Æthelstan and Wærwulf – on translating Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great's</a> treatise <i><a href="/wiki/Pastoral_Care" title="Pastoral Care">Pastoral Care</a></i> into Old English.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Plegmund&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Canterbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Plegmund was selected for the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a> in 890 by King Alfred.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE270_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE270-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His election to the Archbishopric of Canterbury is recorded in <a href="/wiki/Peterborough_Chronicle" title="Peterborough Chronicle">Manuscript E</a> of the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i>: "Here Archbishop Plegmund was elected by God and all the people."<sup id="cite_ref-ASC_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASC-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Fulk,_Archbishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Fulk, Archbishop of Reims">Fulk, Archbishop of Reims</a>, praised the election of Plegmund, stating that he would help root out the last remnants of paganism in the people.<sup id="cite_ref-Duckett113_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duckett113-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> However, there was a gap in time between the death of the previous Archbishop of Canterbury, <a href="/wiki/Ethelred,_Archbishop_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethelred, Archbishop of Canterbury">Ethelred</a>, and the consecration of Plegmund; this may have been because the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> had been offered to <a href="/wiki/Grimbald" title="Grimbald">Grimbald</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Flemish_people" title="Flemish people">Flemish</a> monk and scholar, who refused it.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley215_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley215-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Abels224_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels224-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Plegmund was granted his <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the 9th century, the see of Canterbury was at a low point. One of Plegmund's responsibilities was to re-establish its authority, and, in an attempt to do this, between 909 and 918 he created new sees within the existing <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Diocese of Winchester</a> in <a href="/wiki/Crediton" title="Crediton">Crediton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ramsbury" title="Ramsbury">Ramsbury</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sherborne" title="Sherborne">Sherborne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wells,_Somerset" title="Wells, Somerset">Wells</a>.  This meant that each future <a href="/wiki/Shire" title="Shire">shire</a> of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> had its own bishop; <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Crediton" title="Bishop of Crediton">of Crediton</a> for <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ramsbury_(ancient)" title="Bishop of Ramsbury (ancient)">of Ramsbury</a> for <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sherborne_(historic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sherborne (historic)">of Sherborne</a> for <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Wells" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Wells">of Wells</a> for <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a>, as well as the diocese of Winchester for <a href="/wiki/Hampshire" title="Hampshire">Hampshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Stafford182_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stafford182-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> To do this, Plegmund had to gain the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_III" title="Pope Sergius III">Pope Sergius III</a>, who had annulled all of the acts of Pope Formosus, and in 908 Plegmund travelled to Rome so that he could be re-granted his pallium.  He was the first archbishop of Canterbury to visit Rome for nearly a century, and he returned with the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Blaise" title="Saint Blaise">Saint Blaise</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Under Plegmund's archbishopric, the quality of the Latin used by his scribes improved, surpassing the poor quality used by the scribes of the previous two archbishops, <a href="/wiki/Ceolnoth" title="Ceolnoth">Ceolnoth</a> and <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_(archbishop)" title="Æthelred (archbishop)">Æthelred</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When Alfred died in 899, Plegmund crowned his son <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Elder" title="Edward the Elder">Edward</a> as king.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to his religious duties, Plegmund was involved in matters of state and he attended the formal councils held by Edward the Elder in 901, 903, 904 and 909.  He dedicated the tall tower of the <a href="/wiki/New_Minster,_Winchester" title="New Minster, Winchester">New Minster</a> at Winchester in 909.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Plegmund&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Plegmund died on 2 August 914<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> or 2 August 923.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> After his death Plegmund was considered a saint, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 2 August. However, his cult dates only from the 13th century.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS438_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS438-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
