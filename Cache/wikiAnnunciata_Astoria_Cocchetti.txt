<p><b>Annunciata Astoria Cocchetti</b> (9 May 1800 – 23 March 1882) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the founder of the Suore di Santa Dorotea di Cemmo - an order dedicated to the educational needs of the poor in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> and the surrounding cities.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Cocchetti assumed her vows as a religious in 1843 and was close with <a href="/wiki/Luca_Passi" title="Luca Passi">Luca Passi</a> who established a congregation similar to hers.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cocchetti received beatification in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> from <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 21 April 1991. She remains the patron of her order and of educators in general.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Annunciata_Astoria_Cocchetti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Annunciata Astoria Cocchetti was born in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> on 9 May 1800 as the third of six children to Marcantonio Cocchetti and Giulia Albarelli; two siblings were Vincenzo and Giuseppina.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was orphaned at the age of seven - in 1807 - and was taken to live with her grandmother. Her uncle Carlo - from Milan - decided to take both Giuseppina and Vincenzo to live with him while Cocchetti was sent to her grandmother's.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She received her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> and her <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation</a> - on 28 February 1810 - from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a> Gabrio Maria Nava.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the age of seventeen - in 1817 - she used her grandmother's home as a school for poor girls. She received her education at Rovato and in 1822 moved her studies to Cemmo Valcamonica.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her grandmother died on 19 April 1823 which prompted her to leave her home and move in with her uncle in Milan.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She resided there until 1829.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During this time she came into contact with <a href="/wiki/Luca_Passi" title="Luca Passi">Luca Passi</a> - who was in the process of establishing his own religious congregation - and remained in touch with him until his death in 1866. Cocchetti learned of a school in Brescia devoted to female education and decided to join it; one evening in 1831 she decided not to attend a gala evening with Carlo at the Teatro alla Scala and instead left a letter on his desk and travelled to Cemmo Valmonica.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1821 Erminia Panzerini (d. 2 May 1842) opened in Brescia a school for girls and entrusted it to the direction of Cocchetti in 1831; it was in 1831 she accepted a teaching position from Panzerini at this school with the encouragement of her spiritual director.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She realized in this place that her true purpose in life was to the education of the poor. At this point Passi wanted the two women to join his own order and aggregate the school to it but this was hindered due to Panzerini's 1842 death. She travelled to <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> in 1842 to establish her order - all the more since Passi was there - and returned in October with two prospective religious; the three made their solemn profession into Cocchetti's new order in 1843.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From this school she formed her own religious congregation - the Suore di Santa Dorotea di Cemmo - and established it on 9 October 1842 after being vested on the previous 3 October. The institute later received the diocesan approval - on 5 February 1855 - of the Bishop of Brescia Giacomo Maria Corna Pellegrini. Her order promoted the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises</a> as an appropriate focus for retreats.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the morning of 18 March 1882 she went to <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> but felt ill after receiving the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>. She was diagnosed with a fever as well as disease ravaging her. She received the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">Last Rites</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Viaticum" title="Viaticum">Viaticum</a> not long after this. Cocchetti died on 23 March 1882 and her remains were relocated on 22 January 1951. The order received the papal decree of praise of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 20 March 1934 and received the formal papal approval - on 10 May 1941 - of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>. Her congregation now works in numerous nations such as <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Uganda" title="Uganda">Uganda</a>. As of 2005 there were 336 religious in a total of 59 houses.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Annunciata_Astoria_Cocchetti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti,_Cemmo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg/220px-Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg/330px-Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg/440px-Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti%2C_Cemmo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2304" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tumba_de_la_Beata_Annunciata_Cocchetti,_Cemmo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Blessed Annunciata Coccheti, St. Dorothy's Chapel, Mother House, Cemmo</div></div></div>
<p>The miracle needed for her beatification involved the cure of Bortolina Milesi - aged thirteen - who had severe bowel complications that could have proven fatal had it not been for the intercession of Cocchetti. The process for investigating the miracle took place in 1952 and concluded in 1953.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
