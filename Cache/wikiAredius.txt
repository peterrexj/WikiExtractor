<p>Saint <b>Aredius</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> also  <span title="French-language text"><span lang="fr" style="font-style: normal;"><b>Yrieix, Abbé d'Attanum</b></span></span> and <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr"><b>Arède d'Atane</b></i></span> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;510</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;25 August 591, at Saint-Yrieix in the <a href="/wiki/Haute-Vienne" title="Haute-Vienne">Haute-Vienne</a>), was chancellor to <a href="/wiki/Theudebert_I" title="Theudebert I">Theudebert I</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>, and later Abbot of Attane (or Atane, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Attanum</i>). He founded the monastery of Attane, which was renamed after his death <i>Saint-Yrieix</i> in his honour. The town at the site became known as <a href="/wiki/Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche" title="Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche">Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche</a>. Several other French <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr"><a href="/wiki/Commune_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Commune in France">communes</a></i></span> are also called <i>Saint-Yrieix</i> after him. 
</p><p>He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>; his feast day is commemorated on the date of his death, 25 August.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background_and_early_years">Background and early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aredius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background and early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Aredius was from a prominent <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> family of <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Limousin" title="Limousin">Limousin</a>, an Occitan-speaking region. He was the son of a noble landowner, Jucundus, and his wife, Pelagia of Limoges. As a young boy he received his education from the abbot Sebastian of the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Vigeois" title="Vigeois">Vigeois</a>. As a young man, he was sent to the court of the Frankish king <a href="/wiki/Theodebert_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodebert I">Theodebert I</a> of Austrasia (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;534–548</span>) at Trier. By 540 he was appointed the king's chancellor.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Nicetius" title="Nicetius">Nicetius</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trier">bishop of Trier</a> persuaded Aredius to leave the dissolute life at court. According to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, one day, while the clerics sang psalms in the church, a dazzling white dove, after flying around Aredius, landed on his head, as if to show that he was already filled with the Holy Spirit. As he was a little shy, he waved it away, and it flittered a little before landing on its shoulder, and followed him all the way to the bishop's house.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERene_de_Montalembert1872477−483_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERene_de_Montalembert1872477−483-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_establishment_and_works">Monastic establishment and works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aredius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monastic establishment and works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon the death of his father, Aredius returned to the Limousin to care for his mother. Entrusting to her the management of his estates, he lived for a time as a hermit in a cave. He founded, between 564 and 572, the monastery of Attane on the land of his inherited estate, located on the rivers <a href="/wiki/Loue_(Isle)" title="Loue (Isle)">Loue</a> and Couchou<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Naming_conventions_(geographic_names)" title="Wikipedia:Naming conventions (geographic names)"><span title="The geographic scope near this tag is ambiguous. (February 2023)">where?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> in Haute-Vienne.  He was the first abbot of the monastery, and the earliest monks were members of his own household. Gregory of Tours says that the house followed the rule of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">John Cassian</a> and later incorporated some aspects of the rule of Saint <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a>. Later, other monks joined them. The site of the monastery was to give rise to the city of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche" title="Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche">Saint-Yrieix-la-Perche</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aredius divided his time between agricultural labor and study. He was known for his evangelical journeys throughout Gaul. He founded monasteries in Vigeois and Excideuil in Périgord and went on pilgrimages, always on foot. Every year he made a pilgrimage to Tours to celebrate the feast of Saint <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>. He would also travel annually to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)">Holy Cross Abbey</a> in Poitiers to visit Queen <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund</a>. He supported the cult of <a href="/wiki/Medardus" title="Medardus">Medardus</a> of Soissons and probably built the church in his honour at Excideuil. Aredius built several churches in honour of saints whose relics he had collected.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography_and_relics">Hagiography and relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aredius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hagiography and relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Miracle stories began to be associated with him. People in the area believed him to have the gift of healing. His hagiographer, Gregory of Tours, says they crowded to Aredius "like bees to a hive".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERene_de_Montalembert1872478−479_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERene_de_Montalembert1872478−479-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> On more than one occasion, he intervened with the Merovingian princes on behalf of the people regarding oppressive taxes.
</p><p>He was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> who wrote the most well-known accounts of Aredius' life. Gregory included Aredius in three of his works <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Historiae</i></span> (sometimes known as <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Historia Francorum</i></span>, 'History of the Franks'), <i>Lives of the Fathers</i> and <i>Glories of the Martyrs</i>. Aredius bequeathed his wealth to, among other beneficiaries, the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Martin,_Tours" title="Basilica of Saint Martin, Tours">church of St Martin of Tours</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The town of Saint-Yrieix-La-Perche has requested that the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art" title="Metropolitan Museum of Art">Metropolitan Museum of Art</a> in New York return a reliquary of Aredius, which the town maintains was illegally purchased in 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li id="cite_note-2"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-2">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Being from an <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a>-speaking region, the abbot's birth name was one native to that language: <span title="Occitan (post 1500)-language text"><i lang="oc">Iriès</i></span>. This was modified to <i>Yrieix</i> for Francophone ears, and then the Latinised <i>Aredius</i> adopted for official use and in religious life. The Latinate form was then reassimilated into French as <i>Arède</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></span>
