<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg/220px-St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg/330px-St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg/440px-St.Altfrid%27sRelics.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a><figcaption>Saint Altfrid's relics on display in Essen Cathedral on his feast day</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Saint Altfrid</b> (or <b>Altfrid of Hildesheim</b>) (died 15 August 874) was a leading figure in Germany in the ninth century. A <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monk, he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hildesheim" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Hildesheim">Bishop of Hildesheim</a>, and founded <a href="/wiki/Essen_Abbey" title="Essen Abbey">Essen Abbey</a>. He was also a close royal adviser to the East Frankish King <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>.
</p><p>He is a Roman Catholic saint. His feast day is celebrated on 15 August, the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_the_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Assumption of the Virgin Mary">Assumption of the Virgin Mary</a>, but also in Essen and Hildesheim on 16 August.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Altfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is no contemporary biography of Altfrid. He is first mentioned by name on 3 October 852, when he took part in a council in <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> as Bishop of Hildesheim.
</p><p>According to the <i>Hildesheim Chronicle</i> Altfrid died "rich in days" in 874, from which a year of birth of around 800 is assumed. He owned land in the <a href="/wiki/Harz" title="Harz">Harzvorland</a> and in central <a href="/wiki/Essen" title="Essen">Essen</a> (<i>Asnithi</i>), which may have been inherited from his family, and it seems likely that he belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxon</a> nobility, and may have been connected to the Imperial family of the <a href="/wiki/Liudolfings" class="mw-redirect" title="Liudolfings">Liudolfings</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who, however, had no influence in Altfrid's foundation Essen Abbey (<i>Stift Essen</i>) until after his death.
</p><p>He was apparently a monk at <a href="/wiki/Corvey_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Corvey Abbey">Corvey Abbey</a> before he was declared Bishop of Hildesheim in 851<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in succession to <a href="/wiki/Ebbo" title="Ebbo">Ebbo</a>, who died on 20 March of that year. Since Ebbo had been installed and removed several times as <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Reims">Archbishop of Reims</a>, Altfrid took the unusual step of repeating all consecrations and ordinations of his predecessor to avoid their invalidation.
</p><p>In 864, Altfrid moved the relics of Saint Marsus from <a href="/wiki/Auxerre" title="Auxerre">Auxerre</a> to an unknown place in Saxony, most likely to Corvey Abbey. His sermon on the arrival of the relics survives. In addition, Altfrid laid the cornerstone of a new <a href="/wiki/Hildesheim_Cathedral" title="Hildesheim Cathedral">cathedral in Hildesheim</a> in 852,<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> a three-aisled cruciform church with a crossing and transept. It was completed in 872 and was consecrated on 1 November 872 in the presence of four bishops and the Abbot of Corvey.
</p><p>Even before his consecration as bishop, Altfrid had been active in the foundation of several female religious communities. Between 845 and 847 he acquired the relics of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian" title="Saints Cosmas and Damian">Saints Cosmas and Damian</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Altfrid also supported the Saxon Count Ricdag in the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Lamspringe_Abbey" title="Lamspringe Abbey">nunnery at Lamspringe</a> by procuring for it the relics of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Hadrian&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Hadrian (page does not exist)">Saint Hadrian</a> from Rome.
</p><p>According to the Hildesheim Chronicle Altfrid also founded a Benedictine monastery on his own land in the Harzvorland, of which no further details of either location or duration are known. More important is Altfrid's other foundation, <a href="/wiki/Essen_Abbey" title="Essen Abbey">Essen Abbey</a> (<i>Stift Essen</i>), on his own property in Essen (<i>Asthnide</i>) on the Hellweg. The first Abbess of Essen was his kinswoman Gerswith, often referred to as his sister, although there is no direct evidence of this.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As in Hildesheim, Altfrid also built a church<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> for the canonesses, the <i>Stiftskirche</i>, later known as the <i>Essener Münster</i> and from 1958 as <a href="/wiki/Essen_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Essen Cathedral">Essen Cathedral</a>. Altfrid was buried here after his death on 15 August 874, according to his wishes. A <a href="/wiki/Gothic_architecture" title="Gothic architecture">Gothic</a> tomb stands over his grave in the east crypt, which is named after him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diplomat">Diplomat</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Altfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Diplomat">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Altfrid, was a close confidant of <a href="/wiki/Ludwig_the_German" class="mw-redirect" title="Ludwig the German">Ludwig the German</a>, and from 860 took an influential role in the power struggles between the various parts of the disintegrating Frankish empire. In 860 Altfrid was present at the meeting between Ludwig the German and <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a> in St. Castor's Basilica in <a href="/wiki/Koblenz" title="Koblenz">Koblenz</a>, where the two kings agreed a peace treaty. In the years that followed it is possible to trace a great deal of travelling on Altfrid's part: in 862 he was in <a href="/wiki/Asselt" title="Asselt">Asselt</a>, later in <a href="/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne" title="Compiègne">Compiègne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Savonni%C3%A8res" title="Savonnières">Savonnières</a>, in 864 in <a href="/wiki/Pitres" class="mw-redirect" title="Pitres">Pitres</a>, in 865 at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Synod_of_Thousey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Synod of Thousey (page does not exist)">Synod of Thousey</a>, in 867 in <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a> and in 868 at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Worms_(868)" title="Synod of Worms (868)">Synod of Worms</a>. Altfrid had decisive influence on the form of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Meerssen" title="Treaty of Meerssen">Treaty of Meerssen</a>, which divided <a href="/wiki/Lotharingia" title="Lotharingia">Lotharingia</a> on 9 August 870 between <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Francia</a> and <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Francia</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_after">Death and after</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Altfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and after">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The place of Altfrid's death is not known, although the date is recorded by Abbot Altbert of Lobbes as the feast of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary, i.e., 15 August 874.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was buried, according to his wishes, in the church of Essen Abbey.
</p><p>After his death Altfrid was particularly venerated at his tomb in Essen. Around the year 1000 miracles were reported at his grave, which greatly increased the veneration, and the effects of a supposedly healing spring close to the church were also ascribed to his intercession. After a serious fire in the church in the 13th century a Gothic stone <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> was obtained for his bones. Altfrid's feast day - which in Essen was celebrated on 16 August, rather than on 15 August -  was the most festive in the abbey's yearly calendar.
</p><p>Nevertheless, Altfrid was not a canonised saint, and when the abbey was secularised in 1803 his veneration fell off, only to revive during the <i><a href="/wiki/Kulturkampf" title="Kulturkampf">Kulturkampf</a></i> at the end of the century. After the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Essen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Essen">Bishopric of Essen</a> in 1958 the first bishop asked Rome to confirm the celebration of Altfrid's feast day as an official church festival, a request which was granted in 1965.
</p>
