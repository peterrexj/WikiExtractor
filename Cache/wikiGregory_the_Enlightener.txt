<p><b>Gregory the Illuminator</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Armenian_orthography" title="Classical Armenian orthography">classical</a> <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Գրիգոր Լուսաւորիչ</span>, <a href="/wiki/Armenian_orthography_reform" title="Armenian orthography reform">reformed spelling</a>: Գրիգոր Լուսավորիչ, <i>Grigor Lusavorich</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>  <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;257</span>&#160;&#8211; <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;328</span>) was the founder and first <a href="/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians" title="Catholicos of All Armenians">head</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Apostolic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> He converted <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a> from <a href="/wiki/Armenian_mythology" title="Armenian mythology">paganism</a> to Christianity in the early fourth century (traditionally dated to 301), making Armenia the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the Armenian Apostolic Church and in some other churches. 
</p><p>Gregory is said to have been the son of a <a href="/wiki/Parthian_Empire" title="Parthian Empire">Parthian</a> nobleman, <a href="/wiki/Anak_the_Parthian" title="Anak the Parthian">Anak</a>, who assassinated the <a href="/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia" title="Arsacid dynasty of Armenia">Arsacid</a> king of Armenia <a href="/wiki/Khosrov_II_of_Armenia" title="Khosrov II of Armenia">Khosrov II</a>. The young Gregory was saved from the extermination of Anak's family and was raised as a Christian in <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_(Mazaca)" title="Caesarea (Mazaca)">Caesarea</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>. Gregory returned to Armenia as an adult and entered the service of King <a href="/wiki/Tiridates_III_of_Armenia" title="Tiridates III of Armenia">Tiridates III</a>, who had Gregory tortured after he refused to sacrifice to a pagan goddess. After discovering Gregory's true identity, Tiridates had him thrown into a deep pit called <a href="/wiki/Khor_Virap" title="Khor Virap">Khor Virap</a>. Gregory was miraculously saved from death and released after many years with the help of Tiridates' sister <a href="/wiki/Khosrovidukht_(sister_of_Tiridates_III_of_Armenia)" title="Khosrovidukht (sister of Tiridates III of Armenia)">Khosrovidukht</a>. Gregory then healed Tiridates, who the hagiographical sources say had been transfomed into a boar for his sins, and preached Christianity in Armenia. He was consecrated bishop of Armenia at Caesarea, baptized King Tiridates and the Armenian people, and traveled throughout Armenia, destroying pagan temples and building churches in their place.
</p><p>Gregory eventually gave up the patriarchate to live as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and was succeeded by his son <a href="/wiki/St._Aristaces_I" title="St. Aristaces I">Aristaces</a>. Gregory's descendants, called the <a href="/wiki/Gregorids" title="Gregorids">Gregorids</a>, hereditarily held the office of Patriarch of Armenia with some interruptions until the fifth century. It is in Gregory's honor that the Armenian Church is sometimes called <span title="Armenian-language romanization"><i lang="hy-Latn">lusavorchʻakan</i></span> ("of the Illuminator") or Gregorian.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Armenian tradition, the standard version of the life of Gregory the Illuminator derives from the fifth-century history attributed to <a href="/wiki/Agathangelos" title="Agathangelos">Agathangelos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199415_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199415-8">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to Agathangelos's account, Gregory was the son of the <a href="/wiki/Parthian_Empire" title="Parthian Empire">Parthian</a> nobleman <a href="/wiki/Anak_the_Parthian" title="Anak the Parthian">Anak</a>; the later Armenian historian <a href="/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi" title="Movses Khorenatsi">Movses Khorenatsi</a> identifies Anak as a member of the Parthian noble house of <a href="/wiki/House_of_Suren" title="House of Suren">Suren</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELang1970155–156_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELang1970155–156-9">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989347_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989347-10">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the incitation of the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_Empire" title="Sasanian Empire">Sasanian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Ardashir_I" title="Ardashir I">Ardashir I</a>, who promised to return Anak his domain as a reward, the Parthian nobleman went to Armenia and assassinated the <a href="/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia" title="Arsacid dynasty of Armenia">Arsacid</a> king of Armenia <a href="/wiki/Khosrov_II_of_Armenia" title="Khosrov II of Armenia">Khosrov II</a> after gaining his confidence.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199772_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199772-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Anak was then put to death by the Armenian nobles along with his entire family․<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199772_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199772-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Anak's son Gregory narrowly escaped execution with the help of his nurse, whom Khorenatsi calls Sophy, sister of a Cappadocian notable named Euthalius (Ewtʻagh).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Gregory was taken to <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_(Mazaca)" title="Caesarea (Mazaca)">Caesarea</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>, where he received a Christian upbringing.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199415–16_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199415–16-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Khorenatsi, upon coming of age, Gregory married Mariam, daughter of a Christian named David.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228–229_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228–229-14">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He had two children with Mariam: <a href="/wiki/St._Aristaces_I" title="St. Aristaces I">Aristaces</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Vrtanes_I" title="St. Vrtanes I">Vrtanes</a>, who would later succeed Gregory as patriarchs of Armenia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228–229_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1978228–229-14">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Christianization_of_Armenia">Christianization of Armenia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Christianization of Armenia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the birth of their sons, Mariam and Gregory separated, and Gregory went to Armenia to enter the service of King <a href="/wiki/Tiridates_III_of_Armenia" title="Tiridates III of Armenia">Tiridates III</a>, son of the assassinated king Khosrov II.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxi–xxxii-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> After Gregory refused to sacrifice to the goddess <a href="/wiki/Anahit" title="Anahit">Anahit</a>, the king had Gregory imprisoned and subjected to many tortures.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xlii–xliii_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xlii–xliii-19">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Once Tiridates discovered that Gregory was the son of his father's killer, he had Gregory thrown into a deep pit called <a href="/wiki/Khor_Virap" title="Khor Virap">Khor Virap</a> near <a href="/wiki/Artaxata" title="Artaxata">Artaxata</a>, where he remained for thirteen (or fifteen) years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELang1970156_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELang1970156-20">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1984_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1984-21">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In Agathangelos's history, Gregory is miraculously saved and brought out from the pit after Tiridates' sister <a href="/wiki/Khosrovidukht_(sister_of_Tiridates_III_of_Armenia)" title="Khosrovidukht (sister of Tiridates III of Armenia)">Khosrovidukht</a> sees a vision.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Gregory then healed the king, who, Agathangelos writes, had been transformed into a wild boar for his sinful behavior.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Tiridates and his court accepted Christianity, making Armenia the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199416_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199416-22">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg/220px-Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="359" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg/330px-Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg 2x" data-file-width="368" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gregory_the_Illuminator.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Baptism of the Armenian People</i> (1892), by <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Aivazovsky" title="Ivan Aivazovsky">Ivan Aivazovsky</a></div></div></div>
<p>After being released, Gregory preached the Christian faith in Armenia and erected shrines to the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gayane_Church" title="Saint Gayane Church">Gayane</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hripsime" title="Hripsime">Hripsime</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vagharshapat" title="Vagharshapat">Vagharshapat</a> on a spot indicated to him in a vision.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;f&#93;</a></sup> Vagharshapat would later become home to the <a href="/wiki/Etchmiadzin_Cathedral" title="Etchmiadzin Cathedral">mother church</a> of Armenian Christianity and, by medieval times, called Ejmiatsin ("descent of the only-begotten") in reference to Gregory's vision.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976478_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976478-27">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;g&#93;</a></sup> Gregory, sometimes accompanied by Tiridates,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199419_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199419-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> went around Armenia destroying pagan temples, defeating the armed resistance of the pagan priests.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERussell2004358_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERussell2004358-29">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Gregory then went to Caesarea with a retinue of Armenian princes and was consecrated bishop of Armenia by <a href="/wiki/Leontius_of_Caesarea" title="Leontius of Caesarea">Leontius of Caesarea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781_16-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Until the death of <a href="/wiki/Nerses_I" title="Nerses I">Nerses I</a> in the late fourth century, Gregory's successors would go to Caesarea to be confirmed as bishops of Armenia, and Armenia remained under the titular authority of the metropolitans of Caesarea.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976lxxix_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976lxxix-30">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Returning to Armenia, Gregory raised churches in place of the destroyed pagan temples and seized their estates and wealth for the Armenian Church and his house.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199419_25-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199419-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;h&#93;</a></sup> On the site of the destroyed temple to <a href="/wiki/Vahagn" title="Vahagn">Vahagn</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ashtishat" title="Ashtishat">Ashtishat</a>, Gregory raised a church which became the original center of the Armenian Church and remained so until after the partition of the country in 387.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781–82_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781–82-33">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199419_25-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199419-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Gregory met King Tiridates near the town of <a href="/wiki/Bagavan" title="Bagavan">Bagavan</a> and baptized the Armenian king, army and people in the <a href="/wiki/Euphrates" title="Euphrates">Euphrates</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781–82_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199781–82-33">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In two non-Armenian versions of Agathangelos's history, Gregory also baptizes together with Tiridates the kings of <a href="/wiki/Caucasian_Albania" title="Caucasian Albania">Caucasian Albania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iberia" title="Kingdom of Iberia">Georgia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lazica" title="Lazica">Lazica</a>/Abkhazia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976lxviii–lxix_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976lxviii–lxix-34">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> He founded schools for the Christian education of children, where the languages of instruction were <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976375_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976375-35">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> He established the ecclesiastical structure of Armenia, appointing as bishops some of the children of pagan priests.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976p._379:_&quot;He_took_some_of_the_pagan_priests&#39;_children_and_brought_them_up_in_his_own_sight_and_under_his_own_care,_giving_them_instruction_and_raising_them_with_spiritual_care_and_fear._Those_who_were_worthy_of_attaining_the_rank_of_bishop_received_ordination_from_him&quot;_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976p._379:_&quot;He_took_some_of_the_pagan_priests&#39;_children_and_brought_them_up_in_his_own_sight_and_under_his_own_care,_giving_them_instruction_and_raising_them_with_spiritual_care_and_fear._Those_who_were_worthy_of_attaining_the_rank_of_bishop_received_ordination_from_him&quot;-36">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Gregory is also said to have journeyed to Rome with King Tiridates in an embassy to the recently converted <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a>, but scholar <a href="/wiki/Robert_W._Thomson" title="Robert W. Thomson">Robert W. Thomson</a> views this as fictional.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199416_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199416-22">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The conversion of Armenia to Christianity is traditionally dated to 301, but modern scholarship considers a later date, approximately 314, to be a more likely.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782-23">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Additionally, the history of Agathangelos depicts the spread of Christianity of Armenia as having occurred practically entirely within Gregory's lifetime, when, in fact, it was a more gradual process.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199422_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199422-37">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782–83_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782–83-38">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Retirement_and_death">Retirement and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Retirement and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Some time after converting Armenia to Christianity, Gregory appointed his younger son Aristaces as his successor and went to live an ascetic life in the "cave of Manē" in the district of <a href="/wiki/Daranali" title="Daranali">Daranali</a> in <a href="/wiki/Upper_Armenia" title="Upper Armenia">Upper Armenia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989375_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989375-39">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;i&#93;</a></sup> The Patriarchate of Armenia would be held as a hereditary office, with some interruptions, by the house of Gregory, called the <a href="/wiki/Gregorids" title="Gregorids">Gregorids</a>, until the death of Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Isaac_of_Armenia" title="Isaac of Armenia">Isaac</a> in the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199783_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199783-42">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerian200576_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerian200576-43">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> According to Movses Khorenatsi, Gregory sometimes came out from his hermitage and traveled around the country until Aristaces returned from the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">Council of Nicaea</a> (325), after which Gregory never appeared to anyone again.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1978248–249_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1978248–249-44">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> He died in seclusion in the cave of Manē and was buried nearby by shepherds who did not know who he was.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1978249–250_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1978249–250-45">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> All of the sources indicate that Gregory's death occurred not long after the Council of Nicaea; <a href="/wiki/Cyril_Toumanoff" title="Cyril Toumanoff">Cyril Toumanoff</a> gives 328 as the year of Gregory's death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEToumanoff1969268_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEToumanoff1969268-46">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics_and_veneration">Relics and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relics and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his death his corpse was removed to the village of Thodanum (T'ordan, modern Doğanköy, near <a href="/wiki/Erzincan" title="Erzincan">Erzincan</a>). His relics were scattered near and far in the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Emperor">Eastern Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Zeno_(emperor)" title="Zeno (emperor)">Zeno</a>. In the 8th century, the <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclast</a> decrees in Greece caused a number of religious orders to flee the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> and seek refuge elsewhere. <a href="/wiki/San_Gregorio_Armeno" title="San Gregorio Armeno">San Gregorio Armeno</a> in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> was built in that century over the remains of a Roman temple dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Ceres_(mythology)" title="Ceres (mythology)">Ceres</a>, by a group of nuns escaping from the Byzantine Empire with the relics of Gregory,<sup id="cite_ref-Fortescue_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fortescue-47">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> including his skull, a femur bone, his staff, the leather straps used in his torture and the manacles that held the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> The femur and manacles were returned by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to Catholicos <a href="/wiki/Karekin_II" title="Karekin II">Karekin II</a> and are now enshrined at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Illuminator_Cathedral,_Yerevan" title="Saint Gregory the Illuminator Cathedral, Yerevan">Saint Gregory the Illuminator Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Yerevan" title="Yerevan">Yerevan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Gregoriou_Monastery" title="Gregoriou Monastery">Gregoriou Monastery</a> on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> also claims to have the skull.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Mother_See_of_Holy_Etchmiadzin" title="Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin">Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See_of_Cilicia" title="Holy See of Cilicia">Holy See of Cilicia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Antelias" title="Antelias">Antelias</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a> each claim to have the right arm of the saint, in an arm-shaped reliquary, which is used for the blessing the <a href="/wiki/Chrism" title="Chrism">Holy Myron</a> every seven years.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A number of prayers, and about thirty of the canonical hymns of the Armenian Church, are ascribed to Gregory the Illuminator. Homilies of his appeared for the first time in a work called <i>Haschacnapadum</i> at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 1737; a century afterwards a Greek translation was published at <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> by the Mekhiterists; and they have since been edited in German by J M Schmid (Ratisbon, 1872). The original authorities for Gregory's life are <a href="/wiki/Agathangelos" title="Agathangelos">Agathangelos</a>, whose <i>History of Tiridates</i> was published by the Mekhitarists in 1835; <a href="/wiki/Moses_of_Chorene" class="mw-redirect" title="Moses of Chorene">Moses of Chorene</a>, <i>Historiae Armenicae</i>; and <a href="/wiki/Symeon_the_Metaphrast" title="Symeon the Metaphrast">Symeon the Metaphrast</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A <i>Life of Gregory</i> by the Vartabed Matthew was published in the Armenian language at Venice in 1749 and was translated into English by <a href="/wiki/Solomon_Caesar_Malan" title="Solomon Caesar Malan">Father Malan</a> (1868).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In the calendar of the Armenian Church, the discovery of the relics of St. Gregory is an important feast and is commemorated on the Saturday before the Fourth Sunday after Pentecost.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> Two other feast days in the Armenian Apostolic Church are devoted to St. Gregory: the feast of his entry into <a href="/wiki/Khor_Virap" title="Khor Virap">Khor Virap</a>, the 'deep pit or dungeon' (commemorated on the last Saturday of Lent) and his deliverance from Khor Virap (commemorated on the Saturday before the second Sunday after Pentecost).<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_outside_Armenia">Veneration outside Armenia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration outside Armenia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A 9th century mosaic of Gregory was uncovered in <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a> in 1847–49 during the restoration by the <a href="/wiki/Fossati_brothers" title="Fossati brothers">Fossati brothers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMangoHawkins19726_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMangoHawkins19726-53">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>  Located in the south <a href="/wiki/Tympanum_(architecture)" title="Tympanum (architecture)">tympanum</a>, next to the <a href="/wiki/Fathers_of_the_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Fathers of the Church">Fathers of the Church</a>, it shows Gregory standing in bishop robes, blessing with one hand and holding the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_the_Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of the Gospels">Book of the Gospels</a> with the other.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDer_Nersessian1966386_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDer_Nersessian1966386-54">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> The mosaic, thought to have been destroyed in the <a href="/wiki/1894_Istanbul_earthquake" title="1894 Istanbul earthquake">1894 earthquake</a>, survives in drawing by <a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Salzenberg" class="extiw" title="de:Wilhelm Salzenberg">Wilhelm Salzenberg</a> and the Fossati brothers. <a href="/wiki/Sirarpie_Der_Nersessian" title="Sirarpie Der Nersessian">Sirarpie Der Nersessian</a> argued that his inclusion in the series of the Church Fathers is explained by the myth of the Arsacid origin of <a href="/wiki/Basil_I" title="Basil I">Basil I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDer_Nersessian1966389_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDer_Nersessian1966389-55">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> It may have been fabricated by <a href="/wiki/Photios_I_of_Constantinople" title="Photios I of Constantinople">Photios I of Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMangoHawkins197238_56-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMangoHawkins197238-56">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory is commemorated on September 30 by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, which styles him "Holy Hieromartyr Gregory, Bishop of Greater Armenia, Equal of the Apostles and Enlightener of Armenia." He is listed on September 30 in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ordinary_Form" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordinary Form">Ordinary Form</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>; his feast day  is listed as October 1 in the <a href="/wiki/Extraordinary_Form" class="mw-redirect" title="Extraordinary Form">Extraordinary Form</a>. He is honored with a <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">liturgical calendar of the Episcopal Church (USA)</a> on March 23.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 20 February, 1743, <a href="/wiki/Nard%C3%B2" title="Nardò">Nardò</a>, Italy was hit by a devastating earthquake that destroyed almost the entire city. The only structure to survive intact after the quake was the city's statue of St. Gregory the Illuminator. According to the city's registers, only 350 out of the city's 10,000 inhabitants died in the earthquake, leading the inhabitants to believe that St. Gregory saved the city. Every year, they mark the anniversary of the earthquake by holding three days of celebrations in his honor. Relics of the saint are kept at <a href="/wiki/Nard%C3%B2_Cathedral" title="Nardò Cathedral">Nardò Cathedral</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Notes">Notes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_the_Illuminator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Notes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1011085734">.mw-parser-output .reflist{font-size:90%;margin-bottom:0.5em;list-style-type:decimal}.mw-parser-output .reflist .references{font-size:100%;margin-bottom:0;list-style-type:inherit}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-2{column-width:30em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-3{column-width:25em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns{margin-top:0.3em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns ol{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns li{page-break-inside:avoid;break-inside:avoid-column}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-alpha{list-style-type:upper-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-roman{list-style-type:upper-roman}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-alpha{list-style-type:lower-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-greek{list-style-type:lower-greek}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-roman{list-style-type:lower-roman}</style><div class="reflist reflist-lower-alpha">
<div class="mw-references-wrap"><ol class="references">
<li id="cite_note-3"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-3">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Also less commonly known as <b>Gregory the Enlightener</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Outside Armenia, he is often known as <b>Gregory of Armenia</b>.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-4"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-4">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text"><a href="/wiki/Western_Armenian" title="Western Armenian">Western Armenian</a>: <i>Krikor Lusavorich</i></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-6"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-6">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The Armenian Apostolic Church, as its name suggests, traces its founding to the apostles <a href="/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle" title="Jude the Apostle">Thaddeus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle" title="Bartholomew the Apostle">Bartholomew</a>, who are said to have preached Christianity in Armenia in the first century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELang1970155_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELang1970155-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-18"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-18">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">One Greek version of Agathangelos tells a significantly different story of Gregory's wife, who is called Julitta. In this version, Gregory's wife and sons are implied to have followed him to Armenia, but fled back to Caesarea after Gregory's imprisonment. After Gregory was released from imprisonment, his wife went to Armenia to join him, leaving behind their sons in Caesarea, but Gregory refused to return to married life, instead asking the king to put his wife in charge of the holy virgins and temporarily lead the Christians in worship.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxii–xxxiii_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976xxxii–xxxiii-17">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-24"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-24">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Interpretations that favor an earlier date for Tiridates' conversion argue that the Armenian king had grown disillusioned with his alliance with Rome and stopped following <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian's</a> anti-Christian policy, instead adopting Christianity to strengthen the state and further separate Armenia from Rome and Persia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&quot;Grigor_I_Lusavorichʻ,&quot;_Armenian_Soviet_Encyclopedia1977-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Those who favor the later date of 314 argue that Tiridates, as a loyal client-king of Rome, could not have set up Christianity as Armenia's state religion in contradiction to Rome's anti-Christian policy at the time, and place the conversion after the <a href="/wiki/Edict_of_Milan" title="Edict of Milan">Edict of Milan</a> in 313.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199782-23">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-26"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-26">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Scholar <a href="/wiki/Robert_W._Thomson" title="Robert W. Thomson">Robert W. Thomson</a> notes that, although Vagharshapat-Ejmiatsin had "clearly been a holy shrine" from early on in Christian Armenian history, the association of Gregory with Vagharshapat dates from after the partition of Armenia in 387, when the mother see of the Armenian Church moved to Eastern Armenia. The actual original center of the Armenian Church was at <a href="/wiki/Ashtishat" title="Ashtishat">Ashtishat</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1984_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1984-21">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199419_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199419-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-28"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-28">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The figure who appears to Gregory was later identified with Christ in the Armenian tradition, although this is not explicitly stated in Agathangelos.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson199419_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson199419-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-32"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-32">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">According to the fifth-century history attributed to <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Byzantium" title="Faustus of Byzantium">Faustus of Byzantium</a>, by the time of Gregory's descendant Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Nerses_I" title="Nerses I">Nerses I</a>, the domains of the Gregorid house amounted to fifteen districts (<i>gawaṛ</i>s).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989139_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan1989139-31">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-41"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-41">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Agathangelos writes that King Tiridates sent for Aristaces and Vrtanes and had them brought to Armenia from Caesarea after Gregory went to live in seclusion.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1976393–397_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1976393–397-40">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
</ol></div></div>
