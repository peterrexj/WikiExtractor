<p><b>Saint Leudwinus, Count of Treves</b> (<small><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_Latin" title="Ecclesiastical Latin">Ecclesiastical Latin</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="la-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Latin" title="Help:IPA/Latin">[ˈlɛu̯d.vi.nus]</a></span>; also <i>Leodewin, Liutwin, Ludwin</i>, etc.; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 660 &#8211; 29 September 722 AD in <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>) founded an abbey in <a href="/wiki/Mettlach" title="Mettlach">Mettlach</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Treves" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Treves">Archbishop of Treves</a> and <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Lutwinus_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Lutwinus-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-St._Ludwin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Ludwin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Mettlach" title="Mettlach">Mettlach</a> parish, his relics are carried through the town by procession at the annual <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> celebration.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is September 23. He was the son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Warinus" title="Saint Warinus">Saint Warinus</a>, the paternal grandson of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sigrada" title="Saint Sigrada">Saint Sigrada</a>, and nephew of <a href="/wiki/Leodegar" title="Leodegar">Saint Leodegarius</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leudwinus was born a Frankish nobleman and was a member of one of the most powerful clans in <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His parents were <a href="/wiki/Saint_Warinus" title="Saint Warinus">Warinus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Poitiers" title="Count of Poitiers">Count of Poitiers</a> and Gunza of Metz.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Les_ancêtres_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Les_ancêtres-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Maastricht" title="Lambert of Maastricht">Lambert of Maastricht</a> was his kinsman. His Frankish name is Liutwin.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Leudwinus spent his early life at the royal court of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a><sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and was styled Count of Treves.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He received his education from his maternal uncle, <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Trier">Saint Basinus, Archbishop of Treves</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 697, Leudwinus signed the Deed of Echternach with his uncle.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Initially uninterested in an ecclesiastical career, Leudwinus married Willigard of Bavaria. Their children were:<sup id="cite_ref-Les_ancêtres_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Les_ancêtres-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Milo_(bishop_of_Trier)" title="Milo (bishop of Trier)">Milo</a>, Count of Treves<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mettlach_Abbey">Mettlach Abbey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Mettlach Abbey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, the abbey in Mettlach was founded after Leudwinus went hunting near <a href="/wiki/Saarland" title="Saarland">Saar</a>. He grew tired and fell asleep under the shade of a tree. As he slept the sun changed positions exposing him to its hot rays, but an eagle swept down and sat on Leudwinus with its wings spread out. When Leudwinus woke up, his servant told him how the eagle had protected him from being burnt by the sun. Coincidentally, Leudwinus happened to be napping at the site of the Miracle Eagle near the chapel of <a href="/wiki/Dionysius_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Dionysius of Paris">St. Denis of Paris</a>. Leudwinus saw this as a God-sent sign to establish a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery at that site, and it soon developed into a Christian missionary center. At the location of the original Dionysius Chapel now stands the parish church of St. Gangolf in Mettlach.
</p><p>When Leudwinus became a widower, he joined the monastery he founded at Mettlach as a simple monk.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Ludwin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Ludwin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Triers">Bishop of Triers</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Bishop of Triers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 697, Leudwinus was appointed coadjutor of his uncle Basinus von Trier.<sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Liutwin_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Liutwin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 698, he cofounded the Echternack Abbey at <a href="/wiki/Mettlach" title="Mettlach">Mettlach</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Liutwin_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Liutwin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Archbishop Basinus died on 4 March 705, Leudwinus succeeded him and was consecrated Archbishop of Treve.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Ludwin_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Ludwin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Liutwin_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Liutwin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Leudwinus was also appointed bishop of <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Ludwin_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Ludwin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Liutwin_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Liutwin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This made him one of the most important church dignitaries of the time in the Frankish kingdom.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leudwinus died on the 29th of September 722 at Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded as Archbishop of Treve by his son, Milo, who brought his father's remains to Treve for burial. However, local customs prevented this, so Leudwinus' family decided to let the dead saint choose his own place of burial. His coffin was placed on a ship without a crew. It sailed by itself first to <a href="/wiki/Moselle" title="Moselle">Moselle</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Saarland" title="Saarland">Saar</a>, and finally docked at <a href="/wiki/Mettlach" title="Mettlach">Mettlach</a>, where the church bells began to ring. Leudwinus was buried in St. Mary's Church at the Abbey at Mettlach.<sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Lutwinus_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Lutwinus-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Mettlach_Church_3-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mettlach_Church-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 990, St. Mary's Church was replaced by a new structure called the Old Tower, the oldest preserved stone building in Saar.
</p><p>In 1247, Leudwinus' relics were transferred to the newly constructed Leudwinus Chapel (<i>Liutwinuskapelle</i>). Some 200 years later, his remains were reburied again in a new chapel connected to the abbey church. During the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, the monastery was purchased by the Boch family, who had the building demolished and built Liutwinus Cathedral in Mettlach, where the relics of the saint are located today. Reports of miracles at Leudwinus' grave in Mettlach have made it a popular pilgrimage site over the centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-Heiliger_Lutwinus_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heiliger_Lutwinus-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Records from Leudwinus' time as bishop are collected in the <a href="/wiki/Gesta_Treverorum" title="Gesta Treverorum">Gesta Treverorum</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_Day_of_St._Leudwinus">Feast Day of St. Leudwinus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leudwinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Feast Day of St. Leudwinus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leudwinus' original feast day was September 29, the day of his death. As this is also the feast day of Saint Michael the Archangel, after the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a> the Feast of Saint Leudwinus was moved to September 23.  It is also the feast day of his uncle, Saint Basinus.
</p>
