					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Yakym Senkivskyi</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Яким Сеньківський</span>; 2 May 1896 – 29 June 1941) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">Ukrainian Greek Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yakym_Senkivskyi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Senkivskyi was born in the village of <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Raion" title="Ternopil Raion">Velyki Hai</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria" title="Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria">Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Oblast</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>).  He studied theology in <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, Ukraine and was ordained a priest on 4 December 1921. He received a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> in theology from <a href="/wiki/Innsbruck" title="Innsbruck">Innsbruck</a>, Austria. In 1923 he went to <a href="/wiki/Krekhiv" title="Krekhiv">Krekhiv</a> and became a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Basil_the_Great" title="Order of Saint Basil the Great">Order of Saint Basil the Great</a>. After he professed his first vows, he was transferred to the village of Krasnopushcha, and later to the village of Lavriv, in the area of Starosambir. From 1931 to 1938 he held different positions in the Monastery of Saint Onufrius in <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, where he served as a chaplain of the Marian Society, ministered to children and youth and organized a Eucharistic Society. In 1939, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> (hegumen) of the monastery in <a href="/wiki/Drohobych" title="Drohobych">Drohobych</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:02_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_beatification">Death and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yakym_Senkivskyi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On June 26, 1941, he was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> <a href="/wiki/NKVD" title="NKVD">NKVD</a>, and on June 29, according to various prisoners, he was boiled in a <a href="/wiki/Cauldron" title="Cauldron">cauldron</a> in the Drohobych prison.<sup id="cite_ref-:02_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on June 27, 2001.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yakym_Senkivskyi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Orest Kupranets recounts the life of Senkivskyi in his memoirs. "From the first days of his time in Drohobych he became the favorite of the whole town. He gained the affection of the population with his remarkable talent, his ability to speak to scholar and labor, young and old, and even to the little child. He was always polite, with a warm smile on his face. In your soul you felt that this person had no malice, and, in addition to the impression of humility and dignity, a true servant of Christ was evident."<sup id="cite_ref-:02_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
