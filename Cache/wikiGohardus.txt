<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cath_st_gohard01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Cath_st_gohard01.jpg/220px-Cath_st_gohard01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="256" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Cath_st_gohard01.jpg/330px-Cath_st_gohard01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Cath_st_gohard01.jpg/440px-Cath_st_gohard01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1028" data-file-height="1198" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cath_st_gohard01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Gohard and the Vikings</div></div></div>
<p><b>Gohard</b> or <b>Gunhard</b> was a 9th-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nantes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nantes">bishop of Nantes</a>, lord of <a href="/wiki/Blain,_Loire-Atlantique" title="Blain, Loire-Atlantique">Blain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">cephalophore</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Gohard was born in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>. It was during his <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">episcopate</a> that the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Blain" title="Battle of Blain">Battle of Blain</a> took place in 843 AD, the first major battle between the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Britons" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons">Britons</a>. Damage caused by struggles between these two factions in the area was compounded by <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Viking</a> raids.
</p><p>On the feast day of St. <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>, a month after the defeat of Blain, the <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> arrived at Nantes. Gohard was celebrating <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nantes_Cathedral" title="Nantes Cathedral">Nantes Cathedral</a> before a large audience when the Vikings attacked, killing the priest and his congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Legend says that Gohard picked up his decapitated head and walked down to the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a> where a boat took him to Angers.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His body was buried in St. Peter's Collegiate Church in Angers, the city where he was born.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gohard was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1096. The <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> of Nantes Cathedral is dedicated to him, and was redesigned shortly after his canonization to house relics brought from Angers. A side chapel of the cathedral is also dedicated to him.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on June 24.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
