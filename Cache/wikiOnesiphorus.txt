<p><b>Onesiphorus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Ονησιφόρος; meaning "bringing profit" or "useful") was a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> referred to in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> letter of <a href="/wiki/Second_Letter_to_Timothy" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Letter to Timothy">Second Timothy</a> (<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/2_Timothy#1:16" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/2 Timothy">2 Tim 1:16–18</a> and <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/2_Timothy#4:19" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/2 Timothy">2 Tim 4:19</a>). According to the letter sent by <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">St. Paul</a>, Onesiphorus sought out Paul who was imprisoned at the time in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesiphorus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Orthodox tradition, Saint Onesiphorus was one of the <a href="/wiki/Seventy_disciples" title="Seventy disciples">seventy disciples</a> chosen and sent by Jesus to preach. They were chosen some time after the selection of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">Twelve Apostles</a> (Luke 10:1-24).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> St Onesiphorus was bishop at Colophon (Asia Minor), and later at Corinth. Both the Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches hold that he died a martyr in the city of Parium (not far from Ephesus) on the shores of the Hellespont.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="2_Timothy">2 Timothy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesiphorus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: 2 Timothy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The persecution of Christians during <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>’s reign made Rome a dangerous city for Christians. Paul praises Onesiphorus for his hospitality, kindness, and courage.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Onesiphorus is contrasted with the other Christians in Asia who have deserted Paul at this time. In <a href="/wiki/2_Timothy_1" title="2 Timothy 1">2 Timothy 1</a>:16-18, Paul sends a greeting to the man’s household in Ephesus and refers to the help he showed Paul earlier in <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Timothy</a>, who led the Ephesian church is familiar with these acts.<sup id="cite_ref-Rutherford_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rutherford-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Paul's praise of Onesiphorus is significant because it was written shortly before Paul's death as a final encouragement to Timothy.
</p><p>But now, at the time of correspondence, only "Luke alone is with (Paul)" (4:11). Because Paul speaks of Onesiphorus only in the past tense, wishes blessings upon his house (family), and mercy for him "in that <a href="/wiki/Judgment_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Judgment Day">day</a>", some scholars believe that Onesiphorus had at this point died.<sup id="cite_ref-Rutherford_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rutherford-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Towards the end of the same letter, in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=2%20Timothy%204:19&amp;version=nrsv">2 Timothy 4:19</a>, Paul sends greetings to "Priscilla and Aquila, and the house of Onesiphorus", again apparently distinguishing the situation of Onesiphorus from that of the still-living Priscilla and Aquila. Paul's reference to Onesiphorus, along with <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=2%20Maccabees%2012:40–46&amp;version=nrsvae">2 Maccabees 12:40–46</a>, is cited by Catholics as one of the early examples of <a href="/wiki/Prayers_for_the_dead" class="mw-redirect" title="Prayers for the dead">prayer for the dead</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> while some Protestants opposing this practice reject such an interpretation.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Remarkably Onesiphorus made a 1000-mile journey, over 3 large bodies of water, from one continent to another (from Ephesus (Turkey) to Rome (Italy))—-to specifically search for &amp; find Paul in prison, so as to comfort him. 
</p><p>He is commemorated on September 6 in Roman Catholic Church,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and September 7 in Orthodox Church.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He should not be confused with the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesiphorus_of_Ephesus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Onesiphorus of Ephesus (page does not exist)">Onesiphorus of Ephesus</a> who was martyred with <a href="/w/index.php?title=Porphyrius_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Porphyrius (martyr) (page does not exist)">Porphyrius</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian persecution">Diocletian persecution</a>, and is commemorated on November 9.
</p>
