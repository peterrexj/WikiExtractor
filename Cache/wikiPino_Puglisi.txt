<p><b>Giuseppe "Pino" Puglisi</b> (<small>Italian pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="it-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian" title="Help:IPA/Italian">[ˈpiːno puʎˈʎiːzi]</a></span>, <small>Sicilian:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="scn-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Sicilian" title="Help:IPA/Sicilian">[pʊɟˈɟiːsɪ]</a></span>; 15 September 1937&#160;– 15 September 1993) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest in the rough <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> neighbourhood of <a href="/wiki/Brancaccio" title="Brancaccio">Brancaccio</a>. He openly challenged the <a href="/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia" title="Sicilian Mafia">Mafia</a> who controlled the neighbourhood, and was killed by them on his 56th birthday. His life story has been retold in a book, <i>Pino Puglisi, il prete che fece tremare la mafia con un sorriso</i> (2013), and portrayed in a film, <i>Come Into the Light</i> (Italian original title <i>Alla luce del sole</i>) in 2005. He is the first person killed by the Mafia who has been beatified by the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ordained_as_priest">Ordained as priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pino_Puglisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Ordained as priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Puglisi was born in <a href="/wiki/Brancaccio" title="Brancaccio">Brancaccio</a>, a working-class neighbourhood in <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>), into a family of modest means. His father was a shoemaker and his mother a dressmaker. He entered the seminary at age sixteen. Following ordination, he worked in various parishes, including a country parish afflicted by a bloody vendetta.<sup id="cite_ref-cw111002_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cw111002-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Puglisi was ordained as a priest on 2 July 1960 by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Ernesto_Ruffini" title="Ernesto Ruffini">Ernesto Ruffini</a> from Palermo. Ruffini regarded <a href="/wiki/Communism" title="Communism">communism</a> as a greater threat than the Mafia. He once questioned the Mafia's very existence. To a journalist's question of "What is the Mafia?" he responded: "So far as I know, it could be a brand of detergent."<sup id="cite_ref-ncr130902_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr130902-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This denial persuaded Puglisi of the need to challenge church authorities. "We can, we must criticize the church when we feel it doesn't respond to our expectations, because it's absolutely right to seek to improve it," he said. With his trademark humour, Puglisi added: "But we should always criticize it like a mother, never a mother-in-law!"<sup id="cite_ref-ncr130902_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr130902-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Antimafia_priest">Antimafia priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pino_Puglisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Antimafia priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1990, Puglisi returned to his old quarter Brancaccio and became the pastor of San Gaetano's Parish. He spoke out against the Mafia who controlled the area and opened a shelter for underprivileged children. Puglisi had been offered other parishes by the local <a href="/wiki/Curia" title="Curia">curia</a>, in less troublesome Palermo neighborhoods, but he opted for San Gaetano.<sup id="cite_ref-ncr081093_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr081093-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With little support from the Palermo <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese">archdiocese</a>, Puglisi tried to change his parishioners' mentality, which was conditioned by fear, passivity and <a href="/wiki/Omerta" class="mw-redirect" title="Omerta">word omertà</a> (imposed silence). In his sermons, he pleaded to give leads to authorities about the Mafia's illicit activities in Brancaccio, even if they could not actually name names.<sup id="cite_ref-ncr081093_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr081093-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He refused their monies when offered for the traditional feast day celebrations, and would not allow the Mafia "men of honour" to march at the head of religious processions.<sup id="cite_ref-cw111002_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cw111002-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He tried to discourage the children from dropping out of school, robbing, drug dealing and selling contraband cigarettes. He ignored a series of warnings and declined to award a contract to a construction firm which had been "indicated" to him by the Mafia for the restoration of the church, where the roof was collapsing.<sup id="cite_ref-jamieson140_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jamieson140-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Those parishioners that made attempts to reform matters were sent strong messages. A small group who organized for social improvement found the doors of their houses torched, their phones receiving threats, and their families put on notice that worse things lay in store.<sup id="cite_ref-cw111002_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cw111002-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Killing">Killing</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pino_Puglisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Killing">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 15 September 1993 – Puglisi's 56th birthday – he was killed outside his home by a single bullet shot at <a href="/wiki/Point-blank_range" title="Point-blank range">point-blank range</a>. He was taken unconscious to a local hospital, where surgeons could not revive him.<sup id="cite_ref-nyt170993_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nyt170993-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The murder was ordered by the local Mafia bosses, the brothers Filippo and <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Graviano" title="Giuseppe Graviano">Giuseppe Graviano</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-rep230694_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rep230694-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> One of the hitmen who killed Puglisi, Salvatore Grigoli, later confessed and revealed the priest's last words as his killers approached: "I've been expecting you."<sup id="cite_ref-ncr130902_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr130902-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Puglisi's murder shocked Italy. There was an immediate call by eight priests in Palermo for the pope to travel to Palermo to be present at his funeral. Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a>, however, was scheduled to be in Tuscany on that date and did not attend the memorial service. At the funeral Mass the archbishop of Palermo, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Salvatore_Pappalardo" title="Salvatore Pappalardo">Salvatore Pappalardo</a>, spoke out very strongly against the Mafia, echoing the Pope's words on a visit to Agrigento, Sicily, just months earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 14 April 1998, the Mafiosi <a href="/wiki/Gaspare_Spatuzza" title="Gaspare Spatuzza">Gaspare Spatuzza</a>, Nino Mangano, Cosimo Lo Nigro and Luigi Giacalone received life sentences for the killing of Puglisi. The Graviano brothers also received life sentences for ordering the killing.<sup id="cite_ref-rep200201_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rep200201-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pino_Puglisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG/300px-Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG" decoding="async" width="300" height="198" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG/450px-Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG/600px-Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3063" data-file-height="2022" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Giuseppe_Puglisi_-_Cathedral_of_Palermo_-_Italy_2015.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Pino Puglisi at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Palermo" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Palermo">Cathedral of Palermo</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>During his visit to Sicily in November 1994, Pope John Paul II praised Puglisi as a "courageous exponent of the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-jamieson140_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jamieson140-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He urged Sicilians not to allow the priest’s death to have been in vain and warned that silence and passivity about the Mafia was tantamount to <a href="/wiki/Complicity" title="Complicity">complicity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-jamieson140_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jamieson140-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Puglisi's favorite rhetorical stance – "Se ognuno fa qualcosa, allora si può fare molto" (If everyone does something, then we can do a lot)<sup id="cite_ref-laStampaPuglisi_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-laStampaPuglisi-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> – is scrawled on walls in Brancaccio. In 1999, the Cardinal of Palermo started his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> process, proclaiming Puglisi a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ncr130902_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr130902-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
To underscore this anti-Mafia conviction, he composed a parody of the Our Father in the Sicilian language: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>O godfather to me and my family, You are a man of honor and worth. Your name must be respected. Everyone must obey you. Everyone must do what you say for this is the law of those who do not wish to die. You give us bread, work; who wrongs you, pays. Do not pardon; it is an infamy. Those who speak are spies. I put my trust in you, godfather. Free me from the police and the law.<sup id="cite_ref-cw111002_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cw111002-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>On 28 June 2012, Pope Benedict XVI allowed the Vatican Congregation for the Causes of Saints to designate Puglisi a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> in a first step to <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatify</a> the slain priest.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The Pope signed a decree acknowledging that Father Puglisi had been killed "in hatred of the faith", meaning that he can be beatified – the last step before sainthood – without a miracle being attributed to his intercession with God.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pino_Puglisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification of Pino Puglisi took place on 25 May 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The open-air Mass took place at the Foro Italico 'Umberto I', a large green area that forms one of the promenades of Palermo. The Mass was presided over by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Paolo_Romeo" title="Paolo Romeo">Paolo Romeo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Palermo" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Palermo">Metropolitan Archbishop of Palermo</a>, with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Salvatore_De_Giorgi" title="Salvatore De Giorgi">Salvatore De Giorgi</a>, Metropolitan Archbishop Emeritus of Palermo, as the papal legate who performed the rite of beatification. Estimates state that 50,000 people attended the Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> During his <a href="/wiki/Angelus" title="Angelus">Angelus</a> address, the following Sunday, 26 May, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> stated that the newly beatified Puglisi was first and foremost "an exemplary priest and a martyr", as well as condemning mafia groups.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
