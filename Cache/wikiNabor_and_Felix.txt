<p><b>Nabor</b> and <b>Felix</b> (<abbr title="died">d.</abbr> <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;AD&#160;303</span>) were <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyrs</a> thought to have been killed during the <a href="/wiki/Great_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Persecution">Great Persecution</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_Roman_emperors" title="List of Roman emperors">emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. A tomb in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> is believed to contain their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nabor_and_Felix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Apocrypha" title="Apocrypha">apocryphal</a> <i>"Acts of Saints Nabor and Felix"</i> (which are imitated from the Acts of other martyrs (such as those of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Firmus_and_Saint_Rusticus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Firmus and Saint Rusticus">Saint Firmus and Saint Rusticus</a>), the two are said to have been <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Soldiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldiers">soldiers</a> from <a href="/wiki/Mauretania_Caesariensis" title="Mauretania Caesariensis">Mauretania Caesariensis</a> serving under <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>. They were condemned in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and executed by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitation</a> in <a href="/wiki/Laus_Pompeia" class="mw-redirect" title="Laus Pompeia">Laus Pompeia</a> (Lodi Vecchio).
</p><p>A pair of saints "Nabor and Felix" were also said to have been martyred at <a href="/wiki/Nicopolis_(Armenia)" title="Nicopolis (Armenia)">Nicopolis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a> in AD&#160;320 alongside SS&#160;"Januarius and Marinus". They may be distinct or may have been a merging of the story of the Italian saints with the local couple <a href="/wiki/Januarius_and_Pelagia" title="Januarius and Pelagia">Januarius and Pelagia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius,_Marinus,_Nabor,_and_Felix&#93;&quot;_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius,_Marinus,_Nabor,_and_Felix]&quot;-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> The feast day of Januarius and Pelagia was observed on July 11<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius_and_Pelagia&#93;&quot;_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius_and_Pelagia]&quot;-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and that of the quartet on July 10.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius,_Marinus,_Nabor,_and_Felix&#93;&quot;_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003&quot;[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidl-pwoTFp31kCpgPA425_Januarius,_Marinus,_Nabor,_and_Felix]&quot;-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nabor_and_Felix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In early 4th-century, their relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a>, probably by the Bishop of Milan <a href="/wiki/Maternus_(bishop_of_Milan)" title="Maternus (bishop of Milan)">Maternus</a> from their place of interment to a place outside the walls of Milan, placed a few hundred meters north of the present <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Ambrogio" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Ambrogio">Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio</a>. A church (Basilica Naboriana) was built over their new tomb, as recorded by Paulinus of Milan in his life of Saint Ambrose. Tradition states that <a href="/wiki/Savina_of_Milan" title="Savina of Milan">Savina of Milan</a> died while praying at the tomb of Nabor and Felix. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ambrose" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ambrose">Saint Ambrose</a> wrote a hymn about them.
</p><p>When Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa" title="Frederick Barbarossa">Frederick Barbarossa</a> captured <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in 1158, he gave some of the relics of Saints Felix and Nabor to <a href="/wiki/Rainald_of_Dassel" title="Rainald of Dassel">Rainald of Dassel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">archbishop of Cologne</a>, who brought them to his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">episcopal see</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The relics associated with Felix and Nabor are situated in a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral" title="Cologne Cathedral">Cologne Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Nabor and Felix are depicted on the 1181 <i>"<a href="/wiki/Shrine_of_the_Three_Kings_at_Cologne_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Shrine of the Three Kings at Cologne Cathedral">Shrine of the Three Kings</a>"</i> by <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_of_Verdun" title="Nicholas of Verdun">Nicholas of Verdun</a> in Cologne Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1258 their relics were moved to the church of Saint Francis of Assisi that was erected in place of the Basilica Naboriana. On 14-16 April 1798, shortly before the demolition of the church of Saint Francis of Assisi, their relics were translated in the Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio. Their relics are placed today in an ancient <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> in the right nave of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Ambrogio_(Milan)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant&#39;Ambrogio (Milan)">Sant'Ambrogio Basilica</a> along with the relics of Saint <a href="/wiki/Maternus_(bishop_of_Milan)" title="Maternus (bishop of Milan)">Maternus</a> and of <a href="/wiki/Valeria_of_Milan" title="Valeria of Milan">Saint Valeria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DCA_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCA-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nabor_and_Felix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> recognizes Nabor and Felix as <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a>, inserting them, under the date of 12 July, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, its official list of saints.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> They were also included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> from before the 12th century.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> with a feast day that was reduced to a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> when Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Gualbert" title="John Gualbert">John Gualbert</a> was added to the calendar in 1595. The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> removed mention of Nabor and Felix from the General Roman Calendar, but the rules in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a> published in the same year authorizes celebration of their Mass on their feast day everywhere, unless in some locality an obligatory celebration is assigned to that day.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Desventuradas_Islands">Desventuradas Islands</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nabor_and_Felix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Desventuradas Islands">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Desventuradas_Islands" title="Desventuradas Islands">Desventuradas Islands</a>, San Félix and San Ambrosio, were sighted by <a href="/wiki/Juan_Fern%C3%A1ndez_(explorer)" title="Juan Fernández (explorer)">Juan Fernández</a> in 1574. <a href="/wiki/Pedro_Sarmiento_de_Gamboa" title="Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa">Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa</a> wrote in 1579 that "they are now called after St Felix and St Ambor (i.e. <a href="/wiki/Felix_and_Nabor" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix and Nabor">Felix and Nabor</a>)." Due to linguistic corruption, the name Ambor (Nabor) became confused with that of the more famous <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Milan">Bishop of Milan</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ambrose" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ambrose">Saint Ambrose</a> (San Ambrosio).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
