<p><b>Honorius</b> (died 30 September 653) was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a> to <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianize</a> the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> from their native <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a> in 597&#160;AD who later became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. During his archiepiscopate, he <a href="/wiki/Consecrated" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated">consecrated</a> the first native English <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">bishop of Rochester</a> as well as helping the missionary efforts of <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Dunwich" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix of Dunwich">Felix</a> among the <a href="/wiki/East_Anglian" class="mw-redirect" title="East Anglian">East Anglians</a>. Honorius was the last to die among the Gregorian missionaries.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honorius_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Roman</a> by birth, Honorius may have been one of those chosen by Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a> to England, although it seems more likely that he was a member of the second party of missionaries, sent in 601.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley43_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley43-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ASE112_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE112-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is not known if his name was given to him at birth or if he chose it when he became archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Sharpe3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sharpe3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop">Archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honorius_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 627, Honorius was consecrated as archbishop by <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_York" title="Paulinus of York">Paulinus of York</a> at <a href="/wiki/Lincoln,_England" title="Lincoln, England">Lincoln</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair96_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair96-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Honorius wrote to Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I" title="Pope Honorius I">Honorius I</a> asking the pope to raise the <a href="/wiki/See_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="See of York">see of York</a> to an archbishopric, so that when one archbishop in England died, the other would be able to consecrate the deceased bishop's successor. The pope agreed, and sent a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> for Paulinus, but by this time, Paulinus had already been forced to flee from Northumbria.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When Paulinus, after the death of King <a href="/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria" title="Edwin of Northumbria">Edwin of Northumbria</a> in October 633, fled Northumbria, he was received by Honorius and appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">bishopric of Rochester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair96_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair96-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The papal letter is dated to June 634, and implies that news of Edwin's death had not reached the pope. This evidence may mean that the traditional date of Edwin's death may need to be moved to October 634.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest56_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest56-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The papal letter may also mean that the traditional date of consecration for Honorius may need re-dating, as the long gap between 627, when he is said to have been consecrated, and 634, when he finally received a pallium, is much longer than usually found. It may be that Honorius was consecrated closer to 634.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest66_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest66-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The papal letter to Honorius is given in the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Ecclesiastical History</a></i> of the medieval writer <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Comp57_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comp57-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Honorius consolidated the work of converting the English by sending Felix, a Burgundian, to <a href="/wiki/Dunwich" title="Dunwich">Dunwich</a><sup id="cite_ref-Brooks65_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks65-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> after Felix came to the archbishop and made known his desire to go to <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a> as a missionary.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley43_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley43-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Honorius may have consecrated Felix as the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Norwich" title="Bishop of Norwich">bishop of East Anglia</a><sup id="cite_ref-Walsh268_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh268-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> or Felix may have already been consecrated on the continent.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks65_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks65-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Blair108_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair108-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The dating of this episode is unclear, but it is probably close to 631.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest66_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest66-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It is possible that King <a href="/wiki/Sigeberht_of_East_Anglia" title="Sigeberht of East Anglia">Sigeberht of East Anglia</a>, who converted to Christianity while he was in exile on the continent, had already met Felix and was behind Felix's journey to Honorius. As well as his help to Felix, Honorius consecrated the first Anglo-Saxon bishop, <a href="/wiki/Ithamar_(bishop)" title="Ithamar (bishop)">Ithamar</a> of Rochester,<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks65_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks65-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and his successor was also a native of England.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley43_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley43-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Honorius had few conflicts with the <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Irish</a> missionary efforts, and admired <a href="/wiki/Aidan_of_Lindisfarne" title="Aidan of Lindisfarne">Aidan</a>, one of the leading Irish clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Mayr94_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mayr94-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honorius_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Honorius died on 30 September 653,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook213_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook213-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> the last of the Gregorian missionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-Sharpe3_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sharpe3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was buried at the <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">Church of St Augustine</a> in Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was later revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, with his feast day being 30 September.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh268_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh268-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His relics were translated to a new tomb in 1091, and around that same time a hagiography of his life was written by <a href="/wiki/Goscelin" title="Goscelin">Goscelin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair539_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair539-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In the 1120s his relics were still being venerated at St Augustine's.<sup id="cite_ref-Absent217_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Absent217-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
