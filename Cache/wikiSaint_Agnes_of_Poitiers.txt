.mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">St. Agnes of Poitiers</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a <p><b>St. Agnes of Poitiers</b> is a French saint and abbess, who was "recognized for her holiness and intelligence" and called "model of the conventual life".<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-org_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  She served as abbess of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)">Holy Cross convent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, France until her death in 586.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-org_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agnes was the raised in court and was the adopted and "spiritual"<sup id="cite_ref-brennan-341_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brennan-341-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> daughter of <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">St. Radegund</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Thuringii" title="Thuringii">Thuringian</a> princess and <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> queen who founded Holy Cross, a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> that housed 200 nuns and was known as a place of learning, in 557.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-org_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-encyclopedia-com_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encyclopedia-com-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Agnes and Radegund visited Arles to study the Rule of <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Arles" title="Caesarius of Arles">St. Caesarius</a> in circa 570; the rule was eventually adopted at Poitiers.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-org_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-encyclopedia-com_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encyclopedia-com-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Scholar Brian Brennan stated that Agnes and Radegund were "virtually forced"<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> to accept St. Caesarius' rule because they did not have the support of the local bishop.
</p><p>Agnes is remembered for her connection and friendship with the poet and hymn writer <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">St. Venantius Fortunatus</a>, who visited Holy Cross and settled nearby for the remainder of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-encyclopedia-com_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encyclopedia-com-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  She and Radegund became his patrons; they sent him gifts and "mountains of food",<sup id="cite_ref-brennan-341_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brennan-341-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and he wrote them "highly-stylized verse letters,"<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> including greetings to Agnes on the anniversary of her installation as abbess.  According to Brennan, "the tone of these personal poems ls light-hearted and jocular suggesting a close and familiar relationship between the poet and his patronesses".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  The intimacy displayed in Venantius' correspondences to Agnes were construed as "close to being love poems";<sup id="cite_ref-brennan-342_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brennan-342-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> the intimacy displayed in them resulted in a "malicious rumor among Venantius' contemporaries".<sup id="cite_ref-brennan-341_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brennan-341-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He had to write Agnes assuring her that he only thought of her in a "fraternal or spiritual way",<sup id="cite_ref-brennan-342_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brennan-342-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and the nuns excluded him from the monastery for a time.
</p><p>Agnes' relics are preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Sainte-Radegonde_(Poitiers)" title="Church of Sainte-Radegonde (Poitiers)">Church of Saint Radegund</a>; her tomb is a popular pilgrimage in the area.<sup id="cite_ref-encyclopedia-com_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encyclopedia-com-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Her feast day is 13 May.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-org_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
