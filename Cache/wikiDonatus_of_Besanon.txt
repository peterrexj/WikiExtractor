					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Donatus</b> (d. after 658) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Besan%C3%A7on" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Besançon">Besançon</a>, founder of the monastery Palatium (later Saint-Paul) in Besançon and author of a rule for nuns. He is venerated as a saint since the 11th century; his feast day is August 7.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Donatus_of_Besan%C3%A7on&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Donatus was a Frankish aristocrat, son of Duke <a href="/wiki/Waldalenus" title="Waldalenus">Waldelenus</a> and his wife Flavia. His family belonged to the earliest supporters of the Irish monk <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>. He was baptized by Columbanus and entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil</a>. Before 627 he became bishop of Besançon. He founded a monastery in Besançon dedicated to Saint Paul which received them name <i>Palatium</i> because it was built on the ruins of a palace. The monastery followed the Rule of Columbanus and later become a house of <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Augustinian Canons</a>.
</p><p>Most biographical information about Donatus is based on <a href="/wiki/Jonas_of_Bobbio" title="Jonas of Bobbio">Jonas of Bobbio</a>'s <i>Life of Columbanus</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> References to Donatus in other sources (acts of councils and charters) are listed in <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" class="extiw" title="fr:Louis Duchesne">Duchesne's</a> <i>Fastes épiscopaux de l'ancienne Gaule</i>, vol. 3.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Rule_of_Donatus_of_Besan.C3.A7on"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Rule_of_Donatus_of_Besançon">Rule of Donatus of Besançon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Donatus_of_Besan%C3%A7on&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Rule of Donatus of Besançon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Donatus' mother Flavia founded after the death of her husband a convent in Besançon, probably the monastery Jussa Moutier (<i>Iussanum monasterium</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Donatus wrote a monastic rule for this foundation in which he combined elements from the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Rule of Benedict</a>, the Rule of <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Arles" title="Caesarius of Arles">Caesarius of Arles</a> and Columbanus' Rule.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Rule of Donatus is the oldest witness for a textual reception of the Rule of Benedict.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The text is preserved in <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Aniane" title="Benedict of Aniane">Benedict of Aniane</a>'s <i>Codex Regularum</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
