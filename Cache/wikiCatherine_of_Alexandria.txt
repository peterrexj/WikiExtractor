<p><b>Catherine of Alexandria,</b> also spelled <b>Katherine</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Αίκατερίνη) is, according to tradition, a Christian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a>, who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in the early fourth century at the hands of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Maxentius" title="Maxentius">Maxentius</a>. According to her <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, she was both a princess and a noted scholar who became a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> around the age of 14, converted hundreds of people to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Martyred" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyred">martyred</a> around the age of eighteen. More than 1,100 years after Catherine's <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>, <a href="/wiki/Joan_of_Arc" title="Joan of Arc">Joan of Arc</a> identified her as one of the saints who appeared to and counselled her.<sup id="cite_ref-joan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-joan-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> venerates her as a <a href="/wiki/Great_Martyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Martyr">Great Martyr</a> and celebrates her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 24 or 25 November, depending on the regional tradition. In <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>, Catherine is traditionally revered as one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a> and she is commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> on 25 November.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her feast was removed from the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> in 1969, but restored in 2002 as an optional memorial.
</p><p>Some modern scholars consider that the legend of Catherine was probably based on the life and murder of the virgin Saint <a href="/wiki/Dorothea_of_Alexandria" title="Dorothea of Alexandria">Dorothea of Alexandria</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> philosopher <a href="/wiki/Hypatia" title="Hypatia">Hypatia</a>, with reversed role of a Christian and Neo-Platonist in the case of the latter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh20073–26_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh20073–26-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDeakin2007135,_202_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeakin2007135,_202-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> On the other hand, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a> states that, "Although contemporary hagiographers look upon the authenticity of the various texts containing the legend of St. Catherine as more than doubtful, it is not therefore meant to cast even the shadow of a doubt around the existence of the saint."
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the traditional narrative, Catherine was the daughter of Sabinella and Constus (or Costus), the governor of <a href="/wiki/History_of_Alexandria#Late_Roman_and_Byzantine_period" title="History of Alexandria">Alexandria</a> during the reign of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> (286–305).<sup id="cite_ref-oca_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> From a young age she devoted herself to study. A vision of the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Virgin Mary</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Christ_Child" title="Christ Child">Child Jesus</a> persuaded her to become a Christian. When the persecutions began under <a href="/wiki/Maxentius" title="Maxentius">Maxentius</a>, she went to the emperor and rebuked him for his cruelty. The emperor summoned 50 of the best <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Philosophers" class="mw-redirect" title="Philosophers">philosophers</a> and <a href="/wiki/Orators" class="mw-redirect" title="Orators">orators</a> to dispute with her, hoping that they would refute her pro-Christian arguments, but Catherine won the debate. Several of her adversaries, conquered by her eloquence, declared themselves Christians and were at once put to death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Torture_and_martyrdom">Torture and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Torture and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:IconEcaterina.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/IconEcaterina.jpeg/170px-IconEcaterina.jpeg" decoding="async" width="170" height="251" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/IconEcaterina.jpeg/255px-IconEcaterina.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0f/IconEcaterina.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="305" data-file-height="450" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">Icon</a> of Saint Catherine of Alexandria, with scenes from her martyrdom</figcaption></figure>
<p>The emperor gave orders to subject the saint to terrible tortures and then throw her in prison.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> During the confinement she was fed daily by a dove from heaven and Christ also visited her, encouraging her to fight bravely, and promised her the crown of everlasting glory.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Angels tended her wounds with <a href="/wiki/Salve" title="Salve">salve</a>.
</p><p>During her imprisonment more than 200 people came to see her, including Maxentius' wife, <a href="/wiki/Valeria_Maximilla" title="Valeria Maximilla">Valeria Maximilla</a>; all converted to Christianity and were subsequently <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Twelve days later, when the dungeon was opened, a bright light and fragrant perfume filled it and Catherine came forth even more radiant and beautiful.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Upon the failure of Maxentius to make Catherine yield by way of <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">torture</a>, he tried to win the beautiful and wise princess over by proposing marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Catherine refused, declaring that her <a href="/wiki/Spouse" title="Spouse">spouse</a> was <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>, to whom she had consecrated her virginity.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The furious emperor condemned Catherine to death on a spiked <a href="/wiki/Breaking_wheel" title="Breaking wheel">breaking wheel</a>, but, at her touch, it shattered.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Maxentius ordered her to be <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. Catherine herself ordered the execution to commence. A milk-like substance rather than blood flowed from her neck.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMorton1841133_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorton1841133-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the 6th century, the Eastern <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Justinian" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Justinian">Emperor Justinian</a> had established what is now <a href="/wiki/Saint_Catherine%27s_Monastery" title="Saint Catherine&#39;s Monastery">Saint Catherine's Monastery</a> in Egypt, which had been originally built encircling the purported <a href="/wiki/Burning_bush" title="Burning bush">burning bush</a> seen by <a href="/wiki/Moses" title="Moses">Moses</a>. Countless people make the pilgrimage to the Monastery to receive miracle healing from Catherine.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFoleyMcCloskey2009_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFoleyMcCloskey2009-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg/220px-Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="279" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg/330px-Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg/440px-Artemisia_Gentileschi_-_St_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_WGA8571.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1266" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Catherine of Alexandria</i> by <a href="/wiki/Artemisia_Gentileschi" title="Artemisia Gentileschi">Artemisia Gentileschi</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif/lossy-page1-220px-Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="389" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif/lossy-page1-330px-Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif/lossy-page1-440px-Vita_di_santa_Caterina_vergine_e_martire.tif.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1297" data-file-height="2296" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Pietro_Aretino" title="Pietro Aretino">Pietro Aretino</a>, <i>Vita di santa Caterina vergine e martire</i>, 1636.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, while not denying her historicity, states that most of the details that embellish the narrative, as well as the long discourses that are put into the mouth of Catherine are to be rejected as later inventions.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica" title="Encyclopædia Britannica">Encyclopædia Britannica</a></i>, no extant written mention of Catherine of Alexandria is known from before the 9th century, and "her historicity is doubtful".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Donald_Attwater" title="Donald Attwater">Donald Attwater</a> dismisses what he calls the "legend" of Saint Catherine, arguing for a lack of any "positive evidence that she ever existed outside the mind of some Greek writer who first composed what he intended to be simply an edifying romance."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEde_Azevedo2005324_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_Azevedo2005324-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Harold Davis writes that "assiduous research has failed to identify Catherine with any historical personage".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAllen1997214–217_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen1997214–217-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Anna_Brownell_Jameson" title="Anna Brownell Jameson">Anna Brownell Jameson</a> was the first to argue that the life of Catherine was confused with that of the slightly later Neo-Platonist philosopher <a href="/wiki/Hypatia" title="Hypatia">Hypatia of Alexandria</a> (d. 415).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh200710_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh200710-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Hypatia was a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greece" title="Ancient Greece">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Mathematician" title="Mathematician">mathematician</a>, astronomer, and philosopher, who was murdered by the <a href="/wiki/Parabalani" title="Parabalani">Parabalani</a> after being accused of exacerbating a conflict between two prominent figures in Alexandria, the governor, <a href="/wiki/Orestes_(prefect)" class="mw-redirect" title="Orestes (prefect)">Orestes</a>, and the bishop, <a href="/wiki/Cyril_of_Alexandria" title="Cyril of Alexandria">Cyril</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatts2006197–198_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatts2006197–198-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDeakin1994234–243_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeakin1994234–243-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The idea that Catherine's life was either based on or became confused with the life of the pagan Hypatia has become a popular theory among modern scholars since. However, while Christine Walsh accepts the many parallels between Catherine and Hypatia, she does not believe there is any evidence for or against the idea that Catherine was created based on Hypatia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh200710_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh200710-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDeakin2007135,_202_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeakin2007135,_202-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sometimes cited as a possible inspiration of Catherine, the writer <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> wrote, around the year 320, that the Emperor Maximinus had ordered a young Christian woman to come to his palace to become his mistress, and when she refused he had her punished by having her banished and her estates confiscated.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Eusebius did not name the woman.
</p><p>The earliest surviving account of Catherine's life comes around 600 years after the traditional date of her martyrdom, in the <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologium</a></i>, a document compiled for Emperor <a href="/wiki/Basil_II" title="Basil II">Basil II</a> (976), although the alleged rediscovery of her relics at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Catherine%27s_Monastery" title="Saint Catherine&#39;s Monastery">Saint Catherine's Monastery</a> at the foot of Mount Sinai was about 800,<sup id="cite_ref-ArdeeneDobson_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArdeeneDobson-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> and presumably implies an existing cult at that date (though the common name of the monastery developed after the discovery).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>
In her book <i>The Cult of St Katherine of Alexandria in Early Medieval Europe</i>, Christine Walsh discusses "the historical Katherine": <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>As we have seen, the cult of St Katherine of Alexandria probably originated in oral traditions from the 4th-century <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecutions</a> of Christians in Alexandria. There is no evidence that Katherine herself was a historical figure and she may well have been a composite drawn from memories of women persecuted for their faith. Many aspects of her <i>Passio</i> are clearly legendary and conform to well-known hagiographical <a href="/wiki/Literary_topos" title="Literary topos">topoi</a>.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><a href="#CITEREFWalsh2007">Walsh 2007</a>, p.&#160;143</cite></div></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her name appears in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> as <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Αἰκατερίνη</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Aikaterínē</i></span>) or <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἑκατερίνη</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Hekaterínē</i></span>). The etymology is debated: it could derive from <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">ἑκάτερος</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">hekáteros</i></span>, "each of two"); it could derive from the name of the goddess <a href="/wiki/Hecate" title="Hecate">Hecate</a>; it could be related to Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">αἰκία</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">aikía</i></span>, "insult, outrage, suffering, torture"); or it could be from a Coptic name meaning "my consecration of your name". In the early Christian era it became associated with Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">καθαρός</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">katharós</i></span>, "pure"), and the Latin spelling was changed from Katerina to Katharina to reflect this.
</p><p>Reflecting this confusion, <a href="/wiki/Rufinus_(poet)" title="Rufinus (poet)">Rufinus</a> states that her first name was Dorothea (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Δωροθέα</span></span>) and that at her christening she acquired the name Aikaterina (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Αἰκατερίνα</span></span>), a name that signifies her pure, clean and uncontaminated nature (from the Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">αἰὲν καθαρινά</span></span>, 'ever clean').<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Medieval_cult">Medieval cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Medieval cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg/220px-Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="269" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg/330px-Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg/440px-Carlo_Crivelli_014.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2024" data-file-height="2473" /></a><figcaption><i>Catherine of Alexandria</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Crivelli" title="Carlo Crivelli">Carlo Crivelli</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Catherine was one of the most important saints in the religious culture of the <a href="/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages" title="Late Middle Ages">late Middle Ages</a> and arguably considered the most important of the virgin martyrs, a group including <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Rome" title="Agnes of Rome">Agnes of Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret of Antioch">Margaret of Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barbara" title="Saint Barbara">Barbara</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lucy" title="Saint Lucy">Lucia of Syracuse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Valerie_of_Limoges" title="Valerie of Limoges">Valerie of Limoges</a> and many others. Her power as an <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercessor</a> was renowned and firmly established in most versions of her <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, in which she specifically entreats Christ at the moment of her death to answer the prayers of those who remember her martyrdom and invoke her name.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The development of her medieval <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> was spurred by the alleged rediscovery of her body around the year 800 (about 500 years after her death) at Mount Sinai, supposedly with hair still growing and a constant stream of <a href="/wiki/Myrrh" title="Myrrh">healing oil</a> issuing from her body.<sup id="cite_ref-ArdeeneDobson_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ArdeeneDobson-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> There are several <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> narratives that chronicle the journey to Mount Sinai, most notably those of <a href="/wiki/John_Mandeville" class="mw-redirect" title="John Mandeville">John Mandeville</a> and Friar <a href="/wiki/Felix_Fabri" title="Felix Fabri">Felix Fabri</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> However, while the monastery at Mount Sinai was the best-known site of Catherine pilgrimage, it was also the most difficult to reach. The most prominent Western shrine was the monastery in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> that claimed to house Catherine's fingers. It was not alone in the west, however, and was accompanied by many scattered shrines and altars dedicated to Catherine throughout France and England. Some were better-known sites, such as <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury</a> and <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Cathedral" title="Westminster Cathedral">Westminster</a>, which claimed a phial of her oil, brought back from Mount Sinai by <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor">Edward the Confessor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh200331_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh200331-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis200344_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis200344-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Other shrines, such as <a href="/wiki/St._Catherine%27s_Hill,_Hampshire" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Catherine&#39;s Hill, Hampshire">St. Catherine's Hill, Hampshire</a> were the focus of generally local pilgrimage, many of which are only identified by brief mentions in various texts, rather than by physical evidence.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis200349–51_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis200349–51-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Catharine%27s_College,_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg/220px-St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg/330px-St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg/440px-St._Catharine%27s_College%2C_Cambridge_Gate_Catharine_Wheel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3264" data-file-height="2448" /></a><figcaption>St. Catharine's College, Cambridge Gate Catharine Wheel</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/St._Catharine%27s_College,_Cambridge" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Catharine&#39;s College, Cambridge">St. Catharine's College, Cambridge</a> was founded on St Catharine's Day (25 November) 1473 by Robert Woodlark (the then-<a href="/wiki/Provost_(education)" title="Provost (education)">provost</a> of <a href="/wiki/King%27s_College,_Cambridge" title="King&#39;s College, Cambridge">King's College, Cambridge</a>) who sought to create a small community of scholars who would study exclusively theology and philosophy. Wodelarke may have chosen the name in homage to <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Valois" title="Catherine of Valois">Catherine of Valois</a>, mother of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VI_of_England" title="Henry VI of England">Henry VI of England</a>, although it is more likely that it was named as part of the Renaissance cult of Saint Catherine, who was a patron saint of learning.  <a href="/wiki/St_Catherine%27s_College,_Oxford" title="St Catherine&#39;s College, Oxford">St Catherine's College, Oxford</a>, developed from the Delegacy for Unattached Students formed in 1868.
</p><p>Catherine also had a large female following, whose devotion was less likely to be expressed through pilgrimage. The importance of the <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virgin</a> martyrs as the focus of devotion and models for proper feminine behavior increased during the <a href="/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages" title="Late Middle Ages">Late Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBugge2012132_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBugge2012132-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis2000229_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis2000229-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuffy1992174_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuffy1992174-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Among these, St Catherine in particular was used as an <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/exemplar" class="extiw" title="wikt:exemplar">exemplar</a> for women, a status which at times superseded her <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercessory</a> role.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1999_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1999-37">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> Both <a href="/wiki/Christine_de_Pizan" title="Christine de Pizan">Christine de Pizan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Geoffroy_IV_de_la_Tour_Landry" title="Geoffroy IV de la Tour Landry">Geoffrey de la Tour Landry</a> point to Catherine as a paragon for young women, emphasizing her model of virginity and "wifely chastity."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEde_Pizan2003146_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_Pizan2003146-38">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarnhouse2006126,_193_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnhouse2006126,_193-40">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>  From the early 14th century the <a href="/wiki/Mystic_marriage_of_Saint_Catherine" class="mw-redirect" title="Mystic marriage of Saint Catherine">mystic marriage of Saint Catherine</a> first appears in hagiographical literature and, soon after, in art. In the Western church the popularity of her cult began to reduce in the 18th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAllen1997217_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllen1997217-41">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_2">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Saint Catherine of Alexandria Wood Statue at the Korppoo Church in Finland." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg/220px-Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg/330px-Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg/440px-Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_Wood_Statue.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3024" data-file-height="4032" /></a><figcaption>Saint Catherine of Alexandria Wood Statue at the <a href="/wiki/Korpo" title="Korpo">Korppoo</a> Church in <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Her principal symbol is the spiked wheel, which has become known as the <a href="/wiki/Breaking_wheel" title="Breaking wheel">Catherine wheel</a>, and her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 25 November by most Christian churches. However the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian</a>, Polish, <a href="/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church" title="Serbian Orthodox Church">Serbian</a> and Bulgarian <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a> celebrate it on 24 November. The exact origin of this tradition is not known. In 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Kievan_Rus" class="mw-redirect" title="Kievan Rus">Kievan Rus</a>, the feast day was celebrated on 25 November. <a href="/wiki/Dimitry_of_Rostov" class="mw-redirect" title="Dimitry of Rostov">Dimitry of Rostov</a> in his <span title="Russian-language romanization"><i lang="ru-Latn">Kniga zhyty sviatykh</i></span> (<i>Book of the Lives of the Saints</i>), T.1 (1689) places the date of celebration on 24 November. A story that <a href="/wiki/Catherine_II_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Catherine II of Russia">Empress Catherine the Great</a> did not wish to share her patronal feast with the <a href="/wiki/Afterfeast" title="Afterfeast">Leavetaking</a> of the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Presentation_of_Mary" title="Presentation of Mary">Presentation of the Theotokos</a> and hence changed the date is not supported by historical evidence. One of the first Roman Catholic churches to be built in Russia, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_of_St._Catherine_(Saint_Petersburg)" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Church of St. Catherine (Saint Petersburg)">Catholic Church of St. Catherine</a>, was named after Catherine of Alexandria because she was Catherine the Great's patron. A footnote to the entry for 25 November in <i>The Synaxarion</i> compiled by Hieromonk Makarios of Simonos Petra states: "Until the 16th century, the memory of St Catherine was observed on 24 Nov. According to a note by Bartholomew of Koutloumousiou inserted in the <i>Menaion</i>, the Fathers of Sinai transferred the date to 25 Nov. in order that the feast might be kept with greater solemnity."
</p><p>The 1908 <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> describes her historical importance:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Ranked with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Margaret_the_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Margaret the Virgin">St Margaret</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barbara" title="Saint Barbara">St Barbara</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">fourteen most helpful saints in heaven</a>, she was unceasingly praised by preachers and sung by poets. It is believed that <a href="/wiki/Jacques-Benigne_Bossuet" class="mw-redirect" title="Jacques-Benigne Bossuet">Jacques-Benigne Bossuet</a> dedicated to her one of his most beautiful <a href="/wiki/Panegyric" title="Panegyric">panegyrics</a> and that <a href="/wiki/Adam_of_St._Victor" class="mw-redirect" title="Adam of St. Victor">Adam of St. Victor</a> wrote a magnificent poem in her honour: <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Vox Sonora nostri chori</i></span>.</p></blockquote>
<p>In many places her feast was celebrated with the utmost solemnity, servile work being suppressed and the devotions attended by great numbers of people. In several dioceses of France it was observed as a <a href="/wiki/Holy_Day_of_Obligation" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Day of Obligation">Holy Day of Obligation</a> up to the beginning of the 17th century, the splendour of its ceremonial eclipsing that of the feasts of some of the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">apostles</a>. Many chapels were placed under her patronage, and nearly all churches had a statue of her, representing her according to medieval <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> with a wheel, her instrument of torture.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Customs">Customs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Customs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="A carving of Saint Catherine of Alexandria from the O&#39;Crean Tomb in Sligo Abbey dating from 1506." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg/220px-Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="502" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg/330px-Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg/440px-Saint_Catherine_carving_in_Sligo_Abbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="2280" /></a><figcaption>A carving of Saint Catherine of Alexandria from the O'Crean Tomb in <a href="/wiki/Sligo_Abbey" title="Sligo Abbey">Sligo Abbey</a> dating from 1506.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In France, unwed women who had attained the age of 25 were called <a href="/wiki/Catherinette" title="Catherinette">"catherinettes"</a>. They would wear richly decorated bonnets on the day of her feast. This custom gave rise to the French idiom <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">'coiffer Sainte-Catherine'</i></span> ("don St. Catherine's bonnet"), to describe an unmarried woman between the ages of 25 and 30.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In memory of her sacrifice in some homes, Egyptian and other Middle Eastern foods are offered for her feast, such as <a href="/wiki/Hummus" title="Hummus">hummus</a> or <a href="/wiki/Tabbouleh" title="Tabbouleh">tabbouleh</a> salads. Favorites also are melons cut into circles with sherbet "hubs", or cookies shaped as spiked wheels with icing.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Meanwhile, owing to several circumstances in his life, <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_of_Myra" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicholas of Myra">Nicholas of Myra</a> was considered the patron of young <a href="/wiki/Bachelors" class="mw-redirect" title="Bachelors">bachelors</a> and <a href="/wiki/Student" title="Student">students</a>, and Catherine became the patroness of young <a href="/wiki/Maiden" class="mw-redirect" title="Maiden">maidens</a> and female students. Looked upon as the holiest and most illustrious of the virgins of Christ after the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, it was deemed appropriate that she, of all others, should be worthy to watch over the virgins of the cloister and the young women of the world. The spiked wheel having become emblematic of the saint, <a href="/wiki/Wheelwright" title="Wheelwright">wheelwrights</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mechanic" title="Mechanic">mechanics</a> placed themselves under her patronage. Finally, as according to tradition she not only remained a virgin by governing her passions and conquered her executioners by wearying their patience, but triumphed in science by closing the mouths of <a href="/wiki/Sophist" title="Sophist">sophists</a>, her intercession was implored by <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Apologist" class="mw-redirect" title="Apologist">apologists</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pulpit" title="Pulpit">pulpit</a> <a href="/wiki/Orator" title="Orator">orators</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Philosopher" class="mw-redirect" title="Philosopher">philosophers</a>. Before studying, writing, or preaching, they besought her to illumine their minds, guide their pens, and impart eloquence to their words. This devotion to Catherine which assumed such vast proportions in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFoleyMcCloskey2009_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFoleyMcCloskey2009-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> received additional <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%C3%A9clat" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:éclat">éclat</a> in France in the beginning of the 15th century, when it was rumoured that she had spoken to <a href="/wiki/Joan_of_Arc" title="Joan of Arc">Joan of Arc</a> and, together with <a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret of Antioch">Margaret of Antioch</a>, had been divinely appointed Joan's adviser.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEClugnet1908-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Devotion to Catherine remains strong amongst <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholics</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Christians</a>. With the relative ease of travel in the modern age, pilgrimages to Saint Catherine's Monastery on Mount Sinai have increased.
</p><p>Catherine of Alexandria is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/November_25" title="November 25">25 November</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2022, Catherine was officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day she shares with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barbara" title="Saint Barbara">Barbara of Nicomedia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Margaret_the_Virgin" title="Margaret the Virgin">Margaret of Antioch</a> on 24 November.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg/220px-Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg/330px-Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg/440px-Cathedral_of_St._Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>Cathedral dedicated to Catherine of Alexandria located in <a href="/wiki/St._Catharines" title="St. Catharines">St. Catharines</a>, Ontario, Canada.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The pyrotechnic <a href="/wiki/Catherine_wheel_(firework)" title="Catherine wheel (firework)">Catherine wheel</a>, which rotates with sparks flying off in all directions, took its name from the saint's wheel of martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The lunar impact crater <a href="/wiki/Catharina_(crater)" title="Catharina (crater)">Catharina</a> is named after Saint Catherine.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Santa_Catarina_Island" title="Santa Catarina Island">Santa Catarina Island</a> in Brazil and the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catarina_(state)" title="Santa Catarina (state)">State of Santa Catarina</a> are also named after her.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Santa_Catalina" title="Gulf of Santa Catalina">Gulf of Santa Catalina</a> is located in the Pacific Ocean on the west coast of North America. <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catalina_Island_(California)" title="Santa Catalina Island (California)">Santa Catalina Island</a> off the coast of California, was named by <a href="/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno" title="Sebastián Vizcaíno">Sebastián Vizcaíno</a>, who arrived there on her feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-otte_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otte-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catalina_Mountains" title="Santa Catalina Mountains">Santa Catalina Mountains</a> in Arizona are her namesake.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Kaarina" title="Kaarina">Kaarina</a>, Finland, is named after her.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> One accepted origin of the namesake of <a href="/wiki/St._Catharines" title="St. Catharines">St. Catharines</a>, Ontario, is Saint Catherine of Alexandria, but there are other proposed explanations as "no definitive documentation exists to conclusively prove that the founders chose the unique spelling for any one particular reason".<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Catherine_University" title="St. Catherine University">St. Catherine University</a> in St. Paul, Minnesota was founded in 1905 by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_St._Joseph_of_Carondelet" title="Sisters of St. Joseph of Carondelet">Sisters of St. Joseph of Carondelet</a> and named for St. Catherine of Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-Our_History_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Our_History-49">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/University_of_Saint_Katherine" title="University of Saint Katherine">University of Saint Katherine</a> in San Marcos, California is the first Eastern Orthodox Christian university in the United States and the English-speaking world.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> <a href="/wiki/St._Catherine%27s_School,_Richmond,_Virginia" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Catherine&#39;s School, Richmond, Virginia">St. Catherine's School</a>, is an independent Episcopal diocesan school in Richmond, Virginia. <a href="/wiki/St_Helen_and_St_Katharine" title="St Helen and St Katharine">St Helen and St Katharine</a>, girls' school in Oxfordshire, England celebrates "St. Katharine's Day" each November. The name of <a href="/wiki/St._Catharine%27s_School_for_Girls_(Kwun_Tong)" title="St. Catharine&#39;s School for Girls (Kwun Tong)">St. Catharine's School for Girls (Kwun Tong)</a> in Hong Kong was chosen over that of St. Anne because it sounds better when translated into Chinese.
</p><p>St Catherine of Alexandria Parish and School in Oak Lawn, IL, US is named after St. Catherine.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg/220px-German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="93" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg/330px-German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg/440px-German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1798" data-file-height="762" /></a><figcaption><i>Scenes from the Life of Saint Catherine of Alexandria</i>, Germany,  <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;15th century</span>, <a class="external text" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:German_-_Scenes_from_the_Life_of_Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_-_Walters_372638.jpg">Walters Art Museum</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Countless images of Saint Catherine are depicted in art, especially in the late <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, which is also the time that the account of <a href="/wiki/Mystical_marriage_of_Saint_Catherine" title="Mystical marriage of Saint Catherine">Saint Catherine's Mystical Marriage</a> makes its first literary appearance. She can usually be easily recognised as she is richly dressed and crowned, as befits her rank as a princess, and often holds or stands next to a segment of her wheel as an <a href="/wiki/Emblem" title="Emblem">attribute</a>.  She also often carries either a <a href="/wiki/Martyr%27s_palm" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyr&#39;s palm">martyr's palm</a> or the sword with which she was actually executed. She often has long unbound blonde or reddish hair (unbound as she is unmarried). The vision of Saint Catherine of Alexandria usually shows the Infant Christ, held by the Virgin, placing a ring (one of her attributes) on her finger, following some literary accounts, although in the version in the <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> he appears to be adult, and the marriage takes place among a great crowd of angels and "all the celestial court",<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECaxton19001_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECaxton19001-51">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> and these may also be shown.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>She is very frequently shown attending on the Virgin and Child, and is usually prominent in scenes of the <i><a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Virgo_inter_Virgines" title="Master of the Virgo inter Virgines">Master of the Virgo inter Virgines</a></i>, showing a group of virgin saints surrounding the Virgin and Child.  Notable later paintings of Catherine include single figures <a href="/wiki/Saint_Catherine_of_Alexandria_(Raphael)" title="Saint Catherine of Alexandria (Raphael)">by Raphael</a> in the National Gallery, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Catherine_(Caravaggio)" title="Saint Catherine (Caravaggio)">by Caravaggio</a> (in the <a href="/wiki/Thyssen-Bornemisza_Museum" title="Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum">Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum</a>, Madrid).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
