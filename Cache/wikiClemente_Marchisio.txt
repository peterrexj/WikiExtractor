<p><b>Clemente Marchisio</b> (1 March 1833 - 16 December 1903) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who served as a parish priest in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Turin" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Turin">Archdiocese of Turin</a>. Marchisio served as an assistant priest before travelling across Italian cities as word of his mission and holiness spread. In the spirit of evangelical and devotional zeal he established the Daughters of Saint Joseph of Rivalba to suit the religious needs of females. The order had an emphasis on the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and on <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a> himself.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 30 September 1984 he was beatified after one healing was recognized as a miracle attributed to his intercession.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clemente_Marchisio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clemente Marchisio was born on 1 March 1833 in <a href="/wiki/Cuneo" title="Cuneo">Cuneo</a> as the first of five children to a shoemaker. As a child he lived near a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominican</a>-run church and attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> there on a frequent basis; he also harbored a great devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Mother</a> as well as the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While he was going to follow his father in the latter's profession Marchisio received a sudden call to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> and announced as such to his surprised parents; despite this surprise his parents did not oppose their son's wish. Giovanni Battista Sacco aided him in his studies and formation for the priesthood and also rendered economic assistance.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marchisio was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the priesthood on 20 September 1856 from the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Susa" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Susa">Bishop of Susa</a> Giovanni Antonio Oddone since the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Turin" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Turin">Archbishop of Turin</a> was in exile in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>; he required a special dispensation to be ordained since he had not reached the canonical age limit for it. After he was ordained he went for further studies in a boarding school for priests named in honour of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>. He was named as an assistant priest in 1858.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His schedule consisted of waking up at 5:00am and spending two hours in reflection prior to the celebration of Mass. He would then recite two rosaries: one in the morning and one in the evening before he went to sleep. Marchisio said of the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> - to which he had an ardent devotion to - that "I also find sometimes associated under the weight of tribulations, but I assure you that, after five minutes with a living faith before <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus</a> Sacrament, I feel fully revived, so that all that first seemed too hard and became unbearably light and easy".<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He also underwent a visit to <a href="/wiki/Lourdes" title="Lourdes">Lourdes</a> in 1875 for spiritual reflection.
</p><p>The departure of the Albertine nuns left a void of female religious congregations in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> and its Archbishop Lorenzo Gastaldi encouraged him to create a new order for women. In 1871 in <a href="/wiki/Rivalba" title="Rivalba">Rivalba</a> he had opened a weaving workshop for girls as jobs and used that as the basis for his new congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He established it on a formal level on 12 November 1877 and had with him Rosalia Sismonda - a recruit - at his right side in managing it. The order was dedicated to the Eucharist and to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a>. In 1883 the order opened a house in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> said of it in praise: "At last, our Lord!" The pontiff also referred to its members as the "Sisters of the Host".<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marchisio's pastoral mission became so intense that he travelled across various Italian cities and received certificates from heads of dioceses and archdioceses alike in recognition of his good works - one such one came from the Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice" title="Patriarch of Venice">Patriarch of Venice</a> Giuseppe Melchiorre Sarto who would become <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The order would receive the diocesan approval of the Turin Archbishop Gastaldi on 3 May 1877 and received a papal decree of praise of Leo XIII on 6 August 1901.
</p><p>His health started to decline due to his intense schedule and he would celebrate his final Mass on 14 December 1903. He died on the following 16 December uttering the names of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Family" title="Holy Family">Holy Family</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clemente_Marchisio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pope Pius X - whom Marchisio had met in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> - recognized the congregation as one of pontifical right on 9 July 1908.
</p><p>The congregation now operates in <a href="/wiki/Nigeria" title="Nigeria">Nigeria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> amongst other nations and as of 2005 has 322 professed religious into the order in a total of 30 houses.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clemente_Marchisio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The process for beatification commenced in Turin with two processes that saw the collation of both witness testimonies and documentation pertaining to his life and his pastoral life. Those processes spanned from 1933 until 1935 and another from 1946 until 1947; these both occurred despite the fact that the formal introduction to the cause was not given until 28 April 1944 when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation of Rites</a> granted their approval and bestowed upon the late priest the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>.
</p><p>Both processes were declared valid and were ratified in order for the boxes of documents to be taken to Rome for further investigation and allowed - after extensive research and consideration - for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> to declare Marchisio on 4 May 1970 to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> - in the beginning of 1984 - signed a decree recognizing a healing as a miracle and allowed for the beatification of Marchisio to take place; he was beatified on 30 September 1984.
