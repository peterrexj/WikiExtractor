<p><b>Nectarios of Aegina</b><sup id="cite_ref-id_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-id-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Νεκτάριος Αιγίνης</span>; 1 October 1846 – 8 November 1920), Metropolitan of Pentapolis and Wonderworker of <a href="/wiki/Aegina" title="Aegina">Aegina</a>, is one of the most renowned Greek saints, venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and officially recognized by the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> in 1961. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated every year on 9 November.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nectarios_of_Aegina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg/220px-Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="326" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg/330px-Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0c/Saint_Nektarios_of_Aegina_at_Rizario.jpg 2x" data-file-width="376" data-file-height="557" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Saint Nectarios of Aegina</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG/220px-StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="332" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG/330px-StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG/440px-StNectariosHouseSiteSilivri.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3696" /></a><figcaption>Site of Saint Nectarios birth house in <a href="/wiki/Silivri" title="Silivri">Silivri, Istanbul</a>, Turkey</figcaption></figure>
<p>Anastasios Kephalas (Greek: <span lang="el">Αναστάσιος Κεφαλάς</span>), later Nectarios, was born on 1 October 1846 in <a href="/wiki/Silivri" title="Silivri">Selymbria</a>, to a poor family.<sup id="cite_ref-SN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His parents, Dimos and Maria Kephalas, were pious Christians but not wealthy.<sup id="cite_ref-id_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-id-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of 14, he moved to Constantinople, modern-day <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>, to work and further his education. In 1866, at age 20, he moved to the island of <a href="/wiki/Chios" title="Chios">Chios</a> to take a teaching post. On November 7, 1876, he became a monk, at age 30, in the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Nea_Moni" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Nea Moni">Monastery of Nea Moni</a>, for he had long wished to embrace the <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> life.<sup id="cite_ref-SN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three years after becoming a monk, he was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, taking the name Nectarios. He graduated from the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Athens" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Athens">University of Athens</a> in 1885. During his years as a student of the University of Athens he wrote many books, pamphlets, and <a href="/wiki/Bible_commentary" class="mw-redirect" title="Bible commentary">Bible commentaries</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Following his graduation he went to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, where he was ordained a priest and served the Church of Saint Nicholas in <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a>. He was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan bishop</a> of Pentapolis (an ancient Diocese in <a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Cyrenaica</a>, in what is now <a href="/wiki/Libya" title="Libya">Libya</a>) by the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Greece" title="Church of Greece">Greek Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Sophronius_III_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Sophronius III of Constantinople">Sophronios</a> in 1889.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He served as a bishop in Cairo for one year. Nectarios was very popular with the people, which gave rise to jealousy among his colleagues. They were able to persuade his superior that Nectarios had ambitions to displace the Patriarch. Nectarios was suspended from his post without explanation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He then returned to <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> in 1891, and spent several years as a preacher (1891–1894). He was then director of the Rizarios Ecclesiastical School for the education of priests in <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a> for fifteen years. He developed many courses of study, and wrote numerous books, while preaching widely throughout Athens.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 1904, at the request of several nuns, he established Holy Trinity <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a> for them on the island of <a href="/wiki/Aegina" title="Aegina">Aegina</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Nectarios ordained two women as <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconesses</a> in 1911.<sup id="cite_ref-AM_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Up to the 1950s, a few Greek Orthodox nuns also became monastic deaconesses. In 1986, <a href="/wiki/Christodoulos_of_Athens" title="Christodoulos of Athens">Christodoulos</a>, the Metropolitan of Demetrias and later Archbishop of Athens and all of Greece, ordained a woman as deacon in accordance with the "ritual of Saint Nectarios" (the ancient Byzantine text Nectarios had used).<sup id="cite_ref-AM_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In December 1908, at the age of 62, Nectarios resigned from his post as school director and withdrew to the Holy Trinity Convent on Aegina, where he lived out the rest of his life as a monk. He wrote, published, preached, and heard confessions. He also tended the gardens, carried stones, and helped with the construction of the monastery buildings that were built with his own funds.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Nectarios died on November 8, 1920, at the age of 74, following hospitalization for prostate cancer and two months of treatment. His body was taken to the Holy Trinity Convent, where he was buried by his best friend <a href="/wiki/Savvas_the_New_of_Kalymnos" title="Savvas the New of Kalymnos">Savvas of Kalymnos</a>, who later painted the first icon of Nectarios.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The funeral of Nectarios was attended by multitudes of people from all parts of Greece and Egypt.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Saint Nektarios is considered a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> for people who are suffering from diseases; such as cancer, heart trouble, joint pain, epilepsy, arthritis, etc.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nectarios_of_Aegina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The relics of Nectarios were removed from his grave on 2 September 1953. Official recognition of Nectarios as a saint by the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople took place on 20 April 1961. The feast day of Saint Nectarios is celebrated every year on 9 November.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Decision_of_Holy_Synod_of_the_Patriarchate_of_Alexandria">Decision of Holy Synod of the Patriarchate of Alexandria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nectarios_of_Aegina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Decision of Holy Synod of the Patriarchate of Alexandria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On September 15, 1998, the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Alexandria_and_all_Africa" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Alexandria and all Africa">Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Alexandria and all Africa</a> restored the ecclesiastical order of Nectarios:<sup id="cite_ref-SN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Alexandria 15th September 1998</i></dd></dl>
<dl><dd><i>The Holy Spirit has enlightened the gathered members of the Holy Synod of the Patriarchate of Alexandria and all Africa, under the leadership of H.B. Petros VII, Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria and all Africa, more than a century since Saint Nektarios, the great Teacher and Father of the Holy Eastern Orthodox Church was expelled from the Church of Alexandria, to reach the following decision:</i></dd></dl>
<dl><dd><i>Taking into account the resolution of the Church to rank Saint Nektarios amongst the saints because of his innumerable miracles and his acceptance within the religious conscience of Orthodox Christians throughout the world, we appeal to the mercy of the ever-charitable God.</i></dd></dl>
<dl><dd><i>We hereby restore the ecclesiastical order of the Saint of our Century, Saint Nektarios, and grant to him all due credits and honours. We beseech Saint Nektarios to forgive both us, unworthy as we are, and our predecessors, our brothers of the Throne of Alexandria, for opposition to the Saint and for all which, due to human weakness or error, our Holy Father, Bishop of Pentapolis, Saint Nektarios, suffered.</i>
<dl><dd><dl><dd><i>PETROS VII, By the Grace of God</i></dd></dl></dd>
<dd><i>Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria and All Africa.</i></dd></dl></dd></dl>
