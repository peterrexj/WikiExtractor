<p><b>Ælfric of Abingdon</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 16 November 1005) was a late 10th-century <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. He previously held the offices of <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/St_Albans_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St Albans Abbey">St Albans Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ramsbury_(ancient)" title="Bishop of Ramsbury (ancient)">Bishop of Ramsbury</a>, as well as likely being the abbot of <a href="/wiki/Abingdon_Abbey" title="Abingdon Abbey">Abingdon Abbey</a>. After his election to Canterbury, he continued to hold the bishopric of Ramsbury along with the archbishopric of Canterbury until his death in 1005. Ælfric may have altered the composition of Canterbury's <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> by changing the clergy serving in the cathedral from secular clergy to monks. In his will he left a ship to King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_the_Unready" title="Æthelred the Unready">Æthelred II of England</a> as well as more ships to other legatees.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfric_of_Abingdon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ælfric was the son of an earl of Kent<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church125_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church125-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and became a monk of Abingdon Abbey in Berkshire (now Oxfordshire). He was very likely Abbot of Abingdon<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> before becoming Abbot of St Albans Abbey around 975,<sup id="cite_ref-Knowles65_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Knowles65-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> although some historians do not believe that he held the office of Abbot at Abingdon. Although the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Ecclesie_Abbendonensis" title="Historia Ecclesie Abbendonensis">Historia Ecclesie Abbendonensis</a></i>, or <i>History of the Church of Abingdon</i>, names Ælfric as abbot, the abbatial lists do not record him as such. Indirect corroboration of his being abbot at Abingdon is a grant of land to Ælfric personally (instead of to the office he held) while he was archbishop that had previously been unjustly taken from Abingdon. This land was to revert to Abingdon after Ælfric's death.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_and_archbishop">Bishop and archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfric_of_Abingdon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop and archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ælfric's brother, Leofric, succeeded him as <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_St_Albans" title="Abbot of St Albans">Abbot of St Albans</a> when he became bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Stafford169_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stafford169-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Between 991 and 993, Ælfric rose to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ramsbury_(ancient)" title="Bishop of Ramsbury (ancient)">Bishopric of Ramsbury</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook220_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook220-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and possibly continued to hold office of abbot of St Albans while bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 995 he was elevated to the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Translation_(ecclesiastical)" title="Translation (ecclesiastical)">translated</a>, or moved with appropriate ecclesiastical ceremony, to Canterbury on 21 April 995<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> at a <a href="/wiki/Witenagemot" class="mw-redirect" title="Witenagemot">witenagemot</a> held at <a href="/wiki/Amesbury" title="Amesbury">Amesbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams20_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams20-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Here he received the permission of "King Æthelred and all the witan" to be elevated to Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church107_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church107-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Ælfric continued to hold Ramsbury along with Canterbury until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams36_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams36-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The story that his brother was chosen first for Canterbury but refused, stems from confusion on the part of <a href="/wiki/Matthew_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Matthew of Paris">Matthew of Paris</a> and historians generally hold the entire episode to be untrue.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ælfric's appointment to Canterbury caused consternation with the clergy of the cathedral chapter. In reaction, the chapter sent two members to Rome ahead of Ælfric and tried to secure the archbishopric for either of the monks. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_V" title="Pope Gregory V">Pope Gregory V</a>, however, would not appoint a candidate without royal permission, which neither of the monks had.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church103_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church103-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Instead, when Ælfric arrived in Rome in 997, he was appointed and received the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>, a symbol of an archbishop's authority.<sup id="cite_ref-Ortenberg49_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ortenberg49-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He also witnessed some miracles at the gravesite of <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Martyr" title="Edward the Martyr">Edward the Martyr</a> at <a href="/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey" title="Shaftesbury Abbey">Shaftesbury Abbey</a>, helping to lead to Edward's sainthood.<sup id="cite_ref-Emma52_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emma52-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A story was told that Ælfric introduced monks into the cathedral church of <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ Church, Canterbury">Christ Church, Canterbury</a>, replacing the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">secular clerks</a> that had taken over the foundation during the ninth century.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE456_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE456-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Ælfric is said to have done this on the command of the pope. This story originally dates to soon after the <a href="/wiki/Norman_Conquest" title="Norman Conquest">Norman Conquest</a> and originated with the monastic historians of Canterbury, and its veracity is unclear.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic50_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic50-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He likely performed the marriage ceremony of King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_the_Unready" title="Æthelred the Unready">Æthelred the Unready</a> and <a href="/wiki/Emma_of_Normandy" title="Emma of Normandy">Emma of Normandy</a> in 1002.<sup id="cite_ref-Emma31_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emma31-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> A later tradition held that he consecrated a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Llandaff" title="Bishop of Llandaff">Bishop of Llandaff</a> and two <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_St_David%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of St David&#39;s">Bishops of St. David's</a> in Wales, which, if true, would have meant extending Canterbury's jurisdiction into new territory.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church232_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church232-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Either Ælfric or his predecessor Sigeric wrote a letter to <a href="/wiki/Wulfine" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfine">Wulfsige, Bishop of Sherborne</a> about the duties of bishops to make sure that the laity did not despoil churches. The letter also urged Wulfsige to exhort the laity to strive for justice in their dealings with others, help widows and orphans, not fight, as well as other moral precepts.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church64_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church64-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Ælfric also ordered the composition of the first <i>Life of Dunstan</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, or saint's life, of <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>, a predecessor as Archbishop of Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church62_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church62-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> He also acted as a royal judge, once being ordered by King Æthelred to adjudicate a case between <a href="/wiki/Thegn" title="Thegn">thegns</a>, or local noblemen.<sup id="cite_ref-1000Church147_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1000Church147-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfric_of_Abingdon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ælfric died on 16 November 1005<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in Abingdon Abbey, later being <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury Cathedral</a>. His will survives and in it he left ships to the people of <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>, with his best one, equipped for sixty men, going to King Æthelred.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Williams81_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams81-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The hagiography of Dunstan was dedicated to Ælfric at the end of the 10th century.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Darlington389_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Darlington389-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> After his death, he was considered a saint with a feast day of 16 November.<sup id="cite_ref-Dict12_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dict12-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Patron_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Patron-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
