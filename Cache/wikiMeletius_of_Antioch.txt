					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Christian bishop of Antioch from 360 to 381</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Meletian schism" and "Meletians" redirect here. For the similarly-named schism in Egypt, see <a href="/wiki/Melitians" title="Melitians">Melitians</a>. For its namesake, see <a href="/wiki/Melitius_of_Lycopolis" title="Melitius of Lycopolis">Melitius of Lycopolis</a>.</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:StMeletiy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/StMeletiy.jpg/220px-StMeletiy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="296" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/StMeletiy.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="297" data-file-height="400" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:StMeletiy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Meletius of Antioch</div></div></div>
<p><b>Saint Meletius</b> (Greek: Μελέτιος, <i>Meletios</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">bishop of Antioch</a> from 360 until his death in 381. He was opposed by a rival bishop named <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_II_of_Antioch" title="Paulinus II of Antioch">Paulinus</a> and his episcopate was dominated by the schism, usually called the <b>Meletian schism</b>. As a result, he was exiled from Antioch in 361–362, 365–366 and 371–378. One of his last acts was to preside over the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople" title="First Council of Constantinople">First Council of Constantinople</a> in 381.
</p><p>There are contrasting views about his theological position: on the one hand, he was exiled three times under <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> emperors; on the other, he was strongly opposed by those faithful to the memory of the staunchly <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">pro-Nicene</a> <a href="/wiki/Eustathius_of_Antioch" title="Eustathius of Antioch">Eustathius of Antioch</a>, whom the synod of Melitene deposed for his <a href="/wiki/Homoousian" class="mw-redirect" title="Homoousian">Homoousianism</a>, which they considered a heresy, and by Saint <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a>, a firm opponent of Arianism.
</p><p>Meletius' <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> was remarkable in view of his great private wealth. He is venerated as a saint and confessor in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is 12 February.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Sebaste">Bishop of Sebaste</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Bishop of Sebaste">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Meletius was born at <a href="/wiki/Melitene" class="mw-redirect" title="Melitene">Melitene</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a> of wealthy and noble parents. He first appears around 357 as a supporter of <a href="/wiki/Acacius_of_Caesarea" title="Acacius of Caesarea">Acacius</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Palaestina" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Palaestina">Caesarea</a>, the leader of that local faction that supported the <a href="/wiki/Homoean" class="mw-redirect" title="Homoean">Homoean</a> formula by which the emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> sought for a compromise between the <a href="/wiki/Homoiousian" title="Homoiousian">Homoiousians</a> and the Homoousians.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The Homoiousians held that God and Jesus Christ are of like essence, the Homoousians that they are, as stated in the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a>, of the same essence. Meletius thus first appears as an ecclesiastic of the court party, and as such became <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bishop_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bishop of Sebaste (page does not exist)">bishop of Sebaste</a> in succession to <a href="/wiki/Eustathius_of_Sebaste" title="Eustathius of Sebaste">Eustathius</a>, an Arian. The appointment was resented by the Homoousian clergy, and Meletius resigned the see.<sup id="cite_ref-Leclerq_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leclerq-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Antioch">Bishop of Antioch</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop of Antioch">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_period_and_exile">First period and exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: First period and exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Socrates_Scholasticus" class="mw-redirect" title="Socrates Scholasticus">Socrates Scholasticus</a>, Meletius attended the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Seleucia" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Seleucia">synod of Seleucia</a> in the autumn of 359, and then subscribed to the Acacian (Homoean) formula. Early in 360 he became bishop of Antioch, succeeding <a href="/wiki/Eudoxius_of_Antioch" title="Eudoxius of Antioch">Eudoxius</a>, who had been translated to the see of Constantinople. Early the following year (361), he was in exile. According to an old tradition, supported by evidence drawn from <a href="/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Cyprus" class="mw-redirect" title="Epiphanius of Cyprus">Epiphanius of Cyprus</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>, this was due to a sermon preached before the emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a>, in which he revealed Homoousian views. This explanation, however, is rejected by G. F. Loofs on the grounds that the sermon contains nothing inconsistent with the Acacian position favoured by the court party; on the other hand, there is evidence of conflicts with the clergy, quite apart from any questions of orthodoxy, which may have led to the bishop's deposition.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Meletius believed that truth lay in delicate distinctions, but his formula was so indefinite that it is difficult to grasp it with precision. He was neither a thorough Nicene nor a decided Arian.<sup id="cite_ref-Leclerq_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leclerq-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The successor of Meletius was <a href="/w/index.php?title=Euzoeus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Euzoeus (page does not exist)">Euzoeus</a>, an Arian. In Antioch itself Meletius continued to have adherents, who held separate services in the apostolic church in the old town. The Meletian schism was complicated, moreover, by the presence in the city of another anti-Arian sect, stricter adherents of the Homoousian formula, maintaining the tradition of the deposed bishop Eustathius and governed at this time by the presbyter <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_II_of_Antioch" title="Paulinus II of Antioch">Paulinus</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Councils_of_Alexandria" title="Councils of Alexandria">synod of Alexandria</a> (362) sent deputies to attempt an arrangement between the two anti-Arian churches; but before they arrived Paulinus had been consecrated bishop by <a href="/wiki/Lucifer_of_Calaris" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucifer of Calaris">Lucifer of Calaris</a>. When in consequence of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian</a>'s contemptuous policy Meletius returned, he found himself as one of three rival bishops.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_and_third_exiles">Second and third exiles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Second and third exiles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> came to Antioch by order of the emperor, and expressed to Meletius his wish of entering into communion with him. Meletius, ill-advised, delayed answering him, and Athanasius went away having admitted Paulinus, whom he had not yet recognized as bishop, to his communion.<sup id="cite_ref-Leclerq_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leclerq-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The orthodox Nicene party, notably Athanasius himself, held communion with Paulinus only. Twice, in 365 and 371 or 372, Meletius was exiled by decree of the Arian emperor <a href="/wiki/Valens" title="Valens">Valens</a>. A further complication was added when, in 375, <a href="/wiki/Vitalius" title="Vitalius">Vitalius</a>, one of Meletius' presbyters, was consecrated bishop by the heretical bishop <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_of_Laodicea" title="Apollinaris of Laodicea">Apollinaris of Laodicea</a>. After the death of Valens in 378, the Western emperor <a href="/wiki/Gratian" title="Gratian">Gratian</a> removed Euzoeus from Antioch, handing over the churches to Meletius. <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_I" title="Theodosius I">Theodosius I</a>, the new emperor in the East, also favoured Meletius, who had been more and more approximating to the views of the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Triumph">Triumph</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Triumph">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Upon his return to Antioch, Meletius was hailed as the leader of orthodoxy. As such he presided in October 379 over the great <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Antioch">synod of Antioch</a>, in which the dogmatic agreement of East and West was established. He helped <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Nazianzus" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory Nazianzus">Gregory Nazianzus</a> to the see of Constantinople and also presided over the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople" title="First Council of Constantinople">First Council of Constantinople</a>, the second ecumenical council, in 381.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Paulinus, however, was the man favoured by Rome and Alexandria. Jerome accompanied Paulinus back to Rome in order to secure him more support.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Meanwhile, <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a>, bishop of Milan, was dealing with Arians in the West. He persuaded Gratian to call a church synod. The <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Aquileia,_381" title="Council of Aquileia, 381">Council of Aquileia</a> (381) deposed two bishops of the eastern province of Dacia, <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Ratiaria" title="Palladius of Ratiaria">Palladius of Ratiaria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Secundianus_of_Singidunum" title="Secundianus of Singidunum">Secundianus of Singidunum</a>, and requested the emperors Theodosius and Gratian to convene at Alexandria a general council of all bishops in order to put an end to the Meletian schism at Antioch.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The two remaining factions which divided the Antiochene Church were orthodox, the supporters of Meletius and the adherents of Paulinus. Uniting them was a difficult move.<sup id="cite_ref-Leclerq_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leclerq-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A temporary pacification ensued, when six of the leading presbyters took an oath not to seek episcopal consecration themselves but to accept as bishop of Antioch whichever of the two rivals outlived the other.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Schism_after_his_death">Schism after his death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Meletius_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Schism after his death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Meletius died soon after the opening of the First Council of Constantinople and the emperor Theodosius, who had received him with special distinction, ordered his body to be carried to Antioch and buried with the honours of a saint. The Meletian schism, however, did not end immediately with his death. In spite of the advice of Gregory Nazianzus, Paulinus was not recognized as the sole bishop and <a href="/wiki/Flavian_I_of_Antioch" title="Flavian I of Antioch">Flavian</a> was consecrated as Meletius' successor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Eustathians, on the other hand, elected <a href="/wiki/Evagrius_of_Antioch" title="Evagrius of Antioch">Evagrius</a> as bishop on Paulinus' death in 388.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 399, John Chrysostom, who had been ordained a deacon by Meletius, but later separated from his group and accepted ordination to the priesthood at the hands of Evagrius,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> secured reconciliation between Flavian and the sees of Alexandria and Rome. However, it would take the Eustathians at Antioch until 415 to accept Flavian.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
