<p>The <b>Penitent Thief</b>, also known as the <b>Good Thief</b>, <b>Wise Thief, Grateful Thief</b>, or <b>Thief on the Cross</b>, is one of two unnamed thieves in Luke's account of the <a href="/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus" title="Crucifixion of Jesus">crucifixion of Jesus</a> in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>.  The <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke" title="Gospel of Luke">Gospel of Luke</a> describes him asking Jesus to "remember him" when Jesus comes into <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_God_(Christianity)" title="Kingdom of God (Christianity)">his kingdom</a>.  The other, as the <a href="/wiki/Impenitent_thief" title="Impenitent thief">impenitent thief</a>, challenges Jesus to save himself and both of them to prove that he is the Messiah.
</p><p>He is officially venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.  The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> places his commemoration on 25 March, together with the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Annunciation" title="Feast of the Annunciation">Feast of the Annunciation</a>, because of the ancient Christian tradition<sup id="cite_ref-CathFeast_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathFeast-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> that Christ (and the penitent thief) were crucified and died exactly on the anniversary of Christ's <a href="/wiki/Incarnation_(Christianity)" title="Incarnation (Christianity)">incarnation</a>.
</p><p>He is given the name <b>Dismas</b> in the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Nicodemus" title="Gospel of Nicodemus">Gospel of Nicodemus</a> and is traditionally known in Catholicism as <b>Saint Dismas</b><sup id="cite_ref-Cunningham_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunningham-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (sometimes <b>Dysmas</b>; in Spanish and Portuguese, <i><b>Dimas</b></i>). Other traditions have bestowed other names:
</p>
<ul><li>In <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Orthodox</a> tradition and the <i><a href="/wiki/Joseph_of_Arimathea" title="Joseph of Arimathea">Narrative of Joseph of Arimathea</a></i>, he is named <b>Demas</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-Gabra_2009_120_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gabra_2009_120-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_University_Press_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford_University_Press-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>In the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Colbertinus" title="Codex Colbertinus">Codex Colbertinus</a></i>, he is named <b>Zoatham</b> or <b>Zoathan</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>In the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Infancy_Gospel" title="Syriac Infancy Gospel">Arabic Infancy Gospel</a>, he is named <b>Titus</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>In <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox</a> tradition, he is named <b>Rakh</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: Рах).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Gospel_of_Luke">Gospel of Luke</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Gospel of Luke">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:195px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Good_thief_(16th_c.,_Rostov_Kremlin).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg/193px-Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="193" height="468" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg/290px-Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg/387px-Good_thief_%2816th_c.%2C_Rostov_Kremlin%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1436" data-file-height="3473" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Good_thief_(16th_c.,_Rostov_Kremlin).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Russian Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of The Good Thief in Paradise, circa 16th century in Rostov, Kremlin</div></div></div>
<p>Two men were crucified at the same time as Jesus, one on his right and one on his left,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> which the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark" title="Gospel of Mark">Gospel of Mark</a> interprets as fulfillment of the prophecy of <a href="/wiki/Isaiah_53" title="Isaiah 53">Isaiah 53</a>:12 ("And he was numbered with the transgressors").<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> According to the Gospels of <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew" title="Gospel of Matthew">Matthew</a> and Mark, respectively, both of the thieves mocked Jesus;<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Luke, however, relates:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><sup>39</sup> Now one of the criminals hanging there reviled Jesus, saying, "Are you not the Messiah? Save yourself and us."
</p><p><sup>40</sup> The other, however, rebuking him, said in reply, "Have you no fear of God, for you are subject to the same condemnation?
<sup>41</sup> And indeed, we have been condemned justly, for the sentence we received corresponds to our crimes, but this man has done nothing criminal."
<sup>42</sup> Then he said, "Jesus, remember me, when you come into your kingdom."
</p>
<p><sup>43</sup> He replied to him, "Amen, I say to you, today you will be with me in Paradise."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Attempts have been made to reconcile the apparent contradiction between Luke's account and that of Mark and Matthew. <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> suggested that the authors of Mark and Matthew, for the sake of concision, employed a figure of speech whereby the plural was put for the singular.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Later commentators, such as <a href="/wiki/Frederic_Farrar" title="Frederic Farrar">Frederic Farrar</a>, have drawn attention to the difference between the Greek words used: "The two first Synoptists tell us that both the robbers during an early part of the hours of crucifixion <i>reproached</i> Jesus (<i>ὠνείδιζον</i>), but we learn from St Luke that only one of them used injurious and insulting language to Him (<i>ἐβλασφήμει</i>)."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h3><span id=".22Amen_..._today_..._in_paradise.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Amen_..._today_..._in_paradise&quot;">"Amen ... today ... in paradise"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: &quot;Amen ... today ... in paradise&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Paradise" title="Paradise">Paradise</a></div>
<p>The phrase translated "Amen, I say to you, today you will be in paradise" in Luke 23:43 ("Ἀμήν σοι λέγω σήμερον μετ’ ἐμοῦ ἔσῃ ἐν τῷ παραδείσῳ."<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> <i>Amén soi légo sémeron met' emoû ése en tôi paradeísoi</i>) is disputed in a minority of versions and commentaries. The Greek manuscripts are without punctuation, so attribution of the <a href="/wiki/Adverb" title="Adverb">adverb</a> "today" to the <a href="/wiki/Verb" title="Verb">verb</a> "be", as "Amen I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise" (the majority view), or the verb "say", as "Amen I say to you today, you will be with me in paradise" (the minority view), is dependent on analysis of <a href="/wiki/Word_order" title="Word order">word order</a> conventions in <a href="/wiki/Koine_Greek" title="Koine Greek">Koine Greek</a>. The majority of ancient <a href="/wiki/Bible_translations" title="Bible translations">Bible translations</a> also follow the majority view, with only the <a href="/wiki/Aramaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Aramaic language">Aramaic language</a> <a href="/wiki/Curetonian_Gospels" title="Curetonian Gospels">Curetonian Gospels</a> offering significant testimony to the minority view.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> As a result, some prayers recognize the good thief as the only person confirmed as a saint—that is, a person known to be in Paradise after death—by the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a>, and by Jesus himself. <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a> wrote:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The words of The Lord (This day ... in paradise) must therefore be understood not of an earthly or corporeal paradise, but of that spiritual paradise in which all may be, said to be, who are in the enjoyment of the divine glory. Hence to place, the thief went up with Christ to heaven, that he might be with Christ, as it was said to him: "Thou shalt be with Me in Paradise"; but as to reward, he was in Paradise, for he there tasted and enjoyed the divinity of Christ, together with the other saints.<sup id="cite_ref-Gaume_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gaume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CFN_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CFN-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Unnamed">Unnamed</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Unnamed">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Only the Gospel of Luke describes one of the criminals as penitent, and that gospel does not name him.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> does not name the thief, but wonders if he might not have been baptized at some point.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to tradition,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> the Good Thief was crucified to Jesus' right and the other thief was crucified to his left. For this reason, depictions of the <a href="/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus" title="Crucifixion of Jesus">crucifixion of Jesus</a> often show Jesus' head inclined to his right, showing his acceptance of the Good Thief. In the Russian Orthodox Church, both <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifixes</a> and crosses are usually made with three bars: the top one, representing the <i><a href="/wiki/Titulus_Crucis" title="Titulus Crucis">titulus</a></i> (the inscription that <a href="/wiki/Pontius_Pilate" title="Pontius Pilate">Pontius Pilate</a> wrote and was nailed above Jesus' head); the longer crossbar on which Jesus' hands were nailed; and a slanted bar at the bottom representing the footrest to which Jesus' feet were nailed. The footrest is slanted, pointing up towards the Good Thief, and pointing down towards the other.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_(Saint_Dismas)_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_%28Saint_Dismas%29_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg/220px-Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_%28Saint_Dismas%29_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="393" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_%28Saint_Dismas%29_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg/330px-Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_%28Saint_Dismas%29_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f2/Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_%28Saint_Dismas%29_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg 2x" data-file-width="336" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ubekendt_spansk_mester_-_The_Good_Thief_(Saint_Dismas)_-_KMS8561_-_Statens_Museum_for_Kunst.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting from c. 1450</div></div></div>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>, the thief dwelt in the desert and robbed or murdered anyone unlucky enough to cross his path. According to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a>, he "was guilty of blood, even his brother's blood" (<a href="/wiki/Fratricide" title="Fratricide">fratricide</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Gaume_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gaume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CFN_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CFN-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Named">Named</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Named">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id=".22Dismas.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Dismas&quot;">"Dismas"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: &quot;Dismas&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Luke's unnamed penitent thief was later assigned the name Dismas in an early Greek recension of the <i>Acta Pilati</i> and the Latin <i>Gospel of Nicodemus</i>, portions of which may be dated to the late fourth century. The name "Dismas" may have been adapted from a <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> word meaning "sunset" or "death".<sup id="cite_ref-Cunningham_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cunningham-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The other thief's name is given as <a href="/wiki/Impenitent_thief" title="Impenitent thief">Gestas</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Infancy_Gospel" title="Syriac Infancy Gospel">Syriac Infancy Gospel</a>'s <i>Life of the Good Thief</i> (<i>Histoire Du Bon Larron</i> French 1868, English 1882), Augustine of Hippo said, the thief said to Jesus, the child: "O most blessed of children, if ever a time should come when I shall crave Thy Mercy, remember me and forget not what has passed this day."<sup id="cite_ref-Gaume_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gaume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CFN_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CFN-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Anne_Catherine_Emmerich" title="Anne Catherine Emmerich">Anne Catherine Emmerich</a> saw the Holy Family "exhausted and helpless"; according to Augustine of Hippo and <a href="/wiki/Peter_Damian" title="Peter Damian">Peter Damian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Holy_Family" title="Holy Family">the Holy Family</a> met Dismas, in these circumstances.<sup id="cite_ref-Emmerich_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emmerich-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Theophilus_I_of_Alexandria" title="Theophilus I of Alexandria">Pope Theophilus of Alexandria</a> (385–412) wrote a <i><a href="/wiki/Homily_on_the_Crucifixion_and_the_Good_Thief" title="Homily on the Crucifixion and the Good Thief">Homily on the Crucifixion and the Good Thief</a></i>, which is a classic of <a href="/wiki/Coptic_literature" title="Coptic literature">Coptic literature</a>.
</p>
<h4><span id=".22Demas.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Demas&quot;">"Demas"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: &quot;Demas&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In Coptic Orthodoxy, he is named Demas.<sup id="cite_ref-Gabra_2009_120_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gabra_2009_120-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This is the name given to him in the <a href="/wiki/Joseph_of_Arimathea" title="Joseph of Arimathea">Narrative of Joseph of Arimathea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_University_Press_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford_University_Press-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id=".22Titus.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Titus&quot;">"Titus"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: &quot;Titus&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The apocryphal <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Infancy_Gospel" title="Syriac Infancy Gospel">Syriac Infancy Gospel</a> calls the two thieves Titus and Dumachus, and adds a tale about how Titus (the good one) prevented the other thieves in his company from robbing <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Mary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Joseph</a> during their <a href="/wiki/Flight_into_Egypt" title="Flight into Egypt">flight into Egypt</a>.
</p>
<h4><span id=".22Rakh.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Rakh&quot;">"Rakh"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: &quot;Rakh&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In the Russian tradition, the Good Thief's name is "Rakh" (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: Рах).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Catholic Church remembers the Good Thief on 25 March. In the Roman Martyrology, the following entry is given: "Commemoration of the holy thief in Jerusalem who confessed to Christ and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> him by Jesus himself<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> on the cross at that moment and merited to hear from him: 'Today you will be with me in Paradise.<span style="padding-right:.15em;">'</span>"
A number of towns, including <a href="/wiki/San_Dimas,_California" title="San Dimas, California">San Dimas, California</a>, are named after him. Also, <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish churches</a> are named after him, such as the Church of the Good Thief in <a href="/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario" title="Kingston, Ontario">Kingston, Ontario</a>, Canada—built by convicts at nearby <a href="/wiki/Kingston_Penitentiary" title="Kingston Penitentiary">Kingston Penitentiary</a>, Saint Dismas Church in <a href="/wiki/Waukegan,_Illinois" title="Waukegan, Illinois">Waukegan</a>, Illinois, the <a href="/wiki/Old_Catholic_Church" title="Old Catholic Church">Old Catholic</a> Parish of St Dismas in <a href="/wiki/Coseley" title="Coseley">Coseley</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Dismas,_the_Good_Thief" title="Church of St. Dismas, the Good Thief">Church of St. Dismas, the Good Thief</a>, a Catholic church at the <a href="/wiki/Clinton_Correctional_Facility" title="Clinton Correctional Facility">Clinton Correctional Facility</a> in Dannemora, New York.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> remembers him on <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a>, along with the crucifixion. The <a href="/wiki/Synaxarion" class="mw-redirect" title="Synaxarion">Synaxarion</a> offers this couplet in his honor:
</p>
<blockquote>
<div class="poem">
<p>Eden's locked gates the Thief has opened wide,<br />
By putting in the key, "Remember me."
</p>
</div>
</blockquote>
<p>He is commemorated in a traditional Eastern Orthodox prayer said before receiving the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">eucharist</a>: "I will not speak of Thy Mystery to Thine enemies, neither like <a href="/wiki/Judas_Iscariot" title="Judas Iscariot">Judas</a> will I give Thee a kiss; but like the thief will I confess Thee: Remember me, O Lord in Thy Kingdom."<sup id="cite_ref-orthodox-prayer_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodox-prayer-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Art">Art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:5part-icon-Dismas.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/5part-icon-Dismas.jpg/220px-5part-icon-Dismas.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="171" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/5part-icon-Dismas.jpg/330px-5part-icon-Dismas.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/5part-icon-Dismas.jpg 2x" data-file-width="347" data-file-height="269" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:5part-icon-Dismas.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>An icon showing Christ (center) bringing Dismas (left) into <a href="/wiki/Paradise" title="Paradise">Paradise</a>: At the right are the Gates of Paradise, guarded by a <a href="/wiki/Seraph" title="Seraph">seraph</a> (<a href="/wiki/Solovetsky_Monastery" title="Solovetsky Monastery">Solovetsky Monastery</a>, 17th century).</div></div></div>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Medieval_art" title="Medieval art">medieval art</a>, St Dismas is often depicted as accompanying Jesus in the <a href="/wiki/Harrowing_of_Hell" title="Harrowing of Hell">Harrowing of Hell</a> as related in <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/1_Peter#3:19" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/1 Peter">1 Peter 3:19–20</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">Apostles' Creed</a> (though neither text mentions the thief).
</p><p>In the Eastern Orthodox Church, one of the hymns of Good Friday is entitled, "The Good Thief" (or "The Wise Thief", <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Church Slavonic language">Church Slavonic</a>: "<i>Razboinika blagorazumnago</i>"), and speaks of how Christ granted Dismas Paradise.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Several compositions of this hymn<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>  are used in the Russian Orthodox Church and form one of the highlights of the <a href="/wiki/Matins" title="Matins">Matins</a> service on Good Friday.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Beckett" title="Samuel Beckett">Samuel Beckett</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Waiting_for_Godot" title="Waiting for Godot">Waiting for Godot</a></i>, the main characters Vladimir and Estragon briefly discuss the inconsistencies between the <a href="/wiki/Four_Evangelists" title="Four Evangelists">Four Evangelists</a>' accounts of the penitent and impenitent thieves. Vladimir concludes that since only Luke says that one of the two was saved, "then the two of them must have been damned [...] why believe him rather than the others?"<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Penitent_thief&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The thief features in Christian popular music, as in Christian rock band <a href="/wiki/Third_Day" title="Third Day">Third Day</a>'s 1995 song "Thief", and the name of the Christian rock band <a href="/wiki/Dizmas" title="Dizmas">Dizmas</a>. The thief is the narrator in <a href="/wiki/Sydney_Carter" title="Sydney Carter">Sydney Carter</a>'s controversial song "Friday Morning".<sup id="cite_ref-carter_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-carter-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is portrayed by <a href="/wiki/Stelio_Savante" title="Stelio Savante">Stelio Savante</a> in the award-winning Good Friday film <i>Once We Were Slaves</i> directed by Dallas Jenkins<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Dismas is central to the early plot of the video game <i><a href="/wiki/Uncharted_4:_A_Thief%27s_End" title="Uncharted 4: A Thief&#39;s End">Uncharted 4: A Thief's End</a></i> in which treasure hunter <a href="/wiki/Nathan_Drake_(character)" class="mw-redirect" title="Nathan Drake (character)">Nathan Drake</a> uses a St. Dismas statue to aid in his search for pirate treasure.
</p><p>Dismas is the name of one of two starting characters in the video game <i><a href="/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon" title="Darkest Dungeon">Darkest Dungeon</a></i>. He is also referred to as a rogue, thief, and highwayman in the in-game descriptions. A comic showing his backstory, as well as in-game item descriptions, implies that he is attempting to redeem himself after killing an innocent woman and her child. A special achievement is granted if both starting characters reach the game's final challenge, fittingly titled "On the old road, we found redemption."
</p><p>In the 1967 romantic comedy caper film <i><a href="/wiki/Fitzwilly" title="Fitzwilly">Fitzwilly</a></i>, butler mastermind Claude Fitzwilliam (<a href="/wiki/Dick_Van_Dyke" title="Dick Van Dyke">Dick Van Dyke</a>) and his larcenous staff operate St. Dismas Thrift Shoppe in Philadelphia, a fictional charity where they send and store their stolen loot.
</p><p>St. Dismas is prominently mentioned throughout the 1946 film <i><a href="/wiki/The_Hoodlum_Saint" title="The Hoodlum Saint">The Hoodlum Saint</a></i> starring <a href="/wiki/William_Powell" title="William Powell">William Powell</a>, <a href="/wiki/Esther_Williams" title="Esther Williams">Esther Williams</a> and <a href="/wiki/Angela_Lansbury" title="Angela Lansbury">Angela Lansbury</a>.
</p><p>Dismas Hardy is the main protagonist in a series of legal and crime thriller novels by <a href="/wiki/John_Lescroart" title="John Lescroart">John Lescroart</a>.
</p><p>In the 2022 film <i><a href="/wiki/Clerks_III" title="Clerks III">Clerks III</a></i>, Elias mentions the Good Thief multiple times, quoting him as saying "Jesus did no wrong, whereas we are but thieves". In a <a href="/wiki/Running_gag" title="Running gag">running gag</a>, everybody hears "but thieves" as "butt thieves" and wonders out loud what that means.
</p>
