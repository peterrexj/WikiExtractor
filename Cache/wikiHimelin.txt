<p>Saint <b>Himelin</b> (<b>Hymelin</b>, <b>Himelinus</b>) (died <a href="/wiki/Vissenaken" title="Vissenaken">Vissenaken</a>, c. 750 AD) was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a><sup id="cite_ref-stpatrickdc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stpatrickdc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or <a href="/wiki/Scottish_people" title="Scottish people">Scottish</a><sup id="cite_ref-PK_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PK-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who, returning from a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, fell ill when passing through Vissenaken (in present-day part of the municipality <a href="/wiki/Tienen" title="Tienen">Tienen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is said to have been the brother of <a href="/wiki/Rumbold_of_Mechelen" class="mw-redirect" title="Rumbold of Mechelen">Rumbold, patron saint of Mechelen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stpatrickdc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stpatrickdc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend of Saint Himelin states that in Vissenaken he asked a girl for some water.  She refused, as there was <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a> in the area. However, after much insistence from Himelin, she finally gave him a pitcher of water, which <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miraculously</a> turned into <a href="/wiki/Wine" title="Wine">wine</a>. Himelin died three days later of the plague. He is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">venerated</a> on 10 March. His cult is confined to Vissenaken.<sup id="cite_ref-PK_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PK-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
