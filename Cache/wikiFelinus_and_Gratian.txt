					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Saints and martyrs</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saints Felinus and Gratian</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">~250 AD<br />Perugia</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">Santi Martiri Church, Arona</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">June 1; (Joint local feast day with Carpophorus and Fidelis) March 13</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Military attire</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Arona_(NO)" class="mw-redirect" title="Arona (NO)">Arona</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saints <b>Felinus and Gratian(us)</b> (sometimes <b>Gratinian(us)</b>) (d. 250 AD) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches.  They are <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Arona_(NO)" class="mw-redirect" title="Arona (NO)">Arona</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, where their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were enshrined.
</p><p>The city of Arona celebrates two groups of martyrs on March 13: Felinus and Gratian, as well as <a href="/wiki/Carpophorus,_Exanthus,_Cassius,_Severinus,_Secundus,_and_Licinius" title="Carpophorus, Exanthus, Cassius, Severinus, Secundus, and Licinius">Carpophorus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fidelis_of_Como" title="Fidelis of Como">Fidelis</a>.  The festival of <i>Tredicino</i> is celebrated on the shores of <a href="/wiki/Lago_Maggiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Lago Maggiore">Lago Maggiore</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felinus_and_Gratian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Baronius" class="mw-redirect" title="Baronius">Baronius</a> inserted the names of Felinus and Gratian names into the <i>Roman Martyrology</i>, quoting as his authority the <i>Acts</i> preserved at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>. <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a> writes, however, that "the so-called <i>Acts of SS. Gratian and Felinus</i>, used as lections in the <i>Arona Passionale</i>, are extracted from the Acts of SS. Florentinus and Companions, martyrs at Perugia commemorated the same day.  But these Acts are in their turn not genuine; they are, in fact, the Acts of SS. <a href="/wiki/Secundian,_Marcellian_and_Verian" title="Secundian, Marcellian and Verian">Secundianus</a> and Comp. (Aug. 9th)."<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_Baring-Gould_1874_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine_Baring-Gould_1874-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The cult of Gratian and Felinus, therefore, rests on a shaky historical foundation.  The garbling of lives and cults may have been purposeful, consisting of a mere alteration of the names of persons and places so as to make the <i>Acts of Secundianus</i> serve for Felinus and Gratian, and thus in the interests of Perugia.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_Baring-Gould_1874_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine_Baring-Gould_1874-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The oldest document concerning Felinus and Gratian dates from the tenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The manuscript is made up of 249 sheets in <a href="/wiki/Parchment" title="Parchment">parchment</a> and is written in <a href="/wiki/Blackletter" title="Blackletter">Gothic script</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Latin" title="Medieval Latin">medieval Latin</a>.  According to the text, Felinus and Gratian were two soldiers of <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, who were  converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by the bishop of the city. During the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>, they were martyred for their faith. Felinus and Gratian have been identified as being identical to two other martyrs: Gratilianus and Felicissima, with "Felinus and Gratian" being a simple misreading for the names of two actual saints<sup id="cite_ref-Duomo_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duomo-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carpophorus_and_Fidelis">Carpophorus and Fidelis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felinus_and_Gratian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Carpophorus and Fidelis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Legend holds that the associated pair of martyrs, Carpophorus and Fidelis, were members of the <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a>, whose relics were transferred to Arona during a time of war between Milan and <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a>.  Both Como and Arona claim the relics of Fidelis.  The martyrdom of Fidelis is considered to have occurred on the north side of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Como" title="Lake Como">Lake Como</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Samolaco" title="Samolaco">Samolaco</a>.  His relics are considered to have been carried to Como in 964.  In Arona, the presence of the relics of Fidelis and Carpophrous is attested in documents dating back to 1259 and 1321.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Goffredo da Bussero (1220–1289 ca.), discussing a group of martyrs of the Theban Legion killed in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombard</a> territory, states: <i>sed horum duo corpora ad monasterium de Arona dati sunt</i>.  This group has been identified as <a href="/wiki/Carpophorus,_Exanthus,_Cassius,_Severinus,_Secundus,_and_Licinius" title="Carpophorus, Exanthus, Cassius, Severinus, Secundus, and Licinius">Carpophorus, Exanthus, Cassius, Severinus, Secundus, and Licinius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felinus_and_Gratian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 979, the Count of Seprio, Amitto (Amizzone), captain of <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a>, transferred Felinus' and Gratian's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, with the permission of the bishop, to Arona, and built a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> dedicated to them.  Amitto's foundation has been interpreted as a form of penance, since his soldiers had set fire to the <a href="/wiki/Portico" title="Portico">portico</a> of the basilica of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a> had become abbot <i><a href="/wiki/Commendatario" class="mw-redirect" title="Commendatario">commendatario</a></i> of San Felino e San Graziano abbey in Arona on 20 November 1547.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  In 1576, Borromeo transferred the relics of Carpophorus and Fidelisto to Milan. Even though their veneration in Arona had been minimal, Borromeo's proposed transfer angered the people of the city.  On March 13, as a compromise, Borromeo brought the two left forearms of the saints back to Arona.  The city council, which had been pressured by the populace to bring back the relics, decreed that an annual festival should occur on that day. The day has increased in importance in succeeding centuries.  The purpose of the festival was extended to include the celebration for the martyrs Felinus and Gratian, thereby unifying their veneration to that of Carpophorus and Fidelis.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Relics of all four saints are conserved in the church of Santi Martiri, also known as San Graziano, in Arona.
</p><p>There is a statue of Felinus on the South transept spire of the <a href="/wiki/Milan_Cathedral" title="Milan Cathedral">Duomo di Milano</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Duomo_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duomo-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
