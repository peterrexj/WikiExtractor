					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Ukrainian Greek Catholic nun and martyr</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0_%D0%A2%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%8C%D0%BA%D1%96%D0%B2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0_%D0%A2%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%8C%D0%BA%D1%96%D0%B2.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="214" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="150" data-file-height="214" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0_%D0%A2%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%86%D1%8C%D0%BA%D1%96%D0%B2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sister Tarsykija Mazkiw at work</div></div></div>
<p><b>Tarsykiya Matskiv</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Тарсикія Мацьків</span>; 23 March 1919 &#8211; 17 July 1944) was an ethnic Ukrainian Greek Catholic <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p><p>Matskiv was born in <a href="/wiki/Khodoriv" title="Khodoriv">Khodoriv</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship" title="Lwów Voivodeship">Lwów Voivodeship</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Lviv_Oblast" title="Lviv Oblast">Lviv Oblast</a>).  She entered the Sister Servants of Mary Immaculate on 3 May 1938.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  She took her first vows on 5 November 1940, and worked in her <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-u_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-u-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> sewing clothes for the sisters and teaching others the skill.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Even before the arrival of the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Army" title="Soviet Army">Soviet Army</a> to <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, she took vows before her spiritual director, Volodymyr Kovalyk, that she would give up her life for the conversion of <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> and for the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-u_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-u-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 17 July 1944 at around 8 a.m., a Russian soldier rang the convent door. When Matskiv answered the door, expecting to see a priest who was supposed to celebrate the liturgy,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  she was shot without warning and died.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-u_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-u-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  It was later said that she was shot simply "because she was a nun".<sup id="cite_ref-b_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 June 2001.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quote">Quote</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarsykiya_Matskiv&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Quote">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>"Suddenly the bell rang. We thought it was the priest. Sister Tarsykiia opened the door, asked Sister Maria for the key to the front door and went to the main entrance. Then a shot rang out and Sister Tarsykiia fell down dead. The soldier who shot her dead did not really explain why he did it. Later they said that he said he killed her because she was a nun." - From the testimony Sister Daria Hradiuk.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
