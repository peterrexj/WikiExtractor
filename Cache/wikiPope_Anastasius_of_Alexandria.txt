<p><b>Pope Anastasius of Alexandria</b>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_Coptic_Orthodox_Popes_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Coptic Orthodox Popes of Alexandria">36th Pope of Alexandria &amp; Patriarch of the See of St. Mark</a>. During his reign, despite being barred from the city of Alexandria, he met with the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">Patriarch of Antioch</a> as they worked to arrange the unification of their two churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Anastasius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was one of the great noblemen of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>. Before he became a Pope, he was an overseer of the patriarchal court, then he became a priest for the church of Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-coptic_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coptic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Shortly afterwards, he was unanimously selected by the bishops and clergy of the Coptic Church to succeed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damian_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Damian of Alexandria">Pope Damian of Alexandria</a> as Patriarch. His biography was written by <a href="/wiki/Severus_Ibn_al-Muqaffa" class="mw-redirect" title="Severus Ibn al-Muqaffa">Severus Ibn al-Muqaffa</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/History_of_the_Patriarchs_of_Alexandria" title="History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria">History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria</a></i>. His name does not appear in the standard biographical dictionaries of the Christian church. One possible reason for this omission is the fact that, as indicated in the <i>History</i>, the influence of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>'s decisions were very strong at the time, both in Alexandria and in the court of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Not only were Coptic bishops of this time not officially recognized, they were also forbidden to enter <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>. The Coptic church in Alexandria was understandably troubled by this arrangement, which was instituted under the reign of <a href="/wiki/Tiberius_II_Constantine" title="Tiberius II Constantine">Tiberius II Constantine</a>, and his local adjutant named Belisarius. This persecution continued after the death of Tiberius under his son, <a href="/wiki/Maurice_(emperor)" title="Maurice (emperor)">Maurice</a>. After the murder of Maurice by <a href="/wiki/Phocas" title="Phocas">Phocas</a>, who had overthrown him earlier, the situation deteriorated still further. A citizen of Alexandria who adhered to the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>'s edicts, <a href="/wiki/Eulogius_of_Alexandria" title="Eulogius of Alexandria">Eulogius of Alexandria</a>, wrote a defamatory letter to Photas regarding Anastasius. Photas responded by issuing an order to his <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> in Alexandria that the important church of <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_and_Damian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cosmas and Damian">Cosmas and Damian</a>, and all its dependent churches, be turned over to Eulogius,<sup id="cite_ref-coptic_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coptic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who became the recognized Patriarch of Alexandria. Anastasius, deeply saddened by this, left for a nearby monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With the increasing difficulties his church was facing domestically, Anastasius turned more of his attention toward improving relations with other churches. This led to closer contacts with the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Antioch" title="Church of Antioch">Church of Antioch</a>. Athanasius I Gammolo, the new Patriarch of Antioch, did not share the support of the edicts of Chalcedon that his predecessor had. When Anastasius learned of Athanasius's succession there, he wrote him a letter commending him on his wisdom in retracting his predecessor's errors,<sup id="cite_ref-coptic_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coptic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and urged him to help establish closer relations, and perhaps even unity, between the churches of Alexandria and Antioch. Upon receiving the letter, Athanasius convened a council of his bishops regarding the proposal, and they decided to send a delegation to Alexandria to see if it would be possible to unite the two churches. Athanasius, with five of his bishops, set sail for Alexandria. The delegation arrived, and met with Anastasius, presumably outside of the city, as Anastasius was still barred from entering the city, possibly in the monastery in which Anastasius was staying.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the arrival of the delegation, Anastasius summoned all of his bishops and clergy. The leaders of the two churches embraced each other with a kiss of peace, and declared their two churches to be united in doctrine. The two patriarchs remained in the monastery for a month thereafter, discussing a wide variety of topics, including implementing their newfound unity. At the end of the month, Athanasius returned to Antioch.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anastasius spent the remaining years of his patriarchate attending to the affairs of his church, including its now closer relationship with Antioch. He also wrote prolifically on matters of theology. He is said to have written enough to have one book put out every year of his reign, presumably twelve books for the twelve years of his reign. Over the years, however, many of these writings have been lost.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Anastasius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anastasius is considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Church</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 18 December.<sup id="cite_ref-Holweck_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holweck-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
