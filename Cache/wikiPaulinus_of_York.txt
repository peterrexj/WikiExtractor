					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th-century missionary, Bishop of York, and saint</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the 7th century missionary and saint. For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paulinus_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint Paulinus (disambiguation)">Saint Paulinus (disambiguation)</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Paulinus (disambiguation)">Paulinus (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color: #DFB0DF;"><div style="display:inline" class="fn">Paulinus</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader role"><a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Bishop of York</a></td></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Paulinusofyork.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Stone statue of a man wearing a mitre and holding a staff topped by a cross. His other hand is held upright, palm facing out." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Paulinusofyork.jpg" decoding="async" width="171" height="457" data-file-width="171" data-file-height="457" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">Statue at <a href="/wiki/Rochester_Cathedral" title="Rochester Cathedral">Rochester Cathedral</a></div></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Appointed</th><td class="infobox-data">627</td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Term ended</th><td class="infobox-data">633</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Predecessor</th><td class="infobox-data">Founder</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Successor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Chad_of_Mercia" title="Chad of Mercia">Chad</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Orders</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Consecration</th><td class="infobox-data">21 July 625<br />by&#160;<a href="/wiki/Justus" title="Justus">Justus</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Personal details</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">10 October 644<br /><a href="/wiki/Rochester,_Kent" title="Rochester, Kent">Rochester, Kent</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Buried</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Rochester_Cathedral" title="Rochester Cathedral">Rochester Cathedral</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Sainthood</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Feast day</th><td class="infobox-data">10 October</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Paulinus</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 10 October 644) was a Roman missionary and the first <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Bishop of York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> A member of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a> sent in 601 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a> to <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianize</a> the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> from their native <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a>, Paulinus arrived in England by 604 with the second missionary group. Little is known of Paulinus's activities in the following two decades.
</p><p>After some years spent in <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>, perhaps in 625, Paulinus was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> a bishop. He accompanied <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelburg_of_Kent" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelburg of Kent">Æthelburg of Kent</a>, sister of King <a href="/wiki/Eadbald_of_Kent" title="Eadbald of Kent">Eadbald of Kent</a>, on her journey to <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> to marry King <a href="/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria" title="Edwin of Northumbria">Edwin of Northumbria</a>, and eventually succeeded in converting Edwin to Christianity. Paulinus also converted many of Edwin's subjects and built some churches. One of the women Paulinus <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptised</a> was a future saint, <a href="/wiki/Hilda_of_Whitby" title="Hilda of Whitby">Hilda of Whitby</a>. 
</p><p>Following Edwin's death in 633, Paulinus and Æthelburg fled Northumbria, leaving behind a member of Paulinus's clergy, <a href="/wiki/James_the_Deacon" title="James the Deacon">James the Deacon</a>. Paulinus returned to Kent, where he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">Bishop of Rochester</a>. He received a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> from the pope, symbolizing his appointment as Archbishop of York, but too late to be effective. After his death in 644, Paulinus was canonized as a saint and is now venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican</a> Churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paulinus was a monk from Rome sent to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kent" title="Kingdom of Kent">Kingdom of Kent</a> by Pope Gregory I in 601, along with <a href="/wiki/Mellitus" title="Mellitus">Mellitus</a> and others, as part of the second group of missionaries sent to convert the Anglo-Saxons to Christianity. He was probably an Italian by birth.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The second group of missionaries arrived in Kent by 604, but little is known of Paulinus's further activities until he went to Northumbria.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paulinus remained in Kent until 625, when he was consecrated as bishop by <a href="/wiki/Justus" title="Justus">Justus</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, on 21 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He then accompanied Æthelburg, the sister of King Eadbald of Kent, to Northumbria where she was to marry King Edwin of Northumbria. A condition of the marriage was that Edwin had promised that he would allow Æthelburg to remain a Christian and worship as she chose. <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, writing in the early 8th century, reports that Paulinus wished to convert the Northumbrians, as well as provide religious services to the new queen.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is some difficulty with Bede's chronology on the date of Æthelburh's marriage, as surviving papal letters to Edwin urging him to convert imply that Eadbald only recently had become a Christian, which conflicts with Bede's chronology. The historian D. P. Kirby argues that Paulinus and Æthelburh must therefore have gone to Northumbria earlier than 624, and that Paulinus went north, not as a bishop, but as a priest, returning later to be consecrated.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest33_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest33-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The historian <a href="/wiki/Henry_Mayr-Harting" title="Henry Mayr-Harting">Henry Mayr-Harting</a> agrees with Kirby's reasoning.<sup id="cite_ref-Coming66_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming66-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Another historian, <a href="/wiki/Peter_Hunter_Blair" title="Peter Hunter Blair">Peter Hunter Blair</a>, argues that Æthelburh and Edwin were married before 625, but that she did not go to Northumbria until 625.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest33_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest33-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> If Kirby's arguments are accepted, then the date of Paulinus's consecration needs to be changed by a year, to 21 July 626.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest206_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest206-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bede describes Paulinus as "a man tall of stature, a little stooping, with black hair and a thin face, a hooked and thin nose, his aspect both venerable and awe-inspiring".<sup id="cite_ref-Blair95_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair95-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_York">Bishop of York</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop of York">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:England_green_top.svg" class="image"><img alt="Map showing the kingdoms of Dyfed, Powys, and Gwynedd in the west central part of the island of Great Britain. <p>Bede relates that Paulinus told Edwin that the birth of his and Æthelburg's daughter at Easter 626 was because of Paulinus's prayers. The birth coincided with a foiled assassination attempt on the king by a group of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">West Saxons</a> from Wessex. Edwin promised to convert to Christianity and allow his new daughter <a href="/wiki/Eanfl%C3%A6d" title="Eanflæd">Eanflæd</a> to be baptised if he won a victory over Wessex. He did not fulfill his promise immediately after his subsequent military success against the West Saxons however, only converting after Paulinus had revealed the details of a dream the king had before he took the throne, during his exile at the court of King <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A6dwald_of_East_Anglia" title="Rædwald of East Anglia">Rædwald of East Anglia</a>. In this dream, according to Bede, a stranger told Edwin that power would be his in the future when someone laid a hand on his head. As Paulinus was revealing the dream to Edwin, he laid his hand on the king's head, which was the proof Edwin needed. A late seventh-century <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of Pope Gregory I claims that Paulinus was the stranger in the vision;<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> if true, it might suggest that Paulinus spent some time at Rædwald's court,<sup id="cite_ref-Kings28_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kings28-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> although Bede does not mention any such visit.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is unlikely that it was supernatural affairs and Paulinus's persuasion alone that caused Edwin to convert. The Northumbrian nobles seem to have been willing and the king also received letters from Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_V" title="Pope Boniface V">Boniface V</a> urging his conversion.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eventually convinced, Edwin and many of his followers were baptised at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> in 627.<sup id="cite_ref-BSE_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BSE-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> One story relates that during a stay with Edwin and Æthelburg at their palace in <a href="/wiki/Yeavering" title="Yeavering">Yeavering</a>, Paulinus spent 36&#160;days baptising new converts.<sup id="cite_ref-BSE_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BSE-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Paulinus also was an active missionary in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lindsey" title="Kingdom of Lindsey">Lindsey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ASE115_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE115-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and his missionary activities help show the limits of Edwin's royal authority.<sup id="cite_ref-Kingship17_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kingship17-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Gregory's plan had been that York would be England's second <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">see</a>, so Paulinus established his church there.<sup id="cite_ref-BSE_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BSE-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Although built of stone, no trace of it has been found.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Paulinus also built a number of churches on royal estates.<sup id="cite_ref-Conversion161_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conversion161-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His church in <a href="/wiki/Lincoln,_Lincolnshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Lincoln, Lincolnshire">Lincoln</a> has been identified with the earliest building phase of the church of St Paul in the Bail.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Among those baptised by Paulinus were Hilda, later the founding abbess of <a href="/wiki/Whitby_Abbey" title="Whitby Abbey">Whitby Abbey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Blair147_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair147-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and Hilda's successor, Eanflæd, Edwin's daughter.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair149_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair149-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> As the only Roman bishop in England, Paulinus also consecrated another Gregorian missionary, <a href="/wiki/Honorius_of_Canterbury" title="Honorius of Canterbury">Honorius</a>, as Archbishop of Canterbury after Justus' death, some time between 628 and 631.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Rochester">Bishop of Rochester</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bishop of Rochester">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Edwin was defeated by an alliance of Gwynedd Welsh and Mercian Angles, being killed at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Hatfield_Chase" title="Battle of Hatfield Chase">Battle of Hatfield Chase</a>, on a date traditionally given as 12 October 633.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> One problem with the dating of the battle is that Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I" title="Pope Honorius I">Honorius I</a> wrote in June 634 to Paulinus and Archbishop Honorius saying that he was sending a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>, the symbol of an archbishop's authority, to each of them.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest56_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest56-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The pope's letter shows no hint that news of Edwin's death had reached Rome, almost nine months after the supposed date of the battle. The historian D. P. Kirby argues that this lack of awareness makes it more likely that the battle occurred in 634.<sup id="cite_ref-Earliest56_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Earliest56-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Edwin's defeat and death caused his kingdom to fragment into at least two parts.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It also led to a sharp decline in Christianity in Northumbria<sup id="cite_ref-ASE116_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE116-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> when Edwin's immediate successors reverted to paganism.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Widowed queen Æthelburg fled to her brother <a href="/wiki/Eadbald_of_Kent" title="Eadbald of Kent">Eadbald</a>'s Kent kingdom. Paulinus went with her, along with Edwin and Æthelburg's son, daughter, and grandson. The two boys went to the continent for safety, to the court of King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>. Æthelburg, Eanflæd, and Paulinus remained in Kent, where Paulinus was offered the see, or bishopric, of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rochester" title="Diocese of Rochester">Rochester</a>, which he held until his death. Because the pallium did not reach Paulinus until after he had left York, it was of no use to him.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Paulinus's deputy, <a href="/wiki/James_the_Deacon" title="James the Deacon">James the Deacon</a>, remained in the north and struggled to rebuild the Roman mission.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE116_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE116-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paulinus died on 10 October 644 at Rochester,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook221_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook221-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> where he was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Sacristy" title="Sacristy">sacristy</a> of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair97_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair97-21">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> His successor at Rochester was <a href="/wiki/Ithamar_(bishop)" title="Ithamar (bishop)">Ithamar</a>, the first Englishman consecrated to a Gregorian missionary see.<sup id="cite_ref-Ithamar889_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ithamar889-22">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> After Paulinus's death, Paulinus was <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Historical_development_of_the_process" title="Canonization">revered</a> as a saint, with a <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> on 10 October. When a new church was constructed at Rochester in the 1080s his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, or remains, were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> (ritually moved) to a new shrine.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There also were shrines to Paulinus at Canterbury, and at least five churches were dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer418_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer418-23">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Although Rochester held some of Paulinus's relics, the promotion of his cult there appears to have occurred after the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman Conquest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Rollason_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rollason-24">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> He is considered a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OBD409_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OBD409-25">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Walsh475_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh475-26">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paulinus's missionary efforts are difficult to evaluate. Bede implies that the mission in Northumbria was successful, but there is little supporting evidence, and it is more likely that Paulinus's missionary efforts there were relatively ineffectual. Although <a href="/wiki/Osric_of_Deira" title="Osric of Deira">Osric</a>, one of Edwin's successors, was converted to Christianity by Paulinus, he returned to paganism after Edwin's death. Hilda, however, remained a Christian, and eventually went on to become abbess of the influential Whitby Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Northumbria's conversion to Christianity was mainly achieved by <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Irish missionaries</a> brought into the region by Edwin's eventual successor, <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria" title="Oswald of Northumbria">Oswald</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mayr68_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mayr68-27">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
