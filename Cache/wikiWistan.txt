					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">9th-century Mercian royal and saint</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Wistan" redirects here. For the villages in Iran, see <a href="/wiki/Wistan,_Iran_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Wistan, Iran (disambiguation)">Vistan</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Wigstan (Wystan, Wistan)</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">839</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Evesham_Abbey" title="Evesham Abbey">Evesham Abbey</a> (<a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries"><i>destroyed</i></a>)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 June</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Wigstan</b> (died c.840 AD), also known as Saint <b>Wystan</b>, was the son of <a href="/wiki/Wigmund_of_Mercia" title="Wigmund of Mercia">Wigmund of Mercia</a> and Ælfflæd, daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Ceolwulf_I_of_Mercia" title="Ceolwulf I of Mercia">Ceolwulf I of Mercia</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wigstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg/220px-Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="331" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg/330px-Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ec/Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg 2x" data-file-width="426" data-file-height="640" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saxon_crypt_at_Repton_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1089547.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The crypt in <a href="/wiki/St_Wystan%27s_Church,_Repton" title="St Wystan&#39;s Church, Repton">St Wystan's Church, Repton</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Derbyshire" title="Derbyshire">Derbyshire</a>, where Wigstan was originally buried</div></div></div>
<p>Like many Mercians of the period very little is known about Wigstan. He was the son of <a href="/wiki/Wigmund_of_Mercia" title="Wigmund of Mercia">Wigmund</a> and <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lffl%C3%A6d_of_Mercia_(II)" title="Ælfflæd of Mercia (II)">Ælfflæd</a>, both the offspring of Mercian kings, <a href="/wiki/Wiglaf" title="Wiglaf">Wiglaf</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ceolwulf_I_of_Mercia" title="Ceolwulf I of Mercia">Ceolwulf I</a> respectively. Wigmund, according to the <a href="/wiki/Croyland_Chronicle" title="Croyland Chronicle">Croyland Chronicle</a>, died of <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a> before his father King Wiglaf, making Wigstan heir to the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>. However, when Wiglaf died in 839, Wigstan declined the kingship preferring religious life and monastic orders instead. <a href="/wiki/Beorhtwulf_of_Mercia" title="Beorhtwulf of Mercia">Beorhtwulf</a>, possibly Wigstan's great-uncle, became king instead. <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a> claims that Beorhtwulf's son, Beorhtfrith, wished to marry Wigstan's widowed mother, Ælfflæd, but Wigstan forbade the union as they were too closely related.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As revenge Beorhtfrith went to visit the young King ostensibly in peace but, when the two greeted each other, he struck Wigstan on the head with the shaft of his dagger and his servant ran him through with his sword.
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Beorhtfrith, son of Beorhtwulf, king of Mercia, unjustly put to death his cousin, St Wigstan on the Kalends of June [1st June], being the eve of Pentecost. He was grandson of two of the kings of Mercia; his father, Wigmund, being the son of King Wiglaf, and his mother, Ælfflæd, the daughter of King Ceolwulf. His corpse was carried to a monastery which was famous in that age, called <a href="/wiki/Repton" title="Repton">Repton</a>, and buried in the tomb of his grandfather, King Wiglaf. Miracles from heaven were not wanting in testimony of his martyrdom; for a column of light shot up to heaven from the spot where the innocent saint was murdered, and remained visible to the inhabitants of that place for 30 days.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><i><a href="/wiki/Florence_of_Worcester" title="Florence of Worcester">Florence of Worcester</a></i></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>The site of Wigstan's martyrdom has been variously claimed to be <a href="/wiki/Wistow,_Leicestershire" title="Wistow, Leicestershire">Wistow, Leicestershire</a>, with <a href="/wiki/Wigston" title="Wigston">Wigston</a> being the nearest town which happens to be derived from his name. Wigstan became a famous saint and Repton became a centre of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> as a result, which led <a href="/wiki/Cnut_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Cnut the Great">Cnut the Great</a> to move Wigstan's relics to <a href="/wiki/Evesham" title="Evesham">Evesham</a>, where the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Vita Sancti Wistani</i></span> was written by <a href="/wiki/Dominic_of_Evesham" title="Dominic of Evesham">Dominic of Evesham</a>, a medieval prior there.<sup id="cite_ref-vale_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vale-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Writings298_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Writings298-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wigstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The saint's relics were relocated to the Abbey at Evesham.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His <i>vita</i> (meaning "life", a history recording reputed acts of sanctity) has been attributed to the Benedictine chronicler <a href="/wiki/Dominic_of_Evesham" title="Dominic of Evesham">Dominic of Evesham</a>, an early 12th-century Prior at Evesham. The edifice of the abbey (including the tomb of the four saints and many monastic buildings) were demolished during the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Noted Edwardian artist <a href="/wiki/M._E._Aldrich_Rope" title="M. E. Aldrich Rope">Margaret E.A. Rope</a> was commissioned for the windows in the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Wistanstow" title="Wistanstow">Wistanstow</a> in Shropshire dedicated to the miraculous pillar of light, leading to discovery of the earthly remains of the slain martyr.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="See_also">See also</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wigstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: See also">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Repton#Parish_church" title="Repton">Anglo-Saxon crypt tomb</a> at Repton, Derbyshire</li>
<li>British poet <a href="/wiki/W._H._Auden" title="W. H. Auden">W. H. Auden</a> was named in honor of Saint Wystan,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A family connection with both Repton School and Wistanstow church in Shropshire is noted by Auden's biographer <a href="/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter" title="Humphrey Carpenter">Humphrey Carpenter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Mick Sharp's book <i>The Way and the Light: An Illustrated Guide to the Saints and Holy Places of Britain</i> makes the case for Wistow as the likely location of St Wistan's martyrdom, attested to in the legend of the miraculous appearance of human hair on the anniversary of his death, 1 June.</li></ul>
