</tr><tr><td class="sidebar-navbar"><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1129693374"/><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1063604349">.mw-parser-output .navbar{display:inline;font-size:88%;font-weight:normal}.mw-parser-output .navbar-collapse{float:left;text-align:left}.mw-parser-output .navbar-boxtext{word-spacing:0}.mw-parser-output .navbar ul{display:inline-block;white-space:nowrap;line-height:inherit}.mw-parser-output .navbar-brackets::before{margin-right:-0.125em;content:"[ "}.mw-parser-output .navbar-brackets::after{margin-left:-0.125em;content:" ]"}.mw-parser-output .navbar li{word-spacing:-0.125em}.mw-parser-output .navbar a>span,.mw-parser-output .navbar a>abbr{text-decoration:inherit}.mw-parser-output .navbar-mini abbr{font-variant:small-caps;border-bottom:none;text-decoration:none;cursor:inherit}.mw-parser-output .navbar-ct-full{font-size:114%;margin:0 7em}.mw-parser-output .navbar-ct-mini{font-size:114%;margin:0 4em}</style><div class="navbar plainlinks hlist navbar-mini"><ul><li class="nv-view"><a href="/wiki/Template:Eastern_Orthodox_Church_sidebar" title="Template:Eastern Orthodox Church sidebar"><abbr title="View this template">v</abbr></a></li><li class="nv-talk"><a href="/wiki/Template_talk:Eastern_Orthodox_Church_sidebar" title="Template talk:Eastern Orthodox Church sidebar"><abbr title="Discuss this template">t</abbr></a></li><li class="nv-edit"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Template:Eastern_Orthodox_Church_sidebar&amp;action=edit"><abbr title="Edit this template">e</abbr></a></li></ul></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Gregory Palamas</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Γρηγόριος Παλαμᾶς</span>; c. 1296 – 1359<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Cleric" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleric">cleric</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Late_Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Byzantine">late Byzantine</a> period. A <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monk</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>) and later <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thessaloniki" title="Thessaloniki">Thessaloniki</a>, he is famous for his defense of <a href="/wiki/Hesychast" class="mw-redirect" title="Hesychast">hesychast</a> spirituality, the uncreated character of the light of the Transfiguration, and the <a href="/wiki/Essence%E2%80%93energies_distinction" title="Essence–energies distinction">distinction between God's essence and energies</a> (i.e., the divine will, divine grace, etc.). His teaching unfolded over the course of three major controversies, (1) with the Italo-Greek <a href="/wiki/Barlaam_of_Seminara" title="Barlaam of Seminara">Barlaam</a> between 1336 and 1341, (2) with the monk <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Akindynos" title="Gregory Akindynos">Gregory Akindynos</a> between 1341 and 1347, and (3) with the philosopher <a href="/wiki/Nicephorus_Gregoras" title="Nicephorus Gregoras">Gregoras</a>, from 1348 to 1355. His theological contributions are sometimes referred to as <a href="/wiki/Palamism" title="Palamism">Palamism</a>, and his followers as Palamites.
</p><p>Gregory has been venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> since 1368. Within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, he has also been called a saint; <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> repeatedly called Gregory a great theological writer.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Since 1971, the <a href="/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Melkite Greek Catholic Church">Melkite Greek Catholic Church</a> has venerated Gregory as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Some of his writings are collected in the <i><a href="/wiki/Philokalia" title="Philokalia">Philokalia</a></i>, and since the Ottoman period, the second Sunday of <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a> is dedicated to the memory of Gregory Palamas in the Orthodox Church and <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church">Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Byzantine Synodikon of Orthodoxy also celebrates his memory and theology while condemning his opponents, including some anti-Palamites who flourished after Gregory's death.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gregory was born in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> around the year 1296. His father, Constantine, was a courtier of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Andronikos_II_Palaiologos" title="Andronikos II Palaiologos">Andronikos II Palaiologos</a> (1282–1328), but died when Gregory was still young. The Emperor himself took part in the raising and education of the fatherless boy and hoped that the gifted Gregory would devote himself to government service, but Palamas chose monastic life on Mt. Athos. Gregory's mother (Kalloni) and siblings (Theodosios, Makarios, Epicharis, and Theodoti) would also embrace monasticism, and the entire family was canonized by the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> in 2009. 
</p><p>Before leaving for Mt. Athos, Gregory received a broad education at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Constantinople" title="University of Constantinople">University of Constantinople</a>, including the study of Aristotle, which he would display before <a href="/wiki/Theodore_Metochites" title="Theodore Metochites">Theodore Metochites</a> and the Emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Despite the Emperor's ambitions for him, Gregory, then barely twenty years old, withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> in the year 1316 and became a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> there in the <a href="/wiki/Vatopedi_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatopedi monastery">Vatopedi monastery</a> under the guidance of the monastic <a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets">Elder</a> St Nicodemos of Vatopedi. Eventually, he was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, and continued his life of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>. After the demise of the Elder Nicodemus, Gregory spent eight years of spiritual struggle under the guidance of a new Elder, Nicephorus. After this last Elder's repose, Gregory transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Great_Lavra" title="Great Lavra">Great Lavra</a> of St. <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_the_Athonite" title="Athanasius the Athonite">Athanasius the Athonite</a> on Mount Athos, where he served the brethren in the <a href="/wiki/Refectory" title="Refectory">trapeza</a> (refectory) and in church as a <a href="/wiki/Cantor_(church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cantor (church)">cantor</a>. Wishing to devote himself more fully to prayer and asceticism he entered a <a href="/wiki/Skete" title="Skete">skete</a> called <i>Glossia</i>, where he taught the ancient practice of mental prayer known as "prayer of the heart" or <a href="/wiki/Hesychasm" title="Hesychasm">hesychasm</a>.
</p><p>In 1326, because of the threat of Turkish invasions, he and the brethren retreated to the defended city of <a href="/wiki/Thessaloniki" title="Thessaloniki">Thessaloniki</a>, where he was then <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. Dividing his time between his ministry to the people and his pursuit of spiritual perfection, he founded a small community of <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a> near Thessaloniki in a place called <a href="/wiki/Veria" title="Veria">Veria</a>.
</p><p>He served for a short time as Abbot of the Esphigmenou Monastery but was forced to resign in 1335 due to discontentment regarding the austerity of his monastic administration.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hesychast_controversy">Hesychast controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hesychast controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main articles: <a href="/wiki/Hesychasm" title="Hesychasm">Hesychasm</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palamism" title="Palamism">Palamism</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Hesychast_controversy" title="Hesychast controversy">Hesychast controversy</a></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Theosis_(Eastern_Orthodox_theology)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theosis (Eastern Orthodox theology)">Theosis (Eastern Orthodox theology)</a></div>
<p>Hesychasm attracted the attention of <a href="/wiki/Barlaam_of_Calabria" class="mw-redirect" title="Barlaam of Calabria">Barlaam</a>, a man who either converted to Orthodoxy or was baptized Orthodox<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> who encountered Hesychasts and heard descriptions of their practices during a visit to <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>; he had also read the writings of Palamas, himself an Athonite monk. Trained in Western <a href="/wiki/Scholasticism" title="Scholasticism">Scholastic</a> theology, Barlaam was scandalized by hesychasm and began to combat it both orally and in his writings. As a private teacher of theology in the Western Scholastic mode, Barlaam propounded a more intellectual and propositional approach to the knowledge of God than the hesychasts taught.
</p><p>On the hesychast side, the controversy was taken up by Palamas who was asked by his fellow monks on Mt Athos to defend hesychasm from the attacks of Barlaam. Palamas was well-educated in Greek philosophy. Gregory wrote a number of works in its defense and defended hesychasm at six different synods in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> ultimately triumphing over its attackers in the synod of 1351.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_conflict_between_Barlaam_and_Palamas">Early conflict between Barlaam and Palamas</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early conflict between Barlaam and Palamas">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although Barlaam came from southern Italy, his ancestry was Greek and he claimed Eastern Orthodoxy as his Christian faith. Arriving in Constantinople around 1330, Barlaam was working on commentaries on <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Dionysius_the_Areopagite" title="Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite">Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite</a> under the patronage of <a href="/wiki/John_VI_Kantakouzenos" title="John VI Kantakouzenos">John VI Kantakouzenos</a>. Around 1336, Gregory received copies of treatises written by Barlaam against the Latins, condemning their insertion of the <a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">Filioque</a> into the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a>. Although this condemnation was solid Orthodox theology, Palamas took issue with Barlaam's argument in support of it, namely that efforts at demonstrating the nature of God (specifically, the nature of the Holy Spirit) should be abandoned, because God is ultimately unknowable and undemonstrable to humans. Thus, Barlaam asserted that it was impossible to determine from whom the Holy Spirit proceeds. According to Sara J. Denning-Bolle, Palamas viewed Barlaam's argument as "dangerously agnostic". In his response titled "Apodictic Treatises", Palamas insisted that it was indeed demonstrable that the Holy Spirit proceeded from the Father but not from the Son.<sup id="cite_ref-Denning-Bolle_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denning-Bolle-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> A series of letters ensued between the two but they were unable to resolve their differences amicably.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Triads">Triads</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Triads">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In response to Barlaam's attacks, Palamas wrote nine treatises entitled "Triads For The Defense of Those Who Practice Sacred Quietude". The treatises are called "triads" because they were organized as three sets of three treatises.
</p><p>The Triads were written in three stages. The first triad was written in the second half of the 1330s and are based on personal discussions between Palamas and Barlaam although Barlaam is never mentioned by name.<sup id="cite_ref-Denning-Bolle_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denning-Bolle-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory's teaching was affirmed by the superiors and principal monks of Mt. Athos, who met in synod during 1340–1. In early 1341, the monastic communities of Mount Athos wrote the Hagioritic Tome under the supervision and inspiration of Palamas. Although the tome does not mention Barlaam by name, the work clearly takes aim at Barlaam's views. The tome provides a systematic presentation of Palamas' teaching and became the fundamental textbook for Byzantine mysticism.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In response, Barlaam drafted "Against the Messalians", which attacked Gregory by name for the first time.<sup id="cite_ref-HistoricalOverview_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HistoricalOverview-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
Barlaam derisively called the hesychasts <i>omphalopsychoi</i> (men with their souls in their navels) and accused them of the heresy of <a href="/wiki/Messalianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Messalianism">Messalianism</a>, also known as <a href="/wiki/Bogomilism" title="Bogomilism">Bogomilism</a> in the East.<sup id="cite_ref-Denning-Bolle_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denning-Bolle-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> According to Meyendorff, Barlaam viewed "any claim of real and conscious experience of God as Messalianism".<sup id="cite_ref-Messalianism_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Messalianism-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barlaam also took exception to the doctrine held by the hesychasts as to the uncreated nature of the light, the experience of which was said to be the goal of hesychast practice, regarding it as <a href="/wiki/Christian_heresy" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian heresy">heretical</a> and <a href="/wiki/Blasphemy" title="Blasphemy">blasphemous</a>. It was maintained by the hesychasts to be of divine origin and to be identical to <a href="/wiki/Tabor_Light" title="Tabor Light">the light</a> which had been manifested to Jesus' disciples on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Tabor,_Israel" class="mw-redirect" title="Mount Tabor, Israel">Mount Tabor</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Transfiguration_of_Jesus" title="Transfiguration of Jesus">Transfiguration</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  Barlaam viewed this doctrine of "uncreated light" to be <a href="/wiki/Polytheism" title="Polytheism">polytheistic</a> because as it postulated two eternal substances, a visible and an invisible God. Barlaam accuses the use of the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Prayer" title="Jesus Prayer">Jesus Prayer</a> as being a practice of <a href="/wiki/Bogomilism" title="Bogomilism">Bogomilism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The second triad quotes some of Barlaam's writings directly.  In response to this second triad, Barlaam composed the treatise "Against the Messalians" linking the hesychasts to the <a href="/wiki/Messalians" class="mw-redirect" title="Messalians">Messalians</a> and thereby accusing them of heresy.
</p><p>In the third Triad, Palamas refuted Barlaam's charge of Messalianism by demonstrating that the hesychasts did not share the antisacramentalism of the Messalians nor did they claim to physically see the essence of God with their eyes.<sup id="cite_ref-Messalianism_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Messalianism-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  According to Fr. John Meyendorff "Gregory Palamas orients his entire polemic against Barlaam the Calabrian on the issue of the Hellenic wisdom which he considers to be the main source of Barlaam's errors."<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Role_in_the_Byzantine_civil_war">Role in the Byzantine civil war</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Role in the Byzantine civil war">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_civil_war_of_1341%E2%80%931347" title="Byzantine civil war of 1341–1347">civil war</a> between the supporters of <a href="/wiki/John_VI_Kantakouzenos" title="John VI Kantakouzenos">John VI Kantakouzenos</a> and the regents for <a href="/wiki/John_V_Palaeologus" class="mw-redirect" title="John V Palaeologus">John V Palaeologus</a> was not primarily a religious conflict, the theological dispute between the supporters and opponents of Palamas did play a role in the conflict.  Steven Runciman points out that "while the theological dispute embittered the conflict, the religious and political parties did not coincide."<sup id="cite_ref-Runciman_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Runciman-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
The aristocrats supported Palamas largely due to their conservative and anti-Western tendencies as well as their links to the staunchly <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Orthodox</a> monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Although several significant exceptions leave the issue open to question, in the popular mind (and traditional historiography), the supporters of "Palamism" and of "Kantakouzenism" are usually equated.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Thus, the eventual triumph of Kantakouzenos in 1347 also brought with it the conclusive triumph of the Palamists over the anti-Palamists.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fifth_Council_of_Constantinople">Fifth Council of Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Fifth Council of Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox biography vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above" style="font-size:125%;"><div <p>It became clear that the dispute between Barlaam and Palamas was irreconcilable and would require the judgment of an episcopal council. A series of six patriarchal councils were held in Constantinople on 10 June 1341, August 1341, 4 November 1344, 1 February 1347, 8 February 1347, and 28 May 1351  to consider the issues.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Collectively, these councils are accepted as having ecumenical status by <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Orthodox Christians</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> some of whom call them the <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Council_of_Constantinople" title="Fifth Council of Constantinople">Fifth Council of Constantinople</a> and the Ninth Ecumenical Council.
</p><p>The dispute over hesychasm came before a synod held at Constantinople in May 1341 and presided over by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Andronikos_III_Palaiologos" title="Andronikos III Palaiologos">Andronicus III</a>.  The assembly, influenced by the veneration in which the writings of <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Dionysius" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudo-Dionysius">Pseudo-Dionysius</a> were held in the Eastern Church, condemned Barlaam, who <a href="/wiki/Recant" class="mw-redirect" title="Recant">recanted</a>.  The ecumenical patriarch insisted that all of Barlaam's writings be destroyed and thus no complete copies of Barlaam's treatise "Against Messalianism" have survived.<sup id="cite_ref-Denning-Bolle_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denning-Bolle-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barlaam's primary supporter Emperor Andronicus III died just five days after the synod ended. Although Barlaam initially hoped for a second chance to present his case against Palamas, he soon realised the futility of pursuing his cause, and left for Calabria where he converted to the Roman Church and was appointed Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Locri-Gerace" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Locri-Gerace">Gerace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HistoricalOverview_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HistoricalOverview-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Barlaam's departure, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Akindynos" title="Gregory Akindynos">Gregory Akindynos</a> became the chief critic of Palamas. A second council held in Constantinople in August 1341 condemned Akindynos and affirmed the findings of the earlier council. Akindynos and his supporters gained a brief victory at a council held in 1344 which excommunicated Palamas.  
However, the last of these councils, held in May 1351, conclusively exonerated Palamas and condemned his opponents.<sup id="cite_ref-HistoricalOverview_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HistoricalOverview-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  This synod ordered that the Metropolitans of Ephesus and Ganos be defrocked and jailed. All those who were unwilling to submit to the orthodox view were to be excommunicated and kept under surveillance at their residences. A series of anathemas were pronounced against Barlaam, Akindynos and their followers; at the same time, a series of acclamations were also declared in favor of Gregory Palamas and the adherents of his doctrine.<sup id="cite_ref-Jugie4_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jugie4-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> One notable opponent of Palamism was <a href="/wiki/Nicephorus_Gregoras" title="Nicephorus Gregoras">Nicephorus Gregoras</a> who refused to submit to the dictates of the synod and was effectively imprisoned in a monastery for two years.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Gradual_acceptance_of_the_Palamist_doctrine">Gradual acceptance of the Palamist doctrine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Gradual acceptance of the Palamist doctrine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg/250px-Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="167" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg/375px-Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg/500px-Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2885" data-file-height="1926" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Agios_Grig%C3%B2rios_Palam%C3%A0s.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Gregory_Palamas" title="Church of Saint Gregory Palamas">Church of Saint Gregory Palamas</a> housing his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Thessaloniki" title="Thessaloniki">Thessaloniki</a> (architect: <a href="/wiki/Ernst_Ziller" title="Ernst Ziller">Ernst Ziller</a>)</div></div></div>
<p>Kallistos I and the ecumenical patriarchs who succeeded him mounted a vigorous campaign to have the Palamist doctrines accepted by the other Eastern patriarchates as well as all the metropolitan sees under their jurisdiction. However, it took some time to overcome initial resistance to his teachings.  For example, the Metropolitan of Kiev, upon receiving tomes from Kallistos that expounded the Palamist doctrine, rejected it vehemently and composed a reply in refutation. Similarly, the Patriarchate of Antioch remained steadfastly opposed to what they viewed as an innovation; however, by the end of the fourteenth century, Palamism had become accepted there.  Similar acts of resistance were seen in the metropolitan sees that were governed by the Latins as well as in some autonomous ecclesiastical regions, such as the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Cyprus" title="Church of Cyprus">Church of Cyprus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Jugie6_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jugie6-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One notable example of the campaign to enforce the orthodoxy of the Palamist doctrine was the action taken by Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Philotheus_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Philotheus I of Constantinople">Philotheos I</a> to crack down on <a href="/wiki/Prochoros_Kydones" title="Prochoros Kydones">Prochoros Kydones</a>, a monk and priest at Mount Athos who was opposed to the Palamites. Kydones had written a number of anti-Palamist treatises and continued to argue forcefully against Palamism even when brought before the patriarch and enjoined to adhere to the orthodox doctrine. Finally, in exasperation, Philotheos convened a synod against Kydones in April 1368.  However, even this extreme measure failed to effect the submission of Kydones and in the end, he was excommunicated and suspended from the clergy in perpetuity. The long tome that was prepared for the synod concludes with a decree canonizing Palamas who had died in 1357/59.<sup id="cite_ref-Jugie5_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jugie5-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite the initial opposition of some patriarchates and sees, over time the resistance dwindled away and ultimately Palamist doctrine became accepted throughout the Eastern Orthodox Church. During this period, it became the norm for ecumenical patriarchs to profess the Palamite doctrine upon taking possession of their see.<sup id="cite_ref-Jugie6_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jugie6-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Martin Jugie states that the opposition of the Latins and the Latinophrones, who were necessarily hostile to the doctrine, actually contributed to its adoption, and soon Latinism and Antipalamism became equivalent in the minds of many Orthodox Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-Jugie6_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jugie6-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Aristeides Papadakis, "all Orthodox scholars who have written on Palamas — Lossky, Krivosheine, Papamichael, Meyendorff, Christou — assume his voice to be a legitimate expression of Orthodox tradition."<sup id="cite_ref-Papadakis_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Papadakis-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg/220px-%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg/330px-%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg/440px-%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B7_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%89%D0%B0%D0%BC_%D1%81%D0%B2._%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D1%8F_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The reliquary of St. Gregory Palamas</div></div></div>
<p>Palamas's opponents in the hesychast controversy spread slanderous accusations against him, and in 1344 Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_John_XIV_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch John XIV of Constantinople">John XIV</a> imprisoned him for four years. However, in 1347 when Patriarch Isidore came to the Ecumenical Throne, Gregory was released from prison and <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Thessalonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Thessalonica">Metropolitan of Thessalonica</a>. However, since the conflict with Barlaam had not been settled at that point, the people of Thessalonica did not accept him, and he was forced to live in a number of places.  It was not until 1350 that he was able to occupy the episcopal chair.<sup id="cite_ref-Runciman_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Runciman-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
In 1354, during a voyage to Constantinople, the ship he was in fell into the hands of Turkish pirates; he was imprisoned and beaten. He was obliged to spend a year in detention at the Ottoman court where he was well treated.<sup id="cite_ref-Runciman_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Runciman-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  Eventually his ransom was paid and he returned to Thessaloniki, where he served as archbishop for the last three years of his life.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_canonization">Death and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Death and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Palamas died in 1357/59. His dying words were, "To the heights! To the heights!"  He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the Eastern Orthodox Church in 1368 by Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Philotheus_Kokkinos" class="mw-redirect" title="Philotheus Kokkinos">Philotheos of Constantinople</a>, who also wrote his <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a></i> and composed the service which is chanted in his honour. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated twice a year, on November 14, the anniversary of his death, and on the Second Sunday of <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a>, because Gregory's victory over Barlaam is seen as a continuation of the <a href="/wiki/Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Triumph of Orthodoxy">Triumph of Orthodoxy</a>, i.e., the victory of the Church over <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>, celebrated the previous Sunday.
</p><p>Gregory's relics rest in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Gregory_Palamas" title="Church of Saint Gregory Palamas">Church of Saint Gregory Palamas</a> in Thessaloniki.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hymns">Hymns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_Palamas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Hymns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Troparion (Tone 8)
</p>
<dl><dd>O light of Orthodoxy, teacher of the Church, its confirmation,</dd>
<dd>O ideal of monks and invincible champion of theologians,</dd>
<dd>O wonder-working Gregory, glory of Thessaloniki and preacher of grace,</dd>
<dd>always intercede before the Lord that our souls may be saved.</dd></dl>
<p>Kontakion (Tone 8)
</p>
<dl><dd>Holy and divine instrument of wisdom,</dd>
<dd>joyful trumpet of theology,</dd>
<dd>together we sing your praises, O God-inspired Gregory.</dd>
<dd>Since you now stand before the Original Mind, guide our minds to Him, O Father,</dd>
<dd>so that we may sing to you: "Rejoice, preacher of grace."</dd></dl>

<li id="cite_note-8"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-8">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The Ninth Ecumenical Council of 1341 condemned the Platonic mysticism of Barlaam the Calabrian who had come from the West as a convert to Orthodoxy. Of course the rejection of Platonic type of mysticism was traditional practice for the Fathers. But what the Fathers of this Council were completely shocked at was Barlaam's claim that God reveals His will by bringing into existence creatures to be seen and heard and which He passes back into non existence after His revelation has been received. One of these supposed creatures was the Angel of The Lord Himself Who appeared to Moses in the burning bush. For the Fathers of the Ecumenical Councils this Angel is the uncreated Logos Himself. (See <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.romanity.org/htm/rom.00.en.some_underlying_positions_of_this_website.htm">John S. Romanides, <i>Underlying Positions of This Website</i></a>.)</span>
