<p><b>Jeanne Delanoue</b> (<b>Joan of the Cross</b>) (18 June 1666 – 17 August 1736) was the founder of the Congregation of St. Anne of Providence, and is a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jeanne_Delanoue&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jeanne_Delanoue&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Delanoue was born in <a href="/wiki/Saumur" title="Saumur">Saumur</a>, located in the region of <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="Province of Anjou">Anjoú</a>, France, the youngest of twelve children. Her parents had a shop not far from the shrine of Notre-Dame-des-Ardilliers. Her father was a draper, who died when she was six years old. She helped her mother run the shop selling religious goods, catering to pilgrims to the shrine. She took over the business at the age of 25 when her mother died. She also provided accommodations to pilgrims who were visiting the shrine. A skillful businesswoman, she kept the store open even on Sundays and holydays,<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> which was considered somewhat scandalous in 17th century France. Her early life was characterized as one of self-centeredness, pride and avarice.<sup id="cite_ref-radio_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-radio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion">Conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jeanne_Delanoue&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> 1693, Delanoue encountered Francoise Fouchet, a poor widowed pilgrim from Rennes, who predicted that Jeanne would one day spend her life caring for the poor. She greeted this prediction with some skepticism; but as time passed, caring for the less fortunate began to take up more of her time. She began by visiting the poor in her neighborhood. Eventually she closed the family business to commit herself more fully to this work. Her house, near the Loire, had caves and cellars similar to those of wine merchants, in which she provided shelter for orphans, the sick, the aged, and the indigent. Delanoue gave particular attention especially to abandoned single mothers and prostitutes.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A landslide during the earthquake in 1703 destroyed her home, but undeterred, she found another. Many of the poor could only find shelter in the caves carved out of the <a href="/wiki/Tuff" title="Tuff">tuff</a> along the Loire. She made them as comfortable as she could.<sup id="cite_ref-radio_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-radio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1704, she was joined by a few other young women who shared her vision of helping the needy. Thus was founded the Sisters of Saint Anne of Providence of Saumur. Her work was endorsed by noted preacher <a href="/wiki/Louis_de_Montfort" title="Louis de Montfort">Louis de Montfort</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-angers_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-angers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Delanoue founded Providence House, which during the famine of 1709, cared for about 100 people.<sup id="cite_ref-Trochu_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trochu-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
That same year, <a href="/wiki/Michel_Poncet_de_La_Rivi%C3%A8re" title="Michel Poncet de La Rivière">Michel Poncet de La Rivière</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Angers" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Angers">Bishop of Angers</a> approved the constitutions of the small congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  She took the name in religion of Joan of the Cross.
</p><p>In 1715, she established Saumur's first home for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Worn out by her labors, Delanoue died on 17 August 1736 at Fencet, France, of natural causes.  At the time of her death there were twelve communities of her sisters, spread throughout France, serving the poor and needy.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"The poor and the Lord are all one." -Jeanne Delanoue/Joan of the Cross<sup id="cite_ref-angers_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-angers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jeanne_Delanoue&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jeanne Delanoue was beatified on November 8, 1947 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>, and canonized in October 1982 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-radio_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-radio-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is August 17.<sup id="cite_ref-Trochu_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trochu-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"In Saumur, in 1736, Saint Jeanne Delanoue, virgin. With total confidence in the Providence of God, she first welcomed home orphans, old women, the sick, and lost women, then, with companions, she founded the Institute of the Sisters of Saint Anne-of-Providence." --<i>Roman martyrology</i><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The congregation Jeanne founded was renamed the <b>Congregation of the Sisters of St. Anne of Providence</b> on 3 December 1964.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As of 2021, over 400 members serve the poor in France, <a href="/wiki/Madagascar" title="Madagascar">Madagascar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sumatra" title="Sumatra">Sumatra</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Trochu_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trochu-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><i>La Nef ardente</i> is an oratorio by Michel Bosc about Jeanne Delanoue.
</p>
