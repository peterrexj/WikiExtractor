<p><b>Pope Clement IV</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Clemens IV</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 23 November 1190 – 29 November 1268), born <b>Gui Foucois</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Guido Falcodius</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Guy de Foulques</i> or <i><span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Guy Foulques</i></span></i>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and also known as <b>Guy le Gros</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> for "Guy the Fat"; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Guido il Grosso</i>), was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Puy" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Puy">bishop of Le Puy</a> (1257–1260), <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Narbonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Narbonne">archbishop of Narbonne</a> (1259–1261), <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_of_Sabina" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal of Sabina">cardinal of Sabina</a> (1261–1265), and head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> from 5 February 1265 until his death. <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1264%E2%80%931265" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1264–1265">His election as pope</a> occurred at a conclave held at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> that lasted four months while cardinals argued over whether to call in <a href="/wiki/Charles_I_of_Anjou" title="Charles I of Anjou">Charles I of Anjou</a>, the youngest brother of <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a>, to carry on the papal war against the <a href="/wiki/Hohenstaufen" title="Hohenstaufen">Hohenstaufens</a>. Pope Clement was a patron of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a> and of <a href="/wiki/Roger_Bacon" title="Roger Bacon">Roger Bacon</a>, encouraging Bacon in the writing of his <i><a href="/wiki/Opus_Majus" title="Opus Majus">Opus Majus</a></i>, which included important treatises on <a href="/wiki/Optics" title="Optics">optics</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Scientific_method" title="Scientific method">scientific method</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clement was born in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Gilles-du-Gard" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Gilles-du-Gard">Saint-Gilles-du-Gard</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Languedoc" title="Languedoc">Languedoc</a> region of <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, to a successful lawyer, Pierre Foucois, and his wife Marguerite Ruffi. At the age of nineteen, he enrolled as a soldier to fight the Moors in Spain. He then pursued the study of law in Toulouse, Bourges and Orleans, becoming a noted advocate in Paris. In the latter capacity he acted as secretary to <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">King Louis IX</a>, to whose influence he was chiefly indebted for his elevation to the cardinalate. He married the daughter of Simon de Malbois and had two daughters. Upon the death of his wife, he followed his father's example and gave up secular life for the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-loughlin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His rise was rapid. Ordained in the abbey of Saint-Magloire, Paris, he became pastor of Saint-Gilles in 1255. In 1257, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Puy" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Puy">Bishop of Le Puy</a>; in 1259, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Narbonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Narbonne">Archbishop of Narbonne</a>; and in December 1261, he became the first cardinal created by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_IV" title="Pope Urban IV">Pope Urban IV</a>, for the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Sabina" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Sabina">See of Sabina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was the <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> in England between 1262 and 1264.<sup id="cite_ref-loughlin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was named a cardinal (<a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Penitentiary" title="Apostolic Penitentiary">grand penitentiary</a>) in 1263.<sup id="cite_ref-Miranda_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miranda-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In this period, the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Rome">See of Rome</a> was engaged in a conflict with <a href="/wiki/Manfred,_King_of_Sicily" title="Manfred, King of Sicily">Manfred, King of Sicily</a>, the illegitimate son and designated heir of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II of Hohenstaufen</a>, but whom papal loyalists, the <a href="/wiki/Guelf" class="mw-redirect" title="Guelf">Guelfs</a>, called "the usurper of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>". Clement IV, who was in France at the time of his <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1264%E2%80%9365" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1264–65">election</a>, was compelled to enter <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> in disguise. He immediately took steps to ally himself with Charles of Anjou, his erstwhile patron's brother and the impecunious French claimant to the Neapolitan throne. Charles was willing to recognize the Pope as his <a href="/wiki/Feudalism" title="Feudalism">feudal overlord</a> (a bone of contention with the Hohenstaufens) and was crowned by cardinals in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where Clement IV, permanently established at <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, dared not venture, since the anti-papal <a href="/wiki/Ghibelline" class="mw-redirect" title="Ghibelline">Ghibelline</a> party was so firmly in control there.<sup id="cite_ref-loughlin_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then, fortified with papal money and supplies, Charles marched into Naples. Having defeated and slain Manfred in the great <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Benevento" title="Battle of Benevento">Battle of Benevento</a>, Charles established himself firmly in the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> at the conclusive <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tagliacozzo" title="Battle of Tagliacozzo">Battle of Tagliacozzo</a>, in which <a href="/wiki/Conradin" title="Conradin">Conradin</a>, the last of the house of <a href="/wiki/Hohenstaufen" title="Hohenstaufen">Hohenstaufen</a>, was taken prisoner. Clement IV is said to have disapproved of the cruelties committed by his protégé, but the statement by <a href="/wiki/Gregorovius" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorovius">Gregorovius</a> that Clement IV became an accomplice by refusing to intercede for the unfortunate Conradin whom Charles had beheaded in the marketplace of Naples seems contentious. However, Gregorovius may be basing this conclusion on the position of Urban IV's predecessors, Innocent IV and Alexander IV, who were Conradin's official guardians.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Acts">Acts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Acts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.,_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg/220px-Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="320" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg/330px-Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg/440px-Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.%2C_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1209" data-file-height="1761" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Clemens_%E2%80%93_Registres_de_Cl%C3%A9ment_4.,_1893-1945_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13794071.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Collection of writings by Clemens, published in Paris between 1893 and 1945<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></div></div></div>
<p>In 1264, Clement IV renewed the prohibition of the <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">Talmud</a> promulgated by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a>, who had it publicly burnt in France and in Italy. Though Clement did not condemn to death at the stake those who harboured copies of it,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and, responding to a denunciation of the Talmud by <a href="/wiki/Pablo_Christiani" title="Pablo Christiani">Pablo Christiani</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> he ordered that the Jews of <a href="/wiki/Aragon" title="Aragon">Aragon</a> submit their books to Dominican censors for expurgation.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In February 1265 Clement summoned <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a> to Rome to serve as papal theologian.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  It was during this period that Aquinas also served as <a href="/wiki/Regent_master" title="Regent master">regent master</a> for the Dominicans at Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  With the arrival of Aquinas the existing <i>studium conventuale</i> at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a>, which had been founded in 1222, was transformed into the Order's first <i>studium provinciale</i> featuring the study of philosophy (<i>studia philosophiae</i>) as prescribed by Aquinas and others at the chapter of <a href="/wiki/Valenciennes" title="Valenciennes">Valenciennes</a> in 1259, an intermediate school between the <i>studium conventuale</i> and the <i>studium generale</i>.  This <i>studium</i> was the forerunner of the 16th century College of Saint Thomas at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_sopra_Minerva" title="Santa Maria sopra Minerva">Santa Maria sopra Minerva</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas" title="Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas">Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas, <i>Angelicum</i></a>. In 1266, after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Benevento" title="Battle of Benevento">Battle of Benevento</a>, Pope Clement IV conceded for gratitude his coat of arms to the <a href="/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines" title="Guelphs and Ghibellines">Guelph Party</a> of <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> as official approval to their supremacy and therefore they could take power in many of the other northern Italian cities. In 1267–68 Clement engaged in correspondence with the Mongol <a href="/wiki/Ilkhanate" title="Ilkhanate">Ilkhanate</a> rule <a href="/wiki/Abaqa" class="mw-redirect" title="Abaqa">Abaqa</a>. The latter proposed a <a href="/wiki/Franco-Mongol_alliance" title="Franco-Mongol alliance">Franco-Mongol alliance</a> between his forces, those of the West, and the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Michael_VIII_Palaeologos" class="mw-redirect" title="Michael VIII Palaeologos">Michael VIII Palaeologos</a> (Abaqa's father-in-law). Pope Clement welcomed Abaqa's proposal in a non-committal manner, but did inform him of an upcoming Crusade. In 1267, Pope Clement IV and King <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a> sent an ambassador to the Mongol ruler Abaqa in the person of <a href="/wiki/Jayme_Alaric_de_Perpignan" title="Jayme Alaric de Perpignan">Jayme Alaric de Perpignan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In his 1267 letter written from Viterbo, the Pope wrote:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The kings of France and <a href="/wiki/Navarre" title="Navarre">Navarre</a>, taking to heart the situation in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and decorated with the <a href="/wiki/Christian_cross" title="Christian cross">Holy Cross</a>, are readying themselves to attack the enemies of the Cross. You wrote to us that you wished to join your father-in-law (the Greek emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_VIII_Palaiologos" title="Michael VIII Palaiologos">Michael VIII Palaiologos</a>) to assist the Latins. We abundantly praise you for this, but we cannot tell you yet, before having asked to the rulers, what road they are planning to follow. We will transmit to them your advice, so as to enlighten their deliberations, and will inform your Magnificence, through a secure message, of what will have been decided.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Although Clement's successors continued to engage in diplomatic contacts with the Mongols for the rest of the century, they were never able to coordinate an actual alliance.<sup id="cite_ref-atwood-583_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atwood-583-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Within months Clement IV was dead as well, and was buried at the Dominican convent, Santa Maria in Gradi, just outside <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, where he resided throughout his pontificate.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In 1885, his remains were transferred to the church, <a href="/wiki/San_Francesco_alla_Rocca" class="mw-redirect" title="San Francesco alla Rocca">San Francesco alla Rocca</a>, in Viterbo.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Owing to irreconcilable divisions among the cardinals, the papal throne <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1268%E2%80%931271" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1268–1271">remained vacant for nearly three years</a>.
</p><p>Clement IV's private character was praised by contemporaries for his asceticism, and he is especially commended for his indisposition to promote and enrich his own relatives. He also ordered the Franciscan scholar <a href="/wiki/Roger_Bacon" title="Roger Bacon">Roger Bacon</a> to write the <i><a href="/wiki/Opus_Majus" title="Opus Majus">Opus Majus</a></i>, which is addressed to him.
</p>
