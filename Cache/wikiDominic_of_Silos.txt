<p><b>Dominic of Silos, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">O.S.B.</a></b>, (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Santo Domingo de Silos</i>) (1000 – 20 December 1073) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, to whom the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Santo_Domingo_de_Silos" title="Abbey of Santo Domingo de Silos">Abbey of Santo Domingo de Silos</a>, where he served as the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, is dedicated. He is revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. His feast day is 20 December.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_of_Silos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Ca%C3%B1as,_La_Rioja" title="Cañas, La Rioja">Cañas, La Rioja</a>, to a family of peasants, he worked as a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd" title="Shepherd">shepherd</a> before becoming a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Monasteries_of_San_Mill%C3%A1n_de_la_Cogolla" title="Monasteries of San Millán de la Cogolla">Monasteries of San Millán de la Cogolla</a>. He was ordained a priest and soon became <a href="/wiki/Novice_master" title="Novice master">master of novices</a> and then <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a>, before being driven out with two of his fellow monks by King <a href="/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_III_of_Navarre" class="mw-redirect" title="García Sánchez III of Navarre">García Sánchez III of Navarre</a>, for opposing his intention to annex the monastery's lands.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Under the protection of King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Ferdinand I of León">Ferdinand I of León</a>, in 1041 they found refuge in the town of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Domingo_de_Silos" title="Santo Domingo de Silos">Silos</a> at the decaying Abbey of St. Sebastian, occupied by only six monks. (After his death, both the monastery and the town were renamed for him.) Dominic was appointed as the abbot of the community and, inspired by the <a href="/wiki/Cluniac_Reforms" title="Cluniac Reforms">reforms of Cluny</a>, proceeded to rebuild the monastery, both spiritually and physically. He built the cloisters in the Romanesque style, and established a scriptorium, turning the monastery into a center of book design, scholarship, and significant <a href="/wiki/Charity_(practice)" title="Charity (practice)">charity</a> made possible through the proceeds of the gold and silver workshop. Dominic became known for works of healing.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The monastery became one of the centers of the <a href="/wiki/Mozarabic" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozarabic">Mozarabic</a> <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a>, and also preserved the <a href="/wiki/Visigothic" class="mw-redirect" title="Visigothic">Visigothic</a> script of ancient Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wealthy patrons endowed the monastery, and Dominic raised funds to <a href="/wiki/Ransom" title="Ransom">ransom</a> Christians taken prisoner by the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oxref_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxref-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> By the time of his death, on 20 December 1073, the monastery numbered forty monks.   
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_of_Silos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dominic's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to the monastery church on 5 January 1076. Churches and monasteries were dedicated to him as early as 1085.<sup id="cite_ref-oxref_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxref-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His annual <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 20 December.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_of_Silos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The mother of the better-known <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Saint Dominic of Guzmán</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <a href="/wiki/Joan_of_Aza" title="Joan of Aza">Joan of Aza</a>, is said to have prayed at his <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> before she was able to conceive the son she named for him. That son would grow up to found the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. Dominic's special patronage thus became connected with <a href="/wiki/Pregnancy" title="Pregnancy">pregnancy</a>, and until the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic" title="Second Spanish Republic">Second Spanish Republic</a> in 1931, his abbatial <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> was used to bless the queens of Spain, and was placed by their beds when they were in <a href="/wiki/Childbirth" title="Childbirth">labor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Dominic of Silos is patron saint of prisoners, pregnant women, and shepherds.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_of_Silos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The noted 13th-century priest and poet, <a href="/wiki/Gonzalo_de_Berceo" title="Gonzalo de Berceo">Gonzalo de Berceo</a>, wrote an account of his life.
</p><p>In the 19th century Silos became a monastery in the <a href="/wiki/Solesmes_Congregation" title="Solesmes Congregation">Benedictine Congregation of Solesmes</a>, and is notable for its fine double Romanesque cloisters, extensive library, and recordings of <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian chant</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oxref_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxref-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
