<p><b>Zdislava Berka</b> (also <b>Zdislava of Lemberk</b>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1220–1252,<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in what is now the northern part of Czech Republic) was the wife of <a href="/wiki/Havel_of_Markvartice" title="Havel of Markvartice">Havel of Markvartice</a>, Duke of <a href="/wiki/Lemberk_Castle" title="Lemberk Castle">Lemberk</a>, and is a Czech <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. She was a "wife, mother, and one of the earliest lay Dominicans".<sup id="cite_ref-saints101_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints101-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was a "precociously pious child",<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> running away at the age of seven to the forest to become a hermit. She was forced by her family to return home, and when she was 15, they forced her to marry wealthy nobleman <a href="/wiki/Havel_of_Markvartice" title="Havel of Markvartice">Havel of Markvartice</a>. He treated her brutally, but she was eventually able to perform acts of charity, give refuge to the poor and dispossessed at their home, found and support two priories, and join the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic" title="Third Order of Saint Dominic">Third Order of Saint Dominic</a> as a layperson. She died in 1252. She is the patron saint of Bohemia, of difficult marriages, and of those who are ridiculed for their piety. Her feast day is on 1 January.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zdislava_Berka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Zdislava was from the town of <a href="/wiki/Litom%C4%9B%C5%99ice" title="Litoměřice">Litoměřice</a> in what is now the northern part of the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, to a <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemian</a> noble family.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her devout mother was born in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and came to Bohemia as "a member of the retinue" of <a href="/wiki/Kunigunde_of_Hohenstaufen" title="Kunigunde of Hohenstaufen">Queen Kunigunde</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-lemberk_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lemberk-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During her childhood, Zdislava went with her mother to visit Kunigunde, who probably first exposed Zdislava to the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a>. She might have met <a href="/wiki/Ceslaus" title="Ceslaus">Ceslaus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hyacinth_of_Poland" title="Hyacinth of Poland">Hyacinth of Poland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-saints101_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints101-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Zdislava, a "precociously pious child",<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was "extremely pious from her infancy",<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> giving money away to charity at a young age. When she was seven years old, she ran away from her home into the forest to pursue a life of prayer, penance, and a solitary life as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>. Her family found her, though, and forced her to return home.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When she was 15, her family forced her to marry, despite her objections, the wealthy nobleman <a href="/wiki/Havel_of_Markvartice" title="Havel of Markvartice">Havel of Markvartice</a>, who owned Lemberk Castle, a fortified castle in a frontier area that was occasionally attacked by <a href="/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe" title="Mongol invasion of Europe">Mongol invaders</a>. Zdislava and Havel had four children.<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lemberk_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lemberk-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Zdislava's husband was "a man of violent temper"<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and treated her brutally, but "by her patience and gentleness she secured in the end considerable freedom of action in her practices of devotion, her austerities and her many works of charity".<sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She devoted herself to the poor, opening the castle doors to those dispossessed by the invasions. Hagiographer Robert Ellsberg stated that Havel tolerated her "extravagant charity"<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> because she followed his wishes and wore the costly clothes fitting her rank and station and would indulge in his "extravagant feasts"<sup id="cite_ref-saints101_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints101-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> with him. Zdislava had ecstasies and visions, received the Eucharist daily even though it was not a common practice at the time, and performed miracles; one account reports that she even raised the dead.<sup id="cite_ref-saints101_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints101-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cruz-192_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-192-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ellsberg reported that Zdislava donated to hospitals and built churches with her own hands.<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to one story, she gave their bed to a sick, fever-stricken refugee; Havel "became indignant at her hospitality"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and was prepared to eject the man, but found a figure of the crucified Christ there instead. Writer Joan Carroll Cruz called the incident a "miracle",<sup id="cite_ref-cruz-192_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-192-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but one account states that she replaced the bed with a crucifix.<sup id="cite_ref-saints101_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints101-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The incident "deeply impressed"<sup id="cite_ref-cruz-192_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-192-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Havel, though, and he relaxed the restrictions he had placed on her. Eventually, he allowed her to build St. Lawrence Priory (a Dominican <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> for women), donate money to another convent for men in <a href="/wiki/Jablonn%C3%A9_v_Podje%C5%A1t%C4%9Bd%C3%AD" title="Jablonné v Podještědí">Gabel</a>, a nearby town, and join the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic" title="Third Order of Saint Dominic">Third Order of Saint Dominic</a> as a layperson.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cruz-192_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-192-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Hagiographer Alban Bulter states, however, that "the alleged connection of [Zdislava] with the third order of St Dominic remains somewhat of a problem, for the first formal rule for Dominican tertiaries of which we have knowledge belongs to a later date".<sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after founding St. Lawrence Priory, Zdislava fell terminally ill; she consoled her husband and children by telling them that "she hoped to help them more from the next world than she had ever been able to do in this".<sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She died on 1 January 1252, and was buried, at her request, at St. Lawrence.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zdislava_Berka&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_(3).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG/250px-Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="181" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG/375px-Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG/500px-Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_%283%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2402" data-file-height="1735" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Z%C3%A1mek_Lemberk_-_celek_(3).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Lemberk Castle</div></div></div>
<p>Shortly after her death, Zdislava is reported to have appeared to her grieving husband, dressed in a red robe, and comforted him by giving him a piece of the robe.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her appearance to him "greatly strengthened him in his conversion from a life of worldliness".<sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to hagiographer Agnes Dunbar, her room was still being shown to visitors to the Lemberk Castle into the 19th century.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Zdislava was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> in 1907 and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in the Czech Republic in 1995.<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler-14_6-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-14-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-farmer-465_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-465-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> She is the patron saint of Bohemia, of difficult marriages, and of those who are ridiculed for their piety.<sup id="cite_ref-ellsberg-3_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ellsberg-3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-308_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-308-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is 1 January.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer-465_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-465-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
