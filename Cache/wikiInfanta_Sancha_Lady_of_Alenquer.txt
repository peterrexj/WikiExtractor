<p><b>Sancha of Portugal</b> (<span style="font-size:85%;">Portuguese pronunciation:&#160;</span><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pt-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese" title="Help:IPA/Portuguese">[ˈsɐ̃ʃɐ]</a></span>; 1180 in <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a> – 13 March 1229 in Celas Monastery), was a <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Infante" title="Infante">infanta</a></i>, second daughter of <a href="/wiki/List_of_Portuguese_monarchs" title="List of Portuguese monarchs">King</a> <a href="/wiki/Sancho_I_of_Portugal" title="Sancho I of Portugal">Sancho I of Portugal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dulce_of_Aragon" title="Dulce of Aragon">Dulce of Aragon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201084_y_89_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201084_y_89-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESottomayor_Pizarro1997165,_vol._I_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESottomayor_Pizarro1997165,_vol._I-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sancha,_Lady_of_Alenquer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born 1180 and was the feudal Lady of <a href="/wiki/Alenquer_Municipality,_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Alenquer Municipality, Portugal">Alenquer</a>.
</p><p>Sancha and her sisters Teresa and Malfada were very wealthy, having inherited large estates after  the death of their father in 1211. In 1212, their brother <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand,_Count_of_Flanders" title="Ferdinand, Count of Flanders">Ferdinand</a> married <a href="/wiki/Joan,_Countess_of_Flanders" title="Joan, Countess of Flanders">Joan, Countess of Flanders</a> and was imprisoned by the French after <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Bouvines" title="Battle of Bouvines">Battle of Bouvines</a> in 1214.
</p><p>Their brother, King <a href="/wiki/Afonso_II_of_Portugal" title="Afonso II of Portugal">Afonso II of Portugal</a> contested ownership of his sisters' castles of <a href="/wiki/Seia" title="Seia">Seia</a>, Alenquer and <a href="/wiki/Montemor-o-Novo" title="Montemor-o-Novo">Montemor-o-Novo</a> and their right to use the title of queen. Afonso wanted to centralize power and feared that this could create a problem of sovereignty that could come to divide the country. Much of the Portuguese nobles sided with the princesses, but they were defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The king's brother <a href="/wiki/Peter_I,_Count_of_Urgell" title="Peter I, Count of Urgell">Peter</a>, had also sided with his sisters and was banished. On the death of Afonso II, his son <a href="/wiki/Sancho_II_of_Portugal" title="Sancho II of Portugal">Sancho II</a> granted some lands and castles to his aunts but he made them renounce the title of princess-queen. The final peace came in 1223.
</p><p>In 1219, she welcomed the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Berard_of_Carbio" title="Berard of Carbio">Berard of Carbio</a> and his companions to Alenquer. Upon hearing that they planned to go to Morocco to preach the Gospel, she provided them with laymen's clothes so they could pass through Seville, which remained under Muslem rule.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A significant portion of her wealth she spent on acquiring property to establish and support her monastery. Sancha founded the Monastery Santa Maria de Celas, near Coibra, around January 1223. Part of the work of the nuns at Celas was the care of the sick. It later affiliated with the Cistercian order.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sancha died at the Monastery Santa Maria de Celas. Her body was moved to <a href="/wiki/Lorv%C3%A3o_Abbey" title="Lorvão Abbey">Lorvão Abbey</a> by her sister <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_Portugal_(1181-1250)" class="mw-redirect" title="Teresa of Portugal (1181-1250)">Theresa of Portugal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201089_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201089-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 13 December 1705 she was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i>Sollicitudo Pastoralis Offici</i>, along with her sister Theresa of Portugal.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201089_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERodrigues_Oliveira201089-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECarvalho_Correia2008187_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECarvalho_Correia2008187-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day and that of her two sisters Teresa and <a href="/wiki/Mafalda_of_Portugal" title="Mafalda of Portugal">Mafalda</a> is 20 June.
</p>
