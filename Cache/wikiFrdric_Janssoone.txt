<p><b>Frédéric Janssoone, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a></b>, (also known as the Blessed <b>Frédéric of Ghyvelde</b> or <b>Frédéric of Saint-Yves</b>) (19 November 1838, <a href="/wiki/Ghyvelde" title="Ghyvelde">Ghyvelde</a>, France — 4 August 1916, <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, Canada) was a <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a>-born <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> who worked in France, Egypt, <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, where he died. He was a popular preacher who re-established the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> in Canada.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He has been <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Janssoone was born in the town of Ghyvelde, in the French <a href="/wiki/Departments_of_France" title="Departments of France">Department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nord_(French_department)" title="Nord (French department)">Nord</a>, the country's most northwestern corner, on 19 November 1838. It is a part of <a href="/wiki/French_Flanders" title="French Flanders">French Flanders</a> where the local language is a dialect of <a href="/wiki/French_Flemish" title="French Flemish">Flemish</a>. He was the eighth and youngest of the thirteen children<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag may need clarification or removal of jargon. (March 2021)">clarification needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> of Pierre-Antoine Janssoone and Marie-Isabelle Bollengier, and was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">christened</a> <b>Frédéric-Cornil</b>. He was born into a prosperous farming family who prized culture, education and their faith.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Janssone was just nine years old in January 1848 when his father died. Four years later, in response, to a <a href="/wiki/Religious_calling" title="Religious calling">religious calling</a>, the young Frédéric enrolled in a college and then graduated to the Institut Notre-Dame des Dunes in nearby <a href="/wiki/Dunkirk" title="Dunkirk">Dunkirk</a> to prepare for the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a>. In 1855, however, the family fell on hard times, so the boy left school to help support his mother and siblings. He went to work for some textile merchants, for whom he became a traveling salesman. He soon realized that he had a talent for selling. He liked meeting new people and he knew how to explain his products.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His mother died in 1861, at which point Janssoone decided to resume his studies. After their completion, on 24 June 1864, he entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Friars Minor, where he added the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patronage</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ivo_of_Kermartin" title="Ivo of Kermartin">St. Yves</a> to his name, making his <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">profession</a> the following year. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders" title="Holy orders">ordained</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a> on 17 August 1870. His ordination was done earlier than in the normal scheme, due to the outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War" title="Franco-Prussian War">Franco-Prussian War</a> at the time. Military <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplains</a> were in great need, and he was quickly assigned to serve at a military hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the war Jannsoone helped to establish a <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bordeaux" title="Bordeaux">Bordeaux</a>, where he was appointed as <a href="/wiki/Superior_(hierarchy)" title="Superior (hierarchy)">guardian</a> in 1873. Since he found the role unbearable, he was relieved of this responsibility a year later. His release gave him the freedom to take a course in <a href="/wiki/Homiletics" title="Homiletics">homiletics</a>, at which he would excel all his life.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Holy_Land">The <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Holy Land">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vicar_of_the_Custody">Vicar of the Custody</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Vicar of the Custody">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1876 Janssoone applied to serve in the Order's international <a href="/wiki/Custodian_of_the_Holy_Land" class="mw-redirect" title="Custodian of the Holy Land">Custody of the Holy Land</a>, which has been responsible for the Christian sites there and the <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> who visited them since the days of their founder, St. <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>. His superiors accepted his request.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was initially assigned to serve as chaplain for a school in <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a>, Egypt, run by the <a href="/wiki/Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools" class="mw-redirect" title="Brothers of the Christian Schools">Brothers of the Christian Schools</a>. Two years later he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> to serve as <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">Vicar</a> of the Custody, immediately under the <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)"><i>Custos</i></a>. This position was primarily one of handling the administrative duties of the Custody, most especially the maintenance of the Christian shrines scattered throughout <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>. Additionally, to provide a center for their work, Janssoone ordered the building of a new <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a>, St. Catherine of Bethlehem, adjacent to the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity" title="Church of the Nativity">Church of the Nativity</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bethlehem" title="Bethlehem">Bethlehem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To accomplish the demands of his duties, Janssoone had to sort through dozens of documents and manuscripts detailing the rules of procedure for the sharing of these sites with Christians of other Churches which had been worked out over the centuries of rule of the Holy Land under the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>. He compiled a guide to help the friars in their relations with the other Churches, which remains of use to the Custody today. He served in that post for ten years.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to his many administrative duties during that period, Janssoone also served as a guide for the many Christian pilgrims who visited the Holy Lands from around the world. His knowledge and skill in presentation made him a popular guide. It was in this way that he met a priest from Canada, the <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> Léon Provancher, <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cap-Rouge,_Quebec_City" title="Cap-Rouge, Quebec City">Cap-Rouge</a> in <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, who invited him to come to Canada. Janssoone accepted the offer in order to make a fundraising tour to help the various construction projects of the Custody.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="A_mission_to_Canada">A mission to Canada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: A mission to Canada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Janssoone arrived in <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9vis,_Quebec" title="Lévis, Quebec">Lévis, Quebec</a>, on 24 August 1881. He immediately began to preach and give talks around the region. Shortly after the start of his visit, however, he stumbled slightly, through giving a talk on <a href="/wiki/Liberalism" title="Liberalism">liberalism</a>, unaware that this topic was causing a major division between the politicians and the <a href="/wiki/Ultramontanism" title="Ultramontanism">ultramontane</a> Catholic clergy of the province. Otherwise, his many sermons were greeted enthusiastically by the people. He preached throughout Quebec to large crowds. He was even invited to <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French-speaking</a> parishes in <a href="/wiki/New_England" title="New England">New England</a>. Nevertheless, to quiet continuing complaints resulting from the infamous speech by the foreign visitor, that following March, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Quebec" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Quebec">Archbishop of Quebec</a>, <a href="/wiki/Elz%C3%A9ar-Alexandre_Taschereau" title="Elzéar-Alexandre Taschereau">Elzéar-Alexandre Taschereau</a>, ordered Janssoone to leave the Archdiocese. Taschereau tempered his action, though, with a command that collections for the Custody of the Holy Land were to be taken up in all churches of the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">ecclesiastical province</a> on every <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a>. This practice later spread throughout the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Janssoone returned to Palestine in May 1882, exhausted and sick. He resumed his administrative duties and stayed there another six years. He would later write Provancher that, after reflection, were he ever to return to Canada, he would completely avoid the political aspect which had caused such an uproar and focus on the program established by St. Francis, one of preaching charity, penance and peace, focusing on bringing people back to God. He was to have a chance to carry this out this intention.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_new_home_in_Canada">A new home in Canada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: A new home in Canada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The impact of Janssoone's tour upon the Catholics of Quebec was enormous. They saw him as reviving a tradition of the Franciscan missions to that nation which had started at the foundation of <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a> and had ended with the death of the last <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9collets" class="mw-redirect" title="Récollets">Recollect</a> friar (a reform branch of the Order) there in 1813. This was due to the fact that, after its conquest by Great Britain in 1759, further candidates to the Order had been forbidden.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Repeated requests to the authorities of the Minorite Order finally resulted in his assignment to serve the Custody in Canada on a permanent basis.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Janssoone arrived back in Canada at the end of June 1888 on a permanent basis, and by August had begun to build a residence, called St. Joseph Friary, at <a href="/wiki/Trois-Rivi%C3%A8res" title="Trois-Rivières">Trois-Rivières</a>, which was the first community of Friars Minor in Canada in a generation. The friary was considered a <a href="/wiki/Commissariat#Religious_usage" title="Commissariat">Commissariat</a> of the Franciscan Order, a small center of recruitment and fundraising. A new and more permanent structure was built in 1903.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after his arrival, Janssoone was drawn into the promotion of the local <a href="/wiki/Marian_shrine" class="mw-redirect" title="Marian shrine">Marian shrine</a> of <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame-du-Cap_Basilica" title="Notre-Dame-du-Cap Basilica">Notre-Dame-du-Cap</a> by <a href="/wiki/Louis-Fran%C3%A7ois_Richer_Lafl%C3%A8che" title="Louis-François Richer Laflèche">Louis-François Richer Laflèche</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Trois-Rivi%C3%A8res" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Trois-Rivières">Bishop of Trois-Rivières</a>, who wanted revive the sanctuary built there by the pastor of that town. He agreed to this reluctantly. He then set about touring the entire province to promote visits to the shrine. Janssoone would organize travel by train and boat for the many people he started to draw to the site. He would preach there himself and dedicated the people to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary" title="Our Lady of the Rosary">Our Lady of the Rosary</a>, its <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>. Amazing miracles started to occur, which draw ever larger crowds. By the time the work was given to the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates of Mary Immaculate">Oblate Fathers</a> in 1902, visits to the shrine numbered about 30,000-40,000 per year.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While occupied with this, at the request of his superiors Janssoone also started to undertake massive fundraising drives which required travel throughout the province, a task he performed for fifteen years. He would preach in churches and go house-to-house for this, braving the harshness of the Canadian winter and farm dogs, selling some of the many books he wrote at night instead of sleeping. He had only one coat and would frequently sleep on the ground during these trips. Additionally, he also organized pilgrimages to the other great shrine of Quebec, the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupr%C3%A9" title="Basilica of Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré">Basilica of Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In further service to the Franciscan movement, from his first arrival in Canada Janssoone worked to revive the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis#Third_Order_of_St._Francis_in_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, which had been established by the Recollect friars in 1681 for the spiritual growth of the laity, but had dwindled since the incorporation of the colony into Great Britain. He acted as <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> to the surviving <a href="/wiki/Fraternity" title="Fraternity">fraternities</a> scattered around the Province of Quebec. In addition, he was also working within his own Order to plan for its re-establishment in Canada. This was accomplished in 1890 by the establishment of a friary in Montreal. This led to the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">Province</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Joseph" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Joseph">St. Joseph</a> in Canada, which has been closely tied with the mission in the Holy Land since its foundation, due to Janssoone's role in its foundation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Janssoone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Janssoone took to his bed in June 1916 physically worn out by his many exertions in his ministries and in great pain. He was admitted to the Franciscan Infirmary in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, where he was diagnosed as suffering from <a href="/wiki/Stomach_cancer" title="Stomach cancer">stomach cancer</a>. After almost two months of intense pain, he died there on the following 4 August. His body was returned to Trois-Rivières, where he was buried in the small chapel he had built attached to the friary.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cause for his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> was begun in 1927. Inquiries into his life were made in Canada, France and Egypt, and his many writings were examined. On 25 October 1988, Janssoone was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_1-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His tomb is in the Chapel of St. Anthony in Trois-Rivières and has become a place of pilgrimage.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
