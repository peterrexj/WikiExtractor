<p><b>Hilary of Arles</b>, also known by his Latin name <b>Hilarius</b> (c. 403–449), was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arles">bishop of Arles</a> in <a href="/wiki/Southern_France" title="Southern France">Southern France</a>. He is recognized as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Churches, with his feast day celebrated on 5 May.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilary_of_Arles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his early youth, or the 420s, Hilary joined the <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">abbey of Lérins</a> which was, at the time, presided over by his kinsman <a href="/wiki/Saint_Honoratus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Honoratus">Honoratus</a>. Hilary seems to have been living in <a href="/wiki/Dijon" title="Dijon">Dijon</a> before this, although other authorities believe he came from <a href="/wiki/Belgica" class="mw-redirect" title="Belgica">Belgica</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a>. Hilary may have been a relative or "even the son" of the <a href="/wiki/Hilarius_(praefectus_urbi)" title="Hilarius (praefectus urbi)">Hilarius</a> who had been prefect of Gaul in 396 and of Rome in 408.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hilary succeeded his kinsman Honoratus as bishop of Arles in 429. Following the example of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, he is said to have organized his cathedral clergy into a "congregation," devoting a great part of their time to social exercises of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>. He held the rank of <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vienne" title="Vienne">Vienne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>, and attempted to exercise the sort of primacy over the church of south <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, which seemed implied in the <a href="/wiki/Vicariate" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicariate">vicariate</a> granted to his predecessor Patroclus of Arles (417).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hilary deposed the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Besan%C3%A7on" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Besançon">bishop of Besançon</a>, Chelidonus, for ignoring this primacy, and for claiming a metropolitan dignity for Besançon. An appeal was made to Rome, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_I" title="Pope Leo I">Pope Leo I</a> used it, in 444, to extinguish the Gallican vicariate headed by Hilary, thus depriving him of his rights to consecrate bishops, call <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synods</a>, or oversee the church in the province.  The pope also secured the <a href="/wiki/Edict" title="Edict">edict</a> of <a href="/wiki/Valentinian_III" title="Valentinian III">Valentinian III</a>, so important in the history of the Gallican church, which freed the Church of Vienne from all dependence on that of Arles. These papal claims were made imperial law, and violation of them were subject to legal penalties.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Léon Clugnet suggests that the dispute arose from the fact that the respective rights of the Court of Rome and of the metropolitan were not sufficiently clearly established at that time, and that the right of appeal to the pope was not explicitly enough recognized.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following his death in 449, Hilary's name was introduced into the Roman <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrology</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilary_of_Arles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During his lifetime Hilary had a great reputation for learning and eloquence as well as for <a href="/wiki/Piety" title="Piety">piety</a>; his extant works (<i>Vita S. Honorati Arelatensis episcopi</i> and <i>Metrum in Genesin</i>) compare favourably with any similar literary productions of that period.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A poem, <i>De providentia</i>, usually included among the writings of <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a>, is sometimes attributed to Hilary of Arles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
