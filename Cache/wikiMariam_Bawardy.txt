<p><b>Mariam Baouardy</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">مريم بواردي</span>, or <b>Mary of Jesus Crucified</b>, 5 January 1846 – 26 August 1878), was a <a href="/wiki/Discalced_Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Discalced Carmelite">Discalced Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Melkite Greek Catholic Church">Melkite Greek Catholic Church</a>. Born to <a href="/wiki/Palestinians" title="Palestinians">Palestinian</a> <a href="/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Melkite Greek Catholic Church">Greek Catholic</a> parents from the town of <a href="/wiki/Hurfeish" title="Hurfeish">Hurfiesh</a> in the upper <a href="/wiki/Galilee" title="Galilee">Galilee</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  later moved to <a href="/wiki/I%27billin" title="I&#39;billin">I’billin</a>, she was known for her service to the poor. In addition, she became a <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">Christian mystic</a> who suffered the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a>, and has been <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Her path to canonization solidified on 6 December 2014 with the recognition of a final miracle needed for her canonization. In the <a href="/wiki/Papal_consistory" title="Papal consistory">consistory</a> of 14 February 2015, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> announced that she would be canonized on 17 May 2015; she was canonized at the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariam_Baouardy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariam_Baouardy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Baouardy was born on 5 January 1846 (the eve of <a href="/wiki/Epiphany_(holiday)" title="Epiphany (holiday)">Epiphany</a>) in the <a href="/wiki/Galilee" title="Galilee">Galilean</a> village of <a href="/wiki/Hurfeish" title="Hurfeish">Hurfeish</a>, later moved to <a href="/wiki/I%27billin" title="I&#39;billin">I'billin</a>, in Palestine during the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>, to Giries (George) Baouardy and his wife, Mariam Chahine. Mariam was their 13th child and first daughter, and none of her preceding brothers had survived infancy. She was born after the couple made a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> on foot to <a href="/wiki/Bethlehem" title="Bethlehem">Bethlehem</a>, some 70 miles away, out of desperation after the loss of their children. When they were later blessed with the birth of a daughter, they named her after the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>, out of gratitude. She was joined by a new brother, Boulos, two years later.<sup id="cite_ref-DCN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baouardy was not yet three years old when her parents died from an illness in 1848, only a few days apart. The siblings were each taken in by relatives on different sides of her family living in other villages. She was taken by a paternal uncle who lived in the same village, and her brother went to live with a maternal aunt. The brother and sister would never see one another again. She was raised in a loving home in comfortable circumstances. As a child, she had a marked spirit of religious fervor, and at the age of five began to fast on Saturdays in honor of the Blessed Virgin.<sup id="cite_ref-csi_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-csi-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Baouardy was eight, her uncle and his wife moved to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria,_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandria, Egypt">Alexandria, Egypt</a> to improve their situation. Five years later, in 1858, when she was aged 13, in keeping with tradition, she was engaged by her uncle to his wife's brother, who lived in Cairo. The night before the wedding, she had a religious experience in which she felt called not to marry but to offer her life to God. Upon being told this the following morning, her uncle flew into a rage and beat her severely. Despite this and the subsequent ill-treatment she began to experience from her uncle, she stayed firm in her decision.
</p><p>Nonetheless, Baouardy felt depressed and alone. She wrote her brother, then living in <a href="/wiki/Nazareth" title="Nazareth">Nazareth</a>, asking him to visit her. The young male servant she asked to deliver the letter drew out of her the cause for her sadness. Upon learning of this, he attempted to woo her for himself, inviting her to convert to <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a>. She rejected his proposal, which caused the young man to fly into a rage, in which he drew a knife and cut her throat. He then dumped her body in a nearby alley.<sup id="cite_ref-csi_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-csi-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baouardy then experienced what she was convinced was a miracle. As she related later, a "nun dressed in blue" brought her to a grotto which she could never identify, stitched her wounds, and took care of her.<sup id="cite_ref-csi_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-csi-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her voice was affected for the rest of her life as a result of the cut, which a French doctor later measured as being 10&#160;cm. (nearly 4 inches) wide.  After being cared for by this mysterious figure for a month, she recovered enough to leave and find work as a domestic servant in the home of an Arab Christian family in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-eparchy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eparchy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>After a year, Baourardy decided to try to meet her brother and traveled by caravan to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. She felt inspired to make a vow of perpetual virginity there at the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Sepulchre" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Sepulchre">Holy Sepulchre</a>. She then took a boat in <a href="/wiki/Jaffa" title="Jaffa">Jaffa</a> intending to head to <a href="/wiki/Acre,_Israel" title="Acre, Israel">Acre</a>. Due to poor weather, however, the boat had to stop at <a href="/wiki/Beirut" title="Beirut">Beirut</a>. Taking this as a sign from God, she disembarked and found work as a maid. After working there, she suddenly became blind, which lasted for 40 days, when just as suddenly she recovered her vision. Not long after that, she had a severe fall which seemed to leave her for dead. Her employer cared for her for a month until she recovered, wholly healed.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="France_and_Carmel">France and Carmel</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariam_Baouardy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: France and Carmel">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>In May 1863, a generous patron made it possible for Baouardy to move to <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a>, France, where she became the cook for an Arab family. While there, she felt called to enter a religious order. Rejected by the first groups to which she sought admission, in May 1865, she was accepted as a <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulant</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">Congregation</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_St._Joseph_of_the_Apparition" title="Sisters of St. Joseph of the Apparition">Sisters of St. Joseph of the Apparition</a>, who had communities in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> and already had several Palestinian candidates. It was at this point that she received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a> of Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the last month of this period of candidacy, the <a href="/wiki/Mistress_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Mistress of novices">Mistress of novices</a>, Mother Honorine, who had drawn Baouardy's life story from her, was replaced by <a href="/wiki/Mother_Veronica_of_the_Passion" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother Veronica of the Passion">Mother Veronica of the Passion</a>. After two years as a postulant, Baouardy was up for a vote by the community regarding her admission to the congregation. To her dismay, she was rejected by the sisters charged with making the decision.<sup id="cite_ref-eparchy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eparchy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At that point, Mother Veronica had just received permission to transfer to the Discalced Carmelite monastery at <a href="/wiki/Pau,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques" title="Pau, Pyrénées-Atlantiques">Pau</a> to prepare for her forming a new congregation of <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">Religious Sisters</a> serving in India, the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Apostolic_Carmel" title="Sisters of the Apostolic Carmel">Sisters of the Apostolic Carmel</a>. She invited Baouardy to go with her, writing to that community's <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a> and recommending that they accept the young Arab woman. The prioress took Mother Veronica's advice. In June 1867, both women went to Pau, where they received the Carmelite <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a>. Baourdy was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> <i>Mary of Jesus Crucified</i>.
</p><p>In 1870, Baouardy went with the first group of Carmelite Apostolic Sisters to <a href="/wiki/Mangalore" title="Mangalore">Mangalore</a>, India.<sup id="cite_ref-eparchy_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eparchy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She served there for two years before returning to Pau. There she made her profession of <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemn vows">solemn vows</a> in November 1871. In September 1875, she helped to found a new monastery in <a href="/wiki/Bethlehem" title="Bethlehem">Bethlehem</a>, the first of the Order in that region, where she lived until her death.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> During her whole life, she experienced periods of <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">religious ecstasy</a> frequently throughout the day.
</p><p>In April 1878, Baouardy played an important role in the identification of the Biblical <a href="/wiki/Emmaus_Nicopolis" class="mw-redirect" title="Emmaus Nicopolis">Emmaus</a> thanks to a private <a href="/wiki/Revelation" title="Revelation">revelation</a>. She died on 26 August 1878 in Bethlehem from cancer that had developed in her bones due to the fall she had while working in the monastery, which led to <a href="/wiki/Gangrene" title="Gangrene">gangrene</a> that spread to her lungs.<sup id="cite_ref-DCN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariam_Baouardy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cause of canonization started on 18 May 1927 and would culminate with the decree of heroic virtue on 27 November 1981. Baouardy was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 13 November 1983 and was canonized on 17 May 2015 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>. She became the second <a href="/wiki/Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Catholic">Greek Catholic</a> to be canonized a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, the first being <a href="/wiki/Josaphat_Kuntsevych" title="Josaphat Kuntsevych">Josaphat Kuntsevych</a> in 1867.
</p><p>In I'billin, a kindergarten, the <a href="/wiki/Mar_Elias_Educational_Institutions#Miriam_Bawardi_Elementary_School" title="Mar Elias Educational Institutions">Miriam Bawardi Elementary School</a> and a junior high school are named after Baouardy.
</p>
