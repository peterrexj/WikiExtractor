<p><b>Dominic de la Calzada</b> (or <b>Dominic of the Causeway</b>) (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Santo Domingo de la Calzada</i>) (1019 – 12 May 1109) was a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> from a cottage in <a href="/wiki/Burgos" title="Burgos">Burgos</a> very close to <a href="/wiki/La_Rioja_(Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="La Rioja (Spain)">La Rioja</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_de_la_Calzada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born <b>Domingo García</b> in <a href="/wiki/Viloria_de_Rioja" title="Viloria de Rioja">Viloria de Rioja</a>, he was the son of a peasant named Ximeno García.  His mother was named Orodulce. A shepherd, he tried to join the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine order">Benedictine order</a> first at <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_Valvanera" title="Monastery of Nuestra Señora de Valvanera">Valvanera</a> and then at <a href="/wiki/San_Mill%C3%A1n_de_la_Cogolla" title="San Millán de la Cogolla">San Millán de la Cogolla</a>, but was turned away.<sup id="cite_ref-city_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-city-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He then became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in the forests near Ayuela, near the present-day town of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada" title="Santo Domingo de la Calzada">Santo Domingo de la Calzada</a>, until 1039.
</p><p>In 1039, he began working with Gregory IV of Ostia (Gregorio), <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ostia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ostia">bishop of Ostia</a>, who had been sent to <a href="/wiki/Calahorra" title="Calahorra">Calahorra</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Papal" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal">papal</a> envoy to combat a plague of <a href="/wiki/Locusts" class="mw-redirect" title="Locusts">locusts</a> that afflicted <a href="/wiki/Navarre" title="Navarre">Navarre</a> and <a href="/wiki/La_Rioja_(Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="La Rioja (Spain)">La Rioja</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory ordained Dominic a priest. Together they built a wooden bridge over the <a href="/wiki/Oja_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Oja River">Oja River</a> to help pilgrims on the <a href="/wiki/Way_of_St._James" class="mw-redirect" title="Way of St. James">Way of St. James</a>.  Gregory died in 1044, and Dominic returned to Ayuela, where he began developing the area.  He cleared trees, cultivated the earth, and began to build a paved causeway (in Spanish, <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">calzada</i></span>), which served as an alternate route to the traditional <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> causeway between <a href="/wiki/Logro%C3%B1o" title="Logroño">Logroño</a> and <a href="/wiki/Burgos" title="Burgos">Burgos</a>. Dominic's causeway became the principal route between <a href="/wiki/N%C3%A1jera" title="Nájera">Nájera</a> and <a href="/wiki/Redecilla_del_Camino" title="Redecilla del Camino">Redecilla del Camino</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dominic was joined in this work by <a href="/wiki/Juan_de_Ortega_(hermit)" title="Juan de Ortega (hermit)">Juan de Ortega</a>. To better the conditions of the pilgrims that began to use his new causeway, he replaced the wooden bridge that he had built with Gregory with one made of stone, and constructed on the site of an old fort, a building that served as a hospice for travelers. Today, it is the <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">Casa del Santo</i></span>, which is a used as a <a href="/wiki/Hostel" title="Hostel">hostel</a> by modern-day pilgrims.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The town of Santo Domingo de la Calzada began as a few houses built around the hermitage of the saint in his lifetime. At this death in 1109, the village had grown in population. <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VI_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VI of Castile">Alfonso VI of Castile</a> annexed La Rioja in 1076 and seeing that Dominic's efforts contributed to the Castilianization of the region, decided to support him and his projects.  He visited Dominic in 1090 and thereafter Dominic, assisted by his follower Juan de Ortega, began construction on a church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>.  This was consecrated by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Calahorra" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Calahorra">bishop of Calahorra</a> in 1106.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Dominic died in 1109.<sup id="cite_ref-pilgrim_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pilgrim-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His church, later the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Santo Domingo de la Calzada">Cathedral of Santo Domingo de la Calzada</a>, was where he was buried, and it was elevated to the rank of cathedral after being placed in the jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Calahorra" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Calahorra">Diocese of Calahorra</a> in the 1230s.
</p><p>Due to the development of his public works projects he is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_Saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron Saint">Patron Saint</a> of the Spanish <a href="/wiki/Civil_Engineer" class="mw-redirect" title="Civil Engineer">Civil Engineers</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_de_la_Calzada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Miracles are attributed to Dominic, among them the healing of a <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> who had been possessed by the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devil</a> and who was freed of his affliction by visiting the <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> of Dominic.  Another concerns the healing of a German pilgrim named Bernard in the fifteenth century, who was cured of an affliction of the eyes by visiting Dominic's tomb.  Another concerns the healing of a blind <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Norman</a> who was cured when he visited the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_de_la_Calzada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The most famous miracle, however, concerns that of the rooster and the chicken, which is said to have taken place at Santo Domingo de la Calzada.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The story goes that in the 14th century, a German 18-year-old named Hugonell, from <a href="/wiki/Xanten" title="Xanten">Xanten</a>, goes on pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> with his parents.  A Spanish girl at the hostel where they were staying makes sexual advances toward Hugonell; Hugonell rejects her advances.  Angry at this, the girl hides a silver cup in the German's bag and then informs the authorities that the youth had taken it.  Hugonell is sentenced to the <a href="/wiki/Gallows" title="Gallows">gallows</a>, in accordance with the laws of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile" title="Alfonso X of Castile">Alfonso X of Castile</a>.
</p><p>The parents sadly decide to examine their son's body, still hanging on the gallows, but suddenly hear his voice –he tells them that Saint Dominic has saved his life.  His parents quickly make their way to Santiago de Compostela to see the magistrate.  The magistrate, who is at the time eating dinner, remarks: "Your son is as alive as this rooster and chicken that I was feasting on before you interrupted me."  And in that moment, the two birds jump from the plate and begin to sing and crow happily.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG/200px-Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG/300px-Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG/400px-Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2976" data-file-height="3968" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Domingo_de_la_Calzada-Burgos.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Domingo de la Calzada in <a href="/wiki/Burgos" title="Burgos">Burgos</a>.</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Analysis_of_the_story">Analysis of the story</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_de_la_Calzada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Analysis of the story">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The first element of the tale, that of a hanged pilgrim, is found in many collections of miracles, with the salvation from death of the victim attributed to not only Dominic, but also to <a href="/wiki/Saint_James_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint James the Great">Saint James the Great</a>, or to the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>, with the story taking place in various cities<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> such as <a href="/wiki/Rooster_of_Barcelos" title="Rooster of Barcelos">Barcelos, Portugal</a>. Versions of the tale are found in the Milagros de Nuestra Señora by <a href="/wiki/Gonzalo_de_Berceo" title="Gonzalo de Berceo">Gonzalo de Berceo</a> (Miracle No. 6), the 175th <a href="/wiki/Cantigas_de_Santa_Maria" title="Cantigas de Santa Maria">Cantiga de Santa María</a> by Alfonso X, and in the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Calixtinus" title="Codex Calixtinus">Codex Calixtinus</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The second part of the tale, the miracle of the dancing roasted chicken and rooster, is unique to Santo Domingo de la Calzada,<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> although a very similar story appears in the English <a href="/wiki/Carol_(music)" title="Carol (music)">carol</a> "King Herod and the Cock",<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and related notions can also be found in <a href="/wiki/Folklore" title="Folklore">folklore</a> concerning the mythical land of <a href="/wiki/Cockaigne" title="Cockaigne">Cockaigne</a>, "where grilled geese fly directly into one's mouth".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_de_la_Calzada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg/200px-Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg/300px-Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg/400px-Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3635" data-file-height="2727" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lauda_sepulcral_de_Santo_Domingo_de_la_Calzada.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Domingo de la Calzada.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg/220px-SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="338" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg/330px-SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg/440px-SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2632" data-file-height="4046" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SantoDomingoDeLaCalzadaH%C3%BChner.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The rooster is visible behind the bars of its ornate coop.</div></div></div>
<p>In memory of Dominic's miracle, a rooster and chicken, with white feathers, are kept alive at the cathedral all year round.<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A different rooster and chicken are switched each month, although they are called descendants of the original birds who miraculously danced even though roasted.  The pairs of roosters and chickens, when they are not at the cathedral, are kept in a <a href="/wiki/Chicken_coop" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicken coop">chicken coop</a> called the Gallinero de Santo Domingo de la Calzada, which the Cofradía de Santo Domingo (<i>Confraternity of Santo Domingo</i>) maintains with the help of donations.  A <a href="/wiki/Wayside_shrine" title="Wayside shrine">wayside shrine</a> (<span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">hornacina</i></span>) built in 1445 holds a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> associated with the miracle: a piece of wood from the gallows from which Hugonell was hanged.
</p><p>Medieval pilgrims gathered the feathers of these favored birds, or got them from the priest, and would affix them to their hats.<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Another tradition claimed that if the birds ate breadcrumbs directly from the end of the pilgrim's staff, that pilgrim would arrive safely in Compostela.<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The German pilgrim Hermann Künig (15th century) claimed to have seen the room where the roasted birds began to sing and dance; other documents written by pilgrims state that Hugonell's shirt as well as the gallows had been conserved by the church of Santo Domingo.  These artifacts are now lost.<sup id="cite_ref-lacalzada_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lacalzada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A verse commemorating this miracle runs: <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">"Santo Domingo de la Calzada / Donde cantó la gallina después de asada"</i></span> ("Santo Domingo de la Calzada / Where a chicken sang after being roasted").<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chicken-shaped pastries called <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">"milagros del santo"</i></span> ("miracles of the saint") are widely available in the town of Santo Domingo de la Calzada, also in commemoration of the miracle.
</p>
