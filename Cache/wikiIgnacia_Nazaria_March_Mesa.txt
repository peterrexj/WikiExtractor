<p><b>Nazaria Ignacia March Mesa</b> (10 January 1889 – 6 July 1943) – in religious <b>Nazaria of Saint Teresa of Jesus</b> – was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the founder of the Missionaries of the Crusade.<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Mesa immigrated from <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> to <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a> where she joined a religious order that saw her minister in <a href="/wiki/Bolivia" title="Bolivia">Bolivia</a> where she remained for most of her life. She served brief stints in Spain to spread the religious order she founded after she left her own order and relocated to <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> where she later died.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her beatification cause commenced under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 6 September 1966 and she was titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> while <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> later confirmed her <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named her to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 1 September 1988; he later beatified her on 27 September 1992 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> confirmed a miracle attributed to her in 2018 and she was canonized on 14 October 2018.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nazaria_Ignacia_March_Mesa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nazaria Ignacia March Mesa was born in 1889 in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> as the fourth of ten children to José Alejandro March Reus (3 April 1850–???) and Nazaria de Mesa Ramos de Peralta (28 July 1853–???). Her siblings were:
</p>
<ul><li>Melchor March Mesa (3 May 1886–???)</li>
<li>María Josefa March Mesa</li>
<li>José María March Mesa</li>
<li>Manuel María March Mesa (23 March 1890–???)</li>
<li>Ausias Salvador March Mesa (3 February 1891 – 7 August 1958)</li>
<li>Juan Bautista de la Santísima Trinidad March Mesa (18 February 1892–???)</li>
<li>Delfina March Mesa (7 June 1893 – 5 August 1974)</li>
<li>Carmen March Mesa (16 July 1894 –&#160;???)</li>
<li>Amparo Manuela March Mesa</li></ul>
<p>Both Melchor and Delfina immigrated later in life; Melchor to <a href="/wiki/El_Salvador" title="El Salvador">El Salvador</a> and Delfina to <a href="/wiki/Guatemala" title="Guatemala">Guatemala</a>.
</p><p>The infant was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> the month of her birth in the local parish church of Saint Joseph and she later received her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> in 1898. In 1898 she heard the voice of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> call out to her in which He said to her – "You: Nazaria – follow Me". Her parents were not pleased with her desire to enter the religious life and forbade it while also forbidding her to take the sacraments.<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Mesa studied in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a> where the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Order of Saint Augustine">Order of Saint Augustine</a> oversaw her education; she resided with her maternal grandmother at this time. She returned home on 9 September 1901 and received her <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation</a> in 1902 from the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Seville" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Seville">Archbishop of Seville</a> Blessed <a href="/wiki/Marcelo_Spinola_y_Maestre" class="mw-redirect" title="Marcelo Spinola y Maestre">Marcelo Spinola Maestre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her grandmother also granted her permission to join the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> and this saw her parents start to relax their religious restrictions on their daughter.
</p><p>In 1904 her father wanted to move to Mexico but before this happened she and three sisters moved to their grandmother's home so her three sisters could first receive their First Communion before setting off. The Mesa's later relocated to Mexico at the end of 1904 due to tough economic conditions and it was there that she joined the <a href="/wiki/Little_Sisters_of_the_Abandoned_Elderly" title="Little Sisters of the Abandoned Elderly">Little Sisters of the Abandoned Elderly</a> on 12 July 1908.<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was sent to <a href="/wiki/Oruro,_Bolivia" class="mw-redirect" title="Oruro, Bolivia">Oruro</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bolivia" title="Bolivia">Bolivia</a> and was there tending to the old and ill from 1908 until 1920. But there was the brief suspension of her assignment there for she was sent to do her <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> back in Spain in 1912 and she did this in <a href="/wiki/Palencia" title="Palencia">Palencia</a>. Mesa assumed the habit on 9 December 1909 as well as the religious name of "Nazaria of Saint Teresa of Jesus". She made her initial profession on 10 October 1911 and returned to Oruro with nine other religious on 23 December 1912. She made her solemn profession on 1 January 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mesa later met <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Cortesi" title="Filippo Cortesi">Filippo Cortesi</a> who expressed his desire to found a religious congregation dedicated to re-Christianizing the world and he discussed this when the pair met in Oruro. The two met on 22 June 1924 and that August she fell ill with a high fever. Cortesi met her on 15 August 1924 and gave her an image of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> as a token of goodwill and because it was the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Assumption" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Assumption">Feast of the Assumption</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mesa recovered and later went to <a href="/wiki/La_Paz_Department_(Bolivia)" title="La Paz Department (Bolivia)">La Paz</a> on 2 December 1925 to hold a further discussion with her friend Cortesi. On 23 March 1925 she attended a celebration in which Cortesi consecrated five new bishops.
</p><p>Mesa left the order on 16 June 1925 to found a new religious congregation and rallied ten Bolivian women to join her in this effort while Cortesi approved the initial blueprint on 18 August 1925 while later discussing it more with her at length later that same week. This idea came about in 1920 when she attended a spiritual retreat. Mesa founded the Missionaries of the Crusade on 12 December 1926 and it later received diocesan approval on 12 February 1927 while later receiving the decree of praise from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 8 June 1935 and papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 9 June 1947 – the latter after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The first General Chapter for the order on 1 June 1930 saw the unanimous decision to elect her as the Superior General. Mesa later embarked on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> where she met Pope Pius XI in 1934 in a private audience. She told the pope at his feet that she wanted to die for the church but Pius XI told her: "Do not die but live and work for the church".<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Mesa travelled to Madrid in 1935 where she founded a home for the spiritual exercises to be run but left due to the dangerous and anti-religious <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>.
</p><p>The nun arrived in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a> in <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> in 1938.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her illness did not prevent her from taking part in the General Chapter held in Buenos Aires in 1943 before her death. She started to suffer from <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> on 14 May 1943 and was admitted to the <a href="/wiki/Rivadavia_Department,_Santiago_del_Estero" title="Rivadavia Department, Santiago del Estero">Rivadavia</a> hospital to recuperate – her condition worsened over the next two months before her death.
</p><p>Mesa died in 1943 due to <a href="/wiki/Hemoptysis" title="Hemoptysis">hemoptysis</a>. Her remains were relocated to Oruro on 18 June 1972. Her order now operates in places such as <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea" title="Equatorial Guinea">Equatorial Guinea</a> and as of 2005 had 415 religious in a total of 79 houses.<sup id="cite_ref-UC_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nazaria_Ignacia_March_Mesa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:237px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._(44398703435).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg/235px-Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="235" height="157" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg/353px-Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg/470px-Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._%2844398703435%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="6720" data-file-height="4480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ceremonia_de_Canonizaci%C3%B3n_de_Monse%C3%B1or_Romero._(44398703435).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Canonization Mass held in 2018.</div></div></div>
<p>The beatification cause opened in Buenos Aires in an informative process that Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Caggiano" title="Antonio Caggiano">Antonio Caggiano</a> opened on 6 September 1966 and later closed in a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> celebrated on 3 December 1973. Mesa became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> after the cause was launched on 6 September 1966. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> later validated the diocesan process on 20 February 1987, in Rome and received the official <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier from the postulation in 1987.<sup id="cite_ref-HC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Theologians provided their assent to the cause on 24 November 1987, as did the members of the C.C.S. on 12 April 1988. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved that Mesa had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 1 September 1988. The miracle needed for beatification was investigated and then validated in Rome on 25 February 1989, while a medical board later met on 3 October 1991, and approved it. Theologians also assented to it on 20 December 1991, as did the C.C.S. on 21 January 1992. John Paul II approved that the healing was, indeed, a miracle on 7 March 1992, and beatified Mesa in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 27 September 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-HC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The process for the miracle needed for full sainthood took place in <a href="/wiki/Cochabamba" title="Cochabamba">Cochabamba</a> in Bolivia from 17 April 2015, until 20 July 2015. Medical experts who examined the healing deemed it to be a miracle thus approving it on 22 September 2017. The theologians assessed and approved the miracle in a meeting held on the following 26 October. The cardinal and bishop members of the C.C.S. confirmed this miracle also on 16 January 2018. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> confirmed the healing to be a legitimate miracle on 26 January 2018; her canonization was celebrated in Saint Peter's Square on 14 October 2018.<sup id="cite_ref-14oct2018_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14oct2018-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Failed_miracle">Failed miracle</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nazaria_Ignacia_March_Mesa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Failed miracle">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The supposed miracle that could have led to canonization was investigated and then validated on 12 December 2003 though could not proceed further because the medical board disapproved of this in their meeting held on 3 July 2008. The postulation was informed of this failure and so the case was discarded since it was debunked.<sup id="cite_ref-HC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
