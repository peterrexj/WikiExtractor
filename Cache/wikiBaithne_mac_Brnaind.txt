</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Baithéne</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Abbot of Iona</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 534<br /><a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 596-598<br /><a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">9 June</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Baithéne mac Brénaind</b> (also known as <b>Saint Baoithin</b> and <b>Saint Buadán</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, one of Saint <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a>'s followers who accompanied him to <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> around 563, and was the first successor of the abbacy of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Tigernach" title="Annals of Tigernach">Annals of Tigernach</a> record his birth in 534, and his death was likely between 596 and 598 according also to the <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a>. Irish genealogical records indicate him to be the "son of Brendan, son of Fergus, son of <a href="/wiki/Conall_Gulban" title="Conall Gulban">Conall Gulban</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Niall_No%C3%ADgiallach" class="mw-redirect" title="Niall Noígiallach">Niall Noígiallach</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> thus being a member of the <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél Conaill">Cenél Conaill</a> branch of the <a href="/wiki/Northern_U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Northern Uí Néill">Northern Uí Néill</a>, as the abbots of Iona following the death of <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a> often were.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baithéne is still venerated in Ireland, but is most heavily associated with <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">county Donegal</a> where his cult was most active in <a href="/wiki/Taughboyne" title="Taughboyne">Taughboyne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Culdaff" title="Culdaff">Culdaff</a>. Celebrations of the saint in Donegal are ongoing, and a major celebration of the saint's 1400th death anniversary was celebrated in the parish of Taughboyne in 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Folklore about the saint from Donegal further refers to him using various variants of his name.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also evidence of Baithéne being venerated as Saint Bathan and Baodáin in Scotland as <a href="/wiki/Abbey_St_Bathans" title="Abbey St Bathans">Abbey St Bathans</a> in <a href="/wiki/Berwickshire" title="Berwickshire">Berwickshire</a> (south east Scotland) is named after him, as well as several other place names.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The earliest source for Baithéne is <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n_of_Iona" class="mw-redirect" title="Adomnán of Iona">Adomnán of Iona</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_Columba" title="Life of Columba">Life of Columba</a></i> (Vita Columbae), in which he is featured as Columba's close companion and successor. Two 12th-13th century lives of Baithéne also survive in the <a href="/wiki/Codex_Salmanticensis" title="Codex Salmanticensis">Codex Salmanticensis</a> and <i>Codex Insulensis</i> but are heavily influenced by Adomnán's life.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baithéne is recorded in the four major Irish martyrologies as sharing his feast day with Columba on 9 June.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Companion_to_Columba">Companion to Columba</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Baith%C3%A9ne_mac_Br%C3%A9naind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Companion to Columba">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Baithéne is heavily featured in Adomnán's <i>Vita Columbae</i>, where he is featured as Columba's trusted companion and chosen successor.<sup id="cite_ref-Adomnán,_Vita_Columbae_I.2_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adomnán,_Vita_Columbae_I.2-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He is called Columba's <i>alumnus</i>, which has variously been translated as 'foster-son' and 'disciple'.<sup id="cite_ref-Adomnán,_Vita_Columbae_I.2_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adomnán,_Vita_Columbae_I.2-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In one story, there was a very sinful man who came to Iona to request being a monk there, but Columba had foreseen how wicked this man was and said he should not be allowed to come. But when the man came, he said he wanted to see Columba, and Baithéne said that the man should be allowed to do penance and quoted the scriptures. To this, Columba responded that the man had murdered his brother and debauched his mother. When the man finally met Columba, Columba told him that he could do penance by living among the British for twelve years without returning to Ireland, but Columba foretold that he would not fulfill this and instead would return to his sinful ways and head to perdition. And the man did exactly as Columba foretold, going not to Britain but back to Ireland, where he was murdered.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In another story, Baithéne asked Columba to give him a monk to help him go through the psalter and look for mistakes. Columba told him that there was no mistake in the psalter except that the letter I was missing in one place. And they went through the psalter and found that it was just as Columba had said.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another time, Adomnán mentions that Baithéne once visited the island of Eigg.
</p><p>Baithéne also features on a list of twelve companions who followed Columba to Iona.<sup id="cite_ref-Adomnán&#39;s_Life_of_Columba_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adomnán&#39;s_Life_of_Columba-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The list gives Baithéne an alternative name 'Conin' and it mentions that he had a brother called Cobthach.<sup id="cite_ref-Adomnán&#39;s_Life_of_Columba_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Adomnán&#39;s_Life_of_Columba-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It has been suggested that these have been fabricated to create a biblical parallel.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Iona.2C_Tiree.2C_and_Hinba"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Iona,_Tiree,_and_Hinba">Iona, Tiree, and Hinba</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Baith%C3%A9ne_mac_Br%C3%A9naind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Iona, Tiree, and Hinba">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Baithéne's reign as abbot was fairly short as Columba died c. 597, and Baithéne's death is recorded as occurring c. 598, aged 66 according to the Annals of Clonmacnoise. No evident sources contemporaneous with Baithéne's abbacy survive.
</p><p>Baithéne served as prior over a monastery connected to Iona on the island of Tiree.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The name of the place where his monastery was located on Tiree was Mag Luinge or Campus Luinge. The monastery was a house for penitents from Iona. Some historians have thought that it may have supplied food to Iona.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adomnán recorded a story about a voyage he took to the island, when Columba first told a monk who was heading to Tiree that he should not sail directly from Iona to Tiree, because a great whale would frighten him, but his did not listen to his advice and took the direct route, and a huge whale came out of the water and almost destroyed the boat, which terrified those in the boat. When Baithéne departed the following day to Tiree, Columba told him about the whale, and Baithéne responded that both he and the whale were in God's power, to which Columba responded 'Go in peace, your faith in Christ will shield you from danger'. And so Baithéne then took the direct route and the whale came out of the water which terrified all in the boat, except Baithéne, who used his hands to bless the sea and the whale, and the whale then went down into the water.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another story that Adomnán recorded held that Baithéne and <a href="/wiki/Colm%C3%A1n_Elo" title="Colmán Elo">Colmán Elo</a> both approached Columba one time to ask him to pray for a favourable wind on their respective journeys to Tiree and Ireland. Columba said that in the morning the wind would be favourable to travel to Tiree and in the afternoon the Lord would change it to make it favourable to travel to Ireland. And it happened just as Columba said.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another story that Admonan recorded held that one time Columba encountered an army of demons on Iona, and he fought them with the help of angels, such that the demons fled from Iona. But after they fled, they then went to Tiree and attacked the monks there, including the monastery at Mag Luinge headed by Baithéne. While many in the other monasteries died from diseases, which were caused by the demons, only one monk in Baithéne's monastery died and the rest were protected because of their prayer and fasting.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baithéne spent time as prior of Hinba.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In one story, Adomnán claimed that Columba went to Hinba and relaxed the penitential rules on one occasion. However, one monk named Neman refused to abide by the relaxation. Columba rebuked him and said 'Neman, Baithéne and I have allowed a relaxation in the diet and you refuse it. But the time will come when in the company of thieves in the forest you will eat the flesh of a stolen mare'. This monk eventually left the monastery and went back into the world, and one day found himself among thieves eating such meat.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_10-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
