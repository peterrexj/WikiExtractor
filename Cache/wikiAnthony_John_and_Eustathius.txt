<p><b>Anthony, John, and Eustathius</b> (<i>Eustathios, Eustace</i>; Russian: Антоний, Иоанн and Евстафий; Lithuanian: Antanas, Jonas ir Eustachijus) are saints and martyrs (died 1347) of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>.  Their feast day is <a href="/wiki/April_14_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="April 14 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">celebrated on April 14</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Horologion" title="Horologion">horologion</a>.
</p><p>They were attached to the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow" title="Grand Duchy of Moscow">Muscovite</a> missionaries dispatched to the court of <a href="/wiki/Algirdas" title="Algirdas">Algirdas</a>, pagan <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_of_Lithuania" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duke of Lithuania">Grand Duke of Lithuania</a>. Algirdas was married to an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Christianity">Orthodox</a> Christian princess, <a href="/wiki/Maria_of_Vitebsk" title="Maria of Vitebsk">Maria of Vitebsk</a>, and the Orthodox were permitted only to minister to the religious needs of the princess. All outside proselytizing was forbidden.
</p><p>The three youths were arrested for preaching in public, and were ordered by Algirdas to consume meat in his presence during an Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">fasting period</a>. When they refused, they were tortured and executed. Their bodies were kept in a glass <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in a crypt chapel beneath the <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> church in the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_the_Holy_Spirit,_Vilnius" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of the Holy Spirit, Vilnius">Monastery of the Holy Spirit</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>, but has since been moved to the main sanctuary of the church. Their relics are said to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorruptible</a>.  They were added to the Roman Calendar by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1969.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
