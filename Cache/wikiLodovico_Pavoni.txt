<p><b>Lodovico Pavoni</b> (11 September 1784 – 1 April 1849) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> who administered in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> where he lived.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He paid close attention to the needs of males and was concerned with their education. He was to establish – in 1825 – his own religious congregation to assist in his mission: the Sons of Mary Immaculate which came to be known also as the "Pavoniani".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pavoni was beatified on 14 April 2002 and is the patron of his order. The second miracle needed for his sainthood received the official approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> in 2016; a date was determined on 20 June 2016 at a gathering of cardinals was celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 16 October 2016.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lodovico_Pavoni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:192px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg/190px-Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg" decoding="async" width="190" height="246" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg/285px-Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg/380px-Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2188" data-file-height="2831" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Mantella_canonicale_Lodovico_Pavoni.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pavoni's canonical cloak in a Brescia museum.</div></div></div>
<p>Lodovico Pavoni was born in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> on 11 September 1794 as the first of five brothers to Alessandro Pavoni and Lelia Poncarali.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pavoni was a bright child and took a keen interest in the world around him while responding to the social problems of his time. He was skilled at painting and hunting and was also good at horseback riding.
</p><p>He received his theological formation at the home of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominican</a> Father Carlo Domenico Ferrari – the future <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a> – while suffering a stall in his studies due to the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_era" title="Napoleonic era">Napoleonic era</a> in which the <a href="/wiki/French_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="French Emperor">French Emperor</a> closed <a href="/wiki/Seminaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminaries">seminaries</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in 1807. Pavoni was appointed as an assistant to Bishop Gabrio Nava in 1812 and was named as a church rector on 16 March 1818 – he was assigned to the Church of Saint Barnabas.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1818 he received permission to found an orphanage and a vocational school that in 1821 would transform into the "Institute of Saint Barnabas". He decided that the first trade of this school would be in the field of book publishing and so in 1823 set up "The Publishing House of Saint Barnabas" – the precursor to the present Ancora press.
</p><p>He opened a center for males and expanded it into a hostel in 1821 for their shelter and to include a school to teach them a particular trade which included programs to instruct them in being carpenters and silversmiths in addition to teaching them how to be blacksmiths shoemakers while focusing also on dye making.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He extended this to deaf and mute people in 1823. He also purchased a farm around this time to set up an agricultural section for the school.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pavoni founded his own congregation of priests and brothers in 1825 known as the Sons of Mary Immaculate, commonly referred to as the "Pavonians". <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> granted his official assent to the congregation being in Brescia on 31 March 1843 after having received approval from officials in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Roman Curia</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Emperor_of_Austria" title="Emperor of Austria">Emperor of Austria</a> provided imperial assent on 9 December 1846. On 8 December 1847 he and the first members of the congregation made their religious profession following diocesan approval and canonical erection from the vice capitular of the diocese of Brescia Mgr. Luchi.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> epidemic in 1836 prompted him to tend to its victims and to take in more men. On 3 June 1844 he was decorated with the title of <a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Iron_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Iron Crown">Knight of the Iron Crown</a> from the Emperor of Austria <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Austria" title="Ferdinand I of Austria">Ferdinand I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1849 he did his best to assist citizens who were in the middle of a cholera epidemic around the time of the <a href="/wiki/Ten_Days_of_Brescia" title="Ten Days of Brescia">Ten-Days conflict</a> between <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and Brescia where the latter rebelled against the former. Austrians prepared to pillage Brescia and so Pavoni led the men under his care to the novitiate located on the hill of Saiano.
</p><p>Pavoni died at dawn on 1 April 1849 in Saiano – <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a> – as the conflict endured and Brescia in flames.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The order that he established went on to receive full papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 24 September 1882 (and its constitutions in 1897) and went on to inspire fellow priests such as <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">Giovanni Bosco</a> and <a href="/wiki/Leonardo_Murialdo" title="Leonardo Murialdo">Leonardo Murialdo</a>.
</p><p>The order that Pavoni established flourished across the globe and numbers 210 members in a total of six nations including <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a>. As of 2008 there were 34 houses and out of the 210 religious there were 107 of them being priests.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lodovico_Pavoni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Process_and_beatification">Process and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lodovico_Pavoni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Process and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg/250px-Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg/375px-Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg/500px-Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Brescia_-_Chiesa_di_santa_Maria_Immacolata_-_Tomba_Pavoni_2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb in Brescia.</div></div></div>
<p>The beatification process commenced in an informative process that opened in Brescia on 11 February 1908 and concluded its business on 7 October 1912; a team of theologians collated his writings and declared them to be in line with the faith on 12 April 1916.
</p><p>The formal introduction for the cause came on 12 March 1919 after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> made Pavoni known as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>: the first official stage in the process.
</p><p>The informative process received the validation of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Rites">Congregation of Rites</a> at the conclusion of a second process on two occasions on 23 June 1926 and 21 April 1942 while passing it to several committees for further assessment.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> declared that Pavoni had lived a model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and declared him to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 5 June 1947. The promulgated decree cites Pavoni as being the "... other <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a> ... precursor of <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">Giovanni Bosco</a> ... perfect emulator of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Giuseppe_Cottolengo" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Giuseppe Cottolengo">Saint Giuseppe Cottolengo</a>".
</p><p>The miracle that would be investigated for his beatification was the 1909 healing of Maria Stevani who suffered from extreme <a href="/wiki/Typhoid_fever" title="Typhoid fever">typhoid fever</a> and was vomiting and confined to her bed. She kept a relic of Pavoni under her pillow and was in a deep sleep on one night before being completely cured of her ailment in a case ruled as miraculous.
</p><p>This presumed miracle attributed to his intercession was investigated in <a href="/wiki/Cremona" title="Cremona">Cremona</a> from 25 June 1925 to 3 August 1926 and was ratified decades later on 26 January 2001. The medical board voiced their assent to the miracle on 7 June 2001 while consulting theologians did so as well on 26 October 2001. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> also approved the healing on 4 December 2001.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved the healing to be a credible miracle on 20 December 2001 and beatified Pavoni on 14 April 2002.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_miracle">Second miracle</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lodovico_Pavoni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Second miracle">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The second miracle that would be used for his canonization involved the July 2009 healing of Honorio Lopes Martins in <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>. His son Diomar – of Pavoni's order – requested a chain of prayer to Pavoni for a cure and his father was cured at the month's end.
</p><p>The diocesan process for the investigation of the alleged miracle was opened on 4 December 2012 – with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Odilo_Pedro_Scherer" class="mw-redirect" title="Odilo Pedro Scherer">Odilo Pedro Scherer</a> presiding over its inauguration – and concluded its work on 18 June 2013. A supplementary process was opened on 10 April 2014 and concluded on 26 August 2014.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated the previous processes on 12 December 2014. A medical board approved it on 10 March 2016 with theologians to follow on 5 April 2016; the C.C.S. approved it on 3 May 2016 and passed it to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> who approved the healing to be a legitimate miracle on 9 May 2016.
</p><p>A date was set for his canonization at a gathering of cardinals on 20 June 2016. Pavoni was canonized as a saint on 16 October 2016.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Postulation">Postulation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lodovico_Pavoni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Postulation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Father Pietro Riva was the <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> of the cause at the time of the canonization.
</p>
