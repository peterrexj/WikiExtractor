<p><b>Józef Kowalski</b> (13 March 1911 – 4 July 1942) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest from the <a href="/wiki/Salesian_Society" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesian Society">Salesian Society</a> murdered at the <a href="/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp" title="Auschwitz concentration camp">Auschwitz concentration camp</a> during World War II.<sup id="cite_ref-theguardian_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-theguardian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a> on 13 June 1999.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=J%C3%B3zef_Kowalski_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Józef Kowalski was born in <a href="/wiki/Siedliska,_Rzesz%C3%B3w_County" title="Siedliska, Rzeszów County">Siedliska</a> (<a href="/wiki/Partitioned_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Partitioned Poland">partitioned Poland</a>) on 13 March 1911 to Wojciech and Zofia Borowiec, the seventh of their nine children.<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco-Kowalski_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-Kowalski-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained a priest on 29 May 1938 in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a> as member of the <a href="/wiki/Salesian" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesian">Salesian</a> Religious Society,<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco-Kowalski_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-Kowalski-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and took up a post of secretary to the Salesian provincial.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/German_occupation_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="German occupation of Poland">German occupation of Poland</a> the Salesians continued their educational work. The <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a> arrested Kowalski on 23 May 1941, along with eleven other Salesians who worked in Kraków. They were taken to <a href="/wiki/Montelupich_Prison" title="Montelupich Prison">Montelupich Prison</a> and tortured.<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Auschwitz_concentration_camp">Auschwitz concentration camp</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=J%C3%B3zef_Kowalski_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Auschwitz concentration camp">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
Kowalski was sent to Auschwitz <a href="/wiki/Concentration_camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Concentration camp">concentration camp</a> on 26 June 1941 (prisoner number 17,350 or 17,950).<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Neville_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Neville-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> There, he ministered secretly to his fellow prisoners in Block 25, and attempted to strengthen their will to survive day-to-day life in the camp. He <a href="/wiki/Absolution" title="Absolution">absolved</a> condemned victims,<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> usually in secret, but at least once in front of everyone at the moment of mass execution.<sup id="cite_ref-Wysocki_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wysocki-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was known at Auschwitz simply as Father Józef. At one roll call, he was ordered by Blockführer Gerhard Palitzsch to trample upon his <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> when he was discovered with it. Kowalski refused. As punishment, he was assigned to a penal company.  In his last letter to his parents, Józef wrote:<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Do not worry about me; I am in God's hands. I want to assure you that I feel His help at every step. Despite the present situation, I am happy and completely at peace.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>On 3 July 1942 he was mocked, ridiculed and severely beaten by the guards for being a priest. The same night, his oppressors pulled him out of his barracks, gravely beat him outside and possibly drowned him. Kowalski's body was found the next day and burned with others.<sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco-Kowalski_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-Kowalski-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was 31 years of age.<sup id="cite_ref-Neville_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Neville-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Poles began to venerate his memory after World War II. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> had known <a href="/wiki/Minister_(Christianity)#Father" title="Minister (Christianity)">Father</a> Kowalski personally before the war, when Kowalski lived and served with the Parish of St. <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_Kostka" title="Stanislaus Kostka">Stanisław Kostka</a> in <a href="/wiki/D%C4%99bniki,_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Dębniki, Kraków">Dębniki, Kraków</a>. During one of his return visits to Poland, John Paul II beatified Kowalski in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a> at a ceremonial three-hour mass of 13 June 1999, attended by President <a href="/wiki/Aleksander_Kwa%C5%9Bniewski" title="Aleksander Kwaśniewski">Aleksander Kwaśniewski</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-theguardian_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-theguardian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Don_Bosco_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Don_Bosco-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in front of 600,000 people.<sup id="cite_ref-theguardian_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-theguardian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Kowalski was <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">one of 108 Polish Martyrs from World War II</a> beatified by the Pope on that day.<sup id="cite_ref-Neville_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Neville-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
