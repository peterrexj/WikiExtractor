<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Before_the_Arab_Conquest">Before the Arab Conquest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_venerated_persons_from_Africa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Before the Arab Conquest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the first centuries of the Catholic Church, Africa produced many of her leading lights.  The Catholic presence in Africa was weakened by the schism following the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a> which resulted in the separation between the Catholic and Coptic Orthodox Church, and even more so by the rise of Islam.  Following the Arab conquest of northern Africa, the Catholic Church was largely absent from the continent before modern times, although the Coptic, and later Ethiopic, Orthodox Churches remained.  The following are some of the notable saints from the first to seventh centuries, though it is a very incomplete list.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_times">Modern times</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_venerated_persons_from_Africa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Modern times">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It would be difficult to say who the first saint to be associated with Africa after the Arab conquest would be.  <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> famously went on a mission to Egypt in 1219.  <a href="/wiki/Berard_of_Carbio" title="Berard of Carbio">Berardo</a>, Ottone, Pietro, Accursio, Adiuto, martyrs in Morocco (1220).  <a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Fasanella&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Daniel Fasanella (page does not exist)">Daniel Fasanella</a>, Samuele, Angelo, Leone, Niccolò, Ugolino, Domno, martyrs in Morocco (1227).  <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a> died in Tunisia en route from the Holy Land in 1270.  But after the canonization of saints came to be reserved to the papacy around AD 1000, and especially after the establishment of the Congregation of Rites in 1588, the list of official saints with African connections is more clear.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_proposed_causes">Other proposed causes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_venerated_persons_from_Africa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Other proposed causes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Others have been proposed for beatification, and may have active groups supporting their causes. These include:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Anna_Ali" title="Anna Ali">Anna Ali</a>, religious in the order of The Most Holy Eucharist and reported mystic. (Kenya) The bishop of the Catholic Diocese of Eldoret has appointed a task force to investigate the life of Anna Ali. Hundreds of pilgrims visit her burial place every year to commemorate the nun who is reported to have received messages from Jesus for 25 years and to have photographed him.</li></ul>
