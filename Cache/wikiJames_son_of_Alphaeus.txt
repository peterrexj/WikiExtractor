<p><b>James, son of Alphaeus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Koine_Greek" title="Koine Greek">Greek</a>: Ἰάκωβος, <i>Iakōbos</i>; <a href="/wiki/Aramaic" title="Aramaic">Aramaic</a>: ܝܥܩܘܒ ܒܪ ܚܠܦܝ;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: <span lang="he" dir="rtl">יעקב בן חלפי</span> <i>Ya'akov ben Halfai</i>; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop">ⲓⲁⲕⲱⲃⲟⲥ ⲛⲧⲉ ⲁⲗⲫⲉⲟⲥ</span>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">يعقوب بن حلفى</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">Ya'qūb bin Halfā</i></span>) was one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">Twelve Apostles</a> of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>, appearing under this name in all three of the <a href="/wiki/Synoptic_Gospels" title="Synoptic Gospels">Synoptic Gospels</a>' lists of the apostles. He is generally identified with <a href="/wiki/James_the_Less" title="James the Less">James the Less</a> (Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἰάκωβος ὁ μικρός</span></span> <i>Iakōbos ho mikros</i>, <a href="/wiki/Mark_15:40" class="mw-redirect" title="Mark 15:40">Mark 15:40</a>) and commonly known by that name in church tradition. He is also labelled "the Minor", "the Little", "the Lesser", or "the Younger", according to translation. He is distinct from <a href="/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee" class="mw-redirect" title="James, son of Zebedee">James, son of Zebedee</a> and in some interpretations also from <a href="/wiki/James,_brother_of_Jesus" title="James, brother of Jesus">James, brother of Jesus</a> (James the Just).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He appears only four times in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>, each time in a list of the twelve apostles.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identity">Identity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Identity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Possible_identity_with_James_the_Less">Possible identity with James the Less</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Possible identity with James the Less">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>James, son of <a href="/wiki/Alphaeus" title="Alphaeus">Alphaeus</a> is often identified with James the Less, who is only mentioned four times in the Bible, each time in connection with his mother. (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2015:40&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 15:40</a>) refers to "Mary the mother of James the younger and of <a href="/wiki/Joses" title="Joses">Joses</a>", while (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2016:1&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 16:1</a>) and (<a href="/wiki/Matthew_27:56" class="mw-redirect" title="Matthew 27:56">Matthew 27:56</a>) refer to "Mary the mother of James".
</p><p>Since there was already another James (James, son of Zebedee) among the twelve apostles, equating James son of Alphaeus with "James the Less" made sense. (James son of Zebedee was sometimes called "James the Greater").
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> identifies James, son of Alpheus with James the Less writing in his work called <i><a href="/wiki/The_Perpetual_Virginity_of_Blessed_Mary" title="The Perpetual Virginity of Blessed Mary">The Perpetual Virginity of Blessed Mary</a></i> the following:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Do you intend the comparatively unknown James the Less, who is called in Scripture the son of Mary, not however of Mary the mother of our Lord, to be an apostle, or not? If he is an apostle, he must be the son of Alphæus and a believer in Jesus, "For neither did his brethren believe in him."</p></blockquote>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The only conclusion is that the Mary who is described as the mother of James the Less was the wife of Alphæus and sister of Mary the Lord's mother, the one who is called by John the Evangelist "Mary of Clopas".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/Papias_of_Hierapolis" title="Papias of Hierapolis">Papias of Hierapolis</a>, who lived circa 70–163 AD, in the surviving fragments of his work <i>Exposition of the Sayings of the Lord</i> relates that Mary, wife of Alphaeus is mother of James the Less:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Mary, mother of James the Less and Joseph, wife of Alphaeus was the sister of Mary the mother of the Lord, whom John names of Cleophas, either from her father or from the family of the clan, or for some other reason.<sup id="cite_ref-papias_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-papias-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Therefore, James, son of Alphaeus would be the same as James the Less.
</p><p>Modern Biblical scholars are divided on whether this identification is correct. <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_Meier" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul Meier">John Paul Meier</a> finds it unlikely.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Amongst evangelicals, the <i>New Bible Dictionary</i> supports the traditional identification,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> while <a href="/wiki/Don_Carson" class="mw-redirect" title="Don Carson">Don Carson</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and Darrell Bock<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> both regard the identification as possible, but not certain.
</p>

<h3><span id="Possible_identification_with_James.2C_the_brother_of_Jesus"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Possible_identification_with_James,_the_brother_of_Jesus">Possible identification with James, the brother of Jesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Possible identification with James, the brother of Jesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, apparently voicing the general opinion of <a href="/wiki/Early_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Church">Early Church</a>, maintains the doctrine of <a href="/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary" title="Perpetual virginity of Mary">perpetual virginity of Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODCC:brethren_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODCC:brethren-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He proposed that James, son of Alphaeus, was to be identified with "James, the brother of the Lord" (<a href="/wiki/Galatians_1:19" class="mw-redirect" title="Galatians 1:19">Galatians 1:19</a>) and that the term "brother" was to be understood as "cousin."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The view of Jerome, the "Hieronymian view," became widely accepted in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> while <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic">Eastern Catholics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestants</a> tend to distinguish between the two. <a href="/wiki/John_Cunningham_Geikie" title="John Cunningham Geikie">Geike</a> (1884) states that <a href="/wiki/Adolf_Hausrath" class="mw-redirect" title="Adolf Hausrath">Hausrath</a>, <a href="/wiki/Franz_Delitzsch" title="Franz Delitzsch">Delitzsch</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Schenkel" title="Daniel Schenkel">Schenkel</a> think James the brother of Jesus was the son of Clophas-Alphaeus.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In two small but potentially important works ascribed by some to <a href="/wiki/Hippolytus_of_Rome" title="Hippolytus of Rome">Hippolytus</a>, <i>On the Twelve Apostles of Christ</i> and <i>On the <a href="/wiki/Seventy_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Seventy Apostles">Seventy Apostles</a> of Christ,</i> he relates the following:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>And James the son of Alphaeus, when preaching in Jerusalem was stoned to death by the Jews, and was buried there beside the temple.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>It is important to remember that <a href="/wiki/James_the_brother_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="James the brother of Jesus">James, the brother of Jesus</a> is attributed the same death; he was stoned to death by the Jews too. This testimony of "Hippolytus", if authentic, would increase the plausibility that James the son of Alphaeus is the same person as James the brother of Jesus.
</p><p>These two works of "Hippolytus" are often neglected because the manuscripts were lost during most of the church age and then found in Greece in the 19th century. As most scholars consider them spurious, they are often ascribed to "<span class="nowrap"><b>Pseudo-Hippolytus</b></span>". The two are included in an appendix to the works of Hippolytus in the voluminous collection of Early Church Fathers.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the surviving fragments of the work <i>Exposition of the Sayings of the Lord</i> by <a href="/wiki/Papias_of_Hierapolis" title="Papias of Hierapolis">Papias of Hierapolis</a> Cleophas and Alphaeus are the same person, Mary wife of Cleophas or Alphaeus would be the mother of James, the brother of Jesus, and of Simon and Judas (Thaddeus), and of one Joseph.
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>(1) Mary the mother of the Lord; (2) Mary the wife of Cleophas or Alphaeus, who was the mother of James the bishop and apostle, and of Simon and Thaddeus, and of one Joseph; (3) Mary Salome, wife of Zebedee, mother of John the evangelist and James; (4) Mary Magdalene. These four are found in the Gospel...<i>(Fragment X)</i><sup id="cite_ref-papias_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-papias-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Thus, James, the brother of the Lord would be the son of Alphaeus, who is the husband of Mary of Cleophas or Mary the wife of Alphaeus. However, the Anglican theologian <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Lightfoot" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph Lightfoot">J.B. Lightfoot</a> maintains that the fragment in question is spurious.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As reported by <a href="/wiki/The_Golden_Legend" class="mw-redirect" title="The Golden Legend">the Golden Legend</a>, which is a collection of <a href="/wiki/Hagiographies" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographies">hagiographies</a> compiled by <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a> in the thirteenth century:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>James the Apostle is said the Less, how well that was the elder of age than was St. James the More. He was called also the brother of our Lord, because I have resembled much well our Lord in body, in visage, and of manner. He was called <a href="/wiki/James_the_Just" class="mw-redirect" title="James the Just">James the Just</a> for his right great holiness. He was also called James the son of Alpheus. He sang in Jerusalem the first mass that ever was there, and he was first bishop of Jerusalem.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Possible_brother_of_Matthew">Possible brother of Matthew</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Possible brother of Matthew">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Alphaeus" title="Alphaeus">Alphaeus</a> is also the name of the father of the tax-collector Levi mentioned in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%202:14&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 2:14</a>. The publican appears as <a href="/wiki/Matthew_the_Evangelist" class="mw-redirect" title="Matthew the Evangelist">Matthew</a> in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%209:9&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 9:9</a>, which has led some to conclude that James and Matthew might have been brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The four times that James son of Alphaeus is mentioned directly in the Bible (each time in the list of the Apostles) the only family relationship stated is that his father is Alphaeus.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> In two lists of the Apostles, the other James and John are listed as brothers and that their father is Zebedee.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Gospel_of_Mark">Gospel of Mark</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Gospel of Mark">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id="Calling_of_James.2C_son_of_Alphaeus"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Calling_of_James,_son_of_Alphaeus">Calling of James, son of Alphaeus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Calling of James, son of Alphaeus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p><a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Mark the Evangelist</a> is the earliest known source in the Bible to mention "James, son of Alphaeus" as one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">twelve Apostles</a>. <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Mark the Evangelist</a> mentions a "James, son of Alphaeus" only once and this is in his list of the <a href="/wiki/12_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="12 Apostles">12 Apostles</a> (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%203:16–19&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 3:16–19</a>). At the beginning of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>' ministry he first calls <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and his brother <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Andrew</a> and asks them to follow him (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%201:16–17&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 1:16–17</a>). In the next verses it tells the story of how <a href="/wiki/James_the_Greater" class="mw-redirect" title="James the Greater">James the Greater</a> and his brother <a href="/wiki/John_the_Apostle" title="John the Apostle">John the Apostle</a> came to follow Jesus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%201:19–20&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 1:19–20</a>). After some healing by Jesus he meets Levi son of <a href="/wiki/Alphaeus" title="Alphaeus">Alphaeus</a> who was a tax collector and he then asks Levi (better known as Matthew) to follow him (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%202:14&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 2:14</a> and <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%209:9&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 9:9</a>). Peter, Andrew, James the Greater and John the Apostle are listed as Apostles (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%203:16–19&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 3:16–19</a>). Levi, son of Alphaeus is listed as an Apostle under the name of Matthew and James alone is listed as the son of Alphaeus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%203:16–19&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 3:16–19</a>).
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Ambiguous_Jameses">Ambiguous Jameses</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Ambiguous Jameses">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Overall, <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Mark the Evangelist</a> lists three different Jameses: "James, son of Alphaeus", James the Greater and James the brother of Jesus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%206:3&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 6:3</a>). On three separate occasions he writes about a James without clarifying which James he is referring to. There is a James at the transfiguration, <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%209:2&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 9:2</a>, at the <a href="/wiki/Mount_of_Olives" title="Mount of Olives">Mount of Olives</a>, <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2013:3&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 13:3</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Garden_of_Gethsemane" class="mw-redirect" title="Garden of Gethsemane">Garden of Gethsemane</a>, <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2014:33&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 14:33</a>. Although this James is listed alongside John the Apostle, a clear distinction isn't made about which <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostle</a> James is being referred to, even when both Apostles are meant to be in a similar location. All twelve Apostles attend the Last Supper (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2014:33&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 14:33</a>) which immediately precedes the <a href="/wiki/Garden_of_Gethsemane" class="mw-redirect" title="Garden of Gethsemane">Garden of Gethsemane</a>. There is a reference to Mary mother of James the Younger and Joseph (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%2015:40&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 15:40</a>); however, Mark the Evangelist has already told us that James the brother of Jesus has a brother called Joseph (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%206:3&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 6:3</a>).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Gospel_of_Matthew">Gospel of Matthew</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Gospel of Matthew">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id="Calling_of_James.2C_son_of_Alphaeus_2"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Calling_of_James,_son_of_Alphaeus_2">Calling of James, son of Alphaeus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Calling of James, son of Alphaeus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Peter, Andrew, James, son of Zebedee and his brother John were all called to follow Jesus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%204:18–22&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 4:18–22</a>). In a story that parallels the calling of Levi, son of Alphaeus,<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Matthew is called to follow Jesus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%209:9–13&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 9:9–13</a>). Matthew is never referred directly to as being the Son of Alphaeus in the Gospel of Matthew or any other book in the Bible,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> but as Levi, Son of Alphaeus (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Mark%202:14&amp;version=nrsv">Mark 2:14</a>). In Mark he is regarded as a tax collector (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%209:9&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 9:9</a>). In <a href="/wiki/The_Gospel_of_Matthew" class="mw-redirect" title="The Gospel of Matthew">the Gospel of Matthew</a> the tax collector (Matthew) called to follow Jesus is listed as one of the twelve Apostles. James, son of Alphaeus is also listed as one of the 12 Apostles (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2010:3&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 10:3</a>).
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Ambiguous_Jameses_2">Ambiguous Jameses</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Ambiguous Jameses">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Matthew doesn't mention any James in his Gospel that isn't identified without association to his family. There are 3 James that are mentioned by Matthew; James, Brother of Jesus, Joseph, Simon and Judas (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2013:55&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 13:55</a>), James son of Zebedee and brother of John (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2010:2&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 10:2</a>) and James, son of Alphaeus. At the <a href="/wiki/Transfiguration_of_Jesus" title="Transfiguration of Jesus">Transfiguration</a> it is specified that the James is brother of John (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2017:1&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 17:1</a>) and at the <a href="/wiki/Garden_of_Gethsemane" class="mw-redirect" title="Garden of Gethsemane">Garden of Gethsemane</a> it is specified that it is the son of Zebedee (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2026:37&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 26:37</a>). It is not specified by Matthew that there was a James at the <a href="/wiki/Mount_of_Olives" title="Mount of Olives">Mount of Olives</a>; he mentions only <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciples</a> (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2024:3&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 24:3</a>). Matthew also mentions a Mary the mother of James and Joseph who was at the crucifixion. This James is not given the epithet the younger (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2027:56&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 27:56</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James,_son_of_Alphaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A James was arrested along with some other Christians and was executed by King <a href="/wiki/Herod_Agrippa" title="Herod Agrippa">Herod Agrippa</a> in his persecution of the church (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Acts%2012:1&amp;version=nrsv">Acts 12:1,2</a>). However, the James in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Acts%2012:1&amp;version=nrsv">Acts 12:1,2</a> has a brother called John. James, son of Zebedee has a brother called John (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%204:21&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 4:21</a>) and we are never explicitly told that James son of Alphaeus has a brother. Robert Eisenman <sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> and Achille Camerlynck<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> both suggest that the death of James in Acts 12:1–2 is James, son of Zebedee and not James son of Alphaeus.
</p><p>In Christian art, James the Less is depicted holding a <a href="/wiki/Fulling" title="Fulling">fuller's</a> club.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> One tradition maintains that he was crucified at <a href="/wiki/Ostrakine" title="Ostrakine">Ostrakine</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a>, where he was preaching the Gospel.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
