<p><b>Altmann</b> (c.1015 – 8 August 1091) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Passau" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Passau">Bishop of Passau</a> from 1065 until his death. He was an important representative of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_reforms" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian reforms">Gregorian reforms</a>, monastic founder and reformer. He is venerated as a saint,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> but not officially canonised.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Altmann_(bishop_of_Passau)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born between 1013 and 1020 in <a href="/wiki/Westphalia" title="Westphalia">Westphalia</a> to a family of the greater nobility of <a href="/wiki/Saxony" title="Saxony">Saxony</a>. He was educated at the cathedral school at <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a>, of which he later became director.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was also a <a href="/wiki/Prebendary" title="Prebendary">prebendary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a> between 1056 and 1065, court chaplain to <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Henry III</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Goslar" title="Goslar">Goslar</a>.
</p><p>In 1065 he succeeded <a href="/wiki/Egilbert,_Bishop_of_Passau" title="Egilbert, Bishop of Passau">Egilbert</a> as Bishop of Passau and began reforms of the clergy. As bishop he was famous for his care of the poor, his vigor in the reformation of relaxed monasteries, and the building of new ones.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He founded <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Nikola_in_Passau" title="Abbey of St. Nikola in Passau">St. Nicholas' Abbey</a> in Passau in 1070 as a monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Canons Regular</a>, and <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B6ttweig_Abbey" title="Göttweig Abbey">Göttweig Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austria</a> in 1083, later converted into a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in 1094. 
</p><p>In 1074 he announced the reforms of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>, whom he supported in the subsequent <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a>. Altmann was the most zealous promoter of the Church reform in the German lands.<sup id="cite_ref-MOE_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1076, along with the Archbishop of Salzburg, <a href="/wiki/Gebhard_of_Salzburg" title="Gebhard of Salzburg">Gebhard von Helfenstein</a> (who had consecrated Altmann as a bishop), he did not take part in the <a href="/wiki/Reichstag_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reichstag (Holy Roman Empire)">Reichstag</a> of <a href="/wiki/Worms,_Germany" title="Worms, Germany">Worms</a>, and supported the counter-king <a href="/wiki/Rudolf_of_Swabia" class="mw-redirect" title="Rudolf of Swabia">Rudolf of Swabia</a>. He was expelled from Passau by Emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>, who laid the city to waste in 1077/1078. The princely rights over the town of Passau were lost, the king lent them to the Burggrave Ulrich, whom he had employed. These were to be returned to the bishops only after the death of the Burggrave in 1099.
</p><p>Altmann took part in the Fastensynodes 1079 and 1080 in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, was appointed <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">Papal legate</a> for Germany, and was able to win the Margrave <a href="/wiki/Leopold_II,_Margrave_of_Austria" title="Leopold II, Margrave of Austria">Leopold II of Austria</a> over to the Papal party. In 1085 the Emperor deposed him as Bishop of Passau, after which he spent most of his time in the territory of the Austrian margrave, where he reformed the existing monasteries of <a href="/wiki/St._Florian%27s_Priory" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Florian&#39;s Priory">St. Florian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kremsm%C3%BCnster_Abbey" title="Kremsmünster Abbey">Kremsmünster Abbey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Melk_Abbey" title="Melk Abbey">Melk</a> and St. Pölten Abbey, improved the parish church organisation, and had stone churches built at all of them. His influence on the government of the margraviate was at times so strong that he was called the "leader" of Margrave Leopold II.<sup id="cite_ref-MOE_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He died in <a href="/wiki/Zeiselmauer" class="mw-redirect" title="Zeiselmauer">Zeiselmauer</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austria</a> and was buried in the monastery of Göttweig Abbey. He is venerated as a saint, although no official canonization has ever taken place. His feast day is 8 August.
</p><p>The <i>Vita</i> of Altmann of Passau was written by an anonymous monk of Göttweig some fifty years after the bishop's death.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
