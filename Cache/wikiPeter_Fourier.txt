<p><b>Peter Fourier</b> (French: <i lang="fr">Pierre Fourier</i>, <small>pronounced&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[fuʁje]</a></span>; 30 November 1565&#160;&#8211;&#32;9 December 1640) was a French <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a> who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Foregoing offers of high office, he served for many years as an exemplary pastor in the village of <a href="/wiki/Mattaincourt" title="Mattaincourt">Mattaincourt</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Vosges" title="Vosges">Vosges</a>. He was a strong proponent of free education and also helped to found a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canoness" title="Canoness">canonesses</a> regular dedicated to the care of poor children, developing a new <a href="/wiki/Pedagogy" title="Pedagogy">pedagogy</a> for this.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Fourier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fourier was born on 30 November 1565 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Mirecourt" title="Mirecourt">Mirecourt</a>, in what was then the <a href="/wiki/Lorraine_(duchy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorraine (duchy)">Duchy of Lorraine</a>, a part of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> (now the <a href="/wiki/French_department" class="mw-redirect" title="French department">French department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vosges_(department)" title="Vosges (department)">Vosges</a>), which was a bulwark of the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a>. He was the eldest of the three sons of a cloth merchant and his wife, who were faithful Catholics. At the age of 15, his father enrolled him in the new <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> University of <a href="/wiki/Pont-%C3%A0-Mousson" title="Pont-à-Mousson">Pont-à-Mousson</a> (eventually merged into the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Lorraine" title="University of Lorraine">University of Lorraine</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Allaria_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allaria-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1585 Fourier was admitted to the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canons regular</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">Abbey</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chaumousey" title="Chaumousey">Chaumousey</a>, where he made his <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profession of vows</a> two years later. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a> at <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>, at the extraordinary age of 23, on 24 February 1589.<sup id="cite_ref-pingaud_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pingaud-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> then sent him back to the University of Pont-à-Mousson to further his studies. He became a <a href="/wiki/Scholastic_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Scholastic theology">scholastic theologian</a> who knew the <i><a href="/wiki/Summa_Theologica" title="Summa Theologica">Summa Theologica</a></i> by heart,<sup id="cite_ref-Allaria_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allaria-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and earned the great respect of both the university officials and the <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">Count</a>-<a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Metz">Bishop of Metz</a>, who offered him a high <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> post. Fourier chose, instead, to return to his abbey.
</p><p>After his return to his canonical community, however, he was subjected to two years of hostility and abuse by his less fervent fellow canons,<sup id="cite_ref-Weninger_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weninger-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> including. by some accounts, attempted poisoning. He chose not to confront his <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> with the situation and accepted this persecution patiently. The care of local parishes in that region of France was routinely entrusted to the many abbeys and priories of canons. In 1597, when his abbot was assigning him a post, Fourier passed over two prestigious options and accepted the post of <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> of the parish of Mattaincourt in order to combat the indifference to religion widespread in the town, and to counter nascent <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinism</a> in the area. He went on to spend the next twenty years of his life serving its people.<sup id="cite_ref-Allaria_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allaria-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To this end, Fourier instituted two major reforms that showed his intelligence and concern for his flock. The first of these was to improve the financial lives of his community by setting up a community bank, from which the townspeople could borrow without interest. His motto in serving the parish was <i>to feed only one person was to of use to all</i>. His second innovation was in his preaching style, where he employed <a href="/wiki/Dialogue" title="Dialogue">dialogues</a> with small groups of his parishioners to explain better their Catholic faith to them. He had his pupils engage in <a href="/wiki/Dialectic" title="Dialectic">dialectics</a> on Sundays on the various virtues and vices in practice by the congregation. This style proved immensely successful.<sup id="cite_ref-pingaud_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pingaud-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fourier led an extremely <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> way of life while serving the people of his parish. He would spend much of the night in prayer. He refused the services of a housekeeper, even when his own stepmother offered to provide his care. His severe self-denial enabled him to direct much of the income of the parish to the needy of the town. He himself would often spend the night nursing the sick of the town.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reformer_and_founder">Reformer and founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Fourier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reformer and founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The success of Fourier's pastorate in inspiring his flock to a greater fidelity to the faith was brought to the attention of the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholicism)">bishops</a> of the region. They prevailed upon him to go about to different parishes to preach to the people. He did so and, as a result of seeing the situation of the populace throughout the region, he was struck by the depths of their ignorance and superstition.<sup id="cite_ref-pingaud_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pingaud-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_(8).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG/200px-Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="412" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG/300px-Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG/400px-Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_%288%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1453" data-file-height="2995" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coulaures_%C3%A9glise_vitrail_(8).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">Stained glass</a> window depicting St. Peter Fourier, C.R.S.A., with the white <i>sarozium</i> of a <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a><br />Church of Saint Martin<br /><a href="/wiki/Coulaures" title="Coulaures">Coulaures</a>, Dordogne, France</div></div></div>
<p>Together with <a href="/wiki/Alix_Le_Clerc" title="Alix Le Clerc">Alix Le Clerc</a>, in 1597, Fourier founded the Congregation of Notre Dame of <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular#Canonesses_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">Canonesses Regular of St. Augustine</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who were committed to the free education of children, taking a <a href="/wiki/Fourth_vow" title="Fourth vow">fourth vow</a> to that goal. Soon there were six schools run by his spiritual daughters. He played an active role in their education, being credited with the invention of the blackboard and its use in the classroom, as well as the division of students into classes of a similar level of instruction. By the time of his death, the number of schools run by the canonesses had grown to forty. They went on to spread their work throughout France, Germany and England.
</p><p>Fourier's vision also extended to the life of his own Order. He sought to revive a spirit of fervor and discipline in the communities of the canons regular. In 1621 the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Toul" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Toul">Bishop of Toul</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Toul#Bishops" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Diocese of Toul">Jean des Porcellets</a>, chose him to organize the canonical communities in his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a>. He therefore entrusted the ancient Abbey of St. Remy in that city to Fourier and six companions, where they could lead the way of life he envisioned. Within four years, eight houses of the Order had embraced his reform. In 1625 they were formed into a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> of all the <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priories</a> of canons in the duchy. To reinforce the reform, any canons who wished to join had to undergo a new <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> and profession of vows. Otherwise they could retire with a pension from the canonical life. On 11 February 1628 they were officially established as the Congregation of Our Savior by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as a reformed congregation of the Regular Canons of Lorraine.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The method of reform established by Fourier served as a model for the reform of the canons regular in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Kingdom of France</a>, where, with the support of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Rochefoucauld" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Rochefoucauld">Cardinal Rochefoucauld</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_France" title="Congregation of France">Congregation of France</a> was established with these same conditions. 
In 1625 Fourier was charged with preaching to the people of the <a href="/wiki/Salm-Salm" title="Salm-Salm">Principality of Salm-Salm</a>, which had embraced Calvinism.<sup id="cite_ref-Weninger_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weninger-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Within six months his gentle persuasion and efforts were rewarded with the re-establishment of Catholicism in the realm.
</p><p>Fourier himself was elected as Abbot General of the congregation in 1632. He hoped to guide his fellow canons to caring for children, as the canonesses were doing. This vision never took root among the men, however.
</p><p>After the invasion by the Kingdom of France of the Duchy of Lorraine in 1632 under <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu" title="Cardinal Richelieu">Cardinal Richelieu</a>, Fourier refused to swear an oath of loyalty to King <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIII_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XIII of France">Louis XIII of France</a>. Thus he and his community were forced to flee their monastery in 1636, taking refuge in the town of <a href="/wiki/Gray,_Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Gray, Haute-Saône">Gray</a> in the neighboring <a href="/wiki/County_of_Burgundy" title="County of Burgundy">County of Burgundy</a>. Fourier and the canons with him were occupied in that city nursing <a href="/wiki/Epidemic" title="Epidemic">plague</a> victims. It was there that he died on 9 December 1640.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Fourier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter Fourier was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1730<sup id="cite_ref-Weninger_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weninger-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1897. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated in the Roman Catholic Church on 9 December, the anniversary of his death.
</p><p>St Peter Fourier is honored by a statue of him in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> among the founders of religious orders.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Fourier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The vision of Fourier was exported to Canada in 1654 by St. <a href="/wiki/Marguerite_Bourgeoys" title="Marguerite Bourgeoys">Marguerite Bourgeoys</a>, who was the president of a <a href="/wiki/Sodality" title="Sodality">sodality</a> of volunteers associated with the work of the cloistered canonesses. Moving to <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a> at the invitation of its governor, she became one of the early founders of the new colony. There she established the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Notre_Dame_of_Montreal" title="Congregation of Notre Dame of Montreal">Congregation of Notre Dame of Montreal</a>, which was the first to provide education to the children of the colonists, as well as to the <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">Native American</a> children. Her work has been highly successful both there and in the United States of America.
</p>
