<p><b>Katharine Drexel</b>, <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament">SBS</a> (born <b>Catherine Mary Drexel</b>; November 26, 1858 – March 3, 1955) was an American heiress, philanthropist, <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a>, educator, and foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament">Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament</a>. She was the second person born in what is now the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> to be <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and the first one born a U.S. citizen.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_before_taking_religious_vows">Life before taking religious vows</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katharine_Drexel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life before taking religious vows">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Katharine Mary Drexel was born Catherine Marie Drexel in <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Philadelphia, Pennsylvania">Philadelphia, Pennsylvania</a>, on November 26, 1858, to <a href="/wiki/Francis_Anthony_Drexel" title="Francis Anthony Drexel">Francis Anthony Drexel</a> and Hannah Langstroth. She had an older sister, Elizabeth. Her family owned a considerable banking fortune, and her uncle <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Joseph_Drexel" title="Anthony Joseph Drexel">Anthony Joseph Drexel</a> was the founder of <a href="/wiki/Drexel_University" title="Drexel University">Drexel University</a> in Philadelphia. She was a distant cousin of former first lady <a href="/wiki/Jacqueline_Kennedy_Onassis" title="Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis">Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis</a> on her father's side. 
</p><p>Langstroth died five weeks after Katherine's birth and Anthony Joseph and his wife Ellen cared for Katherine and Elizabeth for two years after. Her father remarried Emma Bouvier in 1860, brought his older children home, and had a third daughter, Louise, in 1863.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The girls grew up in a wealthy and religious household with charitable principles. Emma would regularly host people in need at the home to distribute food and clothing.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_work">Religious work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katharine_Drexel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After she took religious <a href="/wiki/Vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Vows">vows</a>, and took the name <i>Mother Katharine</i>, dedicating herself and her inheritance to the needs of oppressed <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">Native Americans</a> and African-Americans in the southern, western and southwestern United States, and was a vocal advocate of racial tolerance. Joined by 13 other women, she then established a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament">Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament</a> to further this cause. She also financed more than 60 missions and schools around the United States, as well as the founding of <a href="/wiki/Xavier_University_of_Louisiana" title="Xavier University of Louisiana">Xavier University of Louisiana</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>&#160;– the only <a href="/wiki/HBCU" class="mw-redirect" title="HBCU">historically Black</a> and Catholic university in the United States.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katharine_Drexel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Drexel is one of only a few <a href="/wiki/List_of_American_candidates_for_sainthood" title="List of American candidates for sainthood">American saints</a> and the second American-born saint (<a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Ann_Seton" title="Elizabeth Ann Seton">Elizabeth Ann Seton</a> was a natural-born US citizen, born in New York City in 1774 and canonized in 1975). 
</p><p>Drexel was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on November 20, 1988, when her first miracle through prayer—healing the severe ear infection of teenage Robert Gutherman in 1974—was accepted.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on October 1, 2000,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> when her 1994 miracle of reversing congenital deafness in 2-year old Amy Wall was recognized.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> cited a fourfold legacy of Drexel: 
</p>
<ul><li>A love of the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and perspective on the unity of all peoples;</li>
<li>courage and initiative in addressing social inequality among minorities;</li>
<li>her efforts to achieve quality education for all;</li>
<li>and selfless service, including the donation of her inheritance, for the victims of injustice. (She is known as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of racial justice and of philanthropists.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2012)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>)</li></ul>
<p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is observed on March 3, the anniversary of her death. She is buried in <a href="/wiki/Cornwells_Heights,_Pennsylvania" title="Cornwells Heights, Pennsylvania">Cornwells Heights</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bensalem_Township,_Pennsylvania" title="Bensalem Township, Pennsylvania">Bensalem Township, Pennsylvania</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Katharine_Drexel_Mission_Center_and_Shrine">Saint Katharine Drexel Mission Center and Shrine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katharine_Drexel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Saint Katharine Drexel Mission Center and Shrine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/List_of_shrines#United_States" title="List of shrines">List of shrines  §&#160;United States</a></div>
<p>The <b>Saint Katharine Drexel Mission Center and National Shrine</b><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> is located in <a href="/wiki/Bensalem,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Bensalem, Pennsylvania">Bensalem, Pennsylvania</a>. The Mission Center<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> offers retreat programs, historic site tours, days of prayer, presentations about Saint Katharine Drexel, as well as lectures and seminars related to her legacy. Furniture and exhibits tell the story of Drexel, the Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament, and the accomplishments of Black and Native American people. Her tomb lies under the main altar in St. Elizabeth Chapel.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Originally known as <a href="/wiki/St._Elizabeth%27s_Convent" title="St. Elizabeth&#39;s Convent">St. Elizabeth's Convent</a>, it was listed on the <a href="/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places" title="National Register of Historic Places">National Register of Historic Places</a> in 1978.<sup id="cite_ref-nris_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nris-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In August, 2018, Drexel's remains were transferred to a new shrine at the.<a href="/wiki/Cathedral_Basilica_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul_(Philadelphia)" title="Cathedral Basilica of Saints Peter and Paul (Philadelphia)">Cathedral Basilica of Saints Peter and Paul</a> in Philadelphia.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Schools">Schools</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katharine_Drexel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Schools">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><i>Schools St. Katharine Drexel founded or funded include (but are not limited to)</i>:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Xavier_University_of_Louisiana" title="Xavier University of Louisiana">Xavier University of Louisiana</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Benedict_the_Moor_School" title="St. Benedict the Moor School">St. Benedict the Moor School</a></li>
<li>Blessed Sacrament Catholic School, Beaumont, Texas</li>
<li>Sacred Heart Catholic School, Port Arthur, Texas.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Joseph_Indian_Normal_School" title="St. Joseph Indian Normal School">St. Joseph Indian Normal School</a>, now called Drexel Hall, on the campus of St. Joseph's College, Rensselaer, Indiana. The Indian Normal School operated from 1888 to 1896. A school for boys, the <a href="/wiki/Federal_Indian_Boarding_School_Initiative" title="Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative">Federal Indian Boarding School Initiative</a> indicates children were "taken" from reservations in order to matriculate here. See page 350 of cited source.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Michael_Indian_School" title="St. Michael Indian School">St. Michael Indian School</a>, serving grades K–12 in <a href="/wiki/St._Michaels,_Arizona" title="St. Michaels, Arizona">St. Michaels, Arizona</a></li>
<li>St. Mark School, the first in New York City for African-American Catholic children</li>
<li>Our Lady of Lourdes Catholic Church and School, Founded 1912, Atlanta, Georgia</li>
<li>St. Vincent De Paul Catholic Church and School, Founded 1932, Nashville, Tennessee</li>
<li>St. Ignatius of Loyola Parish was founded in 1893. St. Katharine Drexel and the Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament opened St. Ignatius of Loyola School in 1926. The school moved to its current facility in 1967 in Philadelphia. <sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>St. Emma's Industrial and Agricultural Institute (later St. Emma Military Academy for Boys) founded on the <a href="/wiki/Belmead_(Powhatan,_Virginia)" title="Belmead (Powhatan, Virginia)">Belmead Plantation</a> near <a href="/wiki/Powhatan,_Virginia" title="Powhatan, Virginia">Powhatan, Virginia</a> in 1897</li>
<li>St. Frances de Sales School for Girls founded on the <a href="/wiki/Belmead_(Powhatan,_Virginia)" title="Belmead (Powhatan, Virginia)">Belmead Plantation</a> near <a href="/wiki/Powhatan,_Virginia" title="Powhatan, Virginia">Powhatan, Virginia</a> in 1899</li>
<li>St. Peter Claver Catholic School in Macon, Georgia, in 1913 with the help of Bishop Benjamin Kiely and Father Ignatius Lissner.</li>
<li>Kate Drexel Industrial Boarding School, on the <a href="/wiki/Umatilla_Indian_Reservation" title="Umatilla Indian Reservation">Umatilla Reservation</a> in Pendleton, Oregon.
