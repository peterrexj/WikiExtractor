<p><b>Symeon</b>, called <b>Metaphrastes</b> or <b>the Metaphrast</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Συμεών ο Μεταφραστής</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="ISO 843 Greek (Greek language) transliteration"><i lang="el-Latn">Symeōn o Metafrastīs</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Symeon Metaphrastes</i>; died c. 1000), was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> writer and official. He is regarded as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> falls on 9 or 28 November.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ODCC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODCC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is best known for his 10-volume <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Menologion" class="mw-redirect" title="Menologion">menologion</a>, or collection of saints' lives.<sup id="cite_ref-Paget2010_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paget2010-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symeon_the_Metaphrast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>About Symeon's life few details are known.<sup id="cite_ref-Paget2010_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paget2010-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He lived in the second half of the 10th century. <a href="/wiki/Ephrem_Mtsire" title="Ephrem Mtsire">Ephrem Mtsire</a> puts him at the peak of his career in the sixth year of <a href="/wiki/Basil_II" title="Basil II">Basil II</a> (982). <a href="/wiki/Yahya_of_Antioch" title="Yahya of Antioch">Yahya of Antioch</a> also makes him a contemporary of Basil II and Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Constantinople" title="Nicholas II of Constantinople">Nicholas II of Constantinople</a> (984–991).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the 15th century, <a href="/wiki/Mark_Eugenikos" class="mw-redirect" title="Mark Eugenikos">Mark Eugenikos</a> wrongly called Symeon a <i><a href="/wiki/Megas_logothetes" title="Megas logothetes">megas logothetes</a></i>. The hagiographer actually lived a generation later than the historian <a href="/wiki/Symeon_Logothete" title="Symeon Logothete">Symeon Logothete</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Symeon wrote mainly <a href="/wiki/Hymnody" title="Hymnody">hymnody</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>. He composed <i><a href="/wiki/Kanones" class="mw-redirect" title="Kanones">kanones</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Stichera" class="mw-redirect" title="Stichera">stichera</a></i> and a hymn to the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>. He also compiled excerpts of the <a href="/wiki/Church_fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Church fathers">church fathers</a>, particularly <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Basil the Great">Basil the Great</a>. His most important work by far, however, is the menologion, which <a href="/wiki/Albert_Ehrhard" title="Albert Ehrhard">Albert Ehrhard</a> labelled "a revolution in the field of hagiography".<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to tradition, it was commissioned by Basil II.<sup id="cite_ref-ODCC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODCC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Symeon's menologion is a product of the <a href="/wiki/Encyclopedism" title="Encyclopedism">encyclopedism</a> characteristic of the <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_Renaissance" title="Macedonian Renaissance">Macedonian Renaissance</a>. He did not merely collect and arrange pre-existing saint's lives, but also reworked them, standardizing their language and embellishing their rhetorical style to bring them in line with the <a href="/wiki/Atticism" title="Atticism">Atticism</a> of the day.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His nickname comes from this act of <a href="/wiki/Metaphrase" title="Metaphrase">metaphrasis</a>. The content of the lives was not altered, however, and historical errors were left intact.<sup id="cite_ref-ODCC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODCC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Symeon arranged them according to their feast days in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_liturgical_calendar" title="Eastern Orthodox liturgical calendar">Eastern Orthodox liturgical calendar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There are about 150 distinct lives.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For his menologion, Symeon received praise from <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_Ouranos" title="Nikephoros Ouranos">Nikephoros Ouranos</a> and <a href="/wiki/Michael_Psellos" title="Michael Psellos">Michael Psellos</a> addressed to him an encomium. It was widely read in monasteries. The standard edition came in ten volumes. Numerous <a href="/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript" title="Illuminated manuscript">illuminated</a> copies were produced in the 11th century.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some orthodox prayers of preparation before Holy Communion<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and prayers of thanksgiving after Holy Communion were composed by him.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symeon_the_Metaphrast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/List_of_venerable_people_(Eastern_Orthodox)" title="List of venerable people (Eastern Orthodox)">Venerable</a> Symeon the Metaphrast is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Michael_Psellos" title="Michael Psellos">Michael Psellos</a> compiled Symeon's biography (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1050) and he composed a liturgical office for him.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
