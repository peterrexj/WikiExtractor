<p><b>Peter of Verona</b> (1205 – April 6, 1252), also known as <b>Saint Peter Martyr</b> and <b>Saint Peter of Verona</b>, was a 13th-century <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a>. He was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and a celebrated preacher. He served as <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor" title="Inquisitor">Inquisitor</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy_(historical_region)" title="Lombardy (historical region)">Lombardy</a>, was killed by an assassin, and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church#Saints_and_devotions" title="Catholic Church">saint</a> 11 months after his death, making this the fastest canonization in history.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Agni of Leontino, Dominican archbishop of Cosenza, and later patriarch of Jerusalem, was the first to write a biography of Peter of Verona. He lived for many years with Peter of Verona and had been his superior.<sup id="cite_ref-shrine_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrine-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter was born in the city of <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a> into a family perhaps sympathetic to the <a href="/wiki/Catharism" title="Catharism">Cathar</a> <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>. Peter went to a Catholic school, and later to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">University of Bologna</a>, where he is said to have maintained his orthodoxy and, at the age of fifteen, met <a href="/wiki/Dominic_of_Osma" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominic of Osma">Dominic of Osma</a>. Peter joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Friars_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of the Friars Preachers">Order of the Friars Preachers</a> (Dominicans) and became a celebrated preacher throughout northern and central <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-allaria_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allaria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From the 1230s on, Peter preached against heresy, and especially Catharism, which had many adherents in thirteenth-century Northern Italy. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a> appointed him General Inquisitor for northern Italy in 1234 and Peter evangelized nearly the whole of Italy, preaching in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-allaria_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allaria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is credited with founding, around 1240, the <a href="/wiki/Venerabile_Arciconfraternita_della_Misericordia_di_Firenze" title="Venerabile Arciconfraternita della Misericordia di Firenze">Venerabile Arciconfraternita della Misericordia di Firenze</a>.
</p><p>In 1243 he recommended the new <a href="/wiki/Servite_Order" title="Servite Order">Servite</a> foundation to the pope for approval. In 1251, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> recognized Peter's virtues (severity of life and doctrine, talent for preaching, and zeal for the orthodox Catholic faith), and appointed him Inquisitor in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy_(historical_region)" title="Lombardy (historical region)">Lombardy</a>. He spent about six months in that office and it is unclear whether he was ever involved in any trials. His one recorded act was a declaration of clemency for those confessing heresy or sympathy to heresy.
</p><p>In his sermons he denounced heresy and also those Catholics who professed the Faith by words, but acted contrary to it in deeds. Crowds came to meet him and followed him; conversions were numerous,<sup id="cite_ref-allaria_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allaria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> including many Cathars who returned to the Catholic church.
</p><p>Because of this, a group of Milanese Cathars conspired to kill him. They hired an assassin, <a href="/wiki/Carino_of_Balsamo" title="Carino of Balsamo">Carino of Balsamo</a>. Carino's accomplice was Manfredo Clitoro of <a href="/wiki/Giussano" title="Giussano">Giussano</a>. On April 6, 1252, when Peter was returning from Como to Milan, the two assassins followed Peter to a lonely spot near <a href="/wiki/Barlassina" title="Barlassina">Barlassina</a>, and there killed him and mortally wounded his companion, a fellow friar named Domenico.<sup id="cite_ref-Henry_Charles_Lea_1887_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Henry_Charles_Lea_1887-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Carino_of_Balsamo" title="Carino of Balsamo">Carino</a> struck Peter's head with an axe and then attacked Domenico. Peter rose to his knees, and recited the first article of the <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">Apostles' Creed</a>). Offering his blood as a sacrifice to God, according to legend, he dipped his fingers in it and wrote on the ground: <i>Credo in Deum</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-allaria_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allaria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the first words of the creed. The blow that killed him cut off the top of his head, but the testimony given at the inquest into his death confirms that he began reciting the Creed when he was attacked.
</p><p>Dominic was carried to <a href="/wiki/Meda,_Lombardy" title="Meda, Lombardy">Meda</a>, where he died five days afterwards.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Dominican tradition Peter often conversed with the saints, including the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_martyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin martyr">virgin martyrs</a> Catherine, Agnes and Cecilia.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Once, when preaching to a vast crowd under the burning sun, the heretics challenged him to procure shade for his listeners. As he prayed, a cloud overshadowed the audience.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter's body was carried to Milan and laid in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Eustorgio" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant&#39;Eustorgio">Sant'Eustorgio</a>, where an ornate <a href="/wiki/Mausoleum" title="Mausoleum">mausoleum</a>, the work of <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_di_Balduccio" title="Giovanni di Balduccio">Balduccio Pisano</a>, was erected to his memory. Since the eighteenth century this has been located in the <a href="/wiki/Portinari_Chapel" title="Portinari Chapel">Portinari Chapel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Henry_Charles_Lea_1887_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Henry_Charles_Lea_1887-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Many <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> were attributed to him while alive, and even more after his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>.
</p><p>Peter was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> on March 9, 1253, the fastest canonization in papal history.<sup id="cite_ref-prudlo_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prudlo-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> St Peter the Martyr's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 6 April<sup id="cite_ref-MR_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> although his Dominican brothers celebrate it on 4 June.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> From 1586, when the feast day was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, to 1969, when it was removed on the grounds of the limited importance now attached to the saint internationally, the celebration was on 29 April.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> (6 April, his death date, was not used because it would too often conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Easter_Triduum" class="mw-redirect" title="Easter Triduum">Easter Triduum</a>.)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Antiqua" title="Santa Maria Antiqua">Santa Maria Antiqua</a> in Verona is co-entitled to him.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Carino_of_Balsamo" title="Carino of Balsamo">Carino</a>, the assassin, later repented and confessed his crime. He converted to the Catholic church and eventually became a lay brother in the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of <a href="/wiki/Forl%C3%AC" title="Forlì">Forlì</a>. He is the subject of a local cult as <a href="/wiki/Carino_of_Balsamo" title="Carino of Balsamo">Blessed Carino of Balsamo</a>.
</p><p>The sculptures on the great door of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Anastasia_(Verona)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant&#39;Anastasia (Verona)">S. Anastasia</a>, the Dominican Church in Verona, represent scenes from the life of St. Peter Martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
