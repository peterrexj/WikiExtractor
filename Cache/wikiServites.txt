<p>The <b>Servite Order</b>, officially known as the <b>Order of Servants of Mary</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ordo Servorum Beatae Mariae Virginis</i>; abbreviation: <b>OSM</b>), is one of the five original <a href="/wiki/Mendicant_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Mendicant order">mendicant orders</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. It includes several branches of friars (priests and brothers), contemplative <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, a congregation of <a href="/wiki/Religious_sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sisters">religious sisters</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay groups</a>. The order's objectives are the sanctification of its members, the preaching of the Gospel, and the propagation of devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mother of God</a>, with special reference to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Sorrows" title="Our Lady of Sorrows">her sorrows</a>. The Servites friars lead a community life in the tradition of the mendicant orders.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation">Foundation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Foundation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Amadio_123.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Amadio_123.JPG/200px-Amadio_123.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="249" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Amadio_123.JPG/300px-Amadio_123.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/Amadio_123.JPG 2x" data-file-width="368" data-file-height="459" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Amadeus_of_the_Amidei" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus of the Amidei">Amadeus of the Amidei</a> (d. 1266), one of the seven founders of the Servite Order.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The order was founded in 1233 by <a href="/wiki/Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order" title="Seven Holy Founders of the Servite Order">"the seven holy founders"</a>, each a member of a patrician family of Florence, Italy. These cloth merchants left their city, families, and professions and withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Senario" title="Monte Senario">Monte Senario</a>, a mountain outside the city of Florence, for a life of poverty and penance.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The seven were: Bonfilius of Florence, born Bonfilius Monaldi (Buonfiglio dei Monaldi); Alexis of Florence, born <a href="/wiki/Alexis_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</a> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <span lang="it" style="font-style: normal;">Alessio Falconieri</span>) (1200 – 17 February 1310); Manettus of Florence, born Benedict dell'Antella (Benedetto dell' Antella); <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_of_the_Amidei" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus of the Amidei">Amideus of Florence</a>, born Bartholemew Amidei (died 1266) (also known as Bartolomeo degli Amidei); <a href="/wiki/Hugh_dei_Lippi_Uggucioni" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugh dei Lippi Uggucioni">Hugh of Florence</a>, born Ricovero Uguccioni (Hugh dei Lippi Uggucioni (Ricovero dei Lippi-Ugguccioni)); Sostene of Florence, born Gerardino Sostegni (Gherardino di Sostegno); and Buonagiunta of Florence, born John Manetti (Giovanni di Buonagiunta (Bonajuncta)).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They were canonized by Pope Leo XIII on 15 January 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-grotto_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grotto-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif/200px-Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif" decoding="async" width="200" height="224" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif/300px-Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif 2x" data-file-width="353" data-file-height="395" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Alexis_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</a> (d. 1310), one of the seven founders of the Servite Order.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The members of the order dedicated themselves to Mary under her title of Mother of Sorrows (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <span lang="it" style="font-style: normal;">Madonna Addolorata</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-grotto_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grotto-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Dedicating their devotion to the mother of Jesus, they adopted Mary's virtues of hospitality and compassion as the order's hallmarks.<sup id="cite_ref-owen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-owen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The distinctive spirit of the order is the sanctification of its members by meditation on the Passion of Jesus and the Sorrows of the Virgin Mary, and spreading abroad this devotion.<sup id="cite_ref-servants_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-servants-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Florence">Bishop of Florence</a>, Ardengo Trotti (Ardengo Dei Foraboschi), approved the group as a religious order sometime between 1240 and 1247. The Servites, like other new orders before them such as the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarians</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominicans</a>, decided to live by the ancient <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Augustine" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Augustine">Rule of St. Augustine</a>, and added to the rule further guidelines that were the expression of their own Marian devotion and dedication. By 1250 a number of Servites had been ordained to the priesthood, thus creating an order with priests as well as brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-secular_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secular-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> favored a plan for the amalgamation of all orders which followed the Rule of St. Augustine. This was accomplished in March 1256, but about the same time a <a href="/wiki/Rescript" title="Rescript">rescript</a> was issued confirming the Servite Order as a separate body with power to elect a general. Four years later a general chapter was convened at which the order was divided into two provinces, Tuscany and Umbria, the former being governed by Manettus and the latter by Sostene. Within five years two new provinces were added, that of Romagna and that of Lombardy.<sup id="cite_ref-griffin_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-griffin-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p><a href="/wiki/Philip_Benizi" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Benizi">Philip Benizi</a> was elected general on 5 June 1267, and afterwards became the great propagator of the order.<sup id="cite_ref-servants_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-servants-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Lyons">Second Council of Lyons</a> in 1274 put into execution the ordinance of the 1215 <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Lateran Council">Fourth Lateran Council</a>, forbidding the foundation of new <a href="/wiki/Religious_orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious orders">religious orders</a>, and suppressed all mendicant institutions not yet approved by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. In the year 1276 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_V" title="Pope Innocent V">Pope Innocent V</a> in a letter to Benizi declared the order suppressed. Benizi set off for Rome to appeal the decision, but before his arrival there Innocent V had died. His successor lived only five weeks. Finally <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXI" title="Pope John XXI">Pope John XXI</a>, decided that the Servite Order should continue as before. It was not definitively approved until <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XI" title="Pope Benedict XI">Pope Benedict XI</a> issued the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> "Dum levamus" on 11 February 1304. Of the seven founders, Alexis alone lived to see their foundation raised to the permanent dignity of an order. He died in 1310.
</p><p>On 30 January 1398 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> granted the Servites the power to confer theological degrees. It was in harmony with the tradition thus established that many centuries later the order established the <a href="/wiki/Marianum" title="Marianum">Marianum</a> faculty in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-marianum_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marianum-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG/200px-2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG/300px-2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG/400px-2709_-_Innsbruck_-_Servitenkirche.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2736" data-file-height="3648" /></a><figcaption>Servite church in <a href="/wiki/Innsbruck" title="Innsbruck">Innsbruck</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The new foundation enjoyed considerable growth in the following decades. Already in the thirteenth century there were houses of the order in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>. By the early years of the fourteenth century the order had more than one hundred houses in locations including <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, and what later became <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. In subsequent periods came missions in <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> (St. Peregrine-Philippine Vicariate), and <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="European_contraction">European contraction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: European contraction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The disturbances which arose during the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> caused the loss of many Servite houses in Germany, but in the south of France the order met with much success. The Convent of <a href="/wiki/San_Marcello_al_Corso" title="San Marcello al Corso">San Marcello al Corso</a> was founded in the city of Rome in 1369 and a second house, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Via" title="Santa Maria in Via">Santa Maria in Via</a>, was established there in 1513.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Beginning in the early part of the eighteenth century the order sustained a series of losses and confiscations from which it has yet to recover. A first blow fell upon the flourishing <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Narbonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Province of Narbonne">Province of Narbonne</a>, which was almost totally destroyed by the plague which swept <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a> in 1720. Thanks to secularizing inroads made by the <a href="/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment" title="Age of Enlightenment">Enlightenment</a>, in 1783 the Servites were expelled from <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> and in 1785 the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Joseph II, Holy Roman Emperor">Joseph II</a> desecrated the shrine of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Waldrast&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Maria Waldrast (page does not exist)">Maria Waldrast</a>. The French Revolution and ensuing hostilities throughout western Europe caused widespread losses. Ten houses were suppressed in Spain in 1835. 
</p><p>After the seizure of Rome under the Italian <a href="/wiki/Italian_unification" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian unification">Risorgimento</a> in 1870, the government of Italy closed the Servite house of studies in the city, along with many other papal institutions. The institute was re-founded as the College of Sant Alessio Falcioneri in 1895.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="New_expansion">New expansion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: New expansion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After a gap of 25 years, in 1895 the house of studies in Rome was re-founded as the College of Sant Alessio Falcioneri. This development went hand in hand at this period with other initiatives and a new foundation was made at <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a> in 1891 and the order was introduced into <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> and <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>, chiefly through the efforts of the Servite priests Bosio and Morini. The latter, having gone to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> in 1864 as director of the affiliated Congregation of the Sisters of Compassion, obtained charge of a parish from <a href="/wiki/Henry_Edward_Manning" title="Henry Edward Manning">Archbishop Manning</a> in 1867. The work prospered and besides St. Mary's Priory in London, convents were opened at <a href="/wiki/Bognor_Regis" title="Bognor Regis">Bognor Regis</a> (1882) and <a href="/wiki/Begbroke" title="Begbroke">Begbroke</a> (1886). In 1870 Morini, Ventura, Giribaldi, and Joseph Camera, at the request of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Melcher" title="Joseph Melcher">Joseph Melcher</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Green_Bay" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Green Bay">Green Bay</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wisconsin" title="Wisconsin">Wisconsin</a>, took up a mission in America, at <a href="/wiki/Neenah,_Wisconsin" title="Neenah, Wisconsin">Neenah</a>. Morini founded at <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a> (1874) the <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Sorrows_Basilica" title="Our Lady of Sorrows Basilica">monastery of Our Lady of Sorrows</a>. A novitiate was opened at <a href="/wiki/Granville,_Wisconsin" title="Granville, Wisconsin">Granville, Wisconsin</a> in 1892 and an American province was formally established in 1908.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Twentieth_century">Twentieth century</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Twentieth century">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The order continued to expand geographically throughout the twentieth century, taking responsibility for missions in <a href="/wiki/Swaziland" class="mw-redirect" title="Swaziland">Swaziland</a> in 1913, <a href="/wiki/Acre_(state)" title="Acre (state)">Acre</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> in 1919, <a href="/wiki/Ais%C3%A9n,_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Aisén, Chile">Aisén in Chile</a> in 1937, and <a href="/wiki/Natal_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Natal Province">Zululand</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South Africa</a>. It also made foundations in <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> from 1914 and more solidly since 1921; <a href="/wiki/Transvaal_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Transvaal Province">Transvaal</a> in South Africa from 1935, <a href="/wiki/Uruguay" title="Uruguay">Uruguay</a> in 1939, <a href="/wiki/Bolivia" title="Bolivia">Bolivia</a> in 1946, <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a> in 1948, <a href="/wiki/Australia" title="Australia">Australia</a> in 1951,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Venezuela" title="Venezuela">Venezuela</a> in 1952, <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a> in 1953, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> in 1974, <a href="/wiki/Mozambique" title="Mozambique">Mozambique</a> in 1984, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> in 1985, <a href="/wiki/Uganda" title="Uganda">Uganda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albania</a> in 1993, and also the refoundations in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> (<a href="/wiki/Eger" title="Eger">Eger</a>) and the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-osmhistory1888_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-osmhistory1888-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In the United States there is currently one province of friars with headquarters in Chicago. There are four provinces of sisters with motherhouses in Wisconsin, Nebraska, and two in Illinois.<sup id="cite_ref-grotto_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grotto-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>, through the Congregation of Seminaries and Universities, elevated the Marianum to a <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_university" title="Pontifical university">pontifical theological faculty</a> on 30 November 1950.
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, the order renewed its Constitutions starting with its 1968 general chapter at <a href="/wiki/Majadahonda" title="Majadahonda">Majadahonda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, a process which was concluded in 1987. In the same year, Prior General Michael M. Sincerny oversaw the creation of the International Union of the Servite Family (UNIFAS).<sup id="cite_ref-osmhistory1888_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-osmhistory1888-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The twentieth century also saw the beatification (1952) and the canonization of Friar <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Maria_Pucci" title="Antonio Maria Pucci">Antonio Maria Pucci</a>; the canonization of <a href="/wiki/Clelia_Barbieri" title="Clelia Barbieri">Clelia Barbieri</a> (d. 1870), foundress of the Minime dell’Addolorata; the beatification in 1999 of <a href="/wiki/Ferdinando_Maria_Baccilieri" title="Ferdinando Maria Baccilieri">Ferdinando Maria Baccilieri</a> of the Servite Secular Order; the beatification in 2001 of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Guadalupe_Ricart_Olmos&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Maria Guadalupe Ricart Olmos (page does not exist)">Maria Guadalupe Ricart Olmos</a>, a Spanish cloistered nun who was martyred during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>; and the beatification of Cecelia Eusepi of the Servite Secular Order.
</p><p>Through the centuries, the Servite Order has spread throughout the world, including all of Europe, parts of Africa, Australia, the Americas, India, and the Philippines. The general headquarters of the Servite Order is in Rome, while many provinces and motherhouses represent the order throughout the world.
</p>
<h2><span id="Devotions.2C_manner_of_life"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Devotions,_manner_of_life">Devotions, manner of life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Devotions, manner of life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata,_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe,_cupola.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG/200px-Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG/300px-Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG/400px-Basilica_della_santissima_annunziata%2C_Cappella_di_San_Giuliano_o_di_San_Giuseppe%2C_cupola.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>Ceiling in the Servite mother church, <a href="/wiki/Santissima_Annunziata,_Florence" title="Santissima Annunziata, Florence">Santissima Annunziata, Florence</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In common with all religious orders strictly so called, the Servites make <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemn vows">solemn vows</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity,_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity, and obedience">poverty, chastity, and obedience</a>. The particular object of the order is to sanctify first its own members, and then all men through devotion to the Mother of God, especially in her desolation during the Passion of her Divine Son.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>All offices in the order are elective and continue for three years, except that of <a href="/wiki/General" class="mw-redirect" title="General">general</a> and assistant-generals which are for six years.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The Servites give missions, have the care of souls, or teach in higher institutions of learning. The <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Sorrows" title="Our Lady of Sorrows">Rosary of the Seven Dolors</a> is one of their devotions, as is also the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Via_Matris&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Via Matris (page does not exist)">Via Matris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">Canonized</a> Servite saints are: <a href="/wiki/Philip_Benizi" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Benizi">Philip Benizi</a> (feast day on 23 August), <a href="/wiki/Peregrine_Laziosi" title="Peregrine Laziosi">Peregrine Laziosi</a> (4 May), <a href="/wiki/Juliana_Falconieri" title="Juliana Falconieri">Juliana Falconieri</a> (19 June). The <a href="/wiki/Seven_Holy_Founders" class="mw-redirect" title="Seven Holy Founders">seven founders of the order</a> were canonized in 1888, and have a common feast day on 17 February. The date first assigned to this feast day was 11 February, the anniversary of the canonical approval of the order in 1304. Since in 1907 this date was assigned to the celebration of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Our Lady of Lourdes">Our Lady of Lourdes</a>, the feast day of the Seven Holy Founders was moved to 12 February. The date was changed again in 1969 to accord more closely with  liturgical tradition, to a date which marks the anniversary of the death of one of them, <a href="/wiki/Alexis_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</a>, which occurred on 17 February 1310.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Connected with the first order of men are the <a href="/wiki/Cloistered" class="mw-redirect" title="Cloistered">cloistered</a> nuns of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">second order</a>, which originated with converts of <a href="/wiki/Philip_Benizi" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Benizi">Philip Benizi</a>. These nuns currently have convents in Spain, Italy, England, the Tyrol, and Germany.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mantellate_Sisters">Mantellate Sisters</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Mantellate Sisters">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Mantellate Sisters are a <a href="/wiki/Third_order" title="Third order">third order</a> of religious women founded by <a href="/wiki/Juliana_Falconieri" title="Juliana Falconieri">Juliana Falconieri</a>, to whom Benizi gave the habit in 1284. From Italy it spread into other countries of Europe. Anna Juliana, Archduchess of Austria, founded several houses and became a Mantellate herself. In 1844 the congregation was introduced into France, and from there extended into England in 1850. The sisters were the first to wear the religious habit publicly in that country after the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> and were active missionaries under <a href="/wiki/Frederick_William_Faber" title="Frederick William Faber">Frederick William Faber</a> and the Oratorians for many years. This branch occupies itself with active works. They devote themselves principally to the education of youth, managing academies and taking charge of parochial schools. They also undertake works of mercy, such as the care of orphans, visiting the sick, and instructing converts.<sup id="cite_ref-servants_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-servants-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Organized into a number of religious congregations, some of pontifical and some of diocesan right, they have houses in Italy, France, Spain, England, and <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>. In the United States they are to be found in the dioceses of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_diocese_of_Sioux_City&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Roman Catholic diocese of Sioux City (page does not exist)">Sioux City</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Omaha" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Omaha">Omaha</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_diocese_of_Charlotte&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Roman Catholic diocese of Charlotte (page does not exist)"> Charlotte NC</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Chicago" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Chicago">Chicago</a>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Servite_Secular_Order">Servite Secular Order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Servite Secular Order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Secular Order of the Servants of Mary (Servite Secular Order) is an approved Catholic organization of lay men and women plus diocesan priests living their Christian faith in the context of the world. They strive toward holiness according to the spirituality of the Servite Order, following the directives of their Rule of Life. Secular Servites are asked to do the following each day: live the Christian virtues of faith, hope, and love; pray and try to read Sacred Scripture each day, and/or the Liturgy of the Hours; and practice acts of reverence for the Mother of God daily, especially by praying the Servite prayer "The Vigil of Our Lady" and/or the Servite Rosary of the Seven Sorrows of Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-usa_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usa-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also a confraternity of the Seven Dolours, branches of which may be erected in any church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mariology_and_the_Marianum">Mariology and the Marianum</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Mariology and the Marianum">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The pontifical theological faculty <i><a href="/wiki/Marianum" title="Marianum">Marianum</a></i> which is now one of the leading centers of <a href="/wiki/Mariology" title="Mariology">Mariology</a> was established by the Servite Order in accord with its tradition of many centuries. In 1398 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> granted the order the right to confer theological degrees. Suppressed by the Kingdom of Italy in 1870, it was reopened in 1895 under the name of Sant'Alessio Falconeri.
</p><p>In 1939 the Servite priest <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_Roschini" title="Gabriel Roschini">Gabriel Roschini</a> founded the journal <i>Marianum</i> and directed it for its first thirty years, establishing it as a respected international specialist journal which is still successfully published by the Marianum theological faculty. 
</p><p>In 1950 Roschini was also instrumental in the reorganization of the Servite house of studies in Rome as the <a href="/wiki/Marianum" title="Marianum">Marianum</a> theological faculty, which, on 8 December 1955 became a pontifical faculty in virtue of the decree <i>Coelesti Honorandae Reginae</i> of the Sacred Congregation of Seminaries and Universities under the authority of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Its particular speciality is the study of the theology and history of the Blessed Virgin Mary and of her veneration in the church.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_notable_Servites">Other notable Servites</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Other notable Servites">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Several of the most distinguished Servites are here grouped under the heading of that particular subject to which they were especially devoted; the dates are those of their death.
