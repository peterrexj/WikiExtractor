<p><b>Olympias</b>, also known as <b>Saint Olympias</b> and sometimes known as <b>Olympias the Younger</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to distinguish her from <a href="/wiki/Olympias_of_Armenia" title="Olympias of Armenia">her aunt of the same name</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ὀλυμπιάς</span>, sometime between 361 and 368-July 25, 408) was a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman noblewoman</a> of <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> descent.
</p><p>Olympias was born and raised either in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> or <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>. She was the daughter born to the Antiochian Greek noblewoman, <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_of_Antioch" title="Alexandra of Antioch">Alexandra</a> and the wealthy <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Rhetoric" title="Rhetoric">Rhetor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seleucus_(son_of_Ablabius)" title="Seleucus (son of Ablabius)">Seleucus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Olympias had a sibling, who was a parent of <a href="/wiki/Olympias_(sister_of_Praetorian_prefect_Seleucus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Olympias (sister of Praetorian prefect Seleucus)">Olympias</a> and <a href="/wiki/Seleucus_(Theodosian_Praetorian_prefect)" class="mw-redirect" title="Seleucus (Theodosian Praetorian prefect)">Seleucus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Olympias was the namesake of her late paternal aunt <a href="/wiki/Olympias_of_Armenia" title="Olympias of Armenia">Olympias</a> who was once engaged to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Constans" title="Constans">Constans</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> who later married the Roman client king of <a href="/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia" title="Arsacid dynasty of Armenia">Arsacid Armenia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arshak_II" title="Arshak II">Arsaces II (Arshak II)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The paternal grandfather of Olympias was <a href="/wiki/Ablabius_(consul_331)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ablabius (consul 331)">Flavius Ablabius</a> who had held consular rank in Constantinople,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> while her maternal uncle was <a href="/wiki/Calliopius_of_Antioch" title="Calliopius of Antioch">Calliopius</a> the <a href="/wiki/Rhetoric" title="Rhetoric">Rhetor</a> who served as a grammaticus and assistant-teacher under the Rhetor, historian <a href="/wiki/Libanius" title="Libanius">Libanius</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and later served as a Roman official under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperors</a> <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>.
</p><p>Olympias is described as the 'beloved daughter' born to Seleucus and Alexandra.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> At eighteen years of age, Olympias married Nebridius, a nobleman who served as prefect of Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> She was widowed after two years of marriage. Having refusing many offers of marriage,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> she dedicated her life to the church, serving as a <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconess</a>. She would later become a friend of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>.
</p><p>Her good works included building a <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a> and an <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a> and looking after <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> who had been led in exile from <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a>. This led John Chrysostom to tell her that she had done almost too much.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Her support for Chrysostom led to her exile in 404.  Having lost her house, she lived the rest of her life in <a href="/wiki/Nicomedia" title="Nicomedia">Nicomedia</a>, dying on July 25, 408, after a long illness. 
</p><p>Olympias is among the one hundred forty saints whose statues adorn the colonnades of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p>
