<p><b>Saints Cantius, Cantianus, and Cantianilla</b> (all died May 31, circa 304 AD) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> church.  
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cantius,_Cantianus,_and_Cantianilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A late legend states that the three were orphaned siblings, members of a noble Roman family, the <a href="/wiki/Anicii" class="mw-redirect" title="Anicii">Anicii</a>, and related to the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Carinus" title="Carinus">Carinus</a>. Protus was their tutor or guardian, and the Christian who converted the three siblings.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> began to persecute Christians, according to the legend, the four left <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, selling their home and giving most of the proceeds to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They reached their estate in <a href="/wiki/Aquileia" title="Aquileia">Aquileia</a>, where they had travelled to visit in his prison the holy priest <a href="/wiki/Saint_Chrysogonus" title="Saint Chrysogonus">Saint Chrysogonus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, they were captured at a place called <i>Aquae Gradatae</i> (now called <a href="/wiki/San_Canzian_d%27Isonzo" title="San Canzian d&#39;Isonzo">San Canzian d'Isonzo</a>).  They were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> after they refused to offer sacrifice to the civic gods.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cantius,_Cantianus,_and_Cantianilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A priest named Zoelus embalmed and buried their bodies at the site.  <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maximus_of_Turin" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Maximus of Turin">Saint Maximus of Turin</a> subsequently preached a <a href="/wiki/Panegyric" title="Panegyric">panegyric</a> in their honor, and the saints are also mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>. His testimony, which he could draw from coeval documents, is very reliable, and states only that they were brothers of blood and that they were martyred together not far from Aquileia, while they were leaving from there in a chariot.  Their cult was anciently diffused in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.  A paleochristian basilica and tomb was discovered at San Canzian in recent times; it contained the remains of three individuals.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
