<p><b>Blaise of Sebaste</b> (<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Սուրբ Վլասի</span>, <i>Surb Vlasi</i>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἅγιος Βλάσιος</span>, <i>Hágios Blásios</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Blasius</i>) was a physician and bishop of <a href="/wiki/Sivas" title="Sivas">Sebastea</a> in historical <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Sivas,_Turkey" class="mw-redirect" title="Sivas, Turkey">Sivas, Turkey</a>) who is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian">Christian</a> saint and martyr. 
</p><p>Blaise is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Churches">Eastern Orthodox</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodox Church">Oriental Orthodox</a> churches and is the patron saint of wool combers and <a href="/wiki/Otorhinolaryngology" title="Otorhinolaryngology">ENT</a> illnesses. In the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a>, his feast falls on 3 February; in the Eastern Churches, on 11 February.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>, he was martyred by being beaten, <a href="/wiki/Combing_(torture)" title="Combing (torture)">tortured with iron combs</a>, and beheaded.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first reference to Blaise is the medical writings of <a href="/wiki/A%C3%ABtius_Amidenus" class="mw-redirect" title="Aëtius Amidenus">Aëtius Amidenus</a> (c. AD 500) where his aid is invoked in treating objects stuck in the throat.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Marco_Polo" title="Marco Polo">Marco Polo</a> reported the place where "Messer Saint Blaise obtained the glorious crown of martyrdom", Sebastea;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the shrine near the citadel mount was mentioned by <a href="/wiki/William_of_Rubruck" title="William of Rubruck">William of Rubruck</a> in 1253.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, it appears to no longer exist.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From being a healer of bodily ailments, Saint Blaise became an expert of souls, then retired for a time to a cavern where he remained in prayer. As bishop of Sebastea, Blaise instructed his people as much by his example as by his words, and the great virtues and sanctity of the servant of God were attested by many miracles. From all parts, the people came flocking to him for the cure of bodily and spiritual ills.<sup id="cite_ref-cosca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cosca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have healed animals (who came to the saint on their own for his assistance) and to have been assisted by animals.
</p><p>In 316 the governor of <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a>, Agricolaus, began a persecution by order of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Licinius" title="Licinius">Licinius</a>, and Saint Blaise was seized. After his interrogation and a severe scourging, he was hurried off to prison<sup id="cite_ref-cosca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cosca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and subsequently beheaded.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Acts_of_St._Blaise">The Acts of St. Blaise</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The Acts of St. Blaise">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg/150px-Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg/225px-Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg/300px-Holy_Trinity_Column-Saint_Blaise.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1728" data-file-height="2304" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Blaise at <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Column_in_Olomouc" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity Column in Olomouc">Holy Trinity Column in Olomouc</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The legendary <i>Acts of St. Blaise</i> were written 400 years after his death, and are apocryphal and, perhaps, fictional.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend is as follows:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Blaise, who had studied philosophy in his youth, was a doctor in Sebaste in Armenia, the city of his birth, who exercised his art with miraculous ability, good-will, and piety.  When the bishop of the city died, he was chosen to succeed him, with the acclamation of all the people.  His holiness was manifest through many miracles: from all around, people came to him to find cures for their spirit and their body; even wild animals came in herds to receive his blessing.  In 316, Agricola, the governor of Cappadocia and of Lesser Armenia, having arrived in Sebastia at the order of the emperor Licinius to kill the Christians, arrested the bishop.  As he was being led to jail, a mother set her only son, choking to death of a fish-bone, at his feet, and the child was cured straight away.  Regardless, the governor, unable to make Blaise renounce his faith, beat him with a stick, ripped his flesh with iron combs, and beheaded him.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_blessing_of_St._Blaise">The blessing of St. Blaise</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The blessing of St. Blaise">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Blessing_of_the_Throats" title="Blessing of the Throats">Blessing of the Throats</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Blaise%27s_Church,_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg/220px-Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="238" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg/330px-Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg/440px-Saint_Blaise%27s_Church%2C_Dubrovnik_-_September_2017.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3061" data-file-height="3309" /></a><figcaption>Church of St. Blasius in <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to the <i>Acts</i>, while Blaise was being taken into custody, a distraught mother, whose only child was choking on a fish bone, threw herself at his feet and implored his intercession. Touched at her grief, he offered up his prayers, and the child was cured. Consequently, Saint Blaise is invoked for protection against injuries and illnesses of the throat.
</p><p>In many places, on the day of his feast the blessing of St. Blaise is given: two candles (sometimes lit), blessed on the feast of the Presentation of the Lord (<a href="/wiki/Candlemas" title="Candlemas">Candlemas</a>), are held in a crossed position by a priest over the heads of the faithful or the people are touched on the throat with them.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the same time the following blessing is given: "Through the intercession of Saint Blaise, bishop and martyr, may God deliver you from every disease of the throat and from every other illness". Then the priest makes the sign of the cross over the faithful.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As the governor's hunters led Blaise back to Sebastea, on the way, the story goes, they met a poor woman whose pig had been seized by a wolf. At the command of Blaise, the wolf restored the pig to its owner, alive and unhurt. When he had reached the capital and was in prison awaiting execution, the old woman whose pig he had saved came to see him, bringing two fine wax candles to dispel the gloom of his dark cell. In the West there was no group honoring St. Blaise prior to the eighth century.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult_of_Saint_Blaise">Cult of Saint Blaise</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Cult of Saint Blaise">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:14Nothelfer.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/14Nothelfer.JPG/160px-14Nothelfer.JPG" decoding="async" width="160" height="233" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/14Nothelfer.JPG/240px-14Nothelfer.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/14Nothelfer.JPG/320px-14Nothelfer.JPG 2x" data-file-width="756" data-file-height="1100" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png/220px-St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png/330px-St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png/440px-St._Blaise_-_National_Flag_of_the_Ragusan_Republic.png 2x" data-file-width="5400" data-file-height="3600" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Flag_of_Dubrovnik" title="Flag of Dubrovnik">Flag of the Republic of Ragusa</a> with the icon and initials of Saint Blaise</figcaption></figure>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Marcello_venusti,_santi_biagio_e_caterina_prima_della_censura,_1549.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Marcello_venusti%2C_santi_biagio_e_caterina_prima_della_censura%2C_1549.jpg/250px-Marcello_venusti%2C_santi_biagio_e_caterina_prima_della_censura%2C_1549.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="252" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5c/Marcello_venusti%2C_santi_biagio_e_caterina_prima_della_censura%2C_1549.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="360" data-file-height="363" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Marcello_Venusti" title="Marcello Venusti">Marcello Venusti</a>'s copy of the original version of Michaelangelo's <i>The Last Judgment</i>; detail showing an uncensored version of <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria" title="Catherine of Alexandria">St. Catherine</a> at the bottom left while above her, the figure of Saint Blaise holding Iron combs at the left had a different head position. St. Catherine was repainted in a dress and St. Blaise was repainted looking up at Jesus.</figcaption></figure>
<p>One of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>, Blaise became one of the most popular saints of the Middle Ages.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His followers became widespread in Europe in the 11th and 12th centuries and his legend is recounted in the 13th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Legenda_Aurea" class="mw-redirect" title="Legenda Aurea">Legenda Aurea</a></i>. Saint Blaise is the saint of the wild beast.
</p><p>He is the patron of the Armenian <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Blaise" title="Order of Saint Blaise">Order of Saint Blaise</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> he is known as <i>San Biagio</i>. In <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>-speaking countries, he is known as San Blas, and has lent his name to many places (see <a href="/wiki/San_Blas_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="San Blas (disambiguation)">San Blas</a>). Several places in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> are also named after him, where he is called São Brás (see <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Br%C3%A1s_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="São Brás (disambiguation)">São Brás</a>).
</p><p>In Italy, Saint Blaise's remains rest at the Basilica over the town of <a href="/wiki/Maratea" title="Maratea">Maratea</a>, shipwrecked there during <a href="/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian" title="Leo III the Isaurian">Leo III the Isaurian</a>'s iconoclastic persecutions.
</p><p>Many German churches, including the former Abbey of St. Blasius in the <a href="/wiki/Black_Forest" title="Black Forest">Black Forest</a> and the church of <a href="/wiki/Balve" title="Balve">Balve</a>, are dedicated to Saint Blaise/Blasius.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Great_Britain">In Great Britain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In Great Britain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> the town of <a href="/wiki/St_Blazey" title="St Blazey">St Blazey</a> and the civil parish of St Blaise derive from his name, where the parish church is still dedicated to Saint Blaise. The council of <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> in 1222 forbade all work on his festival.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
There is a church dedicated to Saint Blaise in the <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> hamlet of Haccombe, near <a href="/wiki/Newton_Abbot" title="Newton Abbot">Newton Abbot</a>, one at Shanklin on the Isle of Wight and another at Milton near Abingdon in Oxfordshire, one of the country's smallest churches. It is located next to Haccombe house which is the family home of the Carew family, descendants of the vice admiral on board the <i><a href="/wiki/Mary_Rose" title="Mary Rose">Mary Rose</a></i> at the time of her sinking. This church, unusually, retains the office of "archpriest".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a <a href="/wiki/St_Blaise%27s_Well" class="mw-redirect" title="St Blaise&#39;s Well">St Blaise's Well</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bromley" title="Bromley">Bromley</a>, London<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> where the water was considered to have medicinal virtues. St Blaise is also associated with <a href="/wiki/Stretford" title="Stretford">Stretford</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a>. A <a href="/wiki/Blessing_of_the_Throats" title="Blessing of the Throats">Blessing of the Throats</a> ceremony is held on February 3 at <a href="/wiki/St_Etheldreda%27s_Church,_London" title="St Etheldreda&#39;s Church, London">St Etheldreda's Church</a> in London and in <a href="/wiki/Balve" title="Balve">Balve</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>. The blessing is performed in many Catholic parish churches, often at the end of a morning Mass.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Blaise_Castle_Estate" title="Blaise Castle Estate">Blaise Castle Estate</a> and the nearby <a href="/wiki/Blaise_Hamlet" title="Blaise Hamlet">Blaise Hamlet</a> in Bristol derive their name from a thirteenth century chapel dedicated to St Blaise, built on a site previously occupied by an Iron Age fort and Roman temple.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Bradford,_West_Yorkshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Bradford, West Yorkshire">Bradford, West Yorkshire</a> a Catholic middle school named after St Blaise was operated by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Diocese_of_Leeds" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Diocese of Leeds">Diocese of Leeds</a> from 1961 to 1995. The name was chosen due to the connections of Bradford to the woollen industry and the method that St Blaise was martyred, with the woolcomb. Due to reorganisation the school closed down when Catholic middle schools were phased out, and the building was sold to Bradford Council to provide replacement accommodation for another local middle school which had burned down. Within a few months, St Blaise school was also severely damaged in a fire, and the remains of the building were demolished. A new primary school was built on the land, and most of the extensive grounds were sold off for housing.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg/220px-St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="519" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg/330px-St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg/440px-St_Blaise_Kutte.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="564" data-file-height="1331" /></a><figcaption>14th-century wall painting of St Blaise in All Saints Church in Kingston upon Thames, UK</figcaption></figure>
<p>There is a 14th-century wall painting of St Blaise in <a href="/wiki/All_Saints_Church,_Kingston_upon_Thames" title="All Saints Church, Kingston upon Thames">All Saints Church, Kingston upon Thames</a>, located by the market place, marking the significance of the wool trade in the economic expansion of the market town in the 14th and 15th centuries.
</p>

<p>Saint Blaise (<a href="/wiki/Croatian_language" title="Croatian language">Croatian</a>: <i lang="hr">Sveti Vlaho or Sveti Blaž</i>) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the city of <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a> and formerly the protector of the independent <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa" title="Republic of Ragusa">Republic of Ragusa</a>. At Dubrovnik his feast is celebrated yearly on 3 February, when relics of the saint, his head, a bit of bone from his throat, his right hand and his left, are paraded in <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquaries</a>. The festivities begin the previous day, <a href="/wiki/Candlemas" title="Candlemas">Candlemas</a>, when white doves are released. Chroniclers of Dubrovnik such as Rastic and <a href="/wiki/House_of_Ranjina" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Ranjina">Ranjina</a> attribute his veneration there to a vision in 971 to warn the inhabitants of an impending attack by the <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venetians</a>, whose galleys had dropped anchor in <a href="/wiki/Gru%C5%BE" title="Gruž">Gruž</a> and near <a href="/wiki/Lokrum" title="Lokrum">Lokrum</a>, ostensibly to resupply their water but furtively to spy out the city's defenses. St. Blaise (Blasius) revealed their pernicious plan to Stojko, a canon of St. Stephen's Cathedral. The Senate summoned Stojko, who told them in detail how St. Blaise had appeared before him as an old man with a long beard and a bishop's mitre and staff. In this form the effigy of Blaise remained on Dubrovnik's state seal and coinage until the Napoleonic era. Croatians all around the world celebrate the feast of Sveti Vlaho every year.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Iceland">In Iceland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: In Iceland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Blaise (Icelandic: <i>Blasíus</i>) was prominent in Iceland, in particular Southwestern Iceland, where he was known for his purported miracle-working powers.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Saint Blaise is mentioned in <i><a href="/wiki/%C3%9Eorl%C3%A1ks_saga_helga" title="Þorláks saga helga">Þorláks saga helga</a></i>, an Icelandic saga about <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thorlak" title="Saint Thorlak">Thorlak Thorhallsson</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Iceland.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_India">In India</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: In India">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>St. Blaise Church, Sao Bras, <a href="/wiki/Goa" title="Goa">Goa</a>, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> was a small Chapel built in 1541 by Croatian sailors and traders settled in the village. It was elevated to a Parish Church in 1563. The church is a replica of the one in Dubrovnik, dedicated to St. Blaise, the patron of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Italy">In Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: In Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the small village of <a href="/wiki/Sicil%C3%AC" title="Sicilì">Sicilì</a> in <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>, Saint Blaise’s feast day is celebrated on February 3 but also May 14. Locals come to the shrine dedicated to him to show their respect and devotion but also to ask him for help with healing someone who has fallen ill where a special prayer is required.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Blaise_and_Blasius_of_Jersey">Blaise and Blasius of Jersey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Blaise and Blasius of Jersey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> in the 18th and 19th centuries Blaise was adopted as mascot of woolworkers'  pageants, particularly in <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norwich" title="Norwich">Norwich</a>. The popular enthusiasm for the saint is explained by the belief that Blaise had brought prosperity (as symbolised by the <a href="/wiki/Woolsack" title="Woolsack">Woolsack</a>) to England by teaching the English to comb wool. According to the tradition as recorded in printed <a href="/wiki/Broadsheet" title="Broadsheet">broadsheets</a>, Blaise came from <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>. Jersey was certainly a centre of export of woollen goods (as witnessed by the name <i><a href="/wiki/Jersey_(clothing)" title="Jersey (clothing)">jersey</a></i> for the woollen textile). However, this legend is probably the result of confusion with a different saint, <a href="/wiki/Blasius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Blasius of Caesarea">Blasius of Caesarea</a> (<i>Caesarea</i> being also the <a href="/wiki/Latin_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin name">Latin name</a> of Jersey).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Blaise&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a>, Blaise is represented holding two crossed candles in his hand (the Blessing of St. Blaise), or in a cave surrounded by wild beasts, as he was found by the hunters of the governor.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He is often shown with the instruments of his martyrdom, steel combs. The similarity of these instruments of <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">torture</a> to wool combs led to his adoption as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Wool" title="Wool">wool</a> combers in particular, and the wool trade in general. He may also be depicted with crossed <a href="/wiki/Candle" title="Candle">candles</a>. Such crossed candles are used for the <a href="/wiki/Blessing_of_the_Throats" title="Blessing of the Throats">blessing of throats</a> on his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>, which falls on 3 February, the day after <a href="/wiki/Candlemas" title="Candlemas">Candlemas</a> on the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>. Blaise is traditionally believed to intercede in cases of <a href="/wiki/Throat" title="Throat">throat</a> illnesses, especially for fish-bones stuck in the throat.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He is also called upon to aid in protection against <a href="/wiki/Obstructive_sleep_apnea" title="Obstructive sleep apnea">obstructive sleep apnea</a> since this involves the throat tissues interfering with breathing during sleep. (Non-OSA <a href="/wiki/Sleep_disorders" class="mw-redirect" title="Sleep disorders">sleep disorders</a> are typically invoked with the intercession of <a href="/wiki/St._Dymphna" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Dymphna">St. Dymphna</a> since these are more neurological in nature.)
</p>

<p>There are multiple relics of Blaise in a variety of churches and chapels, including multiple whole bodies, at least four heads and several jaws, at least eight arms, and so on:<sup id="cite_ref-crit_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crit-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>With a little research, we would find Saint Blaise armed with a hundred arms, like the giant of the fable. The fingers, teeth, feet of this voluminous saint are too scattered for us to undertake to bring them together.
<br />
</p>
<p style="text-align:right">&#8212; Collin de Plancy, 1822<sup id="cite_ref-crit_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crit-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup></p>
</blockquote>
