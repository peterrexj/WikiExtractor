<p><b>Ulrich of Zell</b>, also known as <b>Wulderic</b>, sometimes <b>of Cluny</b> or <b>of Regensburg</b> (c. 1029 &#8211; 1093), was a <a href="/wiki/Cluniac" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac">Cluniac</a> reformer of <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, abbot, founder and saint.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ulrich_of_Zell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ulrich was born at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> (formerly also known as Ratisbon) in early 1029. His father Bernhold was from Bavaria; his mother Bucca from Swabia, a niece of <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Bishop Gebhard II of Regensburg</a> and also related to <a href="/wiki/Ulrich_of_Augsburg" title="Ulrich of Augsburg">Ulrich of Augsburg</a>. Pious and wealthy, they were  childless for many years and made a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Magnus_of_F%C3%BCssen" title="Magnus of Füssen">Magnus of Füssen</a>, vowing to dedicate a son to the religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-Joachim_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Joachim-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Ulrich was probably educated at the school of <a href="/wiki/St._Emmeram%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Emmeram&#39;s Abbey">St. Emmeram's Abbey</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/William_of_Hirsau" title="William of Hirsau">William of Hirsau</a>, with whom he remained friends throughout his life, but in 1043 he was called to the court of his godfather, <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry III, King of the Germans</a> where he acted as page to <a href="/wiki/Agnes_de_Poitou" class="mw-redirect" title="Agnes de Poitou">Queen Agnes</a>, who was of the ducal house of <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a>, patrons of the reforming <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Cluny" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Cluny">Abbey of Cluny</a>. Ulrich later had to leave the court because his father had been accused of collaborating with enemies from Hungary and had been executed.<sup id="cite_ref-Joachim_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Joachim-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ordained deacon by his uncle Nidger, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Freising" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Freising">Bishop of Freising</a>, he was made archdeacon and provost of the cathedral there,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but was deeply moved by the spirit of reform that was sweeping from Cluny through the 11th century church. On his return from a journey to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> he distributed his possessions to the poor, made a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and, after another short visit to Rome, returned to Regensburg, where he founded a religious community, and then entered the Abbey of Cluny in 1061. Here he soon became personal secretary to <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Cluny" title="Hugh of Cluny">Abbot Hugo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Here he was ordained priest and Hugo commissioned Ulrich to spread the Cluny reforms. He was appointed confessor to the convent at Mareigny in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Autun" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Autun">diocese of Autun</a>, and prior of the community of men in the same place. He also lost an eye and was obliged to return to Cluny.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ulrich_of_Zell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Om 1082 Ulrich was named prior at <a href="/wiki/Peterlingen_Priory" class="mw-redirect" title="Peterlingen Priory">Peterlingen</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Payerne" title="Payerne">Payerne</a>) in the Diocese of <a href="/wiki/Lausanne" title="Lausanne">Lausanne</a>. He took part in the synod in 1084 that elected Gebhard III, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Basel" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Basel">bishop pf Basel</a>. The following year, due to troubles with Bishop Burchard von Oltingen, a partisan of <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>, whom he had accused of breaking celibacy, Ulrich returned again to Cluny, where he acted as adviser to the abbot. Around 1072 Lütold of Rümligen granted to Cluny property and estates around <a href="/wiki/R%C3%BCeggisberg_Priory" title="Rüeggisberg Priory">Rüeggisberg</a>. Ulrich as sent to establish a priory. During the construction period he lived in a cave, the "Pfaffenloch". Rüeggisberg became the first <a href="/wiki/Cluniac_Reforms" title="Cluniac Reforms">Cluniac</a> priory in German-speaking lands.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He then went to Augsburg to reform <a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_and_St._Afra%27s_Abbey" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s and St. Afra&#39;s Abbey">St. Ulrich's and St. Afra's Abbey</a>. The project failed because the population drove Ulrich out of the city.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:20100316-DSC_3657.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/20100316-DSC_3657.jpg/220px-20100316-DSC_3657.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/20100316-DSC_3657.jpg/330px-20100316-DSC_3657.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/20100316-DSC_3657.jpg/440px-20100316-DSC_3657.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="797" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:20100316-DSC_3657.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Ulrich im Schwarzwald</div></div></div>
<p>Ulrich took over an already existing monastic community, founded before 1072 on the Tuniberg by Hesso of Eichstetten and Rimsingen. sometime between 1077 and 1080 Kloster Tuniberg moved to <a href="/wiki/Gr%C3%BCningen_Priory" title="Grüningen Priory">Grüningen</a>. Not finding the locality suitable, he and his monks moved in 1087 to <a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_Priory_in_the_Black_Forest" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s Priory in the Black Forest">Zell</a>, in the Möhlin valley, where there had previously been a cell of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Gall" title="Abbey of Saint Gall">Abbey of Saint Gall</a>. Ulrich's high reputation soon brought him many disciples. He enjoyed the good opinion of Bishop Gebhard, whom he probably helped in reforming the monastery of St. Alban. In 1090 he established <a href="/wiki/Bollschweil_Priory" title="Bollschweil Priory">Bollschweil Priory</a>, a Cluniac nunnery at <i>Bolesweiler</i> (now <a href="/wiki/Bollschweil" title="Bollschweil">Bollschweil</a>), about a mile from Zell. Around 1090, Ulrich went blind, resigned as prior in St. Ulrich, but refused the offer to return to Cluny for the sake of better care.<sup id="cite_ref-Joachim_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Joachim-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died at Zell, later known as <a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_Priory_in_the_Black_Forest" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s Priory in the Black Forest">St. Ulrich im Schwarzwald</a>, probably on 10 July 1093. He was buried in the cloister, but three years later his body was brought into the church.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ulrich_of_Zell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg/220px-St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg/330px-St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg/440px-St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3264" data-file-height="2448" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Ulrich_Schwarzwald_Pfarrkirche_Kreuzaltar_Ulrichsgrab_detail2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>tomb of St Ulrich, Schwarzwald Pfarrkirche</div></div></div>
<p>Around 1300, the village of Zell was renamed St. Ulrich in honour of the monastery founder. Ulrich's memory is commemorated at the annual three-day festival in St. Ulrich with a varied programme.
</p><p>His feast was celebrated for the first time on 14 July 1139, and 14 July remains his feast day.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ulrich_of_Zell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His work "Consuetudines cluniacenses" (<i>"Uses of Cluny"</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> was composed at the request of <a href="/wiki/William_of_Hirsau" title="William of Hirsau">William of Hirsau</a>, in three books. The first two, written between 1079 and 1082, treat of liturgy and the education of novices; the third, written not later than 1087, speaks of the administration of monasteries.
</p><p>His life of <a href="/wiki/Hermann_I_of_Baden" class="mw-redirect" title="Hermann I of Baden">Hermann of Zähringen, Margrave of Baden</a>, later a monk of Cluny, is lost.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biographies">Biographies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ulrich_of_Zell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Biographies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Two biographies of him exist: the first<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> (with selected passages from the "Vita posterior") was written anonymously around 1109 by a monk of <a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_Priory_in_the_Black_Forest" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s Priory in the Black Forest">Zell</a> at the request of Adalbert, a recluse near Regensburg; the other (the <i>Vita posterior</i>), also anonymous, was written between 1109 and 1130. Particulars of his life are also contained in his writings.
</p>
