<p><b>Pope Gregory IX</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gregorius IX</i>; born <b>Ugolino di Conti</b>; c. 1145 or before 1170 – 22 August 1241) was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 19 March 1227 until his death in 1241. He is known for issuing the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">Decretales</a></i> and instituting the <a href="/wiki/Papal_Inquisition" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Inquisition">Papal Inquisition</a>, in response to the failures of the episcopal inquisitions established during the time of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Pope Lucius III</a>, by means of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Ad_abolendam" title="Ad abolendam">Ad abolendam</a></i>, issued in 1184.
</p><p>The successor of <a href="/wiki/Honorius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius III">Honorius III</a>, he fully inherited the traditions of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Gregory VII</a> and of his own cousin <a href="/wiki/Innocent_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent III">Innocent III</a> and zealously continued their policy of <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">papal supremacy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ugolino (Hugh) was born in <a href="/wiki/Anagni" title="Anagni">Anagni</a>. The date of his birth varies in sources between c. 1145<sup id="cite_ref-ott_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and 1170.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He received his education at the Universities of Paris and Bologna.
</p><p>He was created <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal-Deacon</a> of the church of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Eustachio" title="Sant&#39;Eustachio">Sant'Eustachio</a> by his cousin<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Innocent III in December 1198. In 1206 he was promoted to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Bishop_of_Ostia_e_Velletri" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Bishop of Ostia e Velletri">Cardinal Bishop of Ostia e Velletri</a>. He became <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_the_Sacred_College_of_Cardinals" class="mw-redirect" title="Dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals">Dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals</a> in 1218 or 1219. Upon the special request of Saint Francis, in 1220, Pope Honorius III appointed him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Protector" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Protector">Cardinal Protector</a> of the order of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>.
</p><p>As <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Bishop_of_Ostia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Bishop of Ostia">Cardinal Bishop of Ostia</a>, he cultivated a wide range of acquaintances, among them the <a href="/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Monarchy of the United Kingdom">Queen of England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Isabella_of_Angoul%C3%AAme" title="Isabella of Angoulême">Isabella of Angoulême</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Abulafia_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abulafia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gregory IX was elevated to the papacy in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1227" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1227">papal election</a> of 1227.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He took the name "Gregory" because he formally assumed the papal office at the monastery of Saint Gregory ad Septem Solia.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> That same year, in one of his earliest acts as pope, he expanded the <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Inquisition" title="Medieval Inquisition">Inquisition</a> powers already assigned to <a href="/wiki/Konrad_von_Marburg" title="Konrad von Marburg">Konrad von Marburg</a> to encompass the investigation of heresy throughout the whole of Germany.
</p><p>Gregory's bull <i><a href="/wiki/Parens_scientiarum" title="Parens scientiarum">Parens scientiarum</a></i> of 1231, after the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris_strike_of_1229" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Paris strike of 1229">University of Paris strike of 1229</a>, resolved differences between the unruly <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">university scholars of Paris</a> and the local authorities. His solution was in the manner of a true follower of Innocent III: he issued what in retrospect has been viewed as the <i>magna carta</i> of the University, assuming direct control by extending papal patronage: his bull allowed future suspension of lectures over a flexible range of provocations, from "monstrous injury or offense" to squabbles over "the right to assess the rents of lodgings".
</p><p>In October 1232, after an investigation by legates, Gregory proclaimed a <a href="/wiki/Stedinger_Crusade" title="Stedinger Crusade">crusade against the Stedinger</a> to be preached in northern Germany. In June 1233, he granted a plenary indulgence to those who took part.<sup id="cite_ref-CSJ_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CSJ-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1233 Gregory IX established the <a href="/wiki/Papal_Inquisition" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Inquisition">Papal Inquisition</a> to regularize the prosecution of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vizzier_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vizzier-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Papal Inquisition was intended to bring order to the haphazard episcopal inquisitions which had been established by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Lucius III</a> in 1184. Gregory's aim was to bring order and legality to the process of dealing with heresy, since there had been tendencies by mobs of townspeople to burn alleged heretics without much of a trial. In 1231 Pope Gregory IX appointed a number of Papal Inquisitors (<i>Inquisitores haereticae pravitatis</i>), mostly <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a> and <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a>, for the various regions of France, Italy and parts of Germany. Contrary to popular belief, the aim was to introduce due process and objective investigation into the beliefs of those accused to the often erratic and unjust persecution of heresy on the part of local ecclesiastical and secular jurisdictions.<sup id="cite_ref-Madden_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Madden-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bologna,_gregorio_IX,_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma,_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X,_1290_ca.,_02.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg/220px-Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg/330px-Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg/440px-Bologna%2C_gregorio_IX%2C_decretales_con_commento_di_bernardo_da_parma%2C_e_testi_di_innocenzo_IV_e_gregorio_X%2C_1290_ca.%2C_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2304" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>Page from a late 13 c. copy of the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">Decretals of Gregory IX</a></i>, now in the <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Medicea_Laurenziana" class="mw-redirect" title="Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana">Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana</a>, Florence</figcaption></figure>
<p>Gregory was a remarkably skillful and learned lawyer. He caused to be prepared <a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX"><i>Nova Compilatio decretalium</i></a>, which was promulgated in numerous copies in 1234 (first printed at <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> in 1473). This <i>New Compilation of Decretals</i> was the culmination of a long process of systematising the mass of pronouncements that had accumulated since the <a href="/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages" title="Early Middle Ages">Early Middle Ages</a>, a process that had been under way since the first half of the 12th century and had come to fruition in the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretum_Gratiani" title="Decretum Gratiani">Decretum</a></i>, compiled and edited by the papally commissioned legist <a href="/wiki/Gratian_(jurist)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gratian (jurist)">Gratian</a> and published in 1140. The supplement completed the work, which provided the foundation for papal legal theory.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">1234 Decretals</a>, he invested the doctrine of <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Perpetua_servitus_iudaeorum&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Perpetua servitus iudaeorum (page does not exist)">perpetua servitus iudaeorum</a></i> – perpetual servitude of the Jews – with the force of canonical law. According to this, the followers of the <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">Talmud</a> would have to remain in a condition of political servitude until <a href="/wiki/Judgment_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Judgment Day">Judgment Day</a>. The doctrine then found its way into the doctrine of <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servitus_camerae_imperialis&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Servitus camerae imperialis (page does not exist)">servitus camerae imperialis</a></i>, or servitude immediately subject to the Emperor's authority, promulgated by <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>. The Jews were thus suppressed from having direct influence over the political process and the life of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> states into the 19th century and the rise of <a href="/wiki/Liberalism" title="Liberalism">liberalism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In 1234, Gregory issued the papal bull <i><a href="/wiki/Rachel_suum_videns" title="Rachel suum videns">Rachel suum videns</a></i> calling for a new crusade to the Holy Land, leading to the <a href="/wiki/Barons%27_Crusade" title="Barons&#39; Crusade">Crusade of 1239</a>.
</p><p>In 1239, under the influence of <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Donin" title="Nicholas Donin">Nicholas Donin</a>, a Jewish convert to Christianity, Gregory ordered that all copies of the Jewish Talmud be confiscated. Following a <a href="/wiki/Disputation_of_Paris" title="Disputation of Paris">public disputation between Christians and Jewish theologians</a>, this culminated in a mass burning of some 12,000 handwritten Talmudic manuscripts on 12 June 1242, in Paris.
</p><p>Gregory was a supporter of the mendicant orders which he saw as an excellent means for counteracting by voluntary poverty the love of luxury and splendour which was possessing many ecclesiastics. He was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Saint Dominic</a> as well as <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a>. On 17 January 1235, he approved the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_of_Mercy" title="Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy">Order of Our Lady of Mercy</a> for the redemption of captives. He appointed ten cardinals<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Saints <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Elisabeth of Hungary">Elisabeth of Hungary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Dominic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">Anthony of Padua</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, of whom he had been a personal friend and early patron. He transformed a chapel to Our Lady in the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Popolo" title="Santa Maria del Popolo">Santa Maria del Popolo</a> in Rome.
</p><p>Gregory IX endorsed the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Crusades" title="Northern Crusades">Northern Crusades</a> and attempts to bring <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavic peoples</a> in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Europe" title="Eastern Europe">Eastern Europe</a> (particularly <a href="/wiki/Pskov_Republic" title="Pskov Republic">Pskov Republic</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Novgorod_Republic" title="Novgorod Republic">Novgorod Republic</a>) under the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">Papacy</a>'s fold.<sup id="cite_ref-Christiansen_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christiansen-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1232, Gregory IX asked the <a href="/wiki/Livonian_Brothers_of_the_Sword" title="Livonian Brothers of the Sword">Livonian Brothers of the Sword</a> to send troops to protect <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>, whose semi-<a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> people were fighting against the Novgorod Republic in the <a href="/wiki/Finnish-Novgorodian_wars" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish-Novgorodian wars">Finnish-Novgorodian wars</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> however, there is no known information if any ever arrived to assist.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Struggle_with_Frederick_II">Struggle with Frederick II</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Struggle with Frederick II">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giorgio_Vasari,_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX,_1572-73,_03_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="202" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg/440px-Giorgio_Vasari%2C_Scomunica_di_Federico_II_da_parte_di_Gregorio_IX%2C_1572-73%2C_03_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2178" data-file-height="1996" /></a><figcaption>Fanciful 16th c. fresco depicting Gregory excommunicating <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Sala_Regia_(Vatican)" title="Sala Regia (Vatican)">Sala Regia</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Giorgio_Vasari" title="Giorgio Vasari">Giorgio Vasari</a>. Since few details where provided to the artist, the excommunication scene is given generically. Fredrick is shown pointing to a book with the word "Concilium" shown, possibly a reference to the general council that the emperor attempted to call to depose Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>At the coronation of Frederick II in Rome, 22 November 1220, the emperor made a vow to embark for the Holy Land in August 1221. Gregory IX began his pontificate by suspending the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>, for dilatoriness in carrying out the promised <a href="/wiki/Sixth_Crusade" title="Sixth Crusade">Sixth Crusade</a>. Frederick II appealed to the sovereigns of Europe complaining of his treatment. The suspension was followed by <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a> and threats of deposition, as deeper rifts appeared. Frederick II went to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> and in fact managed to take possession of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. Gregory IX distrusted the emperor, since Rainald, the imperial Governor of Spoleto, had invaded the Pontifical States during the emperor's absence.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In June 1229, Frederick II returned from the Holy Land, routed the papal army which Gregory IX had sent to invade Sicily, and made new overtures of peace to the pope. The war of 1228–1230 is known as the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Keys" title="War of the Keys">War of the Keys</a>.
</p><p>Gregory IX and Frederick came to a truce, but when Frederick defeated the <a href="/wiki/Lombard_League" title="Lombard League">Lombard League</a> in 1239, the possibility that he might dominate all of Italy, surrounding the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, became a very real threat. A new outbreak of hostilities led to a fresh excommunication of the emperor in 1239 and to a prolonged war. Gregory denounced Frederick II as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_heresy" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian heresy">heretic</a> and summoned a council at Rome to give point to his <a href="/wiki/Anathema" title="Anathema">anathema</a>. Frederick responded by <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Meloria_(1241)" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Meloria (1241)">trying to capture or sink</a> as many ships carrying prelates to the synod as he could. Eberhard II von Truchsees, <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Salzburg">Prince-Archbishop of Salzburg</a>, in 1241 at the Council of <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> declared that Gregory IX was "that man of perdition, whom they call Antichrist, who in his extravagant boasting says, 'I am God, I cannot err'."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He argued that the Pope was the "little horn" of Daniel 7:8:<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote><p>A little horn has grown up with eyes and mouth speaking great things, which is reducing three of these kingdoms – i.e. Sicily, Italy, and Germany – to subserviency, is persecuting the people of Christ and the saints of God with intolerable opposition, is confounding things human and divine, and is attempting things unutterable, execrable.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The struggle only ended with of Gregory IX's death  on 22 August 1241. The pope died before events could reach their climax; it was his successor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Innocent IV</a>, who  in 1245 declared a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> that would finish the Hohenstaufen threat.
