<p><b>Saint Aldate</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/ɔː/: &#39;au&#39; in &#39;fraud&#39;">ɔː</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/eɪ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;face&#39;">eɪ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span></span>/</a></span></span>; died 577) was a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Gloucester" title="Gloucester">Gloucester</a>, venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 4 February. Aldate's life is not detailed historically, but he was probably a <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historical)">Briton</a> killed by the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Deorham" title="Battle of Deorham">Deorham</a>. He is reported to have roused the countryside to resist <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> invasion forces. He is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Sarum_rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Sarum rite">Sarum</a> and other <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>; his feast occurs in a Gloucester calendar (14th-century addition); churches were dedicated to him at Gloucester and <a href="/wiki/St_Aldate%27s_Church" title="St Aldate&#39;s Church">Oxford</a>, as well as a famous Oxford street: <a href="/wiki/St_Aldate%27s,_Oxford" title="St Aldate&#39;s, Oxford">St Aldate's, Oxford</a> and a minor street in Gloucester. But nothing seems to be known of him: it was even suggested that his name was a corruption of "old gate".
</p>
