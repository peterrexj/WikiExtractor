<p><b>Bonfilius</b> (c. 1040 – c. 1125) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, monk and bishop.
</p><p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Osimo" title="Osimo">Osimo</a>, close to Ancona,<sup id="cite_ref-Watkins_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Watkins-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and entered the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery of Santa Maria di Storaco, close to <a href="/wiki/Filottrano" title="Filottrano">Filottrano</a>, as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. There, although being the youngest of the monastery, he was known as the most knowledgable in <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">scripture</a> in the congregation and was elected abbot of the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-Parolin_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parolin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-Watkins_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Watkins-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Between 1072 and 1078, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Foligno" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Foligno">bishop of Foligno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nicolini_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nicolini-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Bonfilius made a donation to the <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canons</a> of the church of Folignano in 1078 and was encouraging Mainardo in the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Sassovivo_Abbey" title="Sassovivo Abbey">abbey of Sassovivo</a> in 1080.<sup id="cite_ref-Nicolini_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nicolini-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1096 he fulfilled his wish to go to the Holy Land and joined the forces of the First Crusade on <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. On the way to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, his companions that he had come with died one by one and he reached <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> by himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Parolin_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parolin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There, he lift as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> until 1104 when he returned to Italy. As in the meantime the vacant position of bishop in Foligno had been filled by a certain Andreas, he returned to the abbey of Storaco before spending his last years again as a hermit at St  Maria of la Far near <a href="/wiki/Cingoli" title="Cingoli">Cingoli</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SantiBeati_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SantiBeati-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-Watkins_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Watkins-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Bonfilius died here a natural death on the 27th of September 1115.<sup id="cite_ref-Nicolini_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nicolini-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His first biographer was St. <a href="/wiki/Sylvester_Gozzolini" title="Sylvester Gozzolini">Sylvester Gozzolini</a>, the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Sylvestrines" title="Sylvestrines">Sylvestrines</a>, who was inspired by Bonfilius' life. His remains were later burried in the <a href="/wiki/Cingoli_Cathedral" title="Cingoli Cathedral">Cingoli Cathedral</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated in Cingoli on 28 January and in Osimo on 27 September. 
</p>
