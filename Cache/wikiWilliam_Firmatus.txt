<p><b>William Firmatus</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr"><b>Guillaume Firmat</b></i>; 1026&#8211;1103) was a <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrim</a> of the eleventh century, now venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Firmatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William Firmatus was a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Physician" title="Physician">physician</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-prenom_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prenom-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Following a spiritual prompting against <a href="/wiki/Seven_deadly_sins#Greed" title="Seven deadly sins">greed</a>, he gave away all his possessions to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He lived a reclusive life with his mother until he entered a hermitage near <a href="/wiki/Laval,_Mayenne" title="Laval, Mayenne">Laval, Mayenne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathon_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He spent the rest of his life on <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimages</a> and as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at <a href="/wiki/Savigny,_Manche" title="Savigny, Manche">Savigny</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mantilly" title="Mantilly">Mantilly</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to legend, he saved the people of <a href="/wiki/Choilley-Dardenay" title="Choilley-Dardenay">Choilley-Dardenay</a> during <a href="/wiki/Drought" title="Drought">drought</a> by striking the ground with his pilgrim's staff, which caused a <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrosphere)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spring (hydrosphere)">spring</a> of water to bubble up. He died in 1103 of natural causes.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg/250px-Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="336" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg/375px-Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg/500px-Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg 2x" data-file-width="956" data-file-height="1284" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Giotto_-_Legend_of_St_Francis_-_-15-_-_Sermon_to_the_Birds.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Sermon to the Birds</i> of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Saint Francis</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Giotto" title="Giotto">Giotto</a> (1266). The life of William Firmatus evinced a similar relationship with nature.</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationship_with_wild_animals">Relationship with wild animals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Firmatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relationship with wild animals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>William is especially noted for his love of wildlife and the unusual level of <a href="/wiki/Human%E2%80%93animal_communication" title="Human–animal communication">communication he seemed to have with animals</a>. This was so much so that the local people used to ask his help with animals that raided their crops.<sup id="cite_ref-taik_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-taik-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> One particular story to this effect involves a <a href="/wiki/Boar" class="mw-redirect" title="Boar">wild boar</a>, which William is said to have led by the ear from a farmer's plot, instructing it to <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fast</a> for the night in a solitary cell.<sup id="cite_ref-spire_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spire-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Little Bollandists</a> go on to record, along with the boar miracle, that
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>It is said of him that even the wildest <a href="/wiki/Bird" title="Bird">birds</a> would approach him without fear, and come and eat out of his hand, and take refuge under his clothes from the cold. When he sat by a pond near his cell, the fish would swim to his feet and readily allow themselves to be taken up by the <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">servant of God</a>, who put them back into the water <a href="/wiki/Catch_and_release" title="Catch and release">without hurting them</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bollandists_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bollandists-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Firmatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon William's death, three townships disputed possession of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">remains</a>. The winner was <a href="/wiki/Mortain" title="Mortain">Mortain</a>, which, to procure the relics, used the full force of "its entire clergy and an innumerable crowd of its people".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint William is also <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> at Savigny and Mantilly.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> mentions William in its article on <a href="/wiki/Coutances" title="Coutances">Coutances</a>, which  accords him special honor as well, and mentions his patronage of the collegiate church of Mortain.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He is a patron against <a href="/wiki/Headache" title="Headache">headaches</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Firmatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Saint_symbology" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint symbology">art</a>, Saint William is often shown thrusting his arm into a fire. Also, he may be depicted with a raven, which is guiding him as a pilgrim to the Holy Land.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
