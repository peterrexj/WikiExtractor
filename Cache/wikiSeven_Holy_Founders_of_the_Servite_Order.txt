					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Seven founders of a Roman Catholic order</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Seven Holy Founders of the Servite Order</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 December 1717 by <a href="/wiki/Clement_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement XI">Clement XI</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 January 1888 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">17 February</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The <b>Seven Holy Founders of the Servite Order</b>, by name Bonfilius, Alexis, Manettus, Amideus, Hugh, Sostene and Buonagiunta of Florence, were seven holy men of the town of Florence whom became bound to each other in a spiritual friendship that were eventually called by the Virgin Mother of God in a vision that they reportedly all shared in one and the same moment (though at first each was unaware that the other saw what he saw), whom they practised an intense devotion towards, to 'Leave the World, the Better to Serve Almighty God'.  
</p><p>Bonfilius of Florence was born Bonfilius Monaldi (Buonfiglio dei Monaldi), Alexis of Florence was born Alexis Falconieri (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <span lang="it" style="font-style: normal;">Alessio Falconieri</span>) (1200 – 17 February 1310), Manettus of Florence was born Benedict dell'Antella (Benedetto dell' Antella), Amideus of Florence was born Bartholemew Amidei (also Bartolomeo degli Amidei; died 1265), Hugh of Florence was born Ricovero Uguccioni (Hugh dei Lippi Uggucioni (Ricovero dei Lippi-Ugguccioni)), Sostene of Florence was born Gerardino Sostegni (Gherardino di Sostegno) and Buonagiunta of Florence was born John Manetti (Giovanni di Buonagiunta (Bonajuncta)).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_founders">The founders</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The founders">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Alexis_Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Alexis Falconieri">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif/220px-Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif" decoding="async" width="220" height="246" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif/330px-Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/Sant_Alessio_Falconieri.gif 2x" data-file-width="353" data-file-height="395" /></a><figcaption>Sant Alessio Falconieri (Second of The Seven Holy Founders by age, Last to pass away).</figcaption></figure>
<p>Falconieri was one of the seven founders of the <a href="/wiki/Servite_Order" title="Servite Order">Servite Order</a>, who are celebrated together on the anniversary of his death.
</p>
<dl><dt>Life</dt></dl>
<p>He was the son of Bernardo Falconieri, a merchant prince of <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, and one of the leaders of the Republic. His family belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines" title="Guelphs and Ghibellines">Guelph</a> party, and opposed the Imperialists whenever they could consistently with their political principles.<sup id="cite_ref-McGinnis_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGinnis-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Falconieri grew up in the practice of the most profound humility. He became a wealthy noble in one of Italy's most wealthy and cultured cities.<sup id="cite_ref-nd_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nd-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Falconieri joined the Laudesi, a pious confraternity of the Blessed Virgin, and there met the six future companions of his life of sanctity. According to tradition, he was favoured with an apparition of the Mother of God, 15 August 1233, as were these companions. The seven soon afterwards founded the <a href="/wiki/Servite" class="mw-redirect" title="Servite">Order of the Servites</a>. Falconieri at once abandoned all, and retired to La Camarzia, a house on the outskirts of the town, and the following year to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Senario" title="Monte Senario">Monte Senario</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McGinnis_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGinnis-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Falconieri was bestowed the title of <i>Founder and Mystic</i>.
</p><p>With humility, he traversed, as a <a href="/wiki/Mendicant" title="Mendicant">mendicant</a>, in quest of alms for his brethren, the streets of the city through which he had lately moved as a prominent citizen. So deep and sincere was his humility that, though he lived to the great age of hundred and ten years, he always refused to enter the priesthood, of which he deemed himself unworthy.<sup id="cite_ref-McGinnis_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGinnis-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His duties were confined principally to the material needs of the various communities in which he lived. In 1252 the new church at Cafaggio, on the outskirts of Florence, was completed under his care, with the financial assistance of Chiarissimo Falconieri. <a href="/wiki/Giuliana_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Giuliana Falconieri">Giuliana Falconieri</a>, his niece, was trained in sanctity under his personal direction.
</p><p>The influence exerted on his countrymen by Falconieri and his companions may be gathered from the fact that in a few years ten thousand persons had enrolled themselves under the banner of the Blessed Virgin in the Servite Order.
</p><p>He died in <a href="/wiki/Monte_Senario" title="Monte Senario">Monte Senario</a> at about 110 years of age on 17 February 1310.
</p><p>His shrine and entombed body is near the church of the <a href="/wiki/Santissima_Annunziata,_Florence" title="Santissima Annunziata, Florence">Santissima Annunziata</a> in Florence. <a href="/wiki/Clement_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement XI">Clement XI</a> declared Falconieri worthy of the veneration of the faithful, 1 December 1717, and accorded the same honour to his six companions on 3 July 1725. 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Amadeus_of_the_Amidei">Amadeus of the Amidei</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Amadeus of the Amidei">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Amadio_12.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Amadio_12.JPG/220px-Amadio_12.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="319" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Amadio_12.JPG/330px-Amadio_12.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Amadio_12.JPG/440px-Amadio_12.JPG 2x" data-file-width="503" data-file-height="730" /></a><figcaption>Fresco of Bartolomeus Amidei (Saint Amadeus degli Amidei)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Amadeus of the Amidei (died 1265) was also known as Bartolomeo degli Amidei.  He was one of the seven founders of the Servite Order in 1233. He was born into the <a href="/wiki/Amidei" title="Amidei">Amidei</a> family in the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Florence" title="Republic of Florence">Republic of Florence</a>, and died at <a href="/wiki/Monte_Senario" title="Monte Senario">Monte Senario</a> in 1265.  His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is with the other six, on 17 February.<sup id="cite_ref-trecc_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trecc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hugh_dei_Lippi_Ugguccioni">Hugh dei Lippi Ugguccioni</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hugh dei Lippi Ugguccioni">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Hugh dei Lippi Ugguccioni was one of the founders of the Order, who were collectively known as the Seven Holy Founders.  He served as the <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Servites" class="mw-redirect" title="Servites">Servites</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, having initially followed <a href="/wiki/Philip_Benizi" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Benizi">Philip Benizi</a> to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He died in Germany on 3 May 1282<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seven_Holy_Founders_of_the_Servite_Order&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> canonized them all on 15 January 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-trecc_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trecc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  After the canonization of the seven founders, their feast was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 11 February, the anniversary of the granting of canonical approval to the order in 1304. In 1909, 11 February became the feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Our Lady of Lourdes">Our Lady of Lourdes</a>, and the feast of the seven founders was moved to 12 February. In the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the calendar, 17 February, the date of death of Alexis Falconieri was judged more appropriate.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
