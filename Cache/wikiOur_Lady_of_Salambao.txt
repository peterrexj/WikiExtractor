<p><b>Our Lady of Salambáo</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_Philippines" title="Spanish language in the Philippines">Spanish</a>: <i>Nuestra Señora de la Inmaculada Concepción de Salambáo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Tagalog_language" title="Tagalog language">Tagalog</a>: <i>Mahal na Birhen ng Salambáo</i> or <i>Birheng ng Salambáw</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines" title="Catholic Church in the Philippines">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Titles_of_Mary" title="Titles of Mary">title</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> venerated in <a href="/wiki/Obando,_Bulacan" title="Obando, Bulacan">Obando, Bulacan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>. The Virgin Mary under this title is venerated as the local <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patroness</a> of fishing, owing to the image's discovery in a <i><a href="/wiki/Salamb%C3%A1w" title="Salambáw">salambáw</a></i>, a type of big fishing net supported by bamboo crosspieces and mounted on a raft.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The image is enshrined with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Clare_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Clare of Assisi">Saint Clare of Assisi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paschal_Baylon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Paschal Baylon">Saint Paschal Baylon</a> inside <a href="/wiki/Obando_Church" title="Obando Church">Obando  Church</a>. The three saints form a triad that is the focus of the annual <a href="/wiki/Obando_Fertility_Rites" title="Obando Fertility Rites">Obando Fertility Rites</a> held from 17 to 19 May.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Our_Lady_of_Salambao&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The date of the image's finding is traditionally held to be 19 June 1763. Three fishermen, named Juan, Julián, and Diego dela Cruz, were fishing at a place known as Hulingduong in the town of Tambobong (today in <a href="/wiki/Malabon" title="Malabon">Malabon</a> City). There, they caught the statue with their <i>salambáw</i>, which is a large type of fishing lift net made from bamboo crosspieces and mounted on a raft. When the fishermen tried to bring the Virgin's image to neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Navotas" title="Navotas">Navotas</a>, their  <i>salambáw</i> suddenly grew heavy and immobile. They decided to head for Obando, and their raft suddenly lightened and became easy to paddle. This they took to be a sign that the Virgin wanted to be enshrined in Obando.
</p><p>The image&#8212;complete with a replica <i>salambáw</i>&#8212;is now housed in a wooden <i>retablo</i> (<a href="/wiki/Reredos" title="Reredos">reredos</a>) above the high altar of San Pascual Baylón Parish, together with statues of Saint Clare and Saint Paschal. A replica of the statue, in its own <i>salambáw</i>, is used for the annual procession on the third day of the Rites.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast">Feast</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Our_Lady_of_Salambao&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Feast">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The feast of Our Lady of Salambáo is on 19 May, which is the last day of a <a href="/wiki/Triduum" title="Triduum">triduum</a> honouring the triad of saints. Spread across the triduum are the Rites, which constitute <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a> and <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">processions</a> where devotees of both sexes joyfully dance the <i><a href="/wiki/Fandango" title="Fandango">fandango</a></i> in supplication for a child and of good livelihood and harvest.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The celebrations are centered on the San Pascual Baylón Parish Church, also known as the National Shrine of Nuestra Señora de la Inmaculada Concepción de Salambáo in Barangay Pag-asa, Obando, Bulacan.
</p>
