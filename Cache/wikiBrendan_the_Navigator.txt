<p><b>Brendan of Clonfert</b> (c. AD 484 – c. 577) is one of the early <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Irish monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> and one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>. He is also referred to as Brendan the Navigator, Brendan the Voyager, Brendan the Anchorite, and Brendan the Bold. The Irish translation of his name is Naomh Bréanainn or Naomh Breandán. He is mainly known for his legendary voyage to find the “Isle of the Blessed” which is sometimes referred to as “Saint Brendan’s Island”. The written narrative of his journey comes from the <a href="/wiki/Immram" title="Immram">immram</a> The Navigatio Sancti Brendani Abbatis (Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot).
</p><p>Saint Brendan's <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 16 May by <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglicans</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Christians</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is very little secure information concerning Brendan's life, although at least the approximate dates of his birth and death, and accounts of some events in his life, are found in Irish annals and genealogies. The earliest mention of Brendan is in the <i>Vita Sancti Columbae</i> (Life of Saint Columba) of <a href="/wiki/Adamnan" class="mw-redirect" title="Adamnan">Adamnan</a> written between AD 679 and 704. The earliest mention of him as a seafarer appears in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i> of the ninth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Allen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The principal works regarding Brendan and his legend are a "Life of Brendan" in several Latin (<i>Vita Brendani</i>) and Irish versions (<i>Betha Brenainn</i>) and the better known <i>Navigatio Sancti Brendani Abbatis</i> (Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot).<sup id="cite_ref-oode_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oode-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Unfortunately, the versions of the <i>Vita</i> and the <i>Navigatio</i> provide little reliable information of his life and travels; they do, however, attest to the development of devotion to him in the centuries after his death. An additional problem is that the precise relationship between the <i>Vita</i> and the <i>Navigatio</i> traditions is uncertain.
</p><p>The date when the <i>Vita</i> tradition began is uncertain. The earliest surviving copies are no earlier than the end of the twelfth century, but scholars suggest that a version of the <i>Vita</i> was composed before AD 1000. The <i>Navigatio</i> was probably written earlier than the <i>Vita</i>, perhaps in the second half of the eighth century.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Aengus_the_Culdee" class="mw-redirect" title="Aengus the Culdee">Aengus the Culdee</a>, in his <i>Litany</i>, composed in the end of the eighth century, invoked "the sixty who accompanied St. Brendan in his quest for the Land of Promise".<sup id="cite_ref-oode_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oode-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Any attempt to reconstruct the facts of the life of Brendan or to understand the nature of his legend must be based principally on Irish annals and genealogies and on the various versions of the <i>Vita Brendani</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brendan was born in AD <a href="/wiki/484_in_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="484 in Ireland">484</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tralee" title="Tralee">Tralee</a>, in <a href="/wiki/County_Kerry" title="County Kerry">County Kerry</a>, in the province of <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a>, in the south-west of Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He was born among the Altraige, an <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clan</a> originally centred around <a href="/wiki/Tralee_Bay" title="Tralee Bay">Tralee Bay</a>, to parents called Finnlug and Cara. Tradition has it that he was born in the Kilfenora/Fenit area on the North side of the bay. He was baptised at Tubrid, near <a href="/wiki/Ardfert" title="Ardfert">Ardfert</a> by <a href="/wiki/Erc_of_Slane" title="Erc of Slane">Erc of Slane</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and was originally to be called "Mobhí" but signs and portents attending his birth and baptism led to him being christened 'Broen-finn' or 'fair-drop'. For five years he was both educated and given in <a href="/wiki/Fosterage" title="Fosterage">fosterage</a> to St. <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Dte_of_Killeedy" title="Íte of Killeedy">Íte of Killeedy</a>, "The Brigid of Munster". When he was six he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Iarlaithe_mac_Loga" class="mw-redirect" title="Iarlaithe mac Loga">Jarlath</a>'s monastery school at <a href="/wiki/Tuam" title="Tuam">Tuam</a> to further his education. Brendan is considered one of the "Twelve Apostles of Ireland", one of those said to have been tutored by the great teacher, <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kerry_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kerry-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundations">Foundations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At age 26, Brendan was ordained a priest by Erc.<sup id="cite_ref-eye_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eye-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Afterward, he founded a number of monasteries. Brendan's first voyage took him to the <a href="/wiki/Aran_Islands" title="Aran Islands">Aran Islands</a>, where he founded a monastery.  He also visited Hinba (<a href="/wiki/Argyll" title="Argyll">Argyll</a>), an island off the <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scottish</a> coast, where he is said to have met <a href="/wiki/Saint_Columba" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Columba">Columcille</a>. On the same voyage he travelled to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and finally to <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, on the northern coast of <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.  
</p><p>Between AD 512 and 530 Brendan built monastic cells at <a href="/wiki/Ardfert" title="Ardfert">Ardfert</a>, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Shanakeel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Shanakeel (page does not exist)">Shanakeel</a> (<i>Seana Cill</i>, usually translated as the "Old Church"), at the foot of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Brandon" title="Mount Brandon">Mount Brandon</a>. From there he is supposed to have embarked on his famous seven-year voyage bound for <a href="/wiki/Paradise" title="Paradise">Paradise</a>. The old Irish calendars assigned a feast for the <i>"egressio familiae Sancti Brendani"</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-oode_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oode-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legendary_journey">Legendary journey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legendary journey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brendan is primarily renowned for his legendary journey to the Isle of the Blessed as described in the <i>Navigatio Sancti Brendani Abbatis</i> (<i>Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot</i>) of the ninth century. Many versions exist that narrate how he set out on the Atlantic Ocean with sixteen <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> (although other versions record fourteen monks and three unbelievers who joined in the last minute) to search for the <a href="/wiki/Garden_of_Eden" title="Garden of Eden">Garden of Eden</a>. One of these companions is said to have been <a href="/wiki/Malo_(saint)" title="Malo (saint)">Malo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The voyage is dated to AD 512–530, before his travel to the island of <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain" title="Great Britain">Great Britain</a>. On his trip, Brendan is supposed to have seen <a href="/wiki/Saint_Brendan%27s_Island" title="Saint Brendan&#39;s Island">Saint Brendan's Island</a>, a blessed island covered with vegetation. He also encountered a <a href="/wiki/Sea_monster" title="Sea monster">sea monster</a>, an adventure he shared with his contemporary Columcille. The most commonly illustrated adventure is his landing on an island which turned out to be a giant sea monster named "<a href="/wiki/Jasconius" class="mw-redirect" title="Jasconius">Jasconius</a>".
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Voyage_of_Saint_Brendan_the_Abbot"><i>The Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: The Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The earliest extant version of the <i>Navigatio Sancti Brendani Abbatis</i> (<i>Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot</i>) was recorded <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> AD 900. There are over 100 manuscripts of the narrative throughout Europe and many translations. The <i>Navigatio</i> is plainly a Christian narrative, but also narrates natural phenomena and fantastical events and places, which appealed to a broad audience. The <i>Navigatio</i> contains many parallels and inter-textual references to <i><a href="/wiki/The_Voyage_of_Bran" title="The Voyage of Bran">Bran</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Voyage_of_M%C3%A1el_D%C3%BAin" title="The Voyage of Máel Dúin">The Voyage of Máel Dúin</a></i>.
</p><p>On the Kerry coast, Brendan built a <a href="/wiki/Currach" title="Currach">currach</a>-like boat of <a href="/wiki/Wattle_(construction)" title="Wattle (construction)">wattle</a>, covered it with hides tanned in oak bark and softened with butter, and set up a mast and a sail. He and a small group of monks fasted for 40 days, and after a prayer on the shore, embarked in the name of the <a href="/wiki/Most_Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Most Holy Trinity">Most Holy Trinity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-brookline_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brookline-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The narrative is characterized by much literary licence, e. g., it refers to <a href="/wiki/Hell" title="Hell">Hell</a> where "great demons threw down lumps of fiery slag from an island with rivers of gold fire" and also to "great crystal pillars". Many speculate that these are references to volcanic activity around <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Iceland</a> and to <a href="/wiki/Icebergs" class="mw-redirect" title="Icebergs">icebergs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-silouan_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silouan-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dt>Synopsis</dt></dl>
<p>The journey of Brendan first begins when he meets with <a href="/wiki/Barvitus" title="Barvitus">Saint Barinthus</a>. Saint Barinthus describes The Promised Land for Saints <i>(Terra Repromissionis Sanctorum)</i>. As Saint Barinthus describes his journey to this island, Brendan decides to also visit this island because it was described as a place of those who lived a certain lifestyle and embraced true faith of Christianity. Brendan assembles a group of fourteen monks who pray together with him in his community to leave with him on his journey. Before departing, Brendan and the fourteen monks fast at three-day intervals for forty nights and set out on the voyage that was described to him by Saint Barinthus. They first embark towards Saint Edna, which is an island. After Brendan and the fourteen monks build a small boat for their journey, three people join after Brendan already chose his companions. These three extras will not return to Ireland, which is prophesied by Brendan because, according to him, this is now an unholy number.<sup id="cite_ref-:02_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For a period of seven years, Brendan and his students travel the seas and come across various locations while searching for the Promised Land. One of the first islands that Brendan and his companions come across is an unnamed uninhabited island. It is on this island that the first of the three extra monks who accompanied Brendan on his travels dies. After the death of their companion, Brendan and his men leave and continue their journey to the Island of Sheep. After a short stay on the Island of Sheep, Brendan and his crew land on the back of a giant fish named Jasconius, which they believe to be an island. But once they light a fire, the island starts to move revealing its true nature.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Other places that Brendan and his companions arrive at include the Island of Birds, the Island of Ailbe inhabited by a community of silent monks, and the Island of Strong Men. In some accounts, it is on the Island of Strong Men where the second of the 3 extra people remains instead of continuing his journey with Brendan and his men. The third is dragged away by demons.<sup id="cite_ref-:02_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After travelling for seven years, visiting some of the same places over and over again, they finally arrive in the Promised Land for Saints. They are welcomed and allowed to enter briefly. Awed by what they have seen, they return to Ireland rejoicing.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackley1_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackley1-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Context">Context</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Context">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg/200px-Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg/300px-Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg/400px-Iarthair_Chorca%C3%AD_038.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a><figcaption>Sculpture of St Brendan, The Square <a href="/wiki/Bantry" title="Bantry">Bantry</a>, County Cork</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <i>Navigatio</i> fits in with a then-popular literary genre, peculiar to <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, denominated an <a href="/wiki/Immram" title="Immram">immram</a>. Irish immrama flourished during the seventh and eighth centuries. Typically, an immram is a tale that describes the hero's series of seafaring adventures. Some of these immrams involved the search for, and visits to, <a href="/wiki/T%C3%ADr_na_n%C3%93g" title="Tír na nÓg">Tír na nÓg</a>, an island far to the west, beyond the edges of the world map. There appear to be similarities with <i><a href="/wiki/The_Voyage_of_Bran" title="The Voyage of Bran">The Voyage of Bran</a></i> written much earlier. In the <i>Navigatio</i>, this style of storytelling meshed with a religious ascetic tradition in which Irish monks travel alone in boats, the same way their desert brothers isolated themselves in caves.
</p><p>Brendan's voyages were one of the most remarkable and enduring of European legends. With many of the facts of Brendan's journeys coming from the <i>Navigatio</i>, it has been difficult for scholars to distinguish fact and folklore. The narrative of Brendan's voyage, developed during this time, shares some characteristics with immram. Like an immram, the <i>Navigatio</i> tells the story of Brendan, who, with some companion monks, sets out to find the <i>terra repromissionis sanctorum</i>, ("Promised Land of the Saints"), i. e., the Earthly Paradise.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jude S. Mackley holds that efforts to identify possible, actual locations referenced in the <i>Navigatio</i> distract from the author's purpose of presenting a legend of "salvation, monastic obedience and the faith required to undertake such a pilgrimage."<sup id="cite_ref-Mackley1_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackley1-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Intertextuality">Intertextuality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Intertextuality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Scholars debate whether the <i>Navigatio</i> influenced <i><a href="/wiki/The_Voyage_of_M%C3%A1el_D%C3%BAin" title="The Voyage of Máel Dúin">The Voyage of Máel Dúin</a></i> or vice versa. Jude Mackley suggests that an early <i>Navigatio</i> influenced an equally early <i>Mael Duin</i> and that inter-borrowing continued as the traditions developed. The <i>Navigatio</i> adapts the <a href="/wiki/Immram" title="Immram">immram</a> traditions to a Christian context.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackley1_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackley1-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A principal similarity between <i>Mael Duin</i> and the <i>Navigatio</i> is the introduction in both of 3 additional passengers. Mael Dúin is joined by his foster brothers, and Brendan by 3 additional monks. Both additions upset the equilibrium of the voyages, and only when the additional persons are no longer on board can each voyage be completed.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackley1_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackley1-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Anglo-Norman_version">Anglo-Norman version</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Anglo-Norman version">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Norman_language" title="Anglo-Norman language">Anglo-Norman</a> <i>Voyage of Saint Brendan</i> is the earliest surviving narrative text of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Norman_literature" title="Anglo-Norman literature">Anglo-Norman literature</a>. It was probably  translated around 1121 by a cleric called Benedeit at the commission of <a href="/wiki/Adeliza_of_Louvain" title="Adeliza of Louvain">Adeliza of Louvain</a>, the second wife of <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I of England</a>. In its use of <a href="/wiki/Octosyllable" title="Octosyllable">octosyllabic</a> couplets to recount a quest narrative drawn from Celtic sources, it has been described as a precursor of later <a href="/wiki/Medieval_French_literature#The_Roman" title="Medieval French literature">Old French romances</a>. Benedeit's version of the legend was itself retranslated several times into Latin prose and verse.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_Dutch_version">Early Dutch version</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Early Dutch version">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>One of the earliest extant written versions of Brendan's legend is the Dutch <span title="Dutch-language text"><i lang="nl"><a href="/wiki/Folklore_of_the_Low_Countries" title="Folklore of the Low Countries">De Reis van Sinte Brandaen</a></i></span> (Mediaeval Dutch for <i>The Voyage of Saint Brendan</i>) of the twelfth century. Scholars believe it is derived from a now lost <a href="/wiki/Middle_High_German" title="Middle High German">Middle High German</a> text combined with <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_languages" class="mw-redirect" title="Gaelic languages">Gaelic</a> elements from Ireland and that it combines <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fairy_tale" title="Fairy tale">fairy tale</a> elements. <span title="Dutch-language text"><i lang="nl">De Reis van Sinte Brandaen</i></span> describes "Brandaen", a monk from <a href="/wiki/Galway" title="Galway">Galway</a>, and his voyage around the world for 9 years. The journey began as a punishment by an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> who saw that Brendan did not believe in the truth of a book of the miracles of creation and saw Brandaen <a href="/wiki/Book_burning" title="Book burning">throw it into a fire</a>. The angel tells him that truth has been destroyed. On his journeys Brandaen encounters the wonders and horrors of the world, such as <a href="/wiki/Judas_Iscariot" title="Judas Iscariot">Judas Iscariot</a> frozen on one side and burning on the other; people with swine heads, dog legs, and wolf teeth carrying bows and arrows; and an enormous fish that encircles Brandaen's ship by <a href="/wiki/Ouroboros" title="Ouroboros">holding its tail in its mouth</a>. The English poem the <i>Life of Saint Brandan</i> is a later derivation from the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a> version.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id="Saint_Brendan.27s_Island"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Brendan's_Island">Saint Brendan's Island</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Saint Brendan&#039;s Island">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg/250px-Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="191" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg/375px-Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg/500px-Faroe_stamp_252_Europe_and_the_Discoveries.jpg 2x" data-file-width="661" data-file-height="505" /></a><figcaption>Faroese stamp depicting Saint Brendan, taking up the version that the island he discovered was in the <a href="/wiki/Faroe_Islands" title="Faroe Islands">Faroe Islands</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>While the narrative is often assumed to be a religious allegory, there has been considerable discussion as to whether the legends are based on fact. There have been many interpretations of the possible geographical location of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Brendan%27s_Island" title="Saint Brendan&#39;s Island">Saint Brendan's Island</a>. Various pre-Columbian sea charts indicated it everywhere from the southern part of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Faroes" class="mw-redirect" title="Faroes">Faroes</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Azores" title="Azores">Azores</a>; to the island of <a href="/wiki/Madeira" title="Madeira">Madeira</a>; to a point 60 degrees west of the first meridian and very near the <a href="/wiki/Equator" title="Equator">equator</a>.
</p>
<h5><span class="mw-headline" id="Tale_of_reaching_North_America">Tale of reaching North America</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Tale of reaching North America">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h5>
<p>Belief in the existence of Saint Brendan's Island was almost completely abandoned until a new theory arose that the Irish were the first Europeans to encounter the Americas.
</p><p>There is no reliable evidence to indicate that Brendan ever reached <a href="/wiki/Greenland" title="Greenland">Greenland</a> or the <a href="/wiki/Americas" title="Americas">Americas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The Saint Brendan Society celebrates the belief that Brendan was the first European to reach <a href="/wiki/North_America" title="North America">North America</a>. <a href="/wiki/Tim_Severin" title="Tim Severin">Tim Severin</a> demonstrated that it is possible for a leather-clad boat such as the one described in the <i>Navigatio</i> to reach North America.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Severin's film <i>The Brendan Voyage</i> of 1978, which documented his team's feat, inspired the Irish composer <a href="/wiki/Shaun_Davey" title="Shaun Davey">Shaun Davey</a> to write his orchestral suite "<a href="/wiki/The_Brendan_Voyage" title="The Brendan Voyage">The Brendan Voyage</a>".
</p><p>The <i>Navigatio</i> was known widely in Europe throughout the Middle Ages.<sup id="cite_ref-Howley_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Howley-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Maps of Christopher Columbus’ time often included an island denominated Saint Brendan's Isle that was placed in the western Atlantic Ocean.
Paul Chapman argues that <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Columbus" title="Christopher Columbus">Christopher Columbus</a> learned from the <i>Navigatio</i> that the currents and winds would favor westbound travel by a southerly route from the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, and eastbound on the return trip by a more northerly route, and hence followed this itinerary on all of his voyages.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brendan travelled to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and the holy island of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, off the west coast of <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>; returning to <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, he founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Annaghdown" title="Annaghdown">Annaghdown</a>, where he spent the rest of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> He also founded a convent at Annaghdown for his sister Briga.<sup id="cite_ref-eye_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eye-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Having established the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/Ardfert" title="Ardfert">Ardfert</a>, Brendan proceeded to <a href="/wiki/Thomond" title="Thomond">Thomond</a>, and founded a monastery at Inis-da-druim (currently Coney Island), in the present parish of Killadysert, <a href="/wiki/County_Clare" title="County Clare">County Clare</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> AD 550. He then journeyed to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and studied under <a href="/wiki/Gildas" title="Gildas">Gildas</a> at <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan" title="Llancarfan">Llancarfan</a>, and thence to <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, for he is said to have left traces of his apostolic zeal at Kil-<i>brandon</i> (near <a href="/wiki/Oban" title="Oban">Oban</a>) and Kil-<i>brennan</i> Sound. After a mission of three years in <a href="/wiki/Britain_(place_name)" title="Britain (place name)">Britain</a> he returned to <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, and evangelized further in various parts of <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, especially at Dysart, <a href="/wiki/County_Kilkenny" title="County Kilkenny">County Kilkenny</a>, Killeney near <a href="/wiki/Durrow,_County_Laois" title="Durrow, County Laois">Durrow</a> (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Tubberboe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Tubberboe (page does not exist)">Tubberboe</a> <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Tóbar Bó</i>, meaning 'Well of the cow'),<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Brandon_Hill" title="Brandon Hill">Brandon Hill</a>. He established churches at <a href="/wiki/Inchiquin" title="Inchiquin">Inchiquin</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Galway" title="County Galway">County Galway</a>, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Inishglora&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Inishglora (page does not exist)">Inishglora</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Mayo" title="County Mayo">County Mayo</a>, and founded <a href="/wiki/Clonfert" title="Clonfert">Clonfert</a> in <a href="/wiki/Galway" title="Galway">Galway</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> AD 557. He died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> AD 577 in <a href="/wiki/Annaghdown" title="Annaghdown">Annaghdown</a>, while visiting his sister Briga. Fearing that after his death his devotees might take his remains as <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, Brendan had previously arranged to have his body secretly returned to the monastery he founded in <a href="/wiki/Clonfert" title="Clonfert">Clonfert</a>, concealed in a luggage cart. He was interred in <a href="/wiki/Clonfert_Cathedral" title="Clonfert Cathedral">Clonfert Cathedral</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brendan was recognised as a saint by the Catholic Church. His feast day is celebrated on 16 May.
As the legend of the seven years voyage spread, crowds of pilgrims and students flocked to Ardfert. Religious houses were formed at Gallerus, Kilmalchedor, Brandon Hill, and the <a href="/wiki/Blasket_Islands" title="Blasket Islands">Blasket Islands</a>, to meet the wants of those who came for spiritual guidance from Brendan.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Brendan is the patron saint of sailors and travellers. At the <a href="/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy" title="United States Naval Academy">United States Naval Academy</a> in <a href="/wiki/Annapolis,_Maryland" title="Annapolis, Maryland">Annapolis, Maryland</a>, a large stained glass window commemorates Brendan's achievements. At <a href="/wiki/Fenit" title="Fenit">Fenit Harbour</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tralee" title="Tralee">Tralee</a>, a substantial bronze sculpture by <a href="/wiki/Tighe_O%27DonoghueRoss" title="Tighe O&#39;DonoghueRoss">Tighe O'Donoghue/Ross</a> was erected to honour the memory of Brendan.  The project, including a Heritage Park and the Slí Bhreanainn (the Brendan way) was headed by Fr. Gearóid Ó Donnchadha and completed through the work of the St. Brendan Committee. 
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brendan the Navigator or Brénainn moccu Alti as he is often known in the medieval Irish tradition is the patron saint of two Irish dioceses, Kerry and Clonfert. He is also a patron saint of boatmen, mariners, travelers, elderly adventurers, and whales,<sup id="cite_ref-Mackley2_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackley2-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> and also of portaging canoes.<sup id="cite_ref-kerry_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kerry-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Establishments">Establishments</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Establishments">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St Brendan's activities as a churchman, however, were developed in Western Ireland, where his most important foundations are found, i.e., Ardfert (Co. Kerry), Inishdadroum (Co. Clare), <a href="/wiki/Annaghdown" title="Annaghdown">Annaghdown</a> (Co. Galway), and Clonfert (Co. Galway). His name is perpetuated in numerous place names and landmarks along the Irish coast (e.g., Brandon Hill, Brandon Point, Mount Brendan, Brandon Well, Brandon Bay, Brandon Head).<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brendan's most celebrated foundation was <a href="/wiki/Clonfert_Cathedral" title="Clonfert Cathedral">Clonfert Cathedral</a>, in the year 563, over which he appointed Moinenn as Prior and Head Master. Brendan was interred in Clonfert.
</p><p>The group of ecclesiastical remains at Ardfert is one of the most interesting and instructive now existing in Ireland. The ruins of the ancient Cathedral of St Brendan, and of its annexed chantries and detached chapels, form a very complete reliquary of Irish ecclesiastical architecture, in its various orders and ages, from the plain but solid Danhliag of the seventh or eighth century to some late and most ornate examples of medieval Gothic. The cathedral, as it now stands, or rather as it stood before it was finally dismantled in A.D. 1641.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sicily">Sicily</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Brendan_the_Navigator&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Sicily">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicilian</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Bronte,_Sicily" title="Bronte, Sicily">Bronte</a> there is a church dedicated to Saint Brendan, whose name in the local dialect is <i>"San Brandanu"</i>. Since 1574, the "<i>Chiesa di San Blandano</i>" ("Church of Saint Brendan") replaced a chapel with such name that existed previously in the same location. The reasons for dedicating a church to Saint Brendan are still unknown and probably untraceable. 
The <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a> and the many settlers that followed the Norman invasion brought into <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> the tradition of Saint Brendan; there are very old papers of the 13th century written in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> that refer to him; in 1799 the countryside surrounding Brontë became the British <i>"Duchy of <a href="/wiki/Horatio_Nelson,_1st_Viscount_Nelson" title="Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson">Horatio Nelson</a>"</i>. The town of <a href="/wiki/Drogheda" title="Drogheda">Drogheda</a> is twinned with Bronte.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<ul><li>Australian novelist <a href="/wiki/Patrick_Holland_(author)" title="Patrick Holland (author)">Patrick Holland</a> re-imagines the Brendan voyage in his 2014 novel <i>Navigatio</i>.</li>
<li>Scottish poet <a href="/wiki/A._B._Jackson_(poet)" title="A. B. Jackson (poet)">A.B. The novel won the Christianity and Literature Book Award for <a href="/wiki/Belles-lettres" title="Belles-lettres">Belles-Lettres</a> in the same year.</li>
<li>The "Brendan Voyage" is an orchestral suite for <a href="/wiki/Uilleann_pipes" title="Uilleann pipes">Uilleann pipes</a>, written by Irish composer <a href="/wiki/Shaun_Davey" title="Shaun Davey">Shaun Davey</a> recorded in 1980, featuring <a href="/wiki/Liam_O%27Flynn" title="Liam O&#39;Flynn">Liam O'Flynn</a>. <li>The <a href="/wiki/Celtic_music" title="Celtic music">Celtic band</a> <a href="/wiki/Iona_(band)" title="Iona (band)">Iona</a> made an entire recording inspired by the voyage of Saint Brendan called <a href="/wiki/Beyond_These_Shores" title="Beyond These Shores">Beyond These Shores</a>, now available as part of the recording "<a href="/wiki/The_River_Flows" class="mw-redirect" title="The River Flows">The River Flows</a>".</li>
<li>Singer songwriter <a href="/wiki/Sarana_VerLin" title="Sarana VerLin">Sarana VerLin</a> wrote an instrumental song titled "St Brendan's Reel" that appears on several albums including "Amadon Crest".</li>
<li>In the 2005 film <i><a href="/wiki/Beowulf_%26_Grendel" title="Beowulf &amp; Grendel">Beowulf &amp; Grendel</a></i>, a travelling monk named Brendan the Celt sails to Denmark circa 521 A.D.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien" title="J. R. R. Tolkien">J. R. R. Tolkien</a> wrote a poem, "The Voyage of Saint Brendan", included in his time-travel story <i><a href="/wiki/The_Notion_Club_Papers" title="The Notion Club Papers">The Notion Club Papers</a></i>, published posthumously (1992) in <i><a href="/wiki/Sauron_Defeated" class="mw-redirect" title="Sauron Defeated">Sauron Defeated</a></i>. He also published a version of the poem, titled "Imram", during his lifetime, in 1955.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Tommy_Makem" title="Tommy Makem">Tommy Makem</a>'s song "Brendan" on the album <i>Rolling Home</i> tells the story (explained in detail on the disk sleeve) of how Brendan had travelled to Newfoundland, down the coast to Florida, and thence back home to Ireland.</li>
<li>Irish rock band <a href="/wiki/The_Elders_(band)" title="The Elders (band)">The Elders</a> have a song on their album <i>Racing the Tide</i> called "Saint Brendan Had a Boat".</li>
<li>Saint Brendan has been adopted by the scuba diving industry as the Patron Saint of Scuba Divers.</li>
<li>Irish folk singer Christy Moore had a humorous track called "<a href="/wiki/St._Brendan%27s_Voyage" title="St. Brendan&#39;s Voyage">St. Brendan's Voyage</a>" on his 1985 album <i><a href="/wiki/Ordinary_Man_(Christy_Moore_album)" title="Ordinary Man (Christy Moore album)">Ordinary Man</a></i>.</li>
<li>Canadian indie band <a href="/wiki/The_Lowest_of_the_Low" title="The Lowest of the Low">The Lowest of the Low</a> correlate the voyage of St Brendan to the Atlantic passage of French and Irish immigrants to eastern Canada in the song "St Brendan's Way" on the album <a href="/wiki/Shakespeare_My_Butt" title="Shakespeare My Butt">Shakespeare My Butt</a>.</li>
<li>Ozarks folk singer <a href="/wiki/Jimmy_Driftwood" title="Jimmy Driftwood">Jimmy Driftwood</a> wrote a humorous song about the voyage of St Brendan called "St. Brendan's Fair Isle".</li>
<li>Irish poet <a href="/wiki/James_Harpur" title="James Harpur">James Harpur</a> wrote a sonnet, "Brendan", included in his 2007 collection <i>The Dark Age</i>; it makes mention of Brendan's encounter with Judas.</li>
<li>Novelist <a href="/wiki/Morgan_Llywelyn" title="Morgan Llywelyn">Morgan Llywelyn</a> wrote a fictional version of Brendan's life in her 2010 book, <i>Brendán</i>.</li>
<li>Novelist <a href="/wiki/Patricia_Kennealy-Morrison" title="Patricia Kennealy-Morrison">Patricia Kennealy-Morrison</a> features a fictional Brendan in her book "The Deers Cry", a story with a science fiction twist. In this book, Brendan is a pagan who decides to leave Earth for another planet because of his dislike for Christianity.</li>
<li>At the climactic scene of Robert E. Howard's story <i><a href="/wiki/The_Cairn_on_the_Headland" title="The Cairn on the Headland">The Cairn on the Headland</a></i>, the protagonist uses a hallowed Cross made by Saint Brendan in order to banish the Norse god <a href="/wiki/Odin" title="Odin">Odin</a>, who was about to come back to life and destroy modern <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
