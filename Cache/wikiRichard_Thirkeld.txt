<p><b>Richard Thirkeld</b> (died 29 May 1583) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1886. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Thirkeld&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thirkeld was born at Coniscliffe, <a href="/wiki/Durham,_England" title="Durham, England">Durham</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>. From <a href="/wiki/Queen%27s_College,_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen&#39;s College, Oxford">Queen's College, Oxford</a>, where he was in 1564-5, he went to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, where he was ordained priest, 18 April 1579.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He left 23 May for the English mission, where he ministered in or about York, and acted as confessor to <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Clitheroe" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret Clitheroe">Margaret Clitheroe</a>. On the eve of the Annunciation, 1583, he was arrested while visiting one of the Catholic prisoners in the Kidcote on Ouse bridge. He at once confessed his priesthood, both to the <a href="/wiki/Pursuivant" title="Pursuivant">pursuivants</a>, who arrested him, and to the mayor before whom he was brought, and for the night was lodged in the house of the <a href="/wiki/High_sheriff" title="High sheriff">high sheriff</a>. The next day his trial took place, at which he managed to appear in his <a href="/wiki/Cassock" title="Cassock">cassock</a>, which made him appear all the more venerable.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The charge was one of having reconciled the Queen's subjects to the Church of Rome. He was found guilty on 27 May and condemned 28 May. He was executed at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> on 29 May 1583, secretly because authorities feared that his public execution would have caused a public demonstration.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Six of his letters survive, and were summarized by <a href="/wiki/Bede_Camm" title="Bede Camm">Bede Camm</a>.
</p>
