<p><b>Sir Joseph Cook</b> <span style="font-size:85%;"><span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_Grand_Cross_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George">GCMG</a></span></span> (7 December 1860 – 30 July 1947) was an Australian politician who served as the sixth <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Prime Minister of Australia">prime minister of Australia</a>, from 1913 to 1914, holding office as the leader of the <a href="/wiki/Fusion_Liberal_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Fusion Liberal Party">Liberal Party</a>, having previously been leader of the <a href="/wiki/Anti-Socialist_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Socialist Party">Anti-Socialist Party</a> from 1908 to 1909.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His victory at the 1913 election marked the first time that a centre-right party had won an outright majority at an Australian Federal election.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook was born in <a href="/wiki/Silverdale,_Staffordshire" title="Silverdale, Staffordshire">Silverdale, Staffordshire</a>, England, and began working in the local coal mines at the age of nine. He emigrated to Australia in 1885, settling in <a href="/wiki/Lithgow,_New_South_Wales" title="Lithgow, New South Wales">Lithgow, New South Wales</a>. He continued to work as a miner, becoming involved with the local <a href="/wiki/Australian_labour_movement" title="Australian labour movement">labour movement</a> as a union official. <a href="/wiki/1891_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1891 New South Wales colonial election">In 1891</a>, Cook was elected to the <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Assembly" title="New South Wales Legislative Assembly">New South Wales Legislative Assembly</a> as a representative of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_(New_South_Wales_Branch)" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Labor Party (New South Wales Branch)">Labor Party</a>, becoming one of its first members of parliament. He was elected party leader in 1893, but the following year left Labor due to a disagreement over <a href="/wiki/Party_discipline" title="Party discipline">party discipline</a>. He was then invited to become a government minister under <a href="/wiki/George_Reid" title="George Reid">George Reid</a>, and joined Reid's <a href="/wiki/Free_Trade_Party" title="Free Trade Party">Free Trade Party</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/1901_Australian_federal_election" title="1901 Australian federal election">In 1901</a>, Cook was elected to the new <a href="/wiki/Federal_Parliament" class="mw-redirect" title="Federal Parliament">Federal Parliament</a> representing the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Parramatta" title="Division of Parramatta">Division of Parramatta</a>. He became deputy leader of the federal Free Trade Party (later renamed the Anti-Socialist Party), again under George Reid, and in 1908 replaced Reid as party leader and <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">Leader of the Opposition</a>. In what became known as "the fusion", Cook agreed to merge his party with <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Protectionist_Party" title="Protectionist Party">Protectionist Party</a> in 1909, forming a unified anti-Labor party for the first time. He became deputy leader of the new Liberal Party, allowing Deakin to become prime minister again, and was <a href="/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister of Defence (Australia)">Minister for Defence</a> until the government's defeat at the <a href="/wiki/1910_Australian_federal_election" title="1910 Australian federal election">1910 election</a>.
</p><p>Cook replaced Deakin as leader of the Liberals in January 1913, and a few months later won a one-seat majority over <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Fisher" title="Andrew Fisher">Andrew Fisher</a>'s Labor Party at the <a href="/wiki/1913_Australian_federal_election" title="1913 Australian federal election">1913 election</a>. His party failed to secure a majority in the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Australian Senate</a>, making governing difficult, and as a result he engineered the first <a href="/wiki/Double_dissolution" title="Double dissolution">double dissolution</a>. <a href="/wiki/1914_Australian_federal_election" title="1914 Australian federal election">A new election</a> was called for September 1914, at which the Liberals lost their majority; Fisher returned as prime minister. Cook was unable to pass much legislation during his time in office, but did oversee the early stages of <a href="/wiki/Military_history_of_Australia_during_World_War_I" title="Military history of Australia during World War I">Australia's involvement</a> in World War I. He subsequently became Leader of the Opposition for a third time.
</p><p>In 1917, Cook was involved in a second party merger, joining the Liberals with <a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a>'s <a href="/wiki/National_Labor_Party" title="National Labor Party">National Labor Party</a> to form the <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_(Australia)" title="Nationalist Party (Australia)">Nationalist Party</a>. He became the <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">deputy prime minister</a> under Hughes, serving as <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_the_Navy_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for the Navy (Australia)">Minister for the Navy</a> (1917–1920) and <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_Australia" title="Treasurer of Australia">Treasurer</a> (1920–1921). He was a delegate to the <a href="/wiki/1919_Paris_Peace_Conference" class="mw-redirect" title="1919 Paris Peace Conference">1919 Paris Peace Conference</a>, where he was a member of the committee that determined the borders of <a href="/wiki/Czechoslovakia" title="Czechoslovakia">Czechoslovakia</a>, and along with Hughes was one of two Australians to sign the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles" title="Treaty of Versailles">Treaty of Versailles</a>. After leaving politics, Cook was <a href="/wiki/High_Commissioner_of_Australia_to_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="High Commissioner of Australia to the United Kingdom">High Commissioner to the United Kingdom</a> from 1921 to 1927. He died at the age of 86 as one of the last survivors of the first federal parliament.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg/220px-Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg/330px-Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg/440px-Joseph_Cook%27s_early_residence_in_Silverdale.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>Cook lived in a small terraced house at 86 Newcastle Street for most of his childhood. The building now has a <a href="/wiki/Blue_plaque" title="Blue plaque">blue plaque</a> commemorating his life.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Cook was born on 7 December 1860 in a small cottage in <a href="/wiki/Silverdale,_Staffordshire" title="Silverdale, Staffordshire">Silverdale, Staffordshire</a>, England. He was the second of seven children born to Margaret (née Fletcher) and William Cooke. His older sister Sarah died in 1865, but his three younger sisters and two younger brothers lived to adulthood. Cook's parents moved to a <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/one-up-one-down" class="extiw" title="wikt:one-up-one-down">one-up-one-down</a> a few months after his birth, before eventually settling in a terraced house on Newcastle Street. The children shared a single room and two beds, and the family could rarely afford meat.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199614_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199614-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Cook's father was a coal miner under the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/butty" class="extiw" title="wikt:butty">butty</a> system at the nearby Hollywood pit.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199613_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199613-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was killed in a mining accident in April 1873, forcing his oldest son to become the family's primary source of income.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199615_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199615-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook's only formal education was at the school attached to St Luke's, the local Anglican church. He left school and began working in the coal mines at the age of nine, earning one <a href="/wiki/Shilling" title="Shilling">shilling</a> per day for ten to twelve hours of work. Beginning at four o'clock in the morning, his tasks were to attend to the horses and clean and oil the mining equipment. After the passage of the <i><a href="/wiki/Elementary_Education_Act_1870" title="Elementary Education Act 1870">Elementary Education Act 1870</a></i>, Cook was allowed to return to school until he reached the legal leaving age.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199615_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199615-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He left school a second time after his father's death and returned to his former employment at the local colliery.  However, as a result of his teacher's attention, together with that of his parents, an exceptionally strong ambition to improve his position became implanted in him. This ambition was to become one of his most prominent characteristics, revealed first in a drive for self-improvement and, later on in life, his determination to succeed in politics. During his teenage years, he embraced <a href="/wiki/Primitive_Methodism" class="mw-redirect" title="Primitive Methodism">Primitive Methodism</a>, and marked his conversion by dropping the "e" from his surname.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> On 8 August 1885, he married <a href="/wiki/Dame_Mary_Cook" title="Dame Mary Cook">Mary Turner</a> at <a href="/wiki/Wolstanton" title="Wolstanton">Wolstanton</a>, Staffordshire, and the couple eventually had five sons and three daughters.
</p><p>Shortly after their marriage, the couple emigrated to <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales" title="New South Wales">New South Wales</a> and settled in <a href="/wiki/Lithgow,_New_South_Wales" title="Lithgow, New South Wales">Lithgow</a>, joining Cook's brother-in-law and a number of other former miners from Silverdale.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Cook worked in the coal mines, becoming General-Secretary of the Western Miners Association in 1887. In 1888, he participated in demonstrations against Chinese immigration. He was also active in the Land Nationalisation League, which was influenced by the ideas of <a href="/wiki/Henry_George" title="Henry George">Henry George</a> and strongly supported <a href="/wiki/Free_trade" title="Free trade">free trade</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-adb_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and was a founding member of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_(New_South_Wales_Branch)" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Labor Party (New South Wales Branch)">Labor Party</a> in 1891.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career">Early political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:JosephCook2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/JosephCook2.jpg/170px-JosephCook2.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="255" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/JosephCook2.jpg/255px-JosephCook2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/JosephCook2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="283" data-file-height="425" /></a><figcaption>Cook in 1894</figcaption></figure>
<p>Cook was elected to the <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Assembly" title="New South Wales Legislative Assembly">New South Wales Legislative Assembly</a> as MP for the coalfields seat of <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Hartley_(New_South_Wales)" title="Electoral district of Hartley (New South Wales)">Hartley</a> in 1891, in Labor's first big breakthrough in Australian politics.<sup id="cite_ref-NSWparl_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NSWparl-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It was the first time Labor had won a seat in any parliament in Australia.
</p><p>In 1894, however, Cook was the leader of those parliamentarians who refused to accept the Labor Party's decision to make all members sign a "pledge" to be bound by decisions of the Parliamentary Labor Party (Caucus).<sup id="cite_ref-adb_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Cook's protest was based on Labor's attitude to the <a href="/wiki/Tariff" title="Tariff">tariff</a> question in particular, with his preference for free trade being increasingly at odds with his party.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It was also based on his religious beliefs, which valued independence of conscience as a necessary moral imperative.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> By the end of the year, he had become a follower of <a href="/wiki/George_Reid" title="George Reid">George Reid</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Free_Trade_Party" title="Free Trade Party">Free Trade Party</a>, and for years afterwards he was seen as a '<a href="/wiki/Class_traitor" title="Class traitor">class traitor</a>' by Labor.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Cook for his part maintained that the original Labor Party of 1891 was an entirely separate entity from the pledged party, hence that he had never been a member of what became the Labor Party.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He became an invaluable ally of Reid, despite the fact that the two men had distinctly different characters, and remained colleagues only at a distance.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook was appointed <a href="/wiki/Postmaster-General_of_New_South_Wales" title="Postmaster-General of New South Wales">Postmaster-General of New South Wales</a> when Reid formed a government in August 1894. He chaired two intercolonial post and telegraph (P&amp;T) conferences in 1896, at which the Australian colonies agreed to fund a <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Cable" class="mw-redirect" title="Pacific Cable">Pacific Cable</a> linking Australia to North America.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELivingston1998127_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingston1998127-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In opening the first conference, he spoke of the "federal spirit [...] animating most of our Australasian national endeavours at the present time". It was eventually resolved that the colonies would contribute equally to funding the cable rather than on a simple per-capita basis, an agreement which "marked a turning point in the achievement of 'practical Federation'" and foreshadowed the development of a <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Senate</a> with equal representation for each state.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELivingston1998128_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingston1998128-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> According to Kevin Livingston, who wrote a history of pre-Federation telecommunications in Australia, he "deserves to be recognised as having played an influential, mediating role in leading the Australian colonies towards technological federalism in the mid-1890s".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELivingston1998129_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingston1998129-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Federal_Parliament">Federal Parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Federal Parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg/220px-Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="169" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg/330px-Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg/440px-Joseph_Cook_and_Alfred_Deakin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="983" /></a><figcaption>Cook and <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a> together in 1909</figcaption></figure>
<p>Despite supporting federation in principle, Cook campaigned for a no vote at the first referendum on federation in 1898.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He believed that a Senate which gave equal representation to each state regardless of population was undemocratic, and he was also a strong believer in the liberal concept of subsidiarity, the idea that political decisions are best made at the most local level.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> For the same reason, he would strongly oppose the 1911 and 1913 referenda seeking to enlarge the powers of the federal government.
</p><p>Cook initially had no plans to enter federal politics, hoping instead to succeed Reid as premier of New South Wales.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199660_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199660-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> However, the party wanted a high-profile candidate to stand against <a href="/wiki/William_Sandford" title="William Sandford">William Sandford</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Parramatta" title="Division of Parramatta">Division of Parramatta</a>, a large electorate spanning from Parramatta on the outskirts of Sydney across the <a href="/wiki/Blue_Mountains_(New_South_Wales)" title="Blue Mountains (New South Wales)">Blue Mountains</a> to Lithgow. Reid offered the position of Postmaster-General in a future government as an inducement, but Cook did not agree to stand until a few weeks before <a href="/wiki/1901_Australian_federal_election" title="1901 Australian federal election">the election</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199661_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199661-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> He was elected with a substantial majority, following a bitter campaign in which he accused Sandford of adopting political positions for self-benefit. The Free Traders failed to win government from the Protectionists, with Reid becoming <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">Leader of the Opposition</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199662_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199662-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the first term of federal parliament, Cook developed a reputation as a master of parliamentary procedure and tactics, "always ready to speak, as often and as long as required".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199663_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199663-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> He spoke in favour of nationalising the iron industry and introducing compulsory conciliation and arbitration, views in line with his previous political affiliation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199664_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199664-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Opposing tariffs because they increased the price of goods needed for working families, in 1902 he would suggest that "no man ought be in the position of Minister for Trade and Customs unless he had at least ten children".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In June 1901 he moved from Lithgow to a larger house in <a href="/wiki/Marrickville,_New_South_Wales" title="Marrickville, New South Wales">Marrickville</a>, in Sydney's <a href="/wiki/Inner_West" title="Inner West">Inner West</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199663_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199663-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Cook was re-elected with an increased majority at the <a href="/wiki/1903_Australian_federal_election" title="1903 Australian federal election">1903 election</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199665_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199665-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He stood for the deputy leadership of the opposition when parliament resumed, but was defeated by <a href="/wiki/Dugald_Thomson" title="Dugald Thomson">Dugald Thomson</a>, and was overlooked for ministerial office when Reid formed a government in August 1904.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199666_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199666-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> This was arguably because he had suffered a severe bout of illness that had kept him absent from Parliament for two months.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1904, Cook had become stridently <a href="/wiki/Anti-socialism" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-socialism">anti-socialist</a>, in line with Reid's decision to reposition the Free Traders as the party of anti-socialism. Some of his previous political positions were abandoned, possibly to gain the trust of party colleagues who had been suspicious of his links with the labour movement. He instead came to espouse liberalism, regarding its views about <a href="/wiki/Personal_freedom" class="mw-redirect" title="Personal freedom">personal freedom</a> as closely aligned with Methodism's understanding of the role of the individual in developing morality. He believed that his own story proved that Australia was a land of great social mobility, and that the nation should continue to support individual opportunity rather than risking a socialist revolution in which the state would be empowered to control individuals.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> In 1905 he accepted the position of deputy chairman of the Australian Liberal League, an organisation formed to support the anti-socialists in the lead-up to the next election.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199667–68_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199667–68-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Reid_government" title="Reid government">Reid government</a>, Cook filled a role similar to the later position of <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_(Australia)" title="Leader of the House (Australia)">Leader of the House</a>, assisting Reid with parliamentary tactics without being burdened by a ministerial portfolio. Reid had hoped to call an early election and entrusted Cook with organising the anti-socialist campaign. However, in June 1905 Protectionist leader <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a> withdrew his support from the government and formed a new administration, with Labor support.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199669_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199669-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook was unanimously elected deputy leader of the Anti-Socialists on 28 July 1905, following Thomson's resignation. He "started a political vendetta against Deakin", which "perfectly suited the mood of the party".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199670_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199670-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The Anti-Socialists ran a negative campaign with few policy specifics at the <a href="/wiki/1906_Australian_federal_election" title="1906 Australian federal election">1906 election</a>, and the Protectionist–ALP alliance continued. He was re-elected unopposed, following a redistribution which saw Parramatta lose much of its working-class areas. In 1908 he moved to <a href="/wiki/Baulkham_Hills,_New_South_Wales" title="Baulkham Hills, New South Wales">Baulkham Hills</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199671–72_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch199671–72-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Reid resigned as party leader on 16 November 1908, Cook succeeded him the following day, and agreed to merge the Anti-Socialist Party (the Free Trade Party had been renamed prior to the <a href="/wiki/1906_Australian_federal_election" title="1906 Australian federal election">1906 federal election</a>) with <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a>'s Protectionists, in an effort to counter Labor's popularity. Cook became deputy leader of the new <i><a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Liberal_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Commonwealth Liberal Party">Commonwealth Liberal Party</a></i>, also known as "the Fusion." Cook was <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Defence_(Australia)" title="Minister for Defence (Australia)">Defence Minister</a> in Deakin's 1909–1910 ministry, then succeeded Deakin as Liberal leader when the government was defeated by Labor in the 1910 elections. Cook had, by this time, become completely philosophically opposed to socialism.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg/170px-Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="222" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg/255px-Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg/340px-Joseph_Cook_-_Broothorn_Studios.jpg 2x" data-file-width="760" data-file-height="992" /></a><figcaption>Cook c. 1914</figcaption></figure>
<p>At the <a href="/wiki/1913_Australian_federal_election" title="1913 Australian federal election">1913 election</a>, Cook championed private enterprise and attacked Labor's "socialist objective" as the "principle that the state must become more 
and more omnipotent, until it eventually takes over all the 
actions of the individual, shaping and determining all our 
production, distribution and exchange".<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> The Commonwealth Liberal Party led by Cook won a one-seat majority in the House of Representatives over the Labor Party, led by <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Fisher" title="Andrew Fisher">Andrew Fisher</a>, and defeated its <a href="/wiki/1913_Australian_referendum" title="1913 Australian referendum">6 referendum proposals</a>. 
</p><p>Cook thus became the sixth <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Prime Minister of Australia">Prime Minister of Australia</a>. However, Labor still had a majority in the Senate. Unable to govern effectively due to the hostile Senate, Cook decided to trigger a <a href="/wiki/Double_dissolution" title="Double dissolution">double dissolution</a> under section 57 of the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia" title="Constitution of Australia">Constitution of Australia</a>, the first time that provision had been used. He introduced a bill abolishing preferential employment for trade union members in the public service. As expected, the Senate rejected the bill, giving Cook an excuse to seek a double dissolution. World War I broke out in the middle of the resulting campaign for the <a href="/wiki/1914_Australian_federal_election" title="1914 Australian federal election">September 1914 election</a>. Andrew Fisher was able to remind voters that it was Labor that had favoured an independent Australian defence force, which the conservatives had opposed. Cook was also greatly hindered by the fact that he had to cut short his campaign to focus on war matters, at a time when there were few alternatives to in-person political rallies.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Cook was defeated after a five-seat swing, and Fisher's Labor Party resumed office.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="World_War_I">World War I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: World War I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Military_history_of_Australia_during_World_War_I" title="Military history of Australia during World War I">Military history of Australia during World War I</a></div>
<p>Cook was prime minister for the first six weeks of <a href="/wiki/Australia_in_World_War_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Australia in World War I">Australia's involvement in World War I</a>. On 30 July 1914, he was informed via telegram that the British government was considering a declaration of war and advised that Australia should take appropriate defence precautions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton201517_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton201517-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> He told an election meeting at <a href="/wiki/Horsham,_Victoria" title="Horsham, Victoria">Horsham, Victoria</a>, the following day to "remember that when the Empire is at war, so is Australia at war".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton201525_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton201525-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> At the suggestion of Governor-General <a href="/wiki/Ronald_Munro_Ferguson" class="mw-redirect" title="Ronald Munro Ferguson">Ronald Munro Ferguson</a>, Cook called an emergency cabinet meeting for 3 August. It was attended by only four members of his ministry, as the others were out campaigning and unable to travel to Melbourne in time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton201518_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton201518-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The government decided to offer an expeditionary force of 20,000 men – "of any suggested composition to any destination desired [...] at complete disposal of the Home Government; cost of despatch and
maintenance would be borne by this Government" – and to give the <a href="/wiki/British_Admiralty" class="mw-redirect" title="British Admiralty">British Admiralty</a> control of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Australian_Navy" title="Royal Australian Navy">Royal Australian Navy</a> "when desired".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton201520_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton201520-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> Australia's offer was made 40 hours before the United Kingdom declared war on Germany, and it has been suggested that it may have intensified the pressure on the British government to enter the war, along with similar offers made by Canada and New Zealand.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENewton201521_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewton201521-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> The United Kingdom formally accepted Australia's offer on 6 August, and Cook subsequently authorised the creation of the <a href="/wiki/First_Australian_Imperial_Force" title="First Australian Imperial Force">Australian Imperial Force</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Naval_and_Military_Expeditionary_Force" title="Australian Naval and Military Expeditionary Force">Australian Naval and Military Expeditionary Force</a>; the latter captured and then occupied <a href="/wiki/German_New_Guinea" title="German New Guinea">German New Guinea</a> within a few months. Writing in 1962, <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_Henry_Ellis" title="Malcolm Henry Ellis">Malcolm Henry Ellis</a> described him as "the activator and originator of Australia's war effort".<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
Cook would have three sons serve in the AIF during the war, one of whom was twice wounded at Gallipoli but miraculously survived.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hughes_government">Hughes government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Hughes government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Fisher resigned from parliament in 1915, <a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a> became Labor leader and prime minister. In 1916, Hughes began a determined push for <a href="/wiki/World_War_I_conscription_in_Australia" title="World War I conscription in Australia">the introduction of conscription</a> for military service, causing a split in the Labor Party over the issue. However, Hughes was able to stay in office after getting parliamentary support from Cook and his party. Cook was initially reluctant to support conscription as it violated his longstanding opposition to compulsion, but eventually he concluded that “manhood suffrage denoted manhood responsibility”.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Later in 1916, the so-called <a href="/wiki/National_Labor_Party" title="National Labor Party">National Labor Party</a>, consisting of those Labor members who supported Hughes, merged with the Commonwealth Liberals to form the <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nationalist Party of Australia">Nationalist Party</a>, as Cook decided to sacrifice his party's liberal identity and philosophy in the name of winning the war at any cost.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> A Federal Liberal Party would not re-emerge until 1944. Although it was dominated by former Liberals, Hughes was named the new party's leader, with Cook as deputy leader.  Cook became <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_the_Navy_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for the Navy (Australia)">Minister for the Navy</a> and <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">Deputy Prime Minister</a> in Hughes' reconfigured government. The Nationalists had substantial victories over Labor in the <a href="/wiki/1917_Australian_federal_election" title="1917 Australian federal election">1917 election</a> and the <a href="/wiki/1919_Australian_federal_election" title="1919 Australian federal election">1919 election</a>.
</p><p>Although Cook was a loyal deputy to Hughes, "at no time did he develop any personal affection for him". He thought Hughes was autocratic and prone to taking credit for things that others had accomplished. He did however admire Hughes' strong leadership and "immense energy", which contrasted with his own cautiousness.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996124_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996124-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> Cook was Acting Prime Minister on a number of occasions when Hughes was overworked or on visits abroad. In parliament, he was effectively the <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_(Australia)" title="Leader of the House (Australia)">Leader of the House</a> (a title which did not yet exist), responsible for the passage of government business.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996125_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996125-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> He campaigned strongly for the "Yes" vote in the <a href="/wiki/1917_Australian_conscription_referendum" title="1917 Australian conscription referendum">second conscription plebiscite</a> in 1917, touring three states and giving multiple speeches each day.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996126_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996126-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> The "No" vote won, and Hughes fulfilled his earlier promise to resign as prime minister, although he remained in office as a caretaker. In determining who should be prime minister, Governor-General <a href="/wiki/Ronald_Munro_Ferguson" class="mw-redirect" title="Ronald Munro Ferguson">Ronald Munro Ferguson</a> spoke first with Opposition Leader <a href="/wiki/Frank_Tudor" title="Frank Tudor">Frank Tudor</a>, who declined to form a government, and then with senior members of the Nationalist Party. Cook's advice that "only Hughes" was suitable proved decisive in Munro Ferguson recommissioning him as prime minister, rather than another Nationalist like <a href="/wiki/John_Forrest" title="John Forrest">John Forrest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996127_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996127-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Overseas_activities">Overseas activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Overseas activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Treaty_of_Versailles_signatures_-_Australia,_South_Africa,_New_Zealand.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Treaty_of_Versailles_signatures_-_Australia%2C_South_Africa%2C_New_Zealand.jpg/220px-Treaty_of_Versailles_signatures_-_Australia%2C_South_Africa%2C_New_Zealand.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="308" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/91/Treaty_of_Versailles_signatures_-_Australia%2C_South_Africa%2C_New_Zealand.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="319" data-file-height="447" /></a><figcaption>Cook's signature on the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles" title="Treaty of Versailles">Treaty of Versailles</a>, situated after that of Hughes and before those of <a href="/wiki/Louis_Botha" title="Louis Botha">Louis Botha</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jan_Smuts" title="Jan Smuts">Jan Smuts</a>, and <a href="/wiki/William_Massey" title="William Massey">William Massey</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Cook and Hughes represented Australia at the 1918 <a href="/wiki/Imperial_War_Conference" title="Imperial War Conference">Imperial War Conference</a> in London. They left together on 26 April 1918, with <a href="/wiki/William_Watt_(Australian_politician)" title="William Watt (Australian politician)">William Watt</a> as Acting Prime Minister in their absence.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996127_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996127-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Cook participated in all fifteen sessions of the conference, but found that the most important work was being undertaken by Hughes behind closed doors; he was generally not consulted.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996128_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996128-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> After the conference concluded he paid an extended visit to the Western Front, accompanied by his adviser <a href="/wiki/John_Latham_(judge)" title="John Latham (judge)">John Latham</a>, author <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Conan_Doyle" title="Arthur Conan Doyle">Arthur Conan Doyle</a>, and war correspondent <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bean" title="Charles Bean">Charles Bean</a>. They were taken within 1,000 yards (910&#160;m) of the <a href="/wiki/Hindenburg_Line" title="Hindenburg Line">Hindenburg Line</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Bullecourt" title="Bullecourt">Bullecourt</a>, and at one point a shell exploded less than a minute before they arrived at a meeting point.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Cook visited Australian Army camps in South England and toured the British dockyards, consulting with Admiral <a href="/wiki/John_Jellicoe" class="mw-redirect" title="John Jellicoe">John Jellicoe</a> about the future of the Royal Australian Navy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996128_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996128-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> He also visited his home town of Silverdale for the first time since he left England in 1886, and paid another visit to celebrate the signing of the <a href="/wiki/Armistice_of_11_November_1918" title="Armistice of 11 November 1918">Armistice of 11 November 1918</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129_47-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook was one of the Australian delegates at the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919" class="mw-redirect" title="Paris Peace Conference, 1919">Paris Peace Conference, 1919</a>, which was led by Hughes. Although Australia and the other <a href="/wiki/Dominion" title="Dominion">Dominions</a> signed the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles" title="Treaty of Versailles">Treaty of Versailles</a> separately and became individual members of the <a href="/wiki/League_of_Nations" title="League of Nations">League of Nations</a>, for the preceding negotiations their representatives (and those of the United Kingdom) were considered to form one single <a href="/wiki/British_Empire" title="British Empire">British Empire</a> delegation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129_47-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Cook was chosen as the lead British delegate on the Commission on Czechoslovak Affairs, which was tasked with determining the final borders of <a href="/wiki/Czechoslovakia" title="Czechoslovakia">Czechoslovakia</a>. He was hampered by his lack of knowledge of European geography and inability to speak French, the contemporary language of diplomacy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPerman1962149_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerman1962149-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Charles_Seymour" title="Charles Seymour">Charles Seymour</a>, one of the American delegates, he was "blissfully ignorant of everything European and practically every word of our discussion was Greek to him". <a href="/wiki/Harold_Nicolson" title="Harold Nicolson">Harold Nicolson</a>, the other British delegate, said that he assumed an attitude of "benevolent boredom" during meetings.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPerman1962149_48-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerman1962149-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> Cook was generally in favour of an enlarged Czechoslovakia, believing that the lands of the <a href="/wiki/Sudeten_Germans" title="Sudeten Germans">Sudeten Germans</a> had to be included in Czechoslovakia for security reasons. He and Nicolson had a difference of opinion over <a href="/wiki/Great_Sch%C3%BCtt" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Schütt">Great Schütt</a>, but French delegation was in agreement with Cook and the island was awarded to Czechoslovakia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPerman1962151_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerman1962151-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles" title="Treaty of Versailles">Treaty of Versailles</a> was signed on 23 June 1919 with Cook and Hughes signing on behalf of Australia. Cook had some private misgivings about the final document. Although he believed Germany needed to be punished, he thought some elements of the treaty were too vindictive.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996130_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996130-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> He was strongly in favour of the creation of the <a href="/wiki/League_of_Nations" title="League of Nations">League of Nations</a>, and <a href="/wiki/David_Lloyd_George" title="David Lloyd George">David Lloyd George</a> considered him to be the most fervent supporter of the League in the entire British delegation. Lloyd George considered him "a man of calm and balanced judgment".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129_47-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996129-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Cook and Hughes arrived back in Australia on 24 August 1919, after an absence of nearly 16 months. They travelled from <a href="/wiki/Fremantle" title="Fremantle">Fremantle</a> to <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a> via the <a href="/wiki/Trans-Australian_Railway" title="Trans-Australian Railway">Trans-Australian Railway</a>, and Cook particularly enjoyed their stop at the small settlement of <a href="/wiki/Cook,_South_Australia" title="Cook, South Australia">Cook, South Australia</a>, which had been named in his honour a few years earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996131_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996131-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> Hughes was feted upon his return, but Cook did not receive similar adulation and returned to Sydney relatively quietly. <a href="/wiki/1919_Australian_federal_election" title="1919 Australian federal election">An early election</a> was called to capitalise on the prime minister's popularity, which saw the Nationalists win re-election with a reduced majority.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Treasurer">Treasurer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Treasurer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/220px-James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="235" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/330px-James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/440px-James_Guthrie_-_Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="642" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Cook by <a href="/wiki/James_Guthrie_(artist)" title="James Guthrie (artist)">James Guthrie</a>, c. 1920</figcaption></figure>
<p>In March 1920, Cook was appointed Acting Treasurer in the absence of <a href="/wiki/William_Watt_(Australian_politician)" title="William Watt (Australian politician)">William Watt</a>, who was attending a conference in London. Watt resigned by cable in June after falling out with Hughes. The position of Treasurer was initially offered to <a href="/wiki/Stanley_Bruce" title="Stanley Bruce">Stanley Bruce</a>, who declined it, and then to Cook, who reluctantly accepted.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132_52-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> He took office at the height of the post-war boom and was faced with high inflation, but also high unemployment as the economy attempted to absorb returned soldiers. Cook was a <a href="/wiki/Fiscal_conservative" class="mw-redirect" title="Fiscal conservative">fiscal conservative</a> by nature, preferring to limit government spending and keep taxes low. He brought down two <a href="/wiki/Australian_federal_budget" title="Australian federal budget">budgets</a> during his tenure, for the 1920–21 and 1921–22 financial years; both were primarily concerned with reducing inflation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHawkins200977_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHawkins200977-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> He was twice faced with significant revenue shortfalls, which he chose to fill primarily with overseas loans and only a small increase in taxation. He found both options distasteful, but preferred lower taxes – the opposite approach to that taken by Canada, which faced a similar situation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996133–134_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996133–134-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook has been viewed as an orthodox but unimaginative Treasurer whose conservatism with regard to government spending may have been unsuited to the needs of post-war reconstruction.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996135_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996135-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> One notable initiative of his was the transfer of responsibility for issuing banknotes from the <a href="/wiki/Department_of_the_Treasury_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Department of the Treasury (Australia)">Treasury Department</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Bank_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Commonwealth Bank of Australia">Commonwealth Bank of Australia</a>. The bank's governor <a href="/wiki/Denison_Miller" title="Denison Miller">Denison Miller</a> regarded this as "the first and most important step in the enlargement [of the Commonwealth Bank] into a national bank in every sense of the word".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHawkins200978_56-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHawkins200978-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> Cook's final months in parliament were spent as Acting Prime Minister, as Hughes was out of the country for five months attending the <a href="/wiki/1921_Imperial_Conference" title="1921 Imperial Conference">1921 Imperial Conference</a> in London. In November 1921, it was announced that he would be appointed as Australia's <a href="/wiki/Australian_High_Commissioner_to_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian High Commissioner to the United Kingdom">High Commissioner to the United Kingdom</a> in place of Andrew Fisher, whose term had ended earlier that year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132_52-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996132-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="High_Commissioner_to_the_United_Kingdom">High Commissioner to the United Kingdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: High Commissioner to the United Kingdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cook arrived in London on 13 January 1922, where career diplomat <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_Shepherd_(public_servant)" title="Malcolm Shepherd (public servant)">Malcolm Shepherd</a> had been <i><a href="/wiki/Charg%C3%A9_d%27affaires" title="Chargé d&#39;affaires">chargé d'affaires</a></i> for a year. His primary duties were to promote immigration, investment, and trade, as well as to assist in securing favourable loans for the state and federal governments.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198879–80_57-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198879–80-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup> He played a key role in organising the Australian pavilion at the <a href="/wiki/British_Empire_Exhibition" title="British Empire Exhibition">British Empire Exhibition</a> of 1924.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198882_58-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198882-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> Cook represented Australia at the <a href="/wiki/International_Labour_Conference" class="mw-redirect" title="International Labour Conference">International Labour Conferences</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Genoa_Conference_(1922)" title="Genoa Conference (1922)">1922 Genoa Conference</a>, but did "little more than attend and subsequently report to his government".<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="a quote needs a direct citation (June 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He was a more active participant at the <a href="/wiki/League_of_Nations" title="League of Nations">League of Nations</a>, where he was Australia's <a href="/wiki/Permanent_representative" title="Permanent representative">chief delegate</a>. He appeared before the <a href="/wiki/Permanent_Mandates_Commission" title="Permanent Mandates Commission">Permanent Mandates Commission</a> on a number of occasions to answer questions about the administration of its <a href="/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate" title="League of Nations mandate">League of Nations mandates</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mandate_of_Nauru" class="mw-redirect" title="Mandate of Nauru">Nauru</a> and <a href="/wiki/Territory_of_New_Guinea" title="Territory of New Guinea">New Guinea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198884_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198884-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup> Cook overhauled the administration of <a href="/wiki/Australia_House" class="mw-redirect" title="Australia House">Australia House</a>, significantly reducing the number of staff and the annual running costs. This brought him into conflict with Shepherd, his official secretary, who complained that he was "not an easy man to get on with".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996138_60-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996138-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook particularly enjoyed the social and ceremonial aspects of his new position. His first major engagement as High Commissioner was to represent Australia at the wedding of <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Princess_Royal_and_Countess_of_Harewood" title="Mary, Princess Royal and Countess of Harewood">Princess Mary</a>, and he also attended the wedding of the future <a href="/wiki/George_VI" title="George VI">George VI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_The_Queen_Mother" title="Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother">Queen Elizabeth</a> and the funeral of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Alexandra" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Alexandra">Queen Alexandra</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198881–83_61-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198881–83-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> He hosted regular social functions at Australia House, and mixed more easily in high society than his predecessor, whose partial deafness tended to make him withdrawn. Observers noted his "bonhomie and accessibility" in comparison to the "asperity and seclusiveness of Mr Fisher".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996137_62-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996137-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> Cook's term as High Commissioner formally concluded on 10 August 1927, after a six-month extension from the original five-year term. Leaving England ten days later, he and his wife were serenaded at the <a href="/wiki/Port_of_Tilbury" title="Port of Tilbury">Port of Tilbury</a> by the Australian opera singer <a href="/wiki/Nellie_Melba" title="Nellie Melba">Nellie Melba</a>, who had become a close friend of theirs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198884_59-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198884-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/John_Cockburn_(Australian_politician)" title="John Cockburn (Australian politician)">John Cockburn</a> wrote that "rarely has the retirement of one of the representatives in London of the Dominions been attended with such widespread expressions of regret".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996138_60-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996138-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_years_and_death">Final years and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Final years and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/220px-Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="264" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/330px-Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg/440px-Sir_Joseph_Cook.jpg 2x" data-file-width="750" data-file-height="900" /></a><figcaption>Cook in later life</figcaption></figure>
<p>Unlike his predecessors Reid and Fisher, Cook did not settle in London permanently after the end of his term as High Commissioner. He arrived back in Sydney in September 1927 and bought a large house in <a href="/wiki/Bellevue_Hill,_New_South_Wales" title="Bellevue Hill, New South Wales">Bellevue Hill</a>, overlooking Sydney Harbour.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198886_63-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198886-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup> In 1930, he demolished the house and built a luxury apartment block called <i>Silchester</i>, designed by <a href="/wiki/Leslie_Wilkinson" title="Leslie Wilkinson">Leslie Wilkinson</a>. He and his wife retired to one flat and lived on the income from the others.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996139_64-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996139-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> In 1928, Cook was appointed chairman of a <a href="/wiki/Royal_commission" title="Royal commission">royal commission</a> into "the finances of South Australia, as affected by Federation". His co-commissioner <a href="/wiki/Herbert_Brookes" title="Herbert Brookes">Herbert Brookes</a> wrote that "it has been a joy to be associated with you again, even though you have had it all your own way". The commission's report, handed down in 1929, found that South Australia had been disadvantaged by federal government policies that favoured New South Wales and Victoria, and should be compensated as a result. The report later became one of several documents used to justify the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Grants_Commission" title="Commonwealth Grants Commission">Commonwealth Grants Commission</a> and the expansion of federal grants to state governments.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996140_65-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurdoch1996140-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook enjoyed a low-profile retirement, with <i><a href="/wiki/Smith%27s_Weekly" title="Smith&#39;s Weekly">Smith's Weekly</a></i> observing in 1936 that no other high-ranking politician had "staged such a swiftly effective fade-out from the public view on retirement from the hurly-burly".<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup> He was interviewed during the <a href="/wiki/Sudeten_Crisis" class="mw-redirect" title="Sudeten Crisis">Sudeten Crisis</a> and after the <a href="/wiki/German_invasion_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="German invasion of Poland">German invasion of Poland</a>, on both occasions defending the Treaty of Versailles and blaming German aggression for the new war.<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup> Cook ignored requests to write his memoirs, and in fact destroyed many of his personal papers; this would later present difficulties for his biographers. His final public-speaking engagement was at a church function in July 1940, where he warned against authoritarianism and told the audience to "beware of those people who want to establish a new world order [...] the old things of the world today are the wisest and best things I know".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198888_69-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198888-69">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook died at his home in Bellevue Hill on 30 July 1947, after a heart-related illness of about three weeks. He was granted a <a href="/wiki/State_funeral" title="State funeral">state funeral</a>, held at the Wesley Chapel on <a href="/wiki/Castlereagh_Street" title="Castlereagh Street">Castlereagh Street</a>, and then cremated at the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Suburbs_Crematorium" class="mw-redirect" title="Northern Suburbs Crematorium">Northern Suburbs Crematorium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198889_70-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198889-70">&#91;70&#93;</a></sup> Pallbearers included Billy Hughes and <a href="/wiki/Willie_Kelly_(politician)" title="Willie Kelly (politician)">Willie Kelly</a>, the latter being the last surviving member of his ministry.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198890_71-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198890-71">&#91;71&#93;</a></sup> Cook died at the age of 86, surpassing George Reid as Australia's longest-lived prime minister; his record was broken by Hughes a few years later. He was the oldest living prime minister for a record span of over 27 years, following the death of Edmund Barton in 1920.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBebbington198890_71-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBebbington198890-71">&#91;71&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Honours">Honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Cook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Honours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg/170px-Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg/255px-Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg/340px-Joseph_Cook_bust.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>Bust of Joseph Cook by sculptor Wallace Anderson located in the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Ministers_Avenue" title="Prime Ministers Avenue">Prime Ministers Avenue</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ballarat_Botanical_Gardens" title="Ballarat Botanical Gardens">Ballarat Botanical Gardens</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Cook was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Privy_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Privy Council">Privy Council</a> on 16 July 1914.<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">&#91;72&#93;</a></sup> He was knighted in 1918 as Knight Grand Cross of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" title="Order of St Michael and St George">Order of St Michael and St George</a> (GCMG).<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">&#91;73&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1972, he was honoured on a postage stamp bearing his portrait issued by <a href="/wiki/Australia_Post" title="Australia Post">Australia Post</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">&#91;74&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although there is a seat called <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Cook" title="Division of Cook">Cook</a>, this was named after Captain <a href="/wiki/James_Cook" title="James Cook">James Cook</a>. In 2006, the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Electoral_Commission" title="Australian Electoral Commission">Australian Electoral Commission</a>'s Redistribution Committee for New South Wales proposed that the division be jointly named for Joseph and James Cook.<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup> However, as of 2022 no such action has been taken, and therefore Cook remains the only (eligible) Prime Minister who does not have a federal electorate named after him.
</p><p>The suburb of <a href="/wiki/Cook,_Australian_Capital_Territory" title="Cook, Australian Capital Territory">Cook</a>, located within the <a href="/wiki/Belconnen" title="Belconnen">Belconnen</a> district of <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a>, Australian Capital Territory is jointly named after Joseph and <a href="/wiki/James_Cook" title="James Cook">Captain James Cook.</a>
</p>
