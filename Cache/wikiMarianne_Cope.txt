<p><b>Marianne Cope</b>, TOSF, also known as <b>Saint Marianne of Moloka&#x2bb;i</b> (January 23, 1838 – August 9, 1918), was a German-born American <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a> who was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis#Sisters_of_St._Francis_of_Philadelphia_(OSF)" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Sisters of St Francis of Syracuse, New York</a>, and founding leader of its St. Joseph's Hospital in the city, among the first of 50 general hospitals in the country.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Known also for her charitable works, in 1883 she relocated with six other <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">sisters</a> to <a href="/wiki/Territory_of_Hawaii" title="Territory of Hawaii">Hawaiʻi</a> to care for persons suffering <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Molokai" title="Molokai">Molokaʻi</a> and aid in developing the medical infrastructure in Hawaiʻi. Despite direct contact with the patients over many years, Cope did not contract the disease.
</p><p>In 2005, Cope was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Cope was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">declared a saint</a> by the same pope on October 21, 2012, along with <a href="/wiki/Kateri_Tekakwitha" title="Kateri Tekakwitha">Kateri Tekakwitha</a>, a 17th-century <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">Native American</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-canonization_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canonization-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Cope is the 11th person in what is now the United States to be canonized by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-canonization_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canonization-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_vocation">Birth and vocation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Birth and vocation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Cope was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> <b>Barbara Koob</b>, later anglicizing her last name to "Cope". She was born on January 23, 1838, in <a href="/wiki/Heppenheim" title="Heppenheim">Heppenheim</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duchy">Grand Duchy</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a> to Peter Koob (1787–1862) and Barbara Witzenbacher (1803–1872). The following year her family emigrated to the United States, settling in the industrial city of <a href="/wiki/Utica,_New_York" title="Utica, New York">Utica, New York</a>. They became members of the <a href="/wiki/St._Joseph%27s_Church_(Utica,_New_York)" title="St. Joseph&#39;s Church (Utica, New York)">Parish of St Joseph</a>, where Cope attended  <a href="/wiki/Parish_school" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish school">parish school</a>. By the time she was in eighth grade, her father had developed a disability. As the oldest child, Cope left school to work in a textile factory to support her family.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Cope, her father, and her siblings became naturalized as an American citizens in the 1850s.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church,_Utica_New_York.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg/170px-Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="242" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg/255px-Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg/340px-Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church%2C_Utica_New_York.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2107" data-file-height="3000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Historic_Old_St._John%27s_Church,_Utica_New_York.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Historic Old St. John's Church, Utica New York</div></div></div>
<p>Cope received her First Holy Communion and her Confirmation at Historic Old St. Johns Church in <a href="/wiki/Utica,_New_York" title="Utica, New York">Utica, New York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By the time their father, Peter Cope, died in 1862, the younger children in the family were of age to support themselves, so Barbara pursued her long-felt <a href="/wiki/Religious_calling" title="Religious calling">religious calling</a>. She entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Sisters of the Third Order Regular of Saint Francis in <a href="/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York" title="Syracuse, New York">Syracuse, New York</a>. After a year of formation, Cope received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of the Franciscan Sisters and the new name <b>Marianne</b>. She became first a teacher and then a principal in newly established schools for the region's <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German-speaking</a> immigrants. Following the revolutions of 1848, more German Catholic immigrants entered the United States. <sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1870, Cope had become a member of the governing council of her <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a>. She helped found the first two Catholic hospitals in <a href="/wiki/Central_New_York" title="Central New York">Central New York</a>, with charters stipulating that medical care was to be provided to all, regardless of race or creed. She was appointed by the <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> to govern St. Joseph's Hospital, the first public hospital in Syracuse, serving from 1870 to 1877.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a hospital administrator, Cope became involved with the move of <a href="/wiki/Geneva_Medical_College#Geneva_Medical_College_of_Hobart_College" title="Geneva Medical College">Geneva Medical College of Hobart College</a> from Geneva, New York, to Syracuse, where it became the <a href="/wiki/Geneva_Medical_College#College_of_Medicine_–_Syracuse_University" title="Geneva Medical College">College of Medicine</a> at <a href="/wiki/Syracuse_University" title="Syracuse University">Syracuse University</a>. She contracted with the college to accept their students for treating patients in her hospital to further their medical education. Her stipulation in the contract—again unique for the period—was the right of the patients to refuse care by the students. These experiences helped prepare her for the special ministry she next pursued.<sup id="cite_ref-bio_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Call_to_Hawaii">Call to Hawaii</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Call to Hawaii">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1883, Cope, by then Superior General of the congregation, received a plea for help from King <a href="/wiki/Kal%C4%81kaua" title="Kalākaua">Kalākaua</a> of Hawaii to care for <a href="/wiki/Hansen%27s_disease" class="mw-redirect" title="Hansen&#39;s disease">leprosy</a> sufferers. More than 50 religious congregations had already declined his request for Sisters to do this because leprosy was considered to be highly contagious. She responded enthusiastically to the letter:
</p>
<blockquote><p>I am hungry for the work and I wish with all my heart to be one of the chosen Ones, whose privilege it will be, to sacrifice themselves for the salvation of the souls of the poor Islanders... I am not afraid of any disease, hence it would be my greatest delight even to minister to the abandoned 'lepers.'<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako,_Honolulu.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg/220px-Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="152" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg/330px-Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg/440px-Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako%2C_Honolulu.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="690" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sisters_of_St._Francis_in_1886_at_the_Branch_Hospital_for_Lepers_in_Kakaako,_Honolulu.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Sisters of St. Francis, at the Kaka&#x2bb;ako Branch Hospital.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg/220px-Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="191" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg/330px-Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg/440px-Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="520" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gibson_and_Mother_Marianne_Cope.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Walter_Murray_Gibson" class="mw-redirect" title="Walter Murray Gibson">Walter Murray Gibson</a> with the Sisters of St. Francis and daughters of Hansen's disease patients, at the Kaka&#x2bb;ako Branch Hospital.</div></div></div>
<p>Cope departed from Syracuse with six other Sisters to travel to <a href="/wiki/Honolulu,_Hawaii" class="mw-redirect" title="Honolulu, Hawaii">Honolulu</a> to answer this call, arriving on November 8, 1883. They traveled on the <i><a href="/wiki/SS_Mariposa_(1883)" title="SS Mariposa (1883)">SS Mariposa</a>.</i> With Mother Marianne as a supervisor, the Sisters' task was to manage <a href="/wiki/Kakaako" class="mw-redirect" title="Kakaako">Kaka&#x2bb;ako</a> Branch Hospital on <a href="/wiki/Oahu" title="Oahu">O&#x2bb;ahu</a>, which served as a receiving station for Hansen's disease patients gathered from all over the islands. The more severe cases were processed and shipped to the island of <a href="/wiki/Molokai" title="Molokai">Moloka&#x2bb;i</a> for confinement in the settlement at Kalawao, and then later at <a href="/wiki/Kalaupapa" class="mw-redirect" title="Kalaupapa">Kalaupapa</a>.
</p><p>The following year, at the government's request, Cope set up Malulani Hospital, the first general hospital on the island of <a href="/wiki/Maui" title="Maui">Maui</a>. Soon, she was called back to the hospital in <a href="/wiki/Oahu" title="Oahu">Oahu</a>. She had to deal with a government-appointed administrator's abuse of the leprosy patients at the Branch Hospital at Kakaako, an area adjoining Honolulu. She told the government that either the administrator had to be dismissed or the Sisters would return to Syracuse. She was given charge of the overcrowded hospital. Her return to Syracuse to re-assume governance of the congregation was delayed, as both the government and church authorities thought she was essential to the mission's success.
</p><p>Two years later, the king awarded Cope with the Cross of a Companion of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Order_of_Kapiolani" title="Royal Order of Kapiolani">Royal Order of Kapiolani</a> for her care of his people.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The work continued to increase. In November 1885, Cope opened the Kapiolani Home with the government's support to provide shelter to homeless female children of leprosy patients. The home was located on a leprosy hospital's grounds because only the Sisters were willing to care for children so closely associated with people suffering from leprosy.
</p><p>In 1887, a new government came into office. It ended the forced exile of leprosy patients to Molokai and closed the specialty hospital in Oahu. A year later, the authorities pleaded with Cope to establish a new home for women and girls on the Kalaupapa <a href="/wiki/Peninsula" title="Peninsula">peninsula</a> of Molokai. She accepted the call, knowing that it might mean she would never return to New York. "We will cheerfully accept the work…" was her response.<sup id="cite_ref-bio_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Molokai">Molokai</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Molokai">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>In November 1888, Cope moved to Kalaupapa. She cared for the dying <a href="/wiki/Father_Damien" title="Father Damien">Father Damien</a>, <a href="/wiki/Picpus_Fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Picpus Fathers">SS.CC.</a>, who was already known internationally for his work in the leper colony and began to take over his burdens. She had met him shortly after her arrival in Hawaii.
</p><p>When Father Damien died on April 15, 1889, the government officially gave Cope a charge for the care of the boys of Kalaupapa and her current role in caring for the colony's female residents. A prominent local businessman, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Perrine_Baldwin" title="Henry Perrine Baldwin">Henry Perrine Baldwin</a>, donated money for the new home. Cope and two assistants, Sister <a href="/wiki/Leopoldina_Burns" title="Leopoldina Burns">Leopoldina Burns</a> and Sister Vincentia McCormick, opened and ran a new girls' school, which she named in Baldwin's honor. A community of <a href="/wiki/Brother_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Brother (Catholic)">Religious Brothers</a> was sought to come and care for the boys. After the arrival of four <a href="/wiki/Brothers_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Brothers of the Sacred Heart">Brothers of the Sacred Heart</a> in 1895,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Cope withdrew the Sisters to the Bishop Home for leprous women and girls. <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Dutton" title="Joseph Dutton">Joseph Dutton</a> was given charge of Baldwin House by the government.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cope died on August 9, 1918, due to natural causes. She was buried on the grounds of the Bishop Home. In 2005, her remains were brought to Syracuse for reinterment at her <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">motherhouse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 2014, her remains were returned to Honolulu and are enshrined at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Peace" title="Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace">Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_honors">Legacy and honors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy and honors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>1927 — Saint Francis Hospital was founded in Honolulu in her memory as a community hospital and trained nurses to work with Hansen's disease patients.</li>
<li>1957 — St. Francis opened the Child Development Center at the Honolulu Community Church.</li>
<li>1962 — St. Francis Home Care Services was established, the first in Hawaii to specialize in home health care for Hawaiian people.</li>
<li>2005, Induction into the <a href="/wiki/National_Women%27s_Hall_of_Fame" title="National Women&#39;s Hall of Fame">National Women's Hall of Fame</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>2006 — The Sisters of St. Francis chose to focus on long-term care, transferring St. Francis Hospital's two facilities to a private board. The facilities are now known as the Hawaii Medical Center East in Liliha, and Hawaii Medical Center West in Ewa.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Both hospitals were closed at the end of 2011.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In August 2012, The Queen's Health Systems agreed to acquire the former Hawaii Medical Center West and reopen the hospital in the fall of 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The Saint Francis School was founded in Cope's honor in 1924, operating as a girls-only school for grades 6–9.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>The community which Cope founded on Molokai continues to minister to the few patients who have Hansen Disease. The Franciscan Sisters work at several schools and minister to parishioners throughout the Hawaiian Islands.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1993, Katherine Dehlia Mahoney was allegedly healed from multiple-organ failure after praying to Marianne Cope for intercession. On October 24, 2003, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> declared Cope to have been "heroically virtuous." On April 19, 2004, Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> issued a papal decree declaring her <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>. On December 20, 2004, after receiving the unanimous affirmation of the Congregation of the Causes of Saints, Pope John Paul II ordered a decree to be issued authenticating this recovery as a miracle to be attributed to the intercession of Cope. On May 14, 2005, Cope was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in his first <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> ceremony.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
Over 100 followers from Hawai&#x2bb;i attended the beatification ceremony, along with 300 members of Cope's religious congregation in Syracuse. At the ceremony, presided over by <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saraiva_Martins" title="José Saraiva Martins">José Saraiva Martins</a>, <a href="/wiki/Claretians" title="Claretians">C.M.F.</a>, the Hawaiian song "<i>Makalapua</i>" (a favorite of Cope) was sung.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was established as January 23 and is celebrated by her own religious congregation, the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Honolulu" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Honolulu">Diocese of Honolulu</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Syracuse" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Syracuse">Diocese of Syracuse</a>.
</p><p>After the announcement by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> of her impending beatification, during January 2005, Cope's remains were moved to the <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">motherhouse</a> of the congregation in Syracuse. A temporary <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> was established to honor her. By 2009, the erection of a marble <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> in the motherhouse chapel was complete. Her remains were interred in the new shrine on her feast day of January 23.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg/220px-Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg/330px-Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg/440px-Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg 2x" data-file-width="612" data-file-height="612" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Mother_Marianne_Cope_statue.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Mother Marianne Cope statue dedicated January 23, 2010, in Honolulu</div></div></div>
<p>In 2007, a statue of her was erected at St Joseph's Church in her native Utica, whose parish school she had attended in her childhood.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On December 6, 2011, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> found that a second miracle could also be attributed to the intercession of Cope. This finding was forwarded to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> by its secretary, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, for papal approval.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> On December 19, 2011, Pope Benedict signed and approved the promulgation of the decree for Cope's <a href="/wiki/Sainthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainthood">sainthood</a> and she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on October 21, 2012;<sup id="cite_ref-blessed_21october_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blessed_21october-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> a relic was carried to Honolulu from her mother church. Dr. Waldery Hilgeman was the Postulator of the Cause of Canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-Downs_2012_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Downs_2012-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After <a href="/wiki/Father_Damien" title="Father Damien">Father Damien</a>, Cope is the second person to be canonized who had served in the Hawaiian Islands. She was both the first Beatification and the last Canonisation under Pope Benedict XVI. In 2014, the church announced that Saint Marianne's remains would be re-interred at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_of_Peace" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Our Lady of Peace">Cathedral of Our Lady of Peace</a> in Honolulu, which was undergoing an extensive renovation. This is a more convenient location for the faithful than the <a href="/wiki/Kalaupapa_National_Historical_Park" title="Kalaupapa National Historical Park">Kalaupapa National Historical Park</a> on Moloka&#x2bb;i, where access is primarily by plane or mule train. She sometimes attended Mass at the cathedral, and it was where Father Damien was ordained. In New York, the Franciscan Convent which held her remains, moved to a new location because its former buildings needed extensive repairs.<sup id="cite_ref-reinter_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reinter-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecumenical_veneration">Ecumenical veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marianne_Cope&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Ecumenical veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Cope is honored jointly with <a href="/wiki/Father_Damien" title="Father Damien">Saint Damien of Moloka'i</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">liturgical calendar of the Episcopal Church (USA)</a>. Their shared <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on April 15.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
