<p><b>Saint Hermes</b>, born in <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>, died in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> in 120, is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.  His name appears in the <i>Martyrologium Hieronymianum</i> as well as entries in the <i>Depositio Martyrum</i> (354).  There was a large <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> over his tomb that was built around 600 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pelagius_I" title="Pope Pelagius I">Pope Pelagius I</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  and restored by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_I" title="Pope Adrian I">Pope Adrian I</a>.  A <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">catacomb</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Salarian_Way" class="mw-redirect" title="Salarian Way">Salarian Way</a> bears his name.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Hermes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a>, his feast is on 28 August. Under that date, he appears in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the official but professedly incomplete list of saints recognized by the Catholic Church. The entry is as follows: "In the Cemetery of Basilia on the Old Salarian Way, Saint Hermes, Martyr, whom, as reported by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Saint Damasus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> sent forth, but Rome kept as its citizen when he died for the holy name."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His existence is attested by his early cult.  However, his <i>Acts</i>, included in those of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_I" title="Pope Alexander I">Pope Alexander I</a>, are legendary. They depict Hermes as a wealthy freedman who with his companions was martyred in Rome, being killed on the orders of a judge named Aurelian.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Hermes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Some of his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were given to <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I"> Pope Gregory the Great</a>.  Other relics went to <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a>; Lothair brought them first to <a href="/wiki/Cornelism%C3%BCnster" class="mw-redirect" title="Cornelismünster">Cornelismünster</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>.  The relics later came to <a href="/wiki/Ronse" title="Ronse">Ronse</a> in the 9th century.  During those times, <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Viking</a> raids forced the monks to flee the town more than once, and the monastery was burnt by the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a> in 880.  The relics were recovered in 940 and housed in a <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a>-style <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> in 1083. The <i>Fiertelommegang</i> (Fiertel Procession) that has been held here since 1090, a procession with the relics throughout the city.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The church of Saint Hermes, which was later built on top of the crypt, was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> in 1129.  A pilgrimage in honour of the saint, who had by then become known for curing mental illnesses, sustained the local economy.  There is still a <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> saying today which translates as "Saint Hermes cures the area's madmen but keeps the Ronse dwellers as they are". Hermes is the patron saint of Ronse.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although he is recognized as a saint of the Catholic Church, the commemoration of Saint Hermes in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> was removed in 1969 because of the paucity of information about him.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
