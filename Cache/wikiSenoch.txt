<p><b>Saint Senoch</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Sénoch</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Senoc(h)us</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Taifal" class="mw-redirect" title="Taifal">Taifal</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.  He was born in <a href="/wiki/Tiffauges" title="Tiffauges">Tiffauges</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Poitou" title="Poitou">Poitou</a>. He founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in 536, serving as abbot. They established themselves at a place now called <a href="/wiki/Saint-Senoch" title="Saint-Senoch">Saint-Senoch</a>, which was the site of some <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman ruins</a>. St. Senoch was famous for his acts of austerity, such as enclosing himself within a four-foot space so narrow that he couldn't move the lower half of his body. This won him the undying love of a vast populace.
</p><p>Senoch knew both <a href="/wiki/Eufronius" title="Eufronius">St. Euphronius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tours">Bishop of Tours</a>, and his successor, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gregory of Tours">Saint Gregory of Tours</a>.
</p>
