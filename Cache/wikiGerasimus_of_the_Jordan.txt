<p><b>Gerasimus of the Jordan</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Γεράσιμος Ἰορδανίτης</span>, <b>Abba Gerasimus</b>, <b>Holy Righteous Father Gerasimus of Jordan</b>—also spelled <b>Gerasimos</b> or <b>Gerasim</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the 5th century AD. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerasimus_of_the_Jordan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gerasimus was born into a wealthy family in the province of <a href="/wiki/Lycia" title="Lycia">Lycia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-roca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-roca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the southern part of <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>, but he left his family wealth and worldly affairs to become a monk. He departed for the region <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Thebaid</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egyptian</a> desert, later again returning to his native Lycia. About the middle of 5th century Saint Gerasimus went to <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> and settled in the wilderness near the <a href="/wiki/Jordan_River" title="Jordan River">Jordan River</a>. There he established a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> and became known for his righteous life of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer">prayer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-roca_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-roca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is reputed to have attended to the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Ecumenical Council">Fourth Ecumenical Council</a> at <a href="/wiki/Chalcedon" title="Chalcedon">Chalcedon</a> in 451.
</p><p>The story of Gerasimus and the lion, when the saint tamed the animal by removing a thorn from its paw and taught it obedience, became widely known in the Christian world.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A parallel tale is preserved about <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">St. Jerome</a>, but, as has been argued, the tale of Jerome may have derived from a confusion with the exploits of Gerasimus, as Jerome in later Latin is "Geronimus".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Gerasimus of the Jordan died in 475 (or, by other accounts, 451). His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 4 March by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and on 5 March by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastery_of_St._Gerasimus">Monastery of St. Gerasimus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerasimus_of_the_Jordan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monastery of St. Gerasimus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Greek Orthodox monastery of <a href="/wiki/Deir_Hajla" title="Deir Hajla">Deir Hajla</a> near <a href="/wiki/Jericho" title="Jericho">Jericho</a> commemorates St. Gerasimus, whose <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">lavra</a> was nearby.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The history of the monastery is also linked to another Christian narrative. The <a href="/wiki/Holy_Family" title="Holy Family">Holy Family</a> were said to have found refuge in a cave here
during their flight from Herod the Great. An underground chapel was built on the spot where the Holy Family is believed to have spent the night.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hymns">Hymns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerasimus_of_the_Jordan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hymns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Troparion - Tone 1</li></ul>
<blockquote>
<p><i>Dweller of the desert and angel in the body, / you were shown to be a wonderworker, our God-bearing Father Gerasimus. / You received heavenly gifts through fasting, vigil, and prayer: / healing the sick and the souls of those drawn to you by faith. / Glory to Him who gave you strength! / Glory to Him who granted you a crown! / Glory to Him who through you grants healing to all!</i>
</p>
</blockquote>
<ul><li>Kontakion - Tone 4</li></ul>
<blockquote>
<p><i>Father, you burned with heavenly love, / preferring the harshness of the Jordan desert to all the delights of the world. / Therefore, a wild beast served you until your death; he died in obedience / in grief on your grave. / Thus God has glorified you, / and when you pray to Him / remember us, Father Gerasimus</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
