<p><b>Hilarion the Great</b> (291–371) was an <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a> who spent most of his life in the desert according to the example of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a> (c. 251–356). While St Anthony is considered to have established Christian monasticism in the Egyptian desert, St Hilarion is considered by some to be the founder of Palestinian monasticism<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and venerated as a saint by the Orthodox and the Roman Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The chief source of information regarding Hilarion is the biography written by <a href="/wiki/St._Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Jerome">St. Jerome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> "The life of Hilarion was written by Jerome in 390 at Bethlehem. Its object was to further the ascetic life to which he was devoted. It contains, amidst much that is legendary, some statements which attach it to genuine history, and is in any case a record of the state of the human mind in the 4th century."
<sup id="cite_ref-schaff_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Hilarion was born in Thabatha, south of <a href="/wiki/History_of_Gaza#Byzantine_rule_and_advent_of_Christianity" title="History of Gaza">Gaza</a> in <a href="/wiki/Syria_Palaestina" title="Syria Palaestina">Syria Palaestina</a> of pagan parents. He successfully studied rhetoric with a <a href="/wiki/Grammarian_(Greco-Roman_world)" class="mw-redirect" title="Grammarian (Greco-Roman world)">grammarian</a> in <a href="/wiki/History_of_Alexandria#Late_Roman_and_Byzantine_period" title="History of Alexandria">Alexandria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It seems that he was converted to Christianity in Alexandria. After that, he shunned the pleasures of his day—theatre, circus and arena—and spent his time attending church. According to St. Jerome, he was a thin and delicate youth of fragile health.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beginnings_of_monastic_life">Beginnings of monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beginnings of monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After hearing of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Anthony_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Anthony the Great">Saint Anthony</a>, whose name (according to St. Jerome), "was in the mouth of all the races of <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>", Hilarion, at the age of fifteen, went to live with him in the desert for two months. As <a href="/wiki/Saint_Anthony_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Anthony the Great">Anthony</a>'s hermitage was busy with visitors seeking cures for diseases or demonic affliction, Hilarion returned home along with some monks. At Thabatha, his parents having died in the meantime, he gave his inheritance to his brothers and the poor and left for the wilderness.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Time_at_Majoma">Time at Majoma</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Time at Majoma">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg/220px-Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="129" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg/330px-Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg/440px-Octave_Tassaert_-_Temptation_of_Saint_Hilarion.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="470" /></a><figcaption>"The Temptation of Saint Hilarion", by <a href="/wiki/Octave_Tassaert" title="Octave Tassaert">Octave Tassaert</a>, c.1857 (<a href="/wiki/Montreal_Museum_of_Fine_Arts" title="Montreal Museum of Fine Arts">Montreal Museum of Fine Arts</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hilarion went to the area southwest of <a href="/wiki/Maiuma" title="Maiuma">Majoma</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Port_of_Gaza" title="Port of Gaza">port of Gaza</a>, that was limited by the sea at one side and marshland on the other. Because the district was notorious for brigandage, and his relatives and friends warned him of the danger he was incurring, it was his practice never to abide long in the same place.<sup id="cite_ref-schaff_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> With him he took only a shirt of coarse linen, a cloak of skins given to him by St. Anthony, and a coarse blanket. He led a nomadic life, and he fasted rigorously, not partaking of his frugal meal until after sunset. He supported himself by weaving baskets.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hilarion lived a life of hardship and simplicity in the desert, where he also experienced spiritual dryness that included temptations to despair.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Beset by carnal thoughts, he fasted even more. He was "so wasted that his bones scarcely held together" (Jerome). According to St. Jerome:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>So many were his temptations and so various the snares of demons night and day, that if I wished to relate them, a volume would not suffice. How often when he lay down did naked women appear to him, how often sumptuous feasts when he was hungry! (Jerome, Life of St Hilarion, 7)</p></blockquote>
<p>He finally built a hut of reeds and <a href="/wiki/Cyperaceae" title="Cyperaceae">sedges</a> at the site of modern-day <a href="/wiki/Deir_al-Balah" title="Deir al-Balah">Deir al-Balah</a>, in which he lived for four years. Afterwards, he constructed a tiny low-ceilinged cell, "a tomb rather than a house",<sup id="cite_ref-schaff_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where he slept on a bed of rushes, and recited the Bible or sang hymns. He never washed his clothes, changed them only when they fell apart, and shaved his hair only once a year. He was once visited by robbers, but they left him alone when they learned that he did not fear death (and had nothing worth stealing, anyway).<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Jerome described Hilarion's diet as a half a pint of <a href="/wiki/Lentil" title="Lentil">lentils</a> moistened with cold water, and after three years he switched to dry bread with salt and water. Eventually, perceiving his sight to grow dim and his body to be subject to an itching with an unnatural roughness, he added a little oil to this diet.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After he had lived in the wilderness for 22 years, he became quite famous in Syria Palaestina. Visitors started to come, begging for his help. The parade of petitioners and would-be disciples drove Hilarion to retire to more remote locations. But they followed him everywhere. First he visited Anthony's retreat in Egypt. Then he withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, later to <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a>, and finally to <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>. He died there in 371.<sup id="cite_ref-loyola_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyola-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Miracles were attributed to him.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His first miracle was when he cured a woman from <a href="/wiki/Bayt_Jibrin" title="Bayt Jibrin">Eleutheropolis</a> (a Roman city in Syria Palaestina) who had been barren for 15 years.<sup id="cite_ref-loyola_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyola-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Later, he cured three children of a fatal illness, healed a paralysed charioteer, and expelled demons.<sup id="cite_ref-loyola_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyola-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastery">Monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Tell_Umm_el-%27Amr" class="mw-redirect" title="Tell Umm el-&#39;Amr">Tell Umm el-'Amr</a></div>
<p>In time, a <a href="/wiki/Tell_Umm_el-%27Amr" class="mw-redirect" title="Tell Umm el-&#39;Amr">monastery</a> grew around his cell, which was so beset by visitors, especially females, that Hilarion fled.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Novelization">Novelization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hilarion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Novelization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Hermann_Hesse" title="Hermann Hesse">Hermann Hesse</a> adapted a biography of St. Hilarion as one of the three <i>Lives of Joseph Knecht</i>, making up his Nobel Prize–winning novel <i><a href="/wiki/The_Glass_Bead_Game" title="The Glass Bead Game">The Glass Bead Game</a></i> (also known as <i>Magister Ludi</i>).
</p>
