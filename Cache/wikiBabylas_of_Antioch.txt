<p><b>Babylas</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Βαβύλας</span>) (died 253) was a <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">patriarch of Antioch</a> (237–253), who died in prison during the <a href="/wiki/Decian_persecution" title="Decian persecution">Decian persecution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> of the Byzantine rite his feast day is September 4, in the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Catholic"> Roman Rite</a>, January 24.  He has the distinction of being the first saint recorded as having had his remains moved or "<a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a>" for religious purposes; a practice that was to become extremely common in later centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Babylas_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Babylas was the successor of Zebinus as Bishop of Antioch in the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Gordian_III" title="Gordian III">Gordian III</a> (238-244), being the twelfth bishop of the see. During the Decian persecution (250) he made an unwavering confession of faith and was thrown into prison where he died from his sufferings. He was, therefore, venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>'s homily upon Babylas and the <i>Acts of the Martyrs</i> report the following story, that Babylas once refused the visiting pagan emperor, on account of his sinful ways, permission to enter the church and had ordered him to take his place among the penitents. John does not give the name of the emperor; the <i>Acts</i> mention <a href="/wiki/Numerian" title="Numerian">Numerian</a>. It is more likely the contemporary <a href="/wiki/Philip_the_Arab" title="Philip the Arab">Philip the Arab</a> of whom Eusebius (<i>Historia ecclesiastica</i>, VI, 34) reports that a bishop would not let him enter the gathering of Christians at the <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> vigil.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Later legend elaborates on this, stating that Babylas demanded that he do penance for his part in the murder of the young <a href="/wiki/Gordian_III" title="Gordian III">Gordian III</a> before he would allow Philip to celebrate Easter.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Babylas_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 351 the Caesar <a href="/wiki/Constantius_Gallus" title="Constantius Gallus">Constantius Gallus</a> built a new church in honor of Babylas at <a href="/wiki/Harbiye,_Antakya" title="Harbiye, Antakya">Daphne</a>, a suburb of Antioch, and had the remains of the bishop transferred to it.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The intention of Gallus in <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translating</a> the remains of Babylas to Daphne was to neutralize the pagan effects of the temple of <a href="/wiki/Apollo" title="Apollo">Apollo</a> located there, or, as Chrysostom expresses it, to "bring a physician to the sick."
</p><p>According to Chrysostom, when Emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian</a> consulted the oracle of Apollo at the temple in Daphne (362), he received no answer, and was told that it was because of the proximity of Babylas.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He therefore, had the sarcophagus of the martyr exhumed and taken back to his original place of burial.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A few days later, on October 22, a mysterious fire broke out in the temple of Apollo, consuming the roof of the building, and the statue of the god,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> copied from <a href="/wiki/Phidias" title="Phidias">Phidias</a>' statue of Zeus at <a href="/wiki/Olympia,_Greece" title="Olympia, Greece">Olympia</a>. Julian, suspecting angry Christians were responsible, ordered the cathedral of Antioch closed,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrendo200786-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and an investigation into the cause of the fire: <a href="/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus" title="Ammianus Marcellinus">Ammianus Marcellinus</a> reports "a frivolous rumor" laid the blame to some candles lit by a worshipper late the previous night (XXII, 13). John Chrysostom claimed a bolt of lightning set the temple on fire. The remains of Babylas were reinterred in a church dedicated to him on the other side of the River <a href="/wiki/Orontes_river" class="mw-redirect" title="Orontes river">Orontes</a>. Near the close of his discourse John Chrysostom refers to the erection of the church dedicated to Babylas, and to the zeal of the Bishop <a href="/wiki/Meletius_of_Antioch" title="Meletius of Antioch">Meletius</a> in promoting it, who actually took part in the work with his own hands.
</p><p>This was the first recorded "translation" of the buried remains of a saint, something that was to become routine in the Middle Ages.
</p><p>The columns and walls of the ruined temple were still pointed out twenty years later. In the Middle Ages, the remains of Babylas are said to have been moved to <a href="/wiki/Cremona" title="Cremona">Cremona</a>.
</p>
