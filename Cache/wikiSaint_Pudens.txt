					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">1st century Christian saint and martyr</div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Pudens.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/84/Saint_Pudens.jpg" decoding="async" width="143" height="221" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="143" data-file-height="221" /></a><figcaption>Russian <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Saint Pudens</b> was an early Christian saint and martyr.
</p><p>He is mentioned as a layman of the Roman Church in <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_Epistles" class="mw-redirect" title="Pastoral Epistles">2 Timothy</a> 4:21.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is said to have been the son of <a href="/wiki/Quintus_Cornelius_Pudens" title="Quintus Cornelius Pudens">Quintus Cornelius Pudens</a>, a Roman <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senate" title="Roman Senate">Senator</a>, and his wife Priscilla. According to tradition, they were among the first converted by <a href="/wiki/St._Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter">St. Peter</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pudens was baptised by Peter, who was a guest in his parents' house in Rome. Pudens was martyred under <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a> (reigned 54–68). He is commemorated on <a href="/wiki/April_14_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="April 14 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">April 14</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Church calendar">Eastern Orthodox Church calendar</a> and May 19 according to the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a>.
</p><p>He is said to have<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> had two sons, <a href="/wiki/Novatus" title="Novatus">Novatus</a> and Timotheus, and two daughters, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Praxedes" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Praxedes">Praxedes</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pudentiana" title="Pudentiana">Pudentiana</a>, all saints, but if Pudens' life is documented, those of his daughters are derived only by the existence of two ancient churches, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a> and <a href="/wiki/Santa_Pudenziana" title="Santa Pudenziana">Santa Pudenziana</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p><p>The acts of the synod of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Symmachus" title="Pope Symmachus">Pope Symmachus</a> (499) show the existence of a <i>titulus Pudentis</i>, a church with the authority to administer sacraments, which was also known as <i>ecclesia Pudentiana</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
