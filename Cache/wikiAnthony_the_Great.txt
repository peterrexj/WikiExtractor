<p><b>Anthony the Great</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἀντώνιος</span></span> <i>Antṓnios</i>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">القديس أنطونيوس الكبير</span>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Antonius</i>; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop">Ⲁⲃⲃⲁ Ⲁⲛⲧⲱⲛⲓ</span>; c. 12 January 251 – 17 January 356) was a Christian monk from Egypt, revered since his death as a saint. He is distinguished from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Anthony_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint Anthony (disambiguation)">other saints named Anthony</a>, such as <span class="nowrap"><a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">Anthony of Padua</a></span>, by various epithets: <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony of Egypt</b></span>, <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony the Abbot</b></span>, <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony of the Desert</b></span>, <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony the Anchorite</b></span>, <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony the Hermit</b></span>, and <span class="nowrap"><b>Anthony of Thebes</b></span>. For his importance among the <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Fathers</a> and to all later <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">Christian monasticism</a>, he is also known as the <span class="nowrap"><b>Father of All Monks</b></span>. His feast day is celebrated on 17 January among the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> churches and on <a href="/wiki/Month_of_Tobi" class="mw-redirect" title="Month of Tobi">Tobi</a>&#160;22 in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_calendar" title="Coptic calendar">Coptic calendar</a>.
</p><p>The biography of Anthony's life by <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> helped to spread the concept of Christian <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monasticism</a>, particularly in Western Europe via its <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> translations. He is often erroneously considered the first Christian monk, but as his biography and other sources make clear, there were many ascetics before him. Anthony was, however, among the first known to go into the wilderness (about AD&#160;270), which seems to have contributed to his renown.<sup id="cite_ref-Endsjo2008_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Endsjo2008-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Accounts of Anthony enduring supernatural temptation during his sojourn in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Desert" title="Eastern Desert">Eastern Desert</a> of Egypt inspired the depiction of <a href="/wiki/Temptation_of_Saint_Anthony_in_visual_arts" title="Temptation of Saint Anthony in visual arts">his temptations in visual art</a> and literature.
</p><p>Anthony is appealed to against infectious diseases, particularly skin diseases. In the past, many such afflictions, including <a href="/wiki/Ergotism" title="Ergotism">ergotism</a>, <a href="/wiki/Erysipelas" title="Erysipelas">erysipelas</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Herpes_zoster" class="mw-redirect" title="Herpes zoster">shingles</a>, were referred to as <i>Saint Anthony's fire</i>.
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="History"></span><span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_of_Anthony"><i>Life of Anthony</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life of Anthony">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Most of what is known about Anthony comes from the <i>Life of Anthony</i>. Written in Greek around 360 by <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a>, it depicts Anthony as an illiterate and holy man who through his existence in a primordial landscape has an absolute connection to the divine truth, which always is in harmony with that of Athanasius as the biographer.<sup id="cite_ref-Endsjo2008_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Endsjo2008-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A continuation of the genre of secular Greek biography,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> it became his most widely read work.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Sometime before 374 it was translated into Latin by <a href="/wiki/Evagrius_of_Antioch" title="Evagrius of Antioch">Evagrius of Antioch</a>. The Latin translation helped the <i>Life</i> become one of the best-known works of literature in the Christian world, a status it would hold through the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Translated into several languages, it became something of a "best seller" in its day and played an important role in the spreading of the <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> ideal in Eastern and Western Christianity. It later served as an inspiration to Christian <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monastics</a> in both the East and the West,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and helped to spread the concept of Christian monasticism, particularly in Western Europe via its Latin translations.
</p><p>Many stories are also told about Anthony in the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>.
</p><p>Anthony probably spoke only his native language, <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>, but his sayings were spread in a <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> translation. He himself dictated letters in Coptic, seven of which are extant.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Anthony was born in <a href="/wiki/Koma,_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="Koma, Egypt">Koma</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a> to wealthy landowner parents. When he was about 20 years old, his parents died and left him with the care of his unmarried sister. Shortly thereafter, he decided to follow the gospel exhortation in <a href="/wiki/Matthew_19" title="Matthew 19">Matthew 19</a>: 21, "If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasures in heaven."  Anthony gave away some of his family's lands to his neighbors, sold the remaining property, and donated the funds to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He then left to live an <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> life,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> placing his sister with a group of <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">Christian virgins</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hermit">Hermit</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hermit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>For the next fifteen years, Anthony remained in the area,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> spending the first years as the disciple of another local <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There are various legends that he worked as a <a href="/wiki/Swineherd" title="Swineherd">swineherd</a> during this period.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i>Temptation of Saint Anthony</i> (1878) by <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9licien_Rops" title="Félicien Rops">Félicien Rops</a>:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Anthony is sometimes considered the first monk,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  and the first to initiate solitary desertification,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> but there were others before him. There were already <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a> (the <i><a href="/wiki/Therapeutae" title="Therapeutae">Therapeutae</a></i>), and loosely organized <a href="/wiki/Cenobitic" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenobitic">cenobitic</a> communities were described by the <a href="/wiki/Jewish_philosophy" title="Jewish philosophy">Jewish philosopher</a> <a href="/wiki/Philo_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Philo of Alexandria">Philo of Alexandria</a> in the 1st century&#160;AD as long established in the harsh environment of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Mareotis" class="mw-redirect" title="Lake Mareotis">Lake Mareotis</a> and in other less accessible regions. Philo opined that "this class of persons may be met with in many places, for both Greece and barbarian countries want to enjoy whatever is perfectly good."<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Christian ascetics such as <a href="/wiki/Thecla" title="Thecla">Thecla</a> had likewise retreated to isolated locations at the outskirts of cities. Anthony is notable for having decided to surpass this tradition and headed out into the desert proper. He left for the alkaline <a href="/wiki/Nitrian_Desert" title="Nitrian Desert">Nitrian Desert</a> (later the location of the noted monasteries of <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kellia" title="Kellia">Kellia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Scetis" class="mw-redirect" title="Scetis">Scetis</a>) on the edge of the <a href="/wiki/Libyan_Desert" title="Libyan Desert">Western Desert</a> about 95&#160;km (59&#160;mi) west of <a href="/wiki/Early_centers_of_Christianity#Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Early centers of Christianity">Alexandria</a>. He remained there for 13 years.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Anthony maintained a very strict ascetic diet. He ate only bread, salt and water and never meat or wine.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> He ate at most only once a day and sometimes <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasted</a> through two or four days.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Athanasius" class="mw-redirect" title="Athanasius">Athanasius</a>, the devil fought Anthony by afflicting him with boredom, laziness, and the phantoms of women, which he overcame by the power of prayer, providing a theme for <a href="/wiki/Christian_art" title="Christian art">Christian art</a>. After that, he moved to one of the tombs near his native village. There it was that the <i>Life</i> records those strange conflicts with demons in the shape of wild beasts, who inflicted blows upon him, and sometimes left him nearly dead.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
After fifteen years of this life, at the age of thirty-five, Anthony determined to withdraw from the habitations of men and retire in absolute solitude. He went into the desert to a mountain by the <a href="/wiki/Nile" title="Nile">Nile</a> called <a href="/wiki/Dayr_al-Maym%C5%ABn" title="Dayr al-Maymūn">Pispir</a> (now Der-el-Memun), opposite <a href="/wiki/Faiyum" title="Faiyum">Arsinoë</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1911-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> There he lived strictly enclosed in an old abandoned <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> fort for some 20 years.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Food was thrown to him over the wall. He was at times visited by pilgrims, whom he refused to see; but gradually a number of would-be disciples established themselves in caves and in huts around the mountain. Thus, a colony of ascetics was formed, who begged Anthony to come forth and be their guide in the spiritual life. Eventually, he yielded to their importunities and, about the year 305, emerged from his retreat. To the surprise of all, he appeared to be not emaciated, but healthy in mind and body.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup></p><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:263px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg/261px-Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg" decoding="async" width="261" height="319" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg/392px-Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg/522px-Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2024" data-file-height="2476" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Piero_di_Cosimo_025.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting of Saint Anthony, a part of <i>The Visitation with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas" title="Saint Nicholas">Saint Nicholas</a> and Saint Anthony Abbot</i> by <a href="/wiki/Piero_di_Cosimo" title="Piero di Cosimo">Piero di Cosimo</a>, <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1480</span></div></div></div><p>For five or six years he devoted himself to the instruction and organization of the great body of monks that had grown up around him; but then he once again withdrew into the inner desert that lay between the Nile and the Red Sea, near the shore of which he fixed his abode on a mountain (<a href="/wiki/Mount_Colzim" title="Mount Colzim">Mount Colzim</a>) where still stands the monastery that bears his name, <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Saint_Anthony" title="Monastery of Saint Anthony">Der Mar Antonios</a>. Here he spent the last forty-five years of his life, in a seclusion, not so strict as Pispir, for he freely saw those who came to visit him, and he used to cross the desert to Pispir with considerable frequency. Amid the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_Persecutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian Persecutions">Diocletian Persecutions</a>, around 311 Anthony went to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> and was conspicuous visiting those who were imprisoned.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Father_of_Monks">Father of Monks</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Father of Monks">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:263px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitale_da_Bologna_(14e_eeuw)_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_(1340)_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg/261px-Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg" decoding="async" width="261" height="564" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg/392px-Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg/522px-Vitale_da_Bologna_%2814e_eeuw%29_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_%281340%29_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1429" data-file-height="3089" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitale_da_Bologna_(14e_eeuw)_-_Vier_verhalen_van_Antonius_van_Egypte_(1340)_-_Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_26-04-2012_9-22-59.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Four tales on Anthony the Great</i> by <a href="/wiki/Vitale_da_Bologna" title="Vitale da Bologna">Vitale da Bologna</a>, c. 1340, at the <a href="/wiki/Pinacoteca_Nazionale_di_Bologna" title="Pinacoteca Nazionale di Bologna">Pinacoteca Nazionale di Bologna</a></div></div></div>
<p>Anthony was not the first ascetic or hermit, but he may properly be called the "Father of Monasticism" in Christianity,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;EB&#39;&#39;1878-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> as he organized his disciples into a community and later, following the spread of Athanasius's hagiography, was the inspiration for similar communities throughout Egypt and elsewhere. <a href="/wiki/Macarius_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Macarius the Great">Macarius the Great</a> was a disciple of Anthony. Visitors traveled great distances to see the celebrated holy man. Anthony is said to have spoken to those of a spiritual disposition, leaving the task of addressing the more worldly visitors to Macarius. Macarius later founded a monastic community in the Scetic desert.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The fame of Anthony spread and reached <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I_(emperor)" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I (emperor)">Emperor Constantine</a>, who wrote to him requesting his prayers. The brethren were pleased with the Emperor's letter, but Anthony was not overawed and wrote back exhorting the Emperor and his sons not to esteem this world but remember the next.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The stories of the meeting of Anthony and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Thebes" title="Paul of Thebes">Paul of Thebes</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Raven" title="Raven">raven</a> who brought them bread, Anthony being sent to fetch the cloak given him by "Athanasius the bishop" to bury Paul's body in, and Paul's death before he returned, are among the familiar legends of the <i>Life</i>. However, belief in the existence of Paul seems to have existed quite independently of the <i>Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 338, he left the desert temporarily to visit Alexandria to help refute the teachings of <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_days">Final days</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Final days">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When Anthony sensed his death approaching, he commanded his disciples to give his staff to <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius of Egypt</a>, and to give one <a href="/wiki/Sheepskin_(material)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sheepskin (material)">sheepskin</a> cloak to <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> and the other sheepskin cloak to <a href="/wiki/Serapion_of_Nitria" title="Serapion of Nitria">Serapion of Thmuis</a>, his disciple.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Anthony was interred, according to his instructions, in a grave next to his cell.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Temptation">Temptation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Temptation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Temptation_of_Saint_Anthony_in_visual_arts" title="Temptation of Saint Anthony in visual arts">Temptation of Saint Anthony in visual arts</a></div>
<p>Accounts of Anthony enduring preternatural temptation during his sojourn in the Eastern Desert of Egypt inspired the often-repeated subject of the temptation of St. Anthony in Western art and literature.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anthony is said to have faced a series of preternatural <a href="/wiki/Temptation" title="Temptation">temptations</a> during his pilgrimage to the desert. The first to report on the temptation was his contemporary <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a>. It is possible these events, like the paintings, are full of rich metaphor or in the case of the animals of the desert, perhaps a vision or dream. Emphasis on these stories, however, did not really begin until the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> when the psychology of the individual became of greater interest.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some of the stories included in Anthony's biography are perpetuated now mostly in paintings, where they give an opportunity for artists to depict their more lurid or bizarre interpretations. Many artists, including <a href="/wiki/Martin_Schongauer" title="Martin Schongauer">Martin Schongauer</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hieronymus_Bosch" title="Hieronymus Bosch">Hieronymus Bosch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dorothea_Tanning" title="Dorothea Tanning">Dorothea Tanning</a>, <a href="/wiki/Max_Ernst" title="Max Ernst">Max Ernst</a>, <a href="/wiki/Leonora_Carrington" title="Leonora Carrington">Leonora Carrington</a> and <a href="/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD" title="Salvador Dalí">Salvador Dalí</a>, have depicted these incidents from the life of Anthony; in prose, the tale was retold and embellished by <a href="/wiki/Gustave_Flaubert" title="Gustave Flaubert">Gustave Flaubert</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Temptation_of_Saint_Anthony_(novel)" title="The Temptation of Saint Anthony (novel)">The Temptation of Saint Anthony</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_satyr_and_the_centaur">The satyr and the centaur</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: The satyr and the centaur">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:263px;"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul,_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg/261px-The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg" decoding="async" width="261" height="368" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg/392px-The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg/522px-The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul%2C_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2128" data-file-height="3000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Meeting_of_Saint_Anthony_and_Saint_Paul,_Master_of_the_Osservanza.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Meeting of Saint Anthony and Saint Paul of Thebes</i>, <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Osservanza" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of the Osservanza">Master of the Osservanza</a>, 15th century, with the centaur at the background.</div></div></div> 
<p>Anthony was on a journey in the desert to find <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Thebes" title="Paul of Thebes">Paul of Thebes</a>, who according to his dream was a better Hermit than he.<sup id="cite_ref-Jero_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jero-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Anthony had been under the impression that he was the first person to ever dwell in the desert; however, due to the dream, Anthony was called into the desert to find his "better", Paul. On his way there, he ran into two creatures in the forms of a <a href="/wiki/Centaur" title="Centaur">centaur</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Satyr" title="Satyr">satyr</a>. Although chroniclers sometimes postulated that they might have been living beings, Western theology considers them to have been <a href="/wiki/Demon" title="Demon">demons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Jero_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jero-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While traveling through the desert, Anthony first found the centaur, a "creature of mingled shape, half horse half-man," whom he asked about directions. The creature tried to speak in an unintelligible language, but ultimately pointed with his hand the way desired, and then ran away and vanished from sight.<sup id="cite_ref-Jero_29-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jero-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> It was interpreted as a demon trying to terrify him, or alternately a creature engendered by the desert.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anthony found next the satyr, "a manikin with hooked snout, horned forehead, and extremities like goats's feet." This creature was peaceful and offered him fruits, and when Anthony asked who he was, the satyr replied, "I'm a mortal being and one of those inhabitants of the desert whom the Gentiles, deluded by various forms of error, worship under the names of <a href="/wiki/Fauns" class="mw-redirect" title="Fauns">Fauns</a>, <a href="/wiki/Satyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Satyrs">Satyrs</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Incubi" class="mw-redirect" title="Incubi">Incubi</a>. I am sent to represent my tribe. We pray you in our behalf to entreat the favor of your Lord and ours, who, we have learnt, came once to save the world, and 'whose sound has gone forth into all the earth.'" Upon hearing this, Anthony was overjoyed and rejoiced over the glory of Christ. He condemned the city of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> for worshipping monsters instead of God while beasts like the satyr spoke about Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-Jero_29-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jero-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Silver_and_gold">Silver and gold</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Silver and gold">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Another time Anthony was travelling in the desert and found a plate of silver coins in his path.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Demons_in_the_cave">Demons in the cave</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Demons in the cave">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Once, Anthony tried to hide in a cave to escape the demons that plagued him. There were so many little demons in the cave though, that Anthony's servant had to carry him out because they had beaten him to death. When the hermits were gathered to Anthony's corpse to mourn his death, Anthony was revived. He demanded that his servants take him back to that cave where the demons had beaten him. When he got there he called out to the demons, and they came back as wild beasts to rip him to shreds. All of a sudden a bright light flashed, and the demons ran away. Anthony knew that the light must have come from God, and he asked God where he was before when the demons attacked him. God replied, "I was here but I would see and abide to see thy battle, and because thou hast mainly fought and well maintained thy battle, I shall make thy name to be spread through all the world."<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_the_Great&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg/220px-Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="129" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg/330px-Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg/440px-Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="294" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vaantjeantoniuswarfhuizenkleiner_copy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pilgrimage banners from the shrine in <a href="/wiki/Warfhuizen" title="Warfhuizen">Warfhuizen</a></div></div></div>
<p>Anthony had been secretly buried on the mountain-top where he had chosen to live. His remains were reportedly discovered in 361, and transferred to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>. Some time later, they were taken from Alexandria to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, so that they might escape the destruction being perpetrated by invading <a href="/wiki/Saracen" title="Saracen">Saracens</a>. In the eleventh century, the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> gave them to the <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">Count</a> Jocelin. Jocelin had them transferred to La-Motte-Saint-Didier, later renamed.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There, Jocelin undertook to build a church to house the remains, but died before the church was even started. The building was finally erected in 1297 and became a centre of veneration and pilgrimage, known as <a href="/wiki/Saint-Antoine-l%27Abbaye" title="Saint-Antoine-l&#39;Abbaye">Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye</a>.
</p><p>Anthony is credited with assisting in a number of miraculous healings, primarily from <a href="/wiki/Ergotism" title="Ergotism">ergotism</a>, which became known as "St. Anthony's Fire". He was credited by two local noblemen of assisting them in recovery from the disease. They then founded the <a href="/wiki/Hospital_Brothers_of_St._Anthony" class="mw-redirect" title="Hospital Brothers of St. Anthony">Hospital Brothers of St. Anthony</a> in honor of him, who specialized in nursing the victims of skin diseases.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is venerated especially by the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Paul_the_First_Hermit" title="Order of Saint Paul the First Hermit">Order of Saint Paul the First Hermit</a> for his close association with St. <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Thebes" title="Paul of Thebes">Paul of Thebes</a> after whom they take their name.  In the <i>Life of St. Paul the First Hermit</i>, by St. Jerome, it is recorded that it was St. Anthony that found St. Paul towards the end of his life and without whom it is doubtful he would be known.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg/250px-SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="141" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg/375px-SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg/500px-SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2816" data-file-height="1593" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SAntoine_Abbaye_01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Saint-Antoine-l%27Abbaye" title="Saint-Antoine-l&#39;Abbaye">Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye</a>, <a href="/wiki/Is%C3%A8re" title="Isère">Isère</a>, France</div></div></div>
<p>Veneration of Anthony in the East is more restrained. There are comparatively few icons and paintings of him. He is, however, regarded as the "first master of the desert and the pinnacle of holy monks", and there are monastic communities of the Maronite, Chaldean, and Orthodox churches which state that they follow his monastic rule.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, Anthony, along with <a href="/wiki/Quirinus_of_Neuss" title="Quirinus of Neuss">Quirinus of Neuss</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Cornelius" title="Pope Cornelius">Cornelius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hubertus" title="Hubertus">Hubertus</a>, was venerated as one of the <a href="/wiki/Four_Holy_Marshals" title="Four Holy Marshals">Four Holy Marshals</a> (<i>Vier Marschälle Gottes</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland" title="Rhineland">Rhineland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anthony is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/January_17" title="January 17">17 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though Anthony himself did not organize or create a monastery, a community grew around him based on his example of living an <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> and isolated life. Athanasius' biography helped propagate Anthony's ideals. Athanasius writes, "For monks, the life of Anthony is a sufficient example of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ButlerLives1991_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ButlerLives1991-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
His story influenced the conversion of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a><sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Examples of purely <a href="/wiki/Coptic_literature" title="Coptic literature">Coptic literature</a> are the works of Anthony and <a href="/wiki/Pachomius" class="mw-redirect" title="Pachomius">Pachomius</a>, who only spoke Coptic, and the sermons and preachings of <a href="/wiki/Shenoute" title="Shenoute">Shenouda the Archmandrite</a>, who chose to only write in Coptic. The earliest original writings in <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic language</a> were the letters by Anthony. During the 3rd and 4th centuries many ecclesiastics and monks wrote in Coptic.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
