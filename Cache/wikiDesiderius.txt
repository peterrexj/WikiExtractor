<p><b>Desiderius</b>, also known as <b>Daufer</b> or <b>Dauferius</b> (born <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;720</span> – died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;786</span>), was king of the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, ruling from 756 to 774. The Frankish king of renown, <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, married Desiderius's daughter and subsequently conquered his realm. Desiderius is remembered for this connection to Charlemagne and for being the last Lombard ruler to exercise regional kingship.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Rise_to_power">Rise to power</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Desiderius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Rise to power">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>, Desiderius was originally a royal officer, the <i>dux</i> of <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscia</a> and he became king after the death of Aistulf in 756.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChristie1998105_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChristie1998105-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> At that time, Aistulf's predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Ratchis" title="Ratchis">Ratchis</a>, left his monastic retreat of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Montecassino</a> and tried to seize the kingdom, but Desiderius put his revolt down quickly with the support of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Pope Stephen II</a>. At his coronation, Desiderius promised to restore many lost papal towns to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> and even enlarge the Papal State.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERiché199373_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERiché199373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 757, Desiderius began securing his power, taking what historian Walter Goffart terms, "vigorous steps to suppress resistance to himself in the powerful duchies of <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>, in central Italy, and Benevento, to the south."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When the upstart, Liutprand <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento" title="Duchy of Benevento">Duke of Benevento</a>, who despised Desiderius, challenged his kingship and threatened to place himself under <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pippin's</a> protection, Desiderius obtained naval assistance from Byzantium and put an end to Luitprand's defiant actions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Lombard king then granted Luitprand's former duchy to his foe's son, <a href="/wiki/Arechis_II_of_Benevento" title="Arechis II of Benevento">Arechis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoffart2012338-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In that same year, Desiderius deposed <a href="/wiki/Alboin_of_Spoleto" title="Alboin of Spoleto">Alboin of Spoleto</a> and exercised the ducal powers there himself.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENoble1984107_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENoble1984107-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Seeking, like his predecessors, to extend the Lombard power in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, he came into collision with the papacy and the southern duchies. In the August of 759, Desiderius made his son <a href="/wiki/Adelchis" class="mw-redirect" title="Adelchis">Adelchis</a> associate King of Lombardy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlunsom2013158_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlunsom2013158-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Shortly after visiting Rome, where he prayed at the tomb of St. Peter, Desiderius "returned to the aggressive expansive policy of his predecessors."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003141_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003141-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He even negotiated with Byzantium in an arrangement that would have eroded papal authority and resulted in further territorial loss for the Holy See in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003141–142_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003141–142-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Sometime in 760, envoys from Pippin convinced Desiderius to return some of the cities he captured back to the papacy, but the Lombard king did not follow through on his promises.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003142_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003142-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointing_Antipope_Philip">Appointing Antipope Philip</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Desiderius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Appointing Antipope Philip">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Intervening in the crisis that ensued after the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_I" title="Pope Paul I">Pope Paul I</a> in 767, Desiderius seized a priest named Philip from the Monastery of <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Vitus">St. Vitus</a><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> on the <a href="/wiki/Esquiline_Hill" title="Esquiline Hill">Esquiline Hill</a> in Rome on Sunday, July 31, 768, and summarily appointed him pope. <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Philip" title="Antipope Philip">Antipope Philip</a> was not recognized and failed to gain a significant following, so he left the same day and returned to his monastery, where he was never heard from or seen again.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEReardon201559_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReardon201559-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carolingian_Intervention">Carolingian Intervention</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Desiderius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Carolingian Intervention">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While Pippin’s previous military campaigns in Italy against Desiderius' Lombard predecessors had been successful, the subsequent relations between the papacy (aligned with the <a href="/wiki/Carolingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingians">Carolingians</a>) and the Lombards were correspondingly strained and in 773, Pope Hadrian openly broke with king Desiderius.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKoenigsberger198785_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoenigsberger198785-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> When Desiderius responded by moving against the papal cities, Hadrian immediately appealed to Charlemagne for help against the Lombards.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKoenigsberger198785_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoenigsberger198785-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Since the Lombards had blocked the passage through the Alps, Pope Hadrian had to send his embassy by sea; they were commissioned to remind Charlemagne that he was the protector of the papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERiché199396–97_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERiché199396–97-14">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Originally, Charlemagne had remained on amicable terms with the Lombards, having been married to Desiderius’ daughter, Desiderata.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFried201543_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFried201543-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> Despite not liking the alliance between Lombards and Franks, Stephen III grudgingly maintained positive diplomatic standing with both kings, but his death in February 772 and the elevation of Pope Hadrian, who wished to undermine this relationship, altered the political environment.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017357–358_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017357–358-18">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Hadrian hedged his bets and took measures to provoke Desiderius; actions designed to make him take an aggressive stance against the Holy See so an appeal could be made for Frankish assistance.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017358–359_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017358–359-19">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Upon hearing the call for help, Charlemagne obliged the Holy See.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames199594–95_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames199594–95-20">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Carloman_I" title="Carloman I">Carloman's</a> death also changed the situation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFried201543_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFried201543-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> It seems the widow of Charlemagne’s brother (Carloman) and her children had taken refuge with Desiderius, who—so it was alleged in the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>—intended to proclaim a Frankish successor. According to historian Roger Collins, the veracity of this claim can be questioned as a possible piece of disinformation from the papacy "intended to ensure the Frankish king’s help against the Lombards."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECollins1999282_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECollins1999282-23">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the spring of 773, Charlemagne sent two Frankish armies against the Lombards and after an eight month siege, captured the capital of Pavia and Desiderius himself.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017359_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017359-24">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;f&#93;</a></sup>  Charlemagne subsequently exiled the Lombard king to the abbey of Corbie in northern France,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERiché199398_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERiché199398-26">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> and as “king of the Franks”, added the title “and of the Lombards”, lengthening his moniker.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames199595_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames199595-27">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> When Charlemagne took the title <i>rex Langobardorum</i>, it marked the first time a Germanic king adopted the title of a kingdom he had conquered.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDavis2015412_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavis2015412-28">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Although Charlemagne had the power to destroy the Lombards outright, he instead permitted them to "retain their laws and pardoned those who were traitors."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERiché199398_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERiché199398-26">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In the end, Desiderius's ambitions brought about the end of the Lombard kingdom and he was the final Lombard king on record.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003143_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003143-29">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Desiderius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Today, the legacy of Desiderius still has significance in Italy. The beautiful monastic church of <a href="/wiki/San_Salvatore,_Brescia" title="San Salvatore, Brescia">San Salvatore in Brescia</a>, a testament to Lombard architecture still enduring, was constructed by Desiderius.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick2001192_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick2001192-30">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> His name in Italian--"Desiderio"--directly translates to "<a href="/wiki/Desire" title="Desire">desire</a>" in English. In the tragedy <i><a href="/wiki/Adelchi" title="Adelchi">Adelchi</a></i>, written by renowned Italian novelist and Poet <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni" title="Alessandro Manzoni">Alessandro Manzoni</a> in 1822, Desiderius is portrayed as vain man, destroying his kingdom and legacy over his desire for power. His son Adelchi (also called <a href="/wiki/Adalgis" title="Adalgis">Adalgis</a>) is torn over his father's will and his desire for peace, and dies of starvation. In the play, the author expresses regret that Desiderius and Lombards failed to unite the Italian peninsula.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis2009235_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis2009235-31">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;g&#93;</a></sup>
<a href="/wiki/Dante_Alighieri" title="Dante Alighieri">Dante Alighieri</a> in Paradiso VI (line 95) refers to Desiderius as the "Lombard tooth" (or "snake") who bit the Holy Church and was subsequently defeated by Charlemagne.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlighieri200761_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlighieri200761-33">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li id="cite_note-3"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-3">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">French historian Pierre Riché claims that Aistulf's death was providential for it "allowed the pope to influence the royal election in Pavia" in favor of Desiderius.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERiché199373_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERiché199373-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-7"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-7">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Adelchis and Desiderius co-ruled the region until they were deposed in June 774.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlunsom2013158_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlunsom2013158-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-11"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-11">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Gregory the Great</a> mentions a monastery dedicated to Vitus, located somewhere in or near Sicily ("Epist.", I, xlviii, P.L., LXXXVII, 511).</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-17"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-17">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Moreover, Desiderius—in concert with Charlemagne—wanted to ensure there was more than one Carolingian king involved with the papacy and with access to Italy, since the title of <i>patricius Romanorum</i> was shared by both Carloman and Charlemagne (Carloman also controlled Frankish access through the Alps).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017357_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBachrachBachrach2017357-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-22"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-22">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">According to the <i>Annales regni francorum</i>, this legate from the Pope to Charlemagne arrived in 773.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick200828_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick200828-21">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-25"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-25">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The renowned Frankish king also took the city of Pavia, where Desiderius' son Adelchis had taken residence with Carloman's remaining family.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003142_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrassetto2003142-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>

</li>
</ol></div></div>
