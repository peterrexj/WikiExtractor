<p>Saint <b>Wendelin of Trier</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vendelinus</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 554 - <span title="circa">c.</span> 617 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. Although not listed in the Roman Martyrology, his cultus is wide-spread in German-speaking areas. He is a patron of country folk and herdsmen. He is honored on October 22.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wendelin_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Because no information about him was available, a biography was written in the Middle Ages, which is based purely on legendary data and tells not so much the life of Saint Wendelinus, but rather how the medieval man of that time imagined the life of a holy hermit from earlier times.<sup id="cite_ref-Akker_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akker-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
There is very little definite information about this <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>; his earliest biographies (two in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and two in <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>), did not appear until after 1417. The name "Wendelin" means "wanderer" or "pilgrim" in <a href="/wiki/Old_High_German" title="Old High German">Old High German</a>.  The biographies state that Wendelin was the son of a Scottish king who led a pious life as a youth before leaving his home in secret to make a pilgrimage to Rome. On his way back he settled as a hermit at Westrich in the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Trier">Diocese of Trier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Weninger_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weninger-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One day when he went to Treves to perform his devotions, and begged his bread from house to house, a nobleman who saw him, and reproved him with great vehemence, saying that begging was the result of laziness and a disorderly life. Wendelin entered his service as a herdsman, but a miracle led the landowner to allow him to return to his solitude.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wendelin then established a company of hermits from which sprang the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Tholey_Abbey" title="Tholey Abbey">Abbey of Tholey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Saarland" title="Saarland">Saarland</a>. It is not clear whether <a href="/wiki/Tholey" title="Tholey">Tholey</a> arose from his hermitage, or whether he left his hermitage to become abbot in Tholey.<sup id="cite_ref-Akker_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akker-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> abbot in approximately 597, according to the later legends, while Tholey was apparently founded as a collegiate body about 630. It is difficult to say how far the later biographers are trustworthy.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wendelin_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG/220px-Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG/330px-Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG/440px-Niedermorschwihr_StGall_21.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="5472" /></a><figcaption>Saint-Wendelin , Église Saint-Gall, Niedermorschwihr StGall</figcaption></figure>
<p>The story is told that when Wendelin was working as a herdsman he often took his flock to a mountain to pray there in silence. The sheep thrived under his care, and jealous servants reported that he drove the sheep to a field so far distant that they were exhausted when they returned to the fold. The master coming upon him there, was angry because he could not imagine that Wendelin had time to get the flock home before sunset. However, when the master arrived home on horseback he discovered the shepherd and his flock already there. Realizing that this was a miracle from God he granted Wendelin his greatest desire and built him his own hermit cell in the vicinity of the farm.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wendelin_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wendelin was buried in his cell, and a chapel was built over the grave and the small town of <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Wendel" title="Sankt Wendel">Sankt Wendel</a> grew up nearby. The saint's intercession was considered powerful in times of pestilence and contagious diseases among cattle. When in 1320 a pestilence was checked through the intercession of the saint, <a href="/wiki/Baldwin,_Archbishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Baldwin, Archbishop of Trier">Baldwin, Archbishop of Trier</a> had the chapel rebuilt. Baldwin's successor, <a href="/wiki/Bohemond_II,_Archbishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bohemond II, Archbishop of Trier">Bohemond II</a>, built the present beautiful <a href="/wiki/Gothic_(architecture)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gothic (architecture)">Gothic</a> church, dedicated in 1360, to which the saint's <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were transferred. Since 1506 they have rested in a stone <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> People make a pilgrimage to Wendalinusbasilica on St. Wendelin's day in October.
</p><p>Wendelin is the patron saint of country people and herdsmen<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and is still venerated in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South Africa</a>. Saint Wendelin is not mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, but his feast is observed in the Diocese of Trier on 22 October.
</p><p>Sacred Heart Church in <a href="/wiki/Perkinsville,_New_York" title="Perkinsville, New York">Perkinsville, New York</a> holds a relic of Wendelin. In 2017, a German film crew visited the hamlet while making a documentary about the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wendelin_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1132942124">.mw-parser-output .portalbox{padding:0;margin:0.5em 0;display:table;box-sizing:border-box;max-width:175px;list-style:none}.mw-parser-output .portalborder{border:solid #aaa 1px;padding:0.1em;background:#f9f9f9}.mw-parser-output .portalbox-entry{display:table-row;font-size:85%;line-height:110%;height:1.9em;font-style:italic;font-weight:bold}.mw-parser-output .portalbox-image{display:table-cell;padding:0.2em;vertical-align:middle;text-align:center}.mw-parser-output .portalbox-link{display:table-cell;padding:0.2em 0.2em 0.2em 0.3em;vertical-align:middle}@media(min-width:720px){.mw-parser-output .portalleft{clear:left;float:left;margin:0.5em 1em 0.5em 0}.mw-parser-output .portalright{clear:right;float:right;margin:0.5em 0 0.5em 1em}}</style><ul role="navigation" aria-label="Portals" class="noprint portalbox portalborder portalright">
<li class="portalbox-entry"><span class="portalbox-image"><span class="noviewer" typeof="mw:File"><span><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Gloriole_blur.svg/32px-Gloriole_blur.svg.png" decoding="async" width="32" height="28" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Gloriole_blur.svg/48px-Gloriole_blur.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Gloriole_blur.svg/64px-Gloriole_blur.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="32" data-file-height="28" /></span></span></span><span class="portalbox-link"><a href="/wiki/Portal:Saints" title="Portal:Saints">Saints portal</a></span></li></ul>
<p>He is represented in art as either a youth or a bearded man, with a shepherd's bag and a book in one hand and a shepherd's crook in the other. He is often pictured with feeding lambs, cattle, and swine, while a crown and a shield appear at his feet.
