<p><b>Saint Ansovinus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sant'Ansovino</i>) (d. 816)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Camerino" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Camerino">bishop of Camerino</a>, and is the patron saint of  agriculture. His feast day is March 13.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansovinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was educated at the cathedral school of Pavia.  After ordination to the priesthood, he became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at Castel Raimondo near <a href="/wiki/Torcello" title="Torcello">Torcello</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before being elected bishop of Camerino, he served as confessor to <a href="/wiki/Louis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis II, Holy Roman Emperor">Louis II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>.  Ansovinus refused to accept the  office of bishop until Louis was agreed that his see be kept exempt from the conscription of the locals into the soldiery.  During this time, bishops were often required to be responsible for recruiting men for the imperial army.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was consecrated at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a>, and returned to this city for the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Rome" title="Council of Rome">Council of Rome</a> held by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> in 861, where records show he signed as <i>Ansuinus Camerinensis</i>.  His episcopate was characterized by his generosity to the poor and his pacification of the city's various factions.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_(detail)_-_WGA5794.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg/220px-Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="266" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg/330px-Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg/440px-Carlo_Crivelli_-_St_Jerome_and_St_Augustine_%28detail%29_-_WGA5794.jpg 2x" data-file-width="911" data-file-height="1100" /></a><figcaption>St Ansovinus and St Jerome(detail), Crivelli</figcaption></figure>
<p>The gift of multiplying food was attributed to Ansovinus. He fed thousands of starving people from the regional granary at Castel Raimondo, but the grain never ran out.<sup id="cite_ref-Realy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansovinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast day is March 13.  The <a href="/wiki/Camerino_Cathedral" title="Camerino Cathedral">cathedral at Camerino</a> includes the marble medieval <a href="/wiki/Arch" title="Arch">arch</a> of Sant'Ansovino.  A monumental <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> erected around 1390 holds his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His festival was once celebrated by Camerino and the nobles of other castles in the region.  The church of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Venanzio_e_Ansovino" title="Santi Venanzio e Ansovino">Santi Venanzio e Ansovino</a> at Rome was dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Additionally, there are rural churches dedicated to him at Avacelli, Casenove, <a href="/wiki/Bevagna" title="Bevagna">Bevagna</a>, and Monsammartino.
</p><p>Ansovinus is the patron saint of small farmers.<sup id="cite_ref-Realy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
