<p><b>Abel</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> is a Biblical figure in the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Genesis" title="Book of Genesis">Book of Genesis</a> within <a href="/wiki/Abrahamic_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Abrahamic religion">Abrahamic religions</a>. He was the younger brother of <a href="/wiki/Cain" title="Cain">Cain</a>, and the younger son of <a href="/wiki/Adam" title="Adam">Adam</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eve" title="Eve">Eve</a>, the first couple in Biblical history.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd" title="Shepherd">shepherd</a> who offered his firstborn flock up to God as an offering. God accepted his offering but not his brother's. Cain then killed Abel out of jealousy.
</p><p>According to Genesis, this was the first murder in the history of mankind.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Interpretations">Interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jewish_and_Christian_interpretations">Jewish and Christian interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Jewish and Christian interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to the narrative in <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Genesis" title="Book of Genesis">Genesis</a>, <b>Abel</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: <span lang="he" dir="rtl">הֶבֶל</span> <i>Hébel</i>, in <a href="/wiki/Pausa" title="Pausa">pausa</a> <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1020748213">.mw-parser-output .script-hebrew,.mw-parser-output .script-Hebr{font-family:"SBL Hebrew","SBL BibLit","Taamey Ashkenaz","Taamey Frank CLM","Frank Ruehl CLM","Ezra SIL","Ezra SIL SR","Keter Aram Tsova","Taamey David CLM","Keter YG","Shofar","David CLM","Hadasim CLM","Simple CLM","Nachlieli",Cardo,Alef,"Noto Serif Hebrew","Noto Sans Hebrew","David Libre",David,"Times New Roman",Gisha,Arial,FreeSerif,FreeSans}</style><span class="script-hebrew" style="font-size: 115%;" dir="rtl">הָבֶל</span>&#8206; <i>Hā́ḇel</i>; <a href="/wiki/Biblical_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Biblical Greek language">Biblical Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἅβελ</span> <i>Hábel</i>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%84" class="extiw" title="wikt:هابيل">هابيل</a></span>, <i>Hābēl</i>) is Eve's second son. His name in Hebrew is composed of the same three consonants as a <a href="/wiki/Semitic_root" title="Semitic root">root</a> meaning "breath". <a href="/wiki/Julius_Wellhausen" title="Julius Wellhausen">Julius Wellhausen</a> has proposed that the name is independent of the root.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Eberhard_Schrader" title="Eberhard Schrader">Eberhard Schrader</a> had previously put forward the <a href="/wiki/Akkadian_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Akkadian (language)">Akkadian</a> (Old Assyrian dialect) <i>ablu</i> ("son") as a more likely etymology.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/220px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="176" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/330px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/440px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3300" data-file-height="2634" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i><a href="/wiki/The_First_Mourning" title="The First Mourning">The First Mourning</a></i> (Adam and Eve mourn the death of Abel); oil on canvas 1888 painting by <a href="/wiki/William-Adolphe_Bouguereau" title="William-Adolphe Bouguereau">William-Adolphe Bouguereau</a></div></div></div>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, comparisons are sometimes made between the death of Abel and that of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>, the former thus seen as being the first martyr. In <span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+23%3A35&amp;version=NRSV">Matthew 23:35</a></span> Jesus speaks of Abel as "righteous", and the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews" title="Epistle to the Hebrews">Epistle to the Hebrews</a> states that "The blood of sprinkling ... [speaks] better things than that of Abel" (<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+12%3A24&amp;version=NRSV">Hebrews 12:24</a></span>). The blood of Jesus is interpreted as bringing mercy; but that of Abel as demanding vengeance (hence the curse and mark).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Abel is invoked in the <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litany</a> for the dying in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, and his sacrifice is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a> along with those of <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a> and <a href="/wiki/Melchizedek" title="Melchizedek">Melchizedek</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Alexandrian_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandrian Rite">Alexandrian Rite</a> commemorates him with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on December 28.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the Coptic <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Adam_and_Eve" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of Adam and Eve">Book of Adam and Eve</a> (at 2:1–15), and the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> <a href="/wiki/Cave_of_Treasures" title="Cave of Treasures">Cave of Treasures</a>, Abel's body, after many days of mourning, was placed in the <i>Cave of Treasures</i>, before which Adam and Eve, and descendants, offered their prayers. In addition, the <a href="/wiki/Seth" title="Seth">Sethite</a> line of the <a href="/wiki/Generations_of_Adam" title="Generations of Adam">Generations of Adam</a> swear by Abel's blood to segregate themselves from the <i>unrighteous</i>.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Enoch" title="Book of Enoch">Book of Enoch</a> (22:7), regarded by most Christian and Jewish traditions as extra-biblical, the soul of Abel is described as having been appointed as the chief of martyrs, crying for vengeance, for the destruction of the seed of Cain. A similar view is later shown in the <a href="/wiki/Testament_of_Abraham" title="Testament of Abraham">Testament of Abraham</a> (A:13 / B:11), where Abel has been raised to the position as the judge of the souls.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Bereshit_Rabbah" class="mw-redirect" title="Bereshit Rabbah">Bereshit Rabbah</a> (22:2), a discussion of Gen. 4:1 ff. has Rabbi <a href="/wiki/Joshua_ben_Karha" title="Joshua ben Karha">Yehoshua ben Korcha</a> mentioning that Cain was born with a twin sister, and Abel with two twin sisters.  This is based on the principle that the otherwise superfluous accusative article "et" always conveys some additional teaching (<a href="/wiki/Pesachim" title="Pesachim">Pesachim</a> 22b). The "et"'s are parsed slightly differently in <a href="/wiki/Yebamot" class="mw-redirect" title="Yebamot">Yebamot</a> 62a where the two "et"'s in Gen. 4:2 indicate Cain and his sister, and Abel and his (one) sister.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sethian_Gnostic_interpretation">Sethian Gnostic interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sethian Gnostic interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Apocryphon_of_John" title="Apocryphon of John">Apocryphon of John</a>, a work belonging to <a href="/wiki/Sethianism" title="Sethianism">Sethian</a> <a href="/wiki/Gnosticism" title="Gnosticism">Gnosticism</a>, Abel is the offspring of <a href="/wiki/Demiurge#Yaldaboath" title="Demiurge">Yaldaboath</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eve" title="Eve">Eve</a>, who is placed over the elements of water and earth as <a href="/wiki/Elohim" title="Elohim">Elohim</a>, but was only given his name as a form of deception.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mandaean_interpretation"><span class="anchor" id="Hibil"></span>Mandaean interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Mandaean interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Hibil" title="Hibil">Hibil</a></div>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Mandaean" class="mw-redirect" title="Mandaean">Mandaean</a> beliefs and scriptures including the <a href="/wiki/Qolusta" class="mw-redirect" title="Qolusta">Qolastā</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Dra%C5%A1a_D-Iahia" class="mw-redirect" title="Draša D-Iahia">Book of John</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ginza_Rba" class="mw-redirect" title="Ginza Rba">Genzā Rabbā</a>, Abel is cognate with the angelic <a href="/wiki/Soteriology" title="Soteriology">soteriological</a> figure <b>Hibil Ziwa</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Mandaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Mandaic language">Classical Mandaic</a>: <span lang="myz" dir="rtl">ࡄࡉࡁࡉࡋ ࡆࡉࡅࡀ‎</span>, sometimes translated "Splendid Hibel"),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> who is spoken of as a son of <a href="/wiki/Hayyi_Rabbi" title="Hayyi Rabbi">Hayyi</a><sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p78_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p78-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> or of <a href="/wiki/Manda_d-Hayyi" title="Manda d-Hayyi">Manda d-Hayyi</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-gloss_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-gloss-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p83_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p83-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and as a brother to <a href="/wiki/Enos_(biblical_figure)#In_Mandaeism" title="Enos (biblical figure)">Anush (Enosh)</a> and to <a href="/wiki/Seth#Mandaeism" title="Seth">Sheetil (Seth)</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> who is the son of <a href="/wiki/Adam" title="Adam">Adam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-l1-1_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-l1-1-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Elsewhere, Anush is spoken of as the son of Sheetil, and Sheetil as the son of Hibil, where Hibil came to Adam and Eve as a young boy when they were still virgins, but was called their son.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r12_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r12-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Hibil is an important <a href="/wiki/Lightworld" class="mw-redirect" title="Lightworld">lightworld</a> being (<a href="/wiki/Uthra" title="Uthra">uthra</a>) who conquered the <a href="/wiki/World_of_Darkness_(Mandaeism)" title="World of Darkness (Mandaeism)">World of Darkness</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-gloss_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-gloss-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> As <b>Yawar Hibil</b>, he is one of multiple figures known as <b><a href="/wiki/Yawar_Ziwa" title="Yawar Ziwa">Yawar</a></b> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Mandaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Mandaic language">Classical Mandaic</a>: <span lang="myz" dir="rtl">ࡉࡀࡅࡀࡓ</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Literal_translation" title="Literal translation">lit.</a>&#8201;</small>&#39;Helper&#39;), being so named by and after his father.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Islamic_interpretation">Islamic interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Islamic interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Shia_Islam" title="Shia Islam">Shi'a Muslim</a> belief, Abel (<i>"Habeel"</i>) is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque" title="Nabi Habeel Mosque">Nabi Habeel Mosque</a>, located on the west mountains of <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>, near the <a href="/wiki/Zabadani" class="mw-redirect" title="Zabadani">Zabadani</a> Valley, overlooking the villages of the <a href="/wiki/Barada" title="Barada">Barada</a> river (Wadi Barada), in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>. Shi'a are frequent visitors of this mosque for <a href="/wiki/Ziyarat" title="Ziyarat">ziyarat</a>. The mosque was built by <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> <a href="/wiki/Wali" title="Wali">Wali</a> Ahmad Pasha in 1599.
</p>
