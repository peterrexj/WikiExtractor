<p><b>Abel</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> is a Biblical figure in the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Genesis" title="Book of Genesis">Book of Genesis</a> within <a href="/wiki/Abrahamic_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Abrahamic religion">Abrahamic religions</a>. He was the younger brother of <a href="/wiki/Cain" title="Cain">Cain</a>, and the younger son of <a href="/wiki/Adam" title="Adam">Adam</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eve" title="Eve">Eve</a>, the first couple in Biblical history.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd" title="Shepherd">shepherd</a> who offered his firstborn flock up to God as an offering. God accepted his offering but not his brother's. Cain then killed Abel out of jealousy. 
</p><p>According to Genesis, this was the first murder in the history of mankind.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_death">Life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="excerpt-block"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066933788">.mw-parser-output .excerpt-hat .mw-editsection-like{font-style:normal}</style><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable dablink excerpt-hat selfref">This section is an excerpt from <a href="/wiki/Cain_and_Abel#Genesis_narrative" title="Cain and Abel">Cain and Abel § Genesis narrative</a>.<span class="mw-editsection-like plainlinks"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cain_and_Abel&amp;action=edit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div><div class="excerpt">
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg/220px-Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="281" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg/330px-Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg/440px-Cain_leadeth_abel_to_death_tissot.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2345" data-file-height="3000" /></a><figcaption><i>Cain leadeth Abel to death</i>, by <a href="/wiki/James_Tissot" title="James Tissot">James Tissot</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1900</span></figcaption></figure>
The story of <a href="/wiki/Cain" title="Cain">Cain</a>'s murder of Abel and its consequences is told in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Genesis%204:1–18&amp;version=nrsv">Genesis 4:1–18</a>:<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> And the human knew Eve his woman and she conceived and bore Cain, and she said, "I have got me a man with the Lord."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> And she bore as well his brother Abel, and Abel became a herder of sheep while Cain was a tiller of the soil. And it happened in the course of time that Cain brought from the fruit of the soil an offering to the Lord. And Abel too had brought from the choice firstlings of his flock, and the Lord regarded Abel and his offering but did not regard Cain and his offering. And Cain was very incensed, and his face fell. And the Lord said to Cain,
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>"Why are you incensed,<br />
and why is your face fallen?<br />
For whether you offer well,<br />
or whether you do not,<br />
at the tent flap sin crouches<br />
and for you is its longing,<br />
but you will rule over it."
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<p><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> And Cain said to Abel his brother, "Let us go out to the field," and when they were in the field Cain rose against Abel his brother and killed him. <sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> And the Lord said to Cain, "Where is Abel your brother? And he said, "I do not know: am I my brother's keeper?" <sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> And He said, "What have you done? Listen! Your brother's blood cries out to me from the soil. And so, cursed shall you be by the soil that gaped with its mouth to take your brother's blood from your hand. If you till the soil, it will no longer give you strength. A restless wanderer shall you be on the earth." And Cain said to the Lord, "My punishment is too great to bear. Now that You have driven me this day from the soil I must hide from Your presence, I shall be a restless wanderer on the earth and whoever finds me will kill me." And the Lord said to him, "Therefore whoever kills Cain shall suffer sevenfold vengeance." And the Lord set a mark upon Cain so that whoever found him would not slay him.
</p>
<p><br />And Cain went out from the Lord's presence and dwelled in the land of Nod east of Eden. And Cain knew his wife and she conceived and bore Enoch. Then he became the builder of a city and he called the name of the city like his son's name, Enoch.</p></blockquote></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Interpretations">Interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jewish_and_Christian_interpretations">Jewish and Christian interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Jewish and Christian interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to the narrative in <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Genesis" title="Book of Genesis">Genesis</a>, <b>Abel</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: <span lang="he" dir="rtl">הֶבֶל</span> <i>Hébel</i>, in <a href="/wiki/Pausa" title="Pausa">pausa</a> <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1020748213">.mw-parser-output .script-hebrew,.mw-parser-output .script-Hebr{font-family:"SBL Hebrew","SBL BibLit","Taamey Ashkenaz","Taamey Frank CLM","Frank Ruehl CLM","Ezra SIL","Ezra SIL SR","Keter Aram Tsova","Taamey David CLM","Keter YG","Shofar","David CLM","Hadasim CLM","Simple CLM","Nachlieli",Cardo,Alef,"Noto Serif Hebrew","Noto Sans Hebrew","David Libre",David,"Times New Roman",Gisha,Arial,FreeSerif,FreeSans}</style><span class="script-hebrew" style="font-size: 115%;" dir="rtl">הָבֶל</span>&#8206; <i>Hā́ḇel</i>; <a href="/wiki/Biblical_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Biblical Greek language">Biblical Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἅβελ</span> <i>Hábel</i>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%84" class="extiw" title="wikt:هابيل">هابيل</a></span>, <i>Hābēl</i>) is Eve's second son. His name in Hebrew is composed of the same three consonants as a <a href="/wiki/Semitic_root" title="Semitic root">root</a> meaning "breath". <a href="/wiki/Julius_Wellhausen" title="Julius Wellhausen">Julius Wellhausen</a> has proposed that the name is independent of the root.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Eberhard_Schrader" title="Eberhard Schrader">Eberhard Schrader</a> had previously put forward the <a href="/wiki/Akkadian_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Akkadian (language)">Akkadian</a> (Old Assyrian dialect) <i>ablu</i> ("son") as a more likely etymology.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/220px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="176" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/330px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg/440px-William_Bouguereau_-_El_primer_duelo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3300" data-file-height="2634" /></a><figcaption><i><a href="/wiki/The_First_Mourning" title="The First Mourning">The First Mourning</a></i> (Adam and Eve mourn the death of Abel); oil on canvas 1888 painting by <a href="/wiki/William-Adolphe_Bouguereau" title="William-Adolphe Bouguereau">William-Adolphe Bouguereau</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, comparisons are sometimes made between the death of Abel and that of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>, the former thus seen as being the first martyr. In <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Matthew%2023:35&amp;version=nrsv">Matthew 23:35</a> Jesus speaks of Abel as "righteous", and the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews" title="Epistle to the Hebrews">Epistle to the Hebrews</a> states that "The blood of sprinkling ... [speaks] better things than that of Abel" (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Hebrews%2012:24&amp;version=nrsv">Hebrews 12:24</a>). The blood of Jesus is interpreted as bringing mercy; but that of Abel as demanding vengeance (hence the curse and mark).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Abel is invoked in the <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litany</a> for the dying in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, and his sacrifice is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a> along with those of <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a> and <a href="/wiki/Melchizedek" title="Melchizedek">Melchizedek</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Alexandrian_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandrian Rite">Alexandrian Rite</a> commemorates him with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on December 28.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the Coptic <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Adam_and_Eve" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of Adam and Eve">Book of Adam and Eve</a> (at 2:1–15), and the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> <a href="/wiki/Cave_of_Treasures" title="Cave of Treasures">Cave of Treasures</a>, Abel's body, after many days of mourning, was placed in the <i>Cave of Treasures</i>, before which Adam and Eve, and descendants, offered their prayers. In addition, the <a href="/wiki/Seth" title="Seth">Sethite</a> line of the <a href="/wiki/Generations_of_Adam" title="Generations of Adam">Generations of Adam</a> swear by Abel's blood to segregate themselves from the <i>unrighteous</i>.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Enoch" title="Book of Enoch">Book of Enoch</a> (22:7), regarded by most Christian and Jewish traditions as extra-biblical, the soul of Abel is described as having been appointed as the chief of martyrs, crying for vengeance, for the destruction of the seed of Cain. A similar view is later shown in the <a href="/wiki/Testament_of_Abraham" title="Testament of Abraham">Testament of Abraham</a> (A:13 / B:11), where Abel has been raised to the position as the judge of the souls.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Bereshit_Rabbah" class="mw-redirect" title="Bereshit Rabbah">Bereshit Rabbah</a> (22:2), a discussion of Gen. 4:1 ff. has Rabbi <a href="/wiki/Joshua_ben_Karha" title="Joshua ben Karha">Yehoshua ben Korcha</a> mentioning that Cain was born with a twin sister, and Abel with two twin sisters.  This is based on the principle that the otherwise superfluous accusative article "et" always conveys some additional teaching (<a href="/wiki/Pesachim" title="Pesachim">Pesachim</a> 22b). The "et"'s are parsed slightly differently in <a href="/wiki/Yebamot" class="mw-redirect" title="Yebamot">Yebamot</a> 62a where the two "et"'s in Gen. 4:2 indicate Cain and his sister, and Abel and his (one) sister.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sethian_Gnostic_interpretation">Sethian Gnostic interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Sethian Gnostic interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Apocryphon_of_John" title="Apocryphon of John">Apocryphon of John</a>, a work belonging to <a href="/wiki/Sethianism" title="Sethianism">Sethian</a> <a href="/wiki/Gnosticism" title="Gnosticism">Gnosticism</a>, Abel is the offspring of <a href="/wiki/Demiurge#Yaldaboath" title="Demiurge">Yaldaboath</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eve" title="Eve">Eve</a>, who is placed over the elements of water and earth as <a href="/wiki/Elohim" title="Elohim">Elohim</a>, but was only given his name as a form of deception.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mandaean_interpretation"><span class="anchor" id="Hibil"></span>Mandaean interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Mandaean interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Hibil" title="Hibil">Hibil</a></div>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Mandaeism" title="Mandaeism">Mandaean</a> beliefs and scriptures including the <a href="/wiki/Qolusta" class="mw-redirect" title="Qolusta">Qolastā</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Dra%C5%A1a_D-Iahia" class="mw-redirect" title="Draša D-Iahia">Book of John</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ginza_Rba" class="mw-redirect" title="Ginza Rba">Genzā Rabbā</a>, Abel is cognate with the angelic <a href="/wiki/Soteriology" title="Soteriology">soteriological</a> figure <b>Hibil Ziwa</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Mandaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Mandaic language">Classical Mandaic</a>: <span lang="myz" dir="rtl">ࡄࡉࡁࡉࡋ ࡆࡉࡅࡀ‎</span>, sometimes translated "Splendid Hibel"),<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> who is spoken of as a son of <a href="/wiki/Hayyi_Rabbi" title="Hayyi Rabbi">Hayyi</a><sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p78_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p78-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> or of <a href="/wiki/Manda_d-Hayyi" title="Manda d-Hayyi">Manda d-Hayyi</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-gloss_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-gloss-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p83_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-p83-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and as a brother to <a href="/wiki/Enos_(biblical_figure)#In_Mandaeism" title="Enos (biblical figure)">Anush (Enosh)</a> and to <a href="/wiki/Seth#Mandaeism" title="Seth">Sheetil (Seth)</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> who is the son of <a href="/wiki/Adam" title="Adam">Adam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-l1-1_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-l1-1-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Elsewhere, Anush is spoken of as the son of Sheetil, and Sheetil as the son of Hibil, where Hibil came to Adam and Eve as a young boy when they were still virgins, but was called their son.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r12_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r12-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Hibil is an important <a href="/wiki/Lightworld" class="mw-redirect" title="Lightworld">lightworld</a> being (<a href="/wiki/Uthra" title="Uthra">uthra</a>) who conquered the <a href="/wiki/World_of_Darkness_(Mandaeism)" title="World of Darkness (Mandaeism)">World of Darkness</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-gloss_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-gloss-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> As <b>Yawar Hibil</b>, he is one of multiple figures known as <b><a href="/wiki/Yawar_Ziwa" title="Yawar Ziwa">Yawar</a></b> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Mandaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Mandaic language">Classical Mandaic</a>: <span lang="myz" dir="rtl">ࡉࡀࡅࡀࡓ</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Literal_translation" title="Literal translation">lit.</a>&#8201;</small>&#39;Helper&#39;), being so named by and after his father.<sup id="cite_ref-ginza-alsaadi-r5_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ginza-alsaadi-r5-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Islamic_interpretation">Islamic interpretation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Islamic interpretation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:NabiHabeel02.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/NabiHabeel02.JPG/220px-NabiHabeel02.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/NabiHabeel02.JPG/330px-NabiHabeel02.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/NabiHabeel02.JPG/440px-NabiHabeel02.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="960" /></a><figcaption>Grave of Abel within the <a href="/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque" title="Nabi Habeel Mosque">Nabi Habeel Mosque</a></figcaption></figure>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main articles: <a href="/wiki/Cain_and_Abel_in_Islam" title="Cain and Abel in Islam">Cain and Abel in Islam</a> and <a href="/wiki/Al-Ma%27ida" class="mw-redirect" title="Al-Ma&#39;ida">Al-Ma'ida</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:NabiHabeel01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/NabiHabeel01.jpg/220px-NabiHabeel01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="179" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/NabiHabeel01.jpg/330px-NabiHabeel01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/NabiHabeel01.jpg/440px-NabiHabeel01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1809" data-file-height="1473" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Mausoleum" title="Mausoleum">Mausoleum</a> of Abel in the <a href="/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque" title="Nabi Habeel Mosque">Nabi Habeel Mosque</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Shia_Islam" title="Shia Islam">Shi'a Muslim</a> belief, Abel (<i>"Habeel"</i>) is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque" title="Nabi Habeel Mosque">Nabi Habeel Mosque</a>, located on the west mountains of <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>, near the <a href="/wiki/Zabadani" class="mw-redirect" title="Zabadani">Zabadani</a> Valley, overlooking the villages of the <a href="/wiki/Barada" title="Barada">Barada</a> river (Wadi Barada), in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>. Shi'a are frequent visitors of this mosque for <a href="/wiki/Ziyarat" title="Ziyarat">ziyarat</a>. The mosque was built by <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> <a href="/wiki/Wali" title="Wali">Wali</a> Ahmad Pasha in 1599.
</p>
