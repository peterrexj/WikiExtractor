<p><b>Tommaso da Cori</b> (4 June 1655 - 11 January 1729) - born <b>Francesco Antonio Placidi</b> - was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> who lived as a hermit for much of his religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He gained fame as a noted preacher throughout the region where his hermitage was located and for this became known as the "Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Lazio" title="Subiaco, Lazio">Sublacense</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification was celebrated under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> on 3 September 1786 and was beatified two centuries later in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 21 November 1999 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tommaso_da_Cori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Francesco Antonio Placidi was born to poor parents on 4 June 1655 in <a href="/wiki/Cori,_Lazio" title="Cori, Lazio">Cori</a> near <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. In his childhood he was dubbed "the little saint" due to the recognition of his personal holiness.<sup id="cite_ref-RC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He earned a living as a shepherd as he grew up. Placidi was pious and soon became aware of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> that had come to the town. Both his parents had died at the time he turned fourteen and was therefore his task to take care of his two sisters and to find them husbands. Placidi had a strong devotion to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> and so after seeing his sisters married off chose almost at once to enter <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">consecrated life</a> as a simple friar and entered the Order of Friars Minor at the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of Santissima Trinitá in 1665 while commencing his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> on 7 February 1667.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He underwent his theological and philosophical studies in <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>.
</p><p>In 1683 - following the successful completion of his studies - he was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a> (he celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in <a href="/wiki/Velletri" title="Velletri">Velletri</a>) and was at once appointed as the assistant <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">master of novices</a> in <a href="/wiki/Orvieto" title="Orvieto">Orvieto</a>. He soon became aware of the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> life which was re-emerging in the order during that period and so joined a <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> in Civitella (present <a href="/wiki/Bellegra" title="Bellegra">Bellegra</a>) where he lived until his death except for a brief period of time in which he was the <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">guardian</a> of a hermitage he founded in <a href="/wiki/Palombara" class="mw-redirect" title="Palombara">Palombara</a> modeled after the one in Civitella.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RC_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fra Tommaso followed the model of hermit life that <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> had established in that he did not remain enclosed within the hermitage but would instead go out preaching in the small villages throughout the region. He became known for his simple and clear manner of preaching the messages of the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> and came to be called dubbed as the "Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Lazio" title="Subiaco, Lazio">Sublacense</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became known during his lifetime as a man of deep contemplation on the Gospel and often spent the night in the convent's chapel in silent meditation.
</p><p>Placidi died in peace in his sleep at the beginning of 1729 in Civitella.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tommaso_da_Cori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The sainthood process commenced on 15 July 1737 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> in which Placidi was titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> while a previous decree issued on 18 April 1737 allowed for the cause to commence and start on a diocesan level. The writings of the late friar received the approval of theologians assigned to assess them on 14 December 1771 and an antepreparatory committee later approved the cause sometime later on 22 February 1774; this allowed for a preparatory committee to do the same on 12 March 1776 as did a general congregation of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Rites">Congregation of Rites</a> on 28 July 1778.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> declared the late friar to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 1 August 1778 upon the confirmation that he led a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and later approved two miracles attributed to the friar on 4 October 1785 - this allowed for the pope to preside over the beatification in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> on 3 September 1786. The pope issued a decree for the cause's resumption a decade later on 4 May 1796. The third and final miracle needed for sainthood was investigated and validated under the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> before receiving the approval of a medical board on 29 January 1998. Theologians approved it on 29 May 1998 as did the C.C.S. on 19 January 1999 at which point <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> issued definitive approval to the miracle on 26 March 1999 and canonized the friar in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 21 November 1999.
