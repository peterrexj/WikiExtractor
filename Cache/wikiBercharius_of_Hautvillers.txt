<p><b>Saint Bercharius</b> (<i>Bererus</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Berchaire</i>) (636 – March 28, 696) was abbot of <a href="/wiki/Hautvillers" title="Hautvillers">Hautvillers</a> in <a href="/wiki/Champagne_(province)" title="Champagne (province)">Champagne</a>.  Descended from a distinguished <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitanian</a> family, he received his instruction from Saint <a href="/wiki/Nivard" title="Nivard">Nivard</a> (Nivo), <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Reims">Archbishop of Reims</a>.
</p><p>Bercharius entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil</a> under Saint <a href="/wiki/Saint_Waldebert" title="Saint Waldebert">Walbert</a>, and soon stood out from the rest of his fellow-novices. Upon his return to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> he persuaded Saint Nivard to establish the monastery of Hautvillers.  Bercharius himself became the first abbot. Entirely given up to prayer and meditation he also instructed his brethren to lead a contemplative life.
</p><p>He founded two religious houses in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Ch%C3%A2lons-sur-Marne" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Châlons-sur-Marne">Diocese of Châlons-sur-Marne</a>, the one (Puisye or <a href="/wiki/Montier-en-Der_Abbey" title="Montier-en-Der Abbey">Montier-en-Der Abbey</a>) for men, the other (Pellmoutier or  <i>Puellarum Monasterium</i>) for women. These institutions he enriched by donations of valuable <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, procured on a journey to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.
</p><p>The monk Daguin, provoked by a reprimand from Bercharius, <a href="/wiki/Stabbing" title="Stabbing">stabbed</a> him during the night. According to one account, Bercharius did not condemn or complain about the injury he received, but instead asked Daguin to perform <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> and to make a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to obtain pardon and absolution. Daguin left the monastery never to return. After two days of severe suffering, the saint succumbed to his wound, and was considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bercharius_of_Hautvillers&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His remains were preserved at Moutier-en-Der until the suppression of religious orders in the 1790s.
</p><p>The commemoration of his name occurs in the martyrology on 16 October.
</p>
