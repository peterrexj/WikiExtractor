					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Saint</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Abbán</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><span class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Frameless"><a href="/wiki/File:Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg/220px-Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg/330px-Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg/440px-Eilean_Mor_Chapel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="426" /></a></span><div class="infobox-caption">Chapel of St Cormac, Christian chapel at Eilean Mòr, Jura, in the <a href="/wiki/MacCormaig_Isles" title="MacCormaig Isles">MacCormaig Isles</a> (Scotland). Like the church of Keills (Kilvickocharmick) on the Scottish mainland, it may be associated with Abbán.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></div></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Eibbán, Moabba</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">520?</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">16 March and 27 October<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some sources also cite 13 May.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Mag Arnaide (Adamstown, County Wexford), Cell Abbáin (Killabban, County Laois), etc.</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Abbán moccu Corbmaic</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Abbanus</i>; d. 520? AD), also <b>Eibbán</b> or <b>Moabba</b>, is a saint in Irish tradition. He was associated, first and foremost, with Mag Arnaide (Moyarney or <a href="/wiki/Adamstown,_County_Wexford" title="Adamstown, County Wexford">Adamstown, County Wexford</a>, near <a href="/wiki/New_Ross" title="New Ross">New Ross</a>) and with Cell Abbáin (Killabban, <a href="/wiki/County_Laois" title="County Laois">County Laois</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His order was, however, also connected to other churches elsewhere in Ireland, notably that of his alleged sister <a href="/wiki/Gobnait" title="Gobnait">Gobnait</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abb%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Three recensions of Abbán's <i>Life</i> survive, two in Latin and one in Irish. The Latin versions are found in the <i>Codex Dublinensis</i> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Salmanticensis" title="Codex Salmanticensis">Codex Salmanticensis</a></i>, while the Irish version is preserved incomplete in two manuscripts: the <a href="/wiki/M%C3%ADche%C3%A1l_%C3%93_Cl%C3%A9irigh" title="Mícheál Ó Cléirigh">Mícheál Ó Cléirigh</a>'s manuscript Brussels, Royal Library MS 2324–40, fos. 145b-150b and also the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy" title="Royal Irish Academy">RIA</a>, Stowe MS A 4, pp.&#160;205–21.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> These <i>Lives</i> probably go back to a Latin exemplar written in <i>ca</i>. 1218 by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ferns" title="Bishop of Ferns">bishop of Ferns</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ailbe_Ua_Ma%C3%ADl_Mhuaidh" class="mw-redirect" title="Ailbe Ua Maíl Mhuaidh">Ailbe Ua Maíl Mhuaidh</a> (Ailbe O'Mulloy), who died in 1223.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His interest in Abbán partly stemmed from the fact that Mag Arnaide lay within the diocese of Ferns, but as this was only a minor church in his time, more must have been involved.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> An episode which shows something of Ailbe's personal attachment to Abbán's order is that where Abbán arrives in the area between <a href="/wiki/%C3%89ile" title="Éile">Éile</a> and Fir Chell, i.e. on the marches between Munster and Leinster: Abbán converts a man of royal rank from the area and baptises his son. Ailbe is known to have been a native of this area, but his own commentary as apparently preserved in the Dublin <i>Life</i> identifies the connection more nearly: "I who gathered together and wrote the Life am a descendant [<i>nepos</i>] of that son"<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> However, the immediate circumstances which prompted the composition of the <i>Life</i> are likely to have been political, relating to Norman presence in the diocese of Ferns. To support his case, Ailbe made much of Abbán's wider connections to other churches and saints, making him travel all across the country and in the case of the anecdote about Abingdon (see below), even inventing tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Other sources for Abbán's life and order include the Irish genealogies of the saints and the entries for his feast-day in the martyrologies. His pedigree is given in the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Leinster" title="Book of Leinster">Book of Leinster</a>, <a href="/wiki/Leabhar_Breac" class="mw-redirect" title="Leabhar Breac">Leabhar Breac</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rawlinson_B_502" class="mw-redirect" title="Rawlinson B 502">Rawlinson B 502</a> and in glosses to his entries in the <i><a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9lire_%C3%93engusso" class="mw-redirect" title="Félire Óengusso">Félire Óengusso</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FO_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FO-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background_and_life">Background and life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abb%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Background and life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg/230px-New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg" decoding="async" width="230" height="153" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg/345px-New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg/460px-New_Ross_Church_of_St._Mary_and_St._Michael_Nave_West_End_Relief_of_Saint_Abban_2012_09_04.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3504" data-file-height="2336" /></a><figcaption>Relief of Saint Abban, Church of Ss Mary and Michael, New Ross</figcaption></figure>
<p>His pedigree in the Irish genealogies, which appear to have been composed in the interest of Cell Abbáin, suggests that he belonged to the Uí Chormaic (also Moccu Chormaic or Dál Chormaic).<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It identifies his father as Laignech (lit. "Leinsterman"), son of Mac Cainnech, son of Cabraid, son of Cormac, son of Cú Corb,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> while an Irish note to the <i>Félire Óengusso</i> (for 27 October) largely agrees if substituting Cabraid for Imchad.<sup id="cite_ref-FO_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FO-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Lives</i>, on the other hand, state that his father was Cormac son of Ailill, king of Leinster, who died in 435 according to the <i>Annals of the Four Masters</i>, and name his mother Mílla, sister to St Ibar.<sup id="cite_ref-Culleton98_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Culleton98-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Lives</i> confuse the time of Abbán's historical floruit by attributing to him a life-span of over 300 years.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He is brought into contact with such illustrious saints as <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brendan_of_Clonfert" class="mw-redirect" title="Brendan of Clonfert">Brendan of Clonfert</a> (d. 577), <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a> (d. 597), <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, <a href="/wiki/Munnu" class="mw-redirect" title="Munnu">Munnu</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mo_Ling" title="Mo Ling">Moling</a>. One of Abbán's foundations is said to have been repeatedly pillaged by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cormac_mac_Diarmata&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cormac mac Diarmata (page does not exist)">Cormac mac Diarmata</a> (<i>fl</i>. 2nd half of the 6th century), king of Leinster from the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Bairrche" title="Uí Bairrche">Uí Bairrche</a>, who is portrayed in much Leinster hagiography as a rival to the Uí Chennselaig. Abbán is also made a contemporary of even earlier figures like <a href="/wiki/Ibar_of_Beggerin" title="Ibar of Beggerin">Íbar</a>, who is claimed to be his maternal uncle, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>.
</p><p>Nothing is known of Abbán's early life. The <i>Lives</i> tell that he was expected to succeed his father in Leinster, but that his devotion to God and the saintly miracles which he wrought while still in fosterage soon made clear that he was destined for a career in the church. The boy was sent to his maternal uncle, the bishop Íbar, with whom he travelled to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In Italy, Abbán's saintly powers proved to be of much use in warding off any danger presented by men, monsters and supernatural phenomena. Throughout the text, Abbán can be seen demonstrating his powers, exercising special authority over rivers and seas.
</p><p>Abbán had six brothers who all appear in the Martyrology of Donegal as bishops: <a href="/wiki/Saint_Daman" title="Saint Daman">Damán</a> Uí Chormaic of Tígh Damhain (Tidowan), in the barony of Marybouragh, County Laois; Miacca Uí Cormaic of Cluain Fodhla in Fiodhmar (borders Uí Duach/Bally Fíodhmor, Ossory); <a href="/wiki/Senach" title="Senach">Senach</a> Uí Chormaic of Cillmór; <a href="/wiki/Lithghean" title="Lithghean">Lithghean</a> Uí Chormaic of Cluain Mór Lethghian in Uí Failge (Barony Ophaly, Co. Kildare); <a href="/wiki/Dubh%C3%A1n_of_Rosglas" title="Dubhán of Rosglas">Dubhán</a> Uí Chormaic; Toimdeach Uí Chormaic of Rosglas, Monasterevin, County Kildare.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dár Cairthaind and Ethne are listed as his sisters in the 'Accent of the Saints',<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> while <a href="/wiki/Gobnait" title="Gobnait">Gobnait</a> of Baile Bhuirne, Cork and Craobh Dearg are mentioned as his sisters in other accounts.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundations">Foundations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abb%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The glosses to the two entries for Abbán in the <i>Félire Óengusso</i> associate him with Mag Arnaide (County Wexford), in the territory of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Chennselaig" class="mw-redirect" title="Uí Chennselaig">Uí Chennselaig</a> (also Uí Buide), and with Cell Abbáin (County Laois), in the territory of the Uí Muiredaig.<sup id="cite_ref-FO_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FO-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, Abbán's activities were also linked to many other parts of Ireland. Of special note is the tradition that Saint <a href="/wiki/Gobnait" title="Gobnait">Gobnait</a> was his sister and that his grave was to be found near her church or nunnery in Bairnech, now <a href="/wiki/Ballyvourney" title="Ballyvourney">Ballyvourney</a> (Muskerry, County Cork).<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> As the later recensions suggest, Ailbe's original <i>Life</i> seems to build on this connection by claiming that Abbán founded Ballyvourney and gave it to his sister.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>According to his <i>Lives</i>, he began to found a string of churches after returning from a second visit to Rome. Other churches said to have been founded by him include <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cell_Ailbe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cell Ailbe (page does not exist)">Cell Ailbe</a> (Co. Meath) and <a href="/wiki/Camross" class="mw-redirect" title="Camross">Camross</a> (Co. Laois), as well as a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Nurney,_County_Carlow" title="Nurney, County Carlow">Nurney, County Carlow</a> of which an <a href="/wiki/Nurney_Cross" title="Nurney Cross">early high cross</a> survives.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> argued that the Abbán of Mag Arnaide and the Abbán of Cell Abbáin were originally two distinct saints, one commemorated on 16 March, the other on 27 October, but that the two were conflated from an early period.<sup id="cite_ref-Culleton97_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Culleton97-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> This conclusion, however, has been rejected by scholars like W.W. Heist and Charles Plummer.<sup id="cite_ref-Culleton97_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Culleton97-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also a brief biographical reference to Abbán in the official <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographical</a> compilation of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <i>The Great <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxaristes</a></i> for <a href="/w/index.php?title=13_May_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="13 May (Eastern Orthodox liturgics) (page does not exist)">13 May</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This source states that he was baptised in 165 AD, became a missionary in the <a href="/wiki/Abingdon,_Oxfordshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Abingdon, Oxfordshire">Abingdon</a> area of England, and reposed in peace.<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abingdon_and_Irish-Norman_relations">Abingdon and Irish-Norman relations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abb%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Abingdon and Irish-Norman relations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Life</i> puts forward the spurious claim that <a href="/wiki/Abingdon,_Oxfordshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Abingdon, Oxfordshire">Abingdon</a>, the town near Oxford, is to be explained etymologically as <i>Abbain dun</i>, "Abbán's town". The aetiological tale goes that the town took its name from Abbán, because he had successfully converted the king and the people of the area.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The story was not an isolated one. The etymology is also brought up by the author who revised the 12th-century chronicle of the house, <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Ecclesie_Abbendonensis" title="Historia Ecclesie Abbendonensis">Historia Ecclesie Abbendonensis</a></i> ("The History of the Church of Abingdon"). As Abingdon Abbey lay in a valley, he prefers the Irish derivation: "For we have learnt from our contemporaries that, according to the language of the Irish, Abingdon is interpreted 'house of Aben'; but according to the language of the English, Abingdon commonly means 'the hill of Aben'."<sup id="cite_ref-HEAxliii_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HEAxliii-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pádraig Ó Riain proposes that the episode in Abbán's <i>Life</i> was intended to offer some counterweight against English propaganda which asserted that the need for religious and ecclesiastical guidance justified English presence in Ireland; and that, in fact, the linguistic convenience was what made Abbán of an otherwise minor church such a suitable protagonist.<sup id="cite_ref-DIB_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DIB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> More specifically, Ailbe may have written his <i>Life</i> in response to his quarrel with <a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Earl_Marshall&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="William Earl Marshall (page does not exist)">William Earl Marshall</a>, who had seized two manors near New Ross, and Normans rather than Irishmen may have been his target audience.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> It has been argued that the formative occasion for the story was a visit to Abingon made in 1080 by <a href="/wiki/Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail" title="Lorcán Ua Tuathail">Lorcán Ua Tuathail</a> (Lawrence O'Toole), Archbishop of Dublin, who stayed there for three weeks before accompanying <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a> to Normandy. Ailbe, being one of the archbishop's disciples, may have been present.<sup id="cite_ref-HEAxliii_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HEAxliii-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abb%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Martyrology of Tallaght, the <i>Félire Óengusso</i> and the Martyrology of Gorman, Abbán has two feast-days: 16 March and 27 October,<sup id="cite_ref-FO_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FO-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> which is identified in the <i>Lives</i> as his death-date. <a href="/wiki/John_Colgan" title="John Colgan">John Colgan</a> and Ó Cléirigh's <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Donegal" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrology of Donegal">Martyrology of Donegal</a></i> only mention Abbán for 16 March. Other sources cite 13 May.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His entries in the <i>Félire Óengusso</i> praise him as an "angelic bush of gold" (<i>doss óir ainglech</i>) and "an abbot fair and train-having" (<i>abb cain clíarach</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FO_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FO-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
