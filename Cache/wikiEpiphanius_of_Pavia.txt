<p><b>Epiphanius of Pavia</b> (438–496), later venerated as <b>Saint Epiphanius of Pavia</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Pavia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Pavia">Bishop of Pavia</a> from 466 until his death in 496. Epiphanius additionally held the offices of <a href="/wiki/Lector" title="Lector">lector</a>, <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his lifetime, Epiphanius undertook several <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">church</a>-related missions and exploits. Two of the most significant of these were his journey, as an <a href="/wiki/Ambassador" title="Ambassador">emissary</a> for the emperor <a href="/wiki/Julius_Nepos" title="Julius Nepos">Julius Nepos</a>, to the Visigothic king <a href="/wiki/Euric" title="Euric">Euric</a>; and his journey to <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, where he confronted <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great" title="Theodoric the Great">Theodoric the Great</a> shortly after his defeat of <a href="/wiki/Odoacer" title="Odoacer">Odoacer</a>, and pleaded for the restoration of the <a href="/wiki/Civic_rights" class="mw-redirect" title="Civic rights">civic rights</a> of Roman aristocrats who had supported Odoacer.<sup id="cite_ref-Alb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Epiphanius_of_Pavia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Our primary source for Epiphanius' life is the <i>Vita Epifanius</i> written by <a href="/wiki/Magnus_Felix_Ennodius" title="Magnus Felix Ennodius">Magnus Felix Ennodius</a>, who knew him personally, travelling with the bishop on his mission to king <a href="/wiki/Gundobad" title="Gundobad">Gundobad</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundians</a> in 494–6. According to Ennodius, Epiphanius' father was Maurus and his mother Focaria, who was related to <a href="/wiki/Mirocles_(bishop_of_Milan)" title="Mirocles (bishop of Milan)">Mirocles bishop of Milan</a> (304-326).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He joined the household of bishop Crispinus at the age of eight and became a lector, learning to read and write, as well as <a href="/wiki/Stenography" class="mw-redirect" title="Stenography">stenography</a>. At the age of 18 he was consecrated subdeacon,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and deacon at 20.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Then Bishop Crispinus, as he lay dying, appointed Epiphanius as his successor in the presence of the ex-<a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavius_Rusticus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Flavius Rusticus (page does not exist)">Flavius Rusticus</a>. Although he resisted the ordination, Epiphanius was made bishop in Milan in his 28th year.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after becoming bishop, Epiphanius was asked to intervene between <a href="/wiki/Anthemius" title="Anthemius">Anthemius</a> and the barbarian leader <a href="/wiki/Ricimer" title="Ricimer">Ricimer</a>, appealing to both parties for peace.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> However, history shows that Epiphanius' efforts were in vain, for violence broke out between Ricimer and Anthemius; Anthemius was besieged in the part of Rome he controlled until his supporters deserted him, and he was caught attempting to flee the city disguised as a supplicant at the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Maria in Trastevere">Santa Maria in Trastevere</a>, where he was beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Most of these confrontations and speakings to high-ranking figures proved to be successful endeavors; religious dignitaries such as Epiphanius had wide-ranging influence on the rulers and <a href="/wiki/Aristocracy_(class)" title="Aristocracy (class)">aristocrats</a> during this period.<sup id="cite_ref-Arm_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arm-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In the eighth year of his episcopate (spring 475),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> the emperor Julius Nepos sent him on a diplomatic mission to Euric about Visigothic incursions. A peace was concluded, although Nepos was deposed by <a href="/wiki/Orestes" title="Orestes">Orestes</a> shortly after.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cook notes that of "the thirteen years of Odoacer's matery in Italy, from the defeat of Orestes in A.D. 476 to the coming of Thodoric, A.D. 489 -- a period which embraced nearly half of the episcopate of Epiphanius -- Ennodius devotes but eight sections of the <i>vita</i> (101 - 107), five of which are taken up with the account of the restoration of churches."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The only other incident Ennodius recorded during these 13 years was his successful petition to Odoacer to alleviate the rapacious demands of the <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_prefect" title="Praetorian prefect">praetorian prefect</a> Pelagius.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another success was Epiphanius' successful negotiations over the <a href="/wiki/Ransom" title="Ransom">ransom</a> of his sister Honorata,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> who had been abducted from the monastery of St. Vincent in Pavia, during the war between <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great" title="Theodoric the Great">Theodoric the Great</a> and Odoacer.<sup id="cite_ref-Arm_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arm-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> After Theodoric had prevailed, one of his acts was to punish the Romans who had supported Odoacer by depriving them of their <a href="/wiki/Civic_rights" class="mw-redirect" title="Civic rights">civic rights</a>; Epiphanius went to the Ostragothic king and pleaded for their restoration. Theodoric granted the bishop's petition, on condition that Epiphanius act on his behalf and negotiate with the Burgundians to ransom the captives they took when they raided <a href="/wiki/Liguria" title="Liguria">Liguria</a> during the war between Theodoric and Odoacer in 489.<sup id="cite_ref-Wolfram-281_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wolfram-281-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Epiphanius died following the rigors of his winter journey to Burgundy, on 21 January 496, in his fifty-eighth year.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> That particular year marked thirty years of his service to the church.<sup id="cite_ref-Gil_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gil-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Epiphanius_of_Pavia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following Epiphanius' death,  Ennodius, his successor as bishop of Pavia, wrote a <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a></i> or "Life" of Epiphanius; based on internal evidence dates its composition between 501 and 504.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Epiphanius' relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Hildesheim" title="Hildesheim">Hildesheim</a> in 963,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> where they are conserved in a grand reliquary <a href="/wiki/Chasse" class="mw-redirect" title="Chasse">chasse</a> in the Dom.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Despite the extensive evidence for the removal of his remains, there was in Pavia a persistent belief in possessing Epiphanius' remains. So strong was this belief that in the 18th century they reinterred what they considered the bishop's corpse in the church of San Francesco da Paola; later these relics were translated to the church of San Francesco Grande. In 1834, Bishop Tosi of Pavia formally recognized the authenticity of these remains.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
