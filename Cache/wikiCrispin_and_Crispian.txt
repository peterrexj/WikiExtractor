<p>Saints <b>Crispin and Crispinian</b> are the Christian <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Shoemaking" title="Shoemaking">cobblers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Currier" title="Currier">curriers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tanning_(leather)" title="Tanning (leather)">tanners</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Leather" title="Leather">leather</a> workers. They were beheaded during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>; the date of their execution is given as 25 October 285 or 286.
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="History"></span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Crispin_and_Crispinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born to a noble <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> family in the 3rd century AD, Crispin and Crispinian fled persecution for their faith, ending up at <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>, where they preached <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Gauls" title="Gauls">Gauls</a> while making <a href="/wiki/Shoe" title="Shoe">shoes</a> by night. It is stated that they were twin brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They earned enough by their trade to support themselves and also to aid the poor. Their success attracted the ire of <a href="/wiki/Rictus_Varus" class="mw-redirect" title="Rictus Varus">Rictus Varus</a>, governor of <a href="/wiki/Belgic_Gaul" class="mw-redirect" title="Belgic Gaul">Belgic Gaul</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who had them <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> and thrown into the river with <a href="/wiki/Millstone" title="Millstone">millstones</a> around their necks. Though they survived, they were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> by <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">the emperor</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 286.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Crispin_and_Crispinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Saints Crispin and Crispinian is <a href="/wiki/25_October" class="mw-redirect" title="25 October">25 October</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Although this feast was removed from the Roman Catholic Church's universal <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_year" title="Liturgical year">liturgical calendar</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, the two saints are still commemorated on that day in the most recent edition of the Roman Church's martyrology.
</p><p>In the sixth century, a stately basilica was erected at Soissons over these saints' graves, and <a href="/wiki/St._Eligius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Eligius">St. Eligius</a>, a famous goldsmith, made a costly shrine for the head of St. Crispinian.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Their remains were afterwards removed, partly by <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> to <a href="/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck" title="Osnabrück">Osnabrück</a>, and partly to the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Lorenzo_in_Panisperna" title="San Lorenzo in Panisperna">San Lorenzo in Panisperna</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They are the patron saints of cobblers, glove makers, lace makers, lace workers, leather workers, saddle makers, saddlers, shoemakers, tanners, and weavers.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Especially in France, but also in England and in other parts of Europe, the festival of St Crispin was for centuries the occasion of solemn processions and merry-making, in which <a href="/wiki/Guild" title="Guild">guilds</a> of shoemakers took the chief part.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
Crispin and Crispinian are <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/October_25" title="October 25">25 October</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_references">Cultural references</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Crispin_and_Crispinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cultural references">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt" title="Battle of Agincourt">Battle of Agincourt</a> was fought on <a href="/wiki/Saint_Crispin%27s_Day" title="Saint Crispin&#39;s Day">Saint Crispin's feastday</a>. (The English tradition placed the twins at <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> rather than Gaul.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>) <a href="/wiki/Shakespeare" class="mw-redirect" title="Shakespeare">Shakespeare</a>'s <a href="/wiki/St._Crispin%27s_Day_Speech" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Crispin&#39;s Day Speech">St. Crispin's Day Speech</a> (sometimes called the "Band Of Brothers" Speech) from his <a href="/wiki/Shakespeare%27s_plays" title="Shakespeare&#39;s plays">play</a> <a href="/wiki/Henry_V_(play)" title="Henry V (play)"><i>Henry V</i></a> has immortalized the day. Also, for the Midsummer's Day Festival in the third act of <i><a href="/wiki/Die_Meistersinger" class="mw-redirect" title="Die Meistersinger">Die Meistersinger</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Wagner" class="mw-redirect" title="Wagner">Wagner</a> has the shoemakers' guild enter singing a song of praise to St. Crispin.
</p><p>A 16th century legend links them to the town of <a href="/wiki/Faversham" title="Faversham">Faversham</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>, England.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> A plaque at Faversham commemorates their association with the town.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> They are also celebrated in the name of the old <a href="/wiki/Public_house" class="mw-redirect" title="Public house">pub</a> "Crispin and Crispianus" at <a href="/wiki/Strood" title="Strood">Strood</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
