<p><b>Andeolus</b> or <b>Andéol</b> is an alleged Christian missionary martyred in Gaul.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andeolus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Andeolus was reportedly born in <a href="/wiki/Smyrna" title="Smyrna">Smyrna</a> in the 2nd century.  A deacon, he was sent by <a href="/wiki/Polycarp" title="Polycarp">Polycarp</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> along with <a href="/wiki/Benignus_of_Dijon" title="Benignus of Dijon">Benignus</a>, to evangelize southern <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. According to <i>The Passio of Saint Benignus</i>, they were shipwrecked on Corsica but managed to make their way to Marseilles. From there they travelled up the Rhone River and the Saône, reaching Autun, they converted Symphorianus, son of the noble Faustus. Symphorianus was later martyred for his faith as Saint <a href="/wiki/Symphorian" class="mw-redirect" title="Symphorian">Symphorian</a>. Andeolus then went to the <a href="/wiki/Vivarais" title="Vivarais">Vivarais</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Septimius_Severus" title="Septimius Severus">Septimius Severus</a>, passing through that region, had him put to death. <sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was executed on May 1, 208, at Bergoiata, a Gallic settlement on a rocky peak over the <a href="/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhône River">Rhône River</a> which would be later known as <a href="/wiki/Bourg-Saint-And%C3%A9ol" title="Bourg-Saint-Andéol">Bourg-Saint-Andéol</a>. The body, thrown into the Rhone, was later found and placed in a <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> by a rich Roman woman, Anycia or Amycia Eucheria Tullia (Blessed Tullie), daughter of senator Eucherius Valerianus (<a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius of Lyon</a>). 
</p><p>A sarcophagus, purported to be that of Andeolus, was rediscovered in 1865 during excavations in the St. Polycarp chapel of the eleventh-century church in <a href="/wiki/Bourg-Saint-And%C3%A9ol" title="Bourg-Saint-Andéol">Bourg-Saint-Andéol</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ard%C3%A8che" title="Ardèche">Ardèche</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Analysis">Analysis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andeolus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Analysis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Johann_Peter_Kirsch" title="Johann Peter Kirsch">Johann Peter Kirsch</a> says that while it is an historical fact that Benignus suffered martyrdom in a persecution of the third century and was publicly honored as a martyr, early in the sixth century no particulars concerning the person and life of Benignus were known at Dijon.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" title="Louis Duchesne">Louis Duchesne</a> has shown that these accounts are part of a group of legends which arose in the early years of the sixth century and were intended to demonstrate the early the beginnings of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in the cities of that region (<a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>, <a href="/wiki/Langres" title="Langres">Langres</a>, <a href="/wiki/Valence,_Dr%C3%B4me" title="Valence, Drôme">Valence</a>). "They are historically unreliable, and the very existence of some of the martyrs connected with these places is doubtful."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Kirsch says, "They are all falsifications by the same hand and possess no historical value."<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
