<p><b>John Christian Watson</b> (born <b>Johan Cristian Tanck</b>; 9 April 1867&#160;&#8211;&#32;18 November 1941) was an Australian politician who was the third <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Australia">prime minister of Australia</a>, in office from 27 April to 18 August 1904. He was the inaugural federal leader of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party" title="Australian Labor Party">Australian Labor Party</a> (ALP) from 1901 to 1907 and was the first member of the party to serve as prime minister.<sup id="cite_ref-moadoph_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moadoph-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson was born in <a href="/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso" title="Valparaíso">Valparaíso</a>, the son of a <a href="/wiki/German_Chilean" class="mw-redirect" title="German Chilean">German Chilean</a> seaman. He grew up on the <a href="/wiki/South_Island" title="South Island">South Island</a> of <a href="/wiki/New_Zealand" title="New Zealand">New Zealand</a>, taking the surname of his step-father when his Irish-born mother remarried. He left school at a young age, working in the printing industry as a <a href="/wiki/Compositor_(typesetting)" class="mw-redirect" title="Compositor (typesetting)">compositor</a>. Watson moved to <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a> in 1886 and became prominent in the local <a href="/wiki/Australian_labour_movement" title="Australian labour movement">labour movement</a>. He helped establish the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_(New_South_Wales_Branch)" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Labor Party (New South Wales Branch)">Labor Electoral League of New South Wales</a> and directed the party's campaign at the <a href="/wiki/1891_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1891 New South Wales colonial election">1891 general election</a>. Watson was elected to the <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Assembly" title="New South Wales Legislative Assembly">New South Wales Legislative Assembly</a> at the <a href="/wiki/1894_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1894 New South Wales colonial election">1894 election</a>, aged 27, and quickly became a leading figure in the ALP. He and most party members opposed <a href="/wiki/Federation_of_Australia" title="Federation of Australia">Federation</a> on the grounds that the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia" title="Constitution of Australia">proposed constitution</a> was undemocratic.
</p><p>In 1901, Watson was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives" title="Australian House of Representatives">House of Representatives</a> at the <a href="/wiki/1901_Australian_federal_election" title="1901 Australian federal election">inaugural federal election</a>. He became a founding member of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_Caucus" title="Australian Labor Party Caucus">ALP caucus</a> in federal parliament and was elected as the party's inaugural leader. During the first term of parliament he supported the <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Protectionist_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Liberal Protectionist Party">Liberal Protectionist</a> governments of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Barton" title="Edmund Barton">Edmund Barton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a>, and was a strong supporter of the <a href="/wiki/White_Australia_policy" title="White Australia policy">White Australia policy</a>. At the <a href="/wiki/1903_Australian_federal_election" title="1903 Australian federal election">1903 election</a>, the ALP secured the <a href="/wiki/Balance_of_power_(parliament)" title="Balance of power (parliament)">balance of power</a> in the House and a strong position in the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Senate</a>. Watson formed a <a href="/wiki/Minority_government" title="Minority government">minority government</a> in April 1904, aged 37, after the ALP withdrew its support from Deakin. He was one of the first socialists to head a government in a parliamentary system, attracting international attention, and remains Australia's youngest prime minister.
</p><p>After less than four months in office, the <a href="/wiki/Watson_government" title="Watson government">Watson government</a> lost a <a href="/wiki/Confidence_motion" class="mw-redirect" title="Confidence motion">confidence motion</a> and Watson was succeeded as prime minister by anti-socialist <a href="/wiki/George_Reid" title="George Reid">George Reid</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">leader of the opposition</a> until 1905, when he helped reinstall Deakin as prime minister. The ALP continued to offer its support to Deakin after the <a href="/wiki/1906_Australian_federal_election" title="1906 Australian federal election">1906 election</a>, despite the opposition of some in the party. Watson resigned the party's leadership in 1907, citing family concerns, and left parliament at the <a href="/wiki/1910_Australian_federal_election" title="1910 Australian federal election">1910 election</a>. He was expelled from the ALP during the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_split_of_1916" title="Australian Labor Party split of 1916">1916 split over conscription</a> and became a <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_(Australia)" title="Nationalist Party (Australia)">Nationalist</a>, although he never again stood for public office. He subsequently had a successful business career, including as president of the <a href="/wiki/NRMA" title="NRMA">NRMA</a> and chairman of <a href="/wiki/Ampol" title="Ampol">Ampol</a>.
</p><p>While Watson did not succeed in passing legislation while in office, his term as prime minister is seen as significant as a demonstration that the ALP could form a competent government. His successor as party leader <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Fisher" title="Andrew Fisher">Andrew Fisher</a> would lead the ALP to a majority government at the 1910 election, in which many of Watson's ministers played a key role.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_family_background">Birth and family background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Birth and family background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Watson was born Johan Cristian Tanck on 9 April 1867 in <a href="/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso" title="Valparaíso">Valparaíso</a>, Chile. He was the only child of Martha (née Minchin) and Johan Cristian Tanck, senior.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father was also born in Valparaíso, a <a href="/wiki/German_Chileans" title="German Chileans">German Chilean</a> whose ancestors had emigrated from the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover" title="Kingdom of Hanover">Kingdom of Hanover</a> and established an import–export firm.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He worked as a merchant seaman, possibly a ship's carpenter, on trade routes across the Pacific.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He arrived in New Zealand aboard <i>La Joven Julia</i> on 24 December 1865 and married Martha Minchin in <a href="/wiki/Port_Chalmers" title="Port Chalmers">Port Chalmers</a> less than a month later, on 19 January 1866.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926–27_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926–27-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Their marriage was later registered at Valparaíso's <a href="/wiki/Iglesia_de_la_Matriz" title="Iglesia de la Matriz">Iglesia de la Matriz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199923-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Watson's mother was born in <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>, Ireland, and was 16 years old at the time of her marriage to Tanck.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199927_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199927-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She joined him on board the <i>Julia</i>, which eventually returned to Chile and docked in Valparaíso a few days before Watson's birth. In the months after his birth the ship worked a regular route carrying timber between Valparaíso and <a href="/wiki/Chilo%C3%A9_Island" title="Chiloé Island">Chiloé Island</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199924-25_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199924-25-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1868, Watson moved to New Zealand with his mother, returning to her family on the <a href="/wiki/South_Island" title="South Island">South Island</a>. The fate of his father is uncertain, as no records of his death have been found.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199926-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 15 February 1869, his mother married George Thomas Watson at the registry office in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Waipori,_New_Zealand&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Waipori, New Zealand (page does not exist)">Waipori</a>, describing herself as a widow. Her second husband was a 30-year-old miner born in <a href="/wiki/Ballymoney" title="Ballymoney">Ballymoney</a>, Ireland, who had come to New Zealand after several years working in Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199929–30_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199929–30-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Watson came to have nine half-siblings from his mother's second marriage, born between 1869 and 1887. He was treated as the biological child of George Watson, adopting his step-father's surname; his given names were also anglicised.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199930–32_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199930–32-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As an adult, Watson gave incorrect and contradictory information about the circumstances of his birth and the identity of his parents. He allowed some biographical profiles to list him as born in New Zealand, while his second wife and daughter understood that he had been born to British parents in <a href="/wiki/International_waters" title="International waters">international waters</a> outside Valparaíso. On legal documents he listed George Watson as his biological father and provided an incorrect <a href="/wiki/Maiden_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Maiden name">maiden name</a> for his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199922–23_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez199922–23-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Watson's biographers have suggested he may have originally concealed his background for convenience, but later deliberately did so for political reasons, including concerns over parliamentary eligibility and possible xenophobia. Birth overseas to a non-British father would have made him an <a href="/wiki/Alien_(law)" title="Alien (law)">alien</a> ineligible for election to federal parliament under <a href="/wiki/Section_44_of_the_Constitution_of_Australia" title="Section 44 of the Constitution of Australia">section 44(i) of the constitution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-jac1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jac1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-jac2_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jac2-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_move_to_Australia">Childhood and move to Australia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Childhood and move to Australia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Watson attended the state school in <a href="/wiki/Oamaru" title="Oamaru">Oamaru</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Otago" title="North Otago">North Otago</a>, <a href="/wiki/New_Zealand" title="New Zealand">New Zealand</a> until ten years of age when he left to become a <a href="/wiki/Rail_nipper" class="mw-redirect" title="Rail nipper">rail nipper</a>. Then after a period of helping on the family farm, at thirteen years of age he was apprenticed as a <a href="/wiki/Compositor_(typesetting)" class="mw-redirect" title="Compositor (typesetting)">compositor</a> at The North Otago Times, a newspaper run by prominent reformist politician <a href="/wiki/William_Steward_(New_Zealand_politician)" title="William Steward (New Zealand politician)">William Steward</a>, with the public affairs exposure augmenting his minor formal schooling. Following the death of his mother and the loss of his job, he migrated to <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a> in 1886 at nineteen years of age. He worked for a month as a stablehand at <a href="/wiki/Government_House,_Sydney" title="Government House, Sydney">Government House</a>, then found employment as a compositor for a number of newspapers including <i><a href="/wiki/Daily_Telegraph_(Sydney)" class="mw-redirect" title="Daily Telegraph (Sydney)">The Daily Telegraph</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Sydney_Morning_Herald" class="mw-redirect" title="Sydney Morning Herald">Sydney Morning Herald</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Australian_Star" title="The Australian Star">The Australian Star</a></i>. Through this proximity to newspapers, books and writers he furthered his education and developed an interest in politics and became active in the printing union. He married <a href="/wiki/Ada_Watson" title="Ada Watson">Ada Jane Low</a>, a British-born Sydney seamstress, at the <a href="/wiki/Unitarianism" title="Unitarianism">Unitarian Church</a> on Liverpool Street in Sydney on 27 November 1889.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-treasury_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-treasury-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-apmey_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmey-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-apmaw_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmaw-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Colonial_parliament">Colonial parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Colonial parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the months prior to the <a href="/wiki/1891_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1891 New South Wales colonial election">1891 New South Wales colonial election</a>, Watson was a founding member of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_(New_South_Wales_Branch)" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Labor Party (New South Wales Branch)">Labour Electoral League of New South Wales</a> which stemmed from the nascent <a href="/wiki/Australian_labour_movement" title="Australian labour movement">Australian labour movement</a> and would later develop into the Australian Labour (later Labor) Party. In the election, Labour won the <a href="/wiki/Balance_of_power_(parliament)" title="Balance of power (parliament)">balance of power</a> and provided <a href="/wiki/Confidence_and_supply" title="Confidence and supply">confidence and supply</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Protectionist_Party" title="Protectionist Party">Protectionist Party</a> <a href="/wiki/Minority_government" title="Minority government">minority government</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Premier_of_New_South_Wales" title="Premier of New South Wales">Premier</a> <a href="/wiki/George_Dibbs" title="George Dibbs">George Dibbs</a> which brought down the incumbent <a href="/wiki/Majority_government" title="Majority government">majority government</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Free_Trade_Party" title="Free Trade Party">Free Trade Party</a> led by Premier <a href="/wiki/Henry_Parkes" title="Henry Parkes">Henry Parkes</a>. Watson was an active trade unionist, and became Vice-President of the <a href="/wiki/Labor_Council_of_New_South_Wales" title="Labor Council of New South Wales">Sydney Trades and Labour Council</a> in January 1892.  In June 1892, he settled a dispute between the Trades and Labour Council and the Labour Party and as a result became the president of the council and chairman of the party.  In 1893 and 1894, he worked hard to resolve the debate over the solidarity pledge and established the Labour Party's basic practices, including the sovereignty of the party conference, caucus solidarity, the pledge required of parliamentarians and the powerful role of the extra-parliamentary executive. At the <a href="/wiki/1894_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1894 New South Wales colonial election">1894 colonial election</a> which saw the defeat of the Protectionist Party government, Watson was elected to the <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Assembly" title="New South Wales Legislative Assembly">New South Wales Legislative Assembly</a> for the country seat of <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Young" title="Electoral district of Young">Young</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the <a href="/wiki/1895_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1895 New South Wales colonial election">1895 colonial election</a> the incumbent Free Trade Party minority government led by Premier <a href="/wiki/George_Reid" title="George Reid">George Reid</a> increased their support  but remained several seats short of a majority. Labour at this time had a policy of "support in return for concessions", and Watson voted with his colleagues to strategically provide such legislative support to the incumbent government. Following the <a href="/wiki/1898_New_South_Wales_colonial_election" title="1898 New South Wales colonial election">1898 colonial election</a>, despite a significant swing against the incumbent government, Watson and Labour leader <a href="/wiki/James_McGowen" title="James McGowen">James McGowen</a> decided to allow the incumbent government to remain so that it could complete the work of establishing the <a href="/wiki/Federation_of_Australia" title="Federation of Australia">Federation of Australia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Federation">Federation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Federation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Federation_of_Australia" title="Federation of Australia">Federation of Australia</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chris_Watson_1904.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Chris_Watson_1904.jpg/170px-Chris_Watson_1904.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="217" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Chris_Watson_1904.jpg/255px-Chris_Watson_1904.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Chris_Watson_1904.jpg/340px-Chris_Watson_1904.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3024" data-file-height="3857" /></a><figcaption>Chris Watson</figcaption></figure>
<p>Watson was involved in shaping party policy regarding the movement for <a href="/wiki/Federation_of_Australia" title="Federation of Australia">Federation</a> from 1895, and was one of ten Labour candidates nominated for the Australasian Federal Convention on 4 March 1897, but none of these candidates managed to be elected. The party endorsed Federation, but nevertheless most leading party figures viewed the draft Commonwealth <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia" title="Constitution of Australia">Constitution</a> as undemocratic, and believed that the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Senate</a> as proposed was much too powerful, similar to the anti-reformist Colonial state upper houses and the UK <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>. When the draft was submitted to a referendum on 3 June 1898, Labour opposed it, with Watson prominent in the campaign, and saw the referendum rejected.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson was devoted to the idea of the referendum as an ideal feature of democracy. To ensure that Reid might finally bring New South Wales into national union on an amended draft constitution, Watson helped to negotiate a deal, involving the party executive, that included the nomination of four Labour members to the <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Council" title="New South Wales Legislative Council">New South Wales Legislative Council</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the March 1899 annual party conference, <a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a> and Holman moved to have those arrangements nullified and party policy on Federation changed, thus thwarting Reid's plans. Although rarely known to resort to anger, on this occasion Watson 'jumped to his feet in a most excited manner and in heated tones ... contended ... that they should not interfere with the referendum'. The motion was lost and the four party men were nominated to the council on 4 April. The bill approving the second referendum, to be held on 20 June 1899, was passed on 20 April.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Labour leaders, including Watson opposed the final terms of the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia" title="Constitution of Australia">Commonwealth Constitution</a>. Nonetheless, they could not stop it from going ahead, and Watson, unlike Holman and Hughes, believed that it should be submitted to the people. Nevertheless, Watson joined all but two of the Labour parliamentarians in campaigning against the 'Yes' vote at the referendum. When the Constitution was accepted, he agreed that 'the mandate of the majority will have to be obeyed'.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Federal_parliament">Federal parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Federal parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg/170px-Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="234" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg/255px-Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg/340px-Chris_Watson_portrait_and_autograph.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2640" data-file-height="3636" /></a><figcaption>Signed photograph of Watson</figcaption></figure>
<p>Watson was elected to the new <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Australia" title="Parliament of Australia">federal Parliament of Australia</a> at the inaugural <a href="/wiki/1901_Australian_federal_election" title="1901 Australian federal election">1901 federal election</a>, representing the rural <a href="/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives" title="Australian House of Representatives">House of Representatives</a> rural seat of <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Bland" title="Division of Bland">Bland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-apme_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apme-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson arrived in <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a>, which at the time served as the temporary seat of government, in May 1901. Watson was elected the first leader of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_Caucus" title="Australian Labor Party Caucus">Federal Parliamentary Labor Party</a> (usually known as the Caucus) on 8 May 1901, the day before the opening of the parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-apmfll_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmfll-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson pursued the same policy as Labor had done in New South Wales, where Labor was the smallest of the three parties but held the <a href="/wiki/Balance_of_power_(parliament)" title="Balance of power (parliament)">balance of power</a>. Under Watson, Labor provided <a href="/wiki/Confidence_and_supply" title="Confidence and supply">confidence and supply</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Protectionist_Party" title="Protectionist Party">Protectionist Party</a> <a href="/wiki/Minority_government" title="Minority government">minority governments</a> of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Barton" title="Edmund Barton">Edmund Barton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a> in exchange for legislation enacting the Labour platform, such as the immensely popular <a href="/wiki/White_Australia_policy" title="White Australia policy">White Australia policy</a> which left the <a href="/wiki/Free_Trade_Party" title="Free Trade Party">free Trade Party</a> led by <a href="/wiki/George_Reid" title="George Reid">George Reid</a> to form the <a href="/wiki/Opposition_(Australia)" title="Opposition (Australia)">opposition</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-apmfll_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmfll-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson, as a Labor moderate, genuinely admired Deakin and shared his liberal views on many subjects. Deakin reciprocated this sentiment. Deakin wrote in one of his anonymous articles in a London newspaper that "The Labour section has much cause for gratitude to Mr Watson, the leader whose tact and judgement have enabled it to achieve many of its Parliamentary successes."<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="White_Australia">White Australia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: White Australia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Watson was a <a href="/wiki/White_nationalism" title="White nationalism">white nationalist</a> and <a href="/wiki/White_supremacy" title="White supremacy">white supremacist</a> who played a key role in the creation of the <a href="/wiki/White_Australia_policy" title="White Australia policy">White Australia policy</a>. According to <a href="#CITEREFHearn2007">Hearn (2007)</a>, the "ideal of a white Australia stood at the centre of Watson's political ideology, a touchstone of Australian identity that Watson repeatedly stressed in interviews, speeches and articles". However, some of his biographers have noted that his racial views were widespread among Australians at the time and that all three major parties supported White Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHearn2007357_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHearn2007357-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the debate over what became the <i><a href="/wiki/Immigration_Restriction_Act_1901" title="Immigration Restriction Act 1901">Immigration Restriction Act 1901</a></i>, Watson stated that the issue of racial purity was "the larger and more important one" for the passage of the bill, where some speakers had emphasised concerns over the economic impact of cheap foreign labour. He opposed the government's <a href="/wiki/Dictation_test" class="mw-redirect" title="Dictation test">dictation test</a> provision on the grounds that it could be easily circumvented, and that "education does not eliminate the objectionable qualities of the Baboo Hindoo". He instead sought to explicitly ban any Asian or African from entering Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHearn2007358_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHearn2007358-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> During the same debate he spoke of "racial contamination" and referred to Chinese people using a racial slur,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHearn2007357_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHearn2007357-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> rhetorically asking "whether we would desire that our sisters or our brothers should be married into any of these races to which we object".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHearn2007358_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHearn2007358-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In 1905, Watson drafted a new plank for the ALP platform calling for "an Australian sentiment based upon the maintenance of racial purity". He successfully moved for its adoption at both state and federal conferences, stating that the party should "cleanse their own doorstep with the hope that thus the street would be cleansed".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHearn2007361_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHearn2007361-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister_in_1904">Prime Minister in 1904</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Prime Minister in 1904">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Watson_government" title="Watson government">Watson government</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg/170px-J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="238" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg/255px-J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg/340px-J._C._Watson_-_T._Humphrey_%26_Co_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1410" data-file-height="1973" /></a><figcaption>Watson c. 1904</figcaption></figure>
<p>Labour under Watson doubled their vote at the <a href="/wiki/1903_Australian_federal_election" title="1903 Australian federal election">1903 federal election</a> and continued to hold the <a href="/wiki/Balance_of_power_(parliament)" title="Balance of power (parliament)">balance of power</a> despite all three parties holding about the same number of seats. In April 1904, however, Watson and Deakin fell out over the issue of extending the scope of industrial relations laws concerning the <a href="/wiki/Conciliation" title="Conciliation">Conciliation</a> and <a href="/wiki/Arbitration" title="Arbitration">Arbitration</a> Bill to cover state public servants, the fallout causing Deakin to resign. Reid declined to take office, which saw Watson become the first Labour <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Prime Minister of Australia">Prime Minister of Australia</a>, the world's first Labour head of government at a national level (<a href="/wiki/Anderson_Dawson" title="Anderson Dawson">Anderson Dawson</a> had led a short-lived Labour government in Queensland in December 1899), indeed the world's first <a href="/wiki/Socialist" class="mw-redirect" title="Socialist">socialist</a> or <a href="/wiki/Social_democratic" class="mw-redirect" title="Social democratic">social democratic</a> government at a national level.<sup id="cite_ref-moadoph_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moadoph-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was aged only 37, and remains the youngest Prime Minister in Australia's history.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a> later recalled the first meeting of the Labour Cabinet with characteristic sharp wit:<sup id="cite_ref-apmfnlg_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmfnlg-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Mr Watson, the new Prime Minister entered the room, and seated himself at the head of the table. All eyes were riveted on him; he was worth going miles to see. He had dressed for the part; his Vandyke beard was exquisitely groomed, his abundant brown hair smoothly brushed. His morning coat and vest, set off by dark striped trousers, beautifully creased and shyly revealing the kind of socks that young men dream about; and shoes to match. He was the perfect picture of the statesman, the leader.</p></blockquote>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Watson_Ministry_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="176" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg/440px-Watson_Ministry_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4604" data-file-height="3688" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Watson_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Watson Ministry">Watson Ministry</a>, 1904</figcaption></figure>
<p>Despite the apparent fitness of the new Prime Minister for his role, the government hung on the fine thread of Deakin's promise of 'fair play'. The triumph of the historic first Australian Labour government was a qualified one – Labour did not have the numbers to implement key policies. The 'three elevens' – the lack of a definite majority in the parliament after the second federal election – dogged Watson just as it had Deakin.<sup id="cite_ref-apmfnlg_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmfnlg-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Six bills were enacted during Watson's brief government. All but one – an amended <i>Acts Interpretation Act 1904</i> – were <a href="/wiki/Money_bill" title="Money bill">supply bills</a>. The most significant legislative achievement of the Watson government was the advancement of the troublesome <a href="/wiki/Conciliation_and_Arbitration_Act_1904" class="mw-redirect" title="Conciliation and Arbitration Act 1904">Conciliation and Arbitration Bill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Another accomplishment was the appointing of a Royal Commission on a Bill related to Navigation and Shipping, whose report (presented a couple of years later) led to "major redrafting of the Navigation Act"<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> and improvements in conditions for Australian seamen.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Once he became the Prime Minister Watson recognized the limitations of his position in the Labour caucus and endorsed the concept of a deputy leader. Andrew Fisher won the position by one vote over the more dynamic Billy Hughes.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Defeat_and_final_years_as_leader">Defeat and final years as leader</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Defeat and final years as leader">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_Christian_Watson.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/John_Christian_Watson.jpg/170px-John_Christian_Watson.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="245" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/John_Christian_Watson.jpg/255px-John_Christian_Watson.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/John_Christian_Watson.jpg/340px-John_Christian_Watson.jpg 2x" data-file-width="929" data-file-height="1341" /></a><figcaption>Watson in 1908</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although Watson sought a dissolution of parliament so that an election could be held, the <a href="/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia" title="Governor-General of Australia">Governor-General</a> <a href="/wiki/Henry_Northcote,_1st_Baron_Northcote" title="Henry Northcote, 1st Baron Northcote">Lord Northcote</a> refused. Unable to command a majority in the House of Representatives, Watson resigned the premiership less than four months after taking office, his term ending on 18 August 1904 (Deakin was later defeated on a similar bill).<sup id="cite_ref-apmio1_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmio1-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Reid became Prime Minister and four months later his government managed to pass the Conciliation and Arbitration Bill after compromising to extend the scope to state public servants as Watson had proposed.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-apmio2_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmio2-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Deakin again became Prime Minister after Reid lost confidence of the parliament in July 1905. Watson led Labour to the <a href="/wiki/1906_Australian_federal_election" title="1906 Australian federal election">1906 federal election</a> and improved their position again. At this election the seat of Bland was abolished, so he shifted to the seat of <a href="/wiki/Division_of_South_Sydney" title="Division of South Sydney">South Sydney</a>. But in October 1907, recognising his work for the foundation of the parliamentary party was complete and also due to fatigue and concern over the health of his wife Ada, he resigned the Labour leadership in favour of <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Fisher" title="Andrew Fisher">Andrew Fisher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From August 1906, Watson was an early influential supporter of <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a>, as the site of the national capital.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson retired from politics, aged only 42, prior to the <a href="/wiki/1910_Australian_federal_election" title="1910 Australian federal election">1910 federal election</a>, at which Labour won with 50 percent of the primary vote. It was the first time a party had been elected to <a href="/wiki/Majority_government" title="Majority government">majority government</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives" title="Australian House of Representatives">House of Representatives</a>, it was also the first time a party won a <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Senate</a> majority, and it was the world's first <a href="/wiki/List_of_Labour_Parties" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Labour Parties">Labour Party</a> majority government at a national level. The ALP vote had risen rapidly, going from 15 percent against two larger and more established parties in 1901, to 50 percent in 1910, after a majority of the Protectionist Party merged with the <a href="/wiki/Anti-Socialist_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Socialist Party">Anti-Socialist Party</a>, creating the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Liberal_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Commonwealth Liberal Party">Commonwealth Liberal Party</a> which received 45 percent.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_activities">Political activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Political activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_split_of_1916" title="Australian Labor Party split of 1916">Australian Labor Party split of 1916</a>, numerous Labor MPs were expelled from the party for supporting <a href="/wiki/World_War_I_conscription_in_Australia" title="World War I conscription in Australia">World War I conscription in Australia</a>. Watson sided with ex-Labor Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Billy_Hughes" title="Billy Hughes">Billy Hughes</a> and the conscriptionists and had his party membership terminated as a result. Watson remained active in the affairs of Hughes' <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nationalist Party of Australia">Nationalist Party</a> until 1922, but after that he drifted out of politics altogether.<sup id="cite_ref-apmao_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmao-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1931 he was state president of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Industries_Protection_League" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Industries Protection League">Australian Industries Protection League</a> and supported the <a href="/wiki/James_Scullin" title="James Scullin">Scullin government</a>'s high-tariff policies.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999148_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999148-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Business_activities">Business activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Business activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chris_Watson,_1915_(John_Longstaff).png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png/170px-Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png" decoding="async" width="170" height="275" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png/255px-Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png/340px-Chris_Watson%2C_1915_%28John_Longstaff%29.png 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="810" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Parliament_House,_Canberra" title="Parliament House, Canberra">Parliament House</a> portrait of Watson by <a href="/wiki/John_Longstaff" title="John Longstaff">John Longstaff</a>, 1915</figcaption></figure>
<p>In December 1910, Watson was recruited by a syndicate of Sydney businessmen to lead a gold-seeking expedition in South Africa. He also engaged in land speculation in <a href="/wiki/Sutherland,_New_South_Wales" title="Sutherland, New South Wales">Sutherland</a>, but development did not occur rapidly enough. He was appointed as a director of Labor Papers Limited, the publisher of the AWU's official newspaper <i><a href="/wiki/The_Australian_Worker" title="The Australian Worker">The Australian Worker</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999120_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999120-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson joined the council of the newly established <a href="/wiki/NRMA#National_Roads_Association" title="NRMA">National Roads Association</a> (NRA) in March 1920,<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and in August was elected as the association's inaugural president. The NRA was intended as a <a href="/wiki/Peak_body" class="mw-redirect" title="Peak body">peak body</a> for New South Wales motorists, lobbying the state government to create a <a href="/wiki/Department_of_Main_Roads_(New_South_Wales)" title="Department of Main Roads (New South Wales)">Main Roads Board</a> and borrow money to improve local highways. It was initially hampered by its small membership and lack of financial resources, with a budget of only £15 per week.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999143_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999143-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Watson was able to attract publicity through the launch of a magazine and a successful campaign to raise <a href="/wiki/Speed_limits" class="mw-redirect" title="Speed limits">speed limits</a>. He was again chosen as president in 1923 when the NRA was reorganised into the <a href="/wiki/NRMA" title="NRMA">NRMA</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999144_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999144-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> and would retain the position for the rest of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999151_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999151-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> By the end of the following year the organisation had grown from 550 to 5,000 members. It subsequently expanded into vehicle insurance and <a href="/wiki/Road_trip" title="Road trip">motor touring</a>, acquiring a series of properties for camping.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999144_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999144-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> As well as his involvement with the <a href="/wiki/NRMA" title="NRMA">NRMA</a>, Watson also was a director of a taxi company, Yellow Cabs of Australia, and as chairman of the state government's Traffic Advisory Committee. In 1936 he became the inaugural chairman of petrol retailer <a href="/wiki/Ampol" title="Ampol">Ampol</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999148_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999148-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Watson's first wife Ada died in 1921.<sup id="cite_ref-apmao_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-apmao-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> On 30 October 1925 he married Antonia Mary Gladys Dowlan in the same church as his first wedding. She was a 23-year-old waitress from Western Australia whom he had met when she served his table at a Sydney club.<sup id="cite_ref-moadoph_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moadoph-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 1927, they had one daughter, Jacqueline Dunn née Watson.<sup id="cite_ref-jac1_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jac1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-jac2_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jac2-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Watson and his second wife moved to a villa in <a href="/wiki/Double_Bay" class="mw-redirect" title="Double Bay">Double Bay</a> in 1934. In retirement he became a keen <a href="/wiki/Contract_bridge" title="Contract bridge">bridge</a> player and was also a regular attendee at <a href="/wiki/Randwick_Racecourse" title="Randwick Racecourse">Randwick Racecourse</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Sydney_Cricket_Ground" title="Sydney Cricket Ground">Sydney Cricket Ground</a>, serving on the <a href="/wiki/Sydney_Cricket_Ground_Trust" title="Sydney Cricket Ground Trust">Sydney Cricket Ground Trust</a>. He visited the United States for business reasons and also returned to New Zealand on a number of occasions in a private capacity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999147–149_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999147–149-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> After several weeks of ill health, Watson died at his home in Double Bay on 18 November 1941, aged 74.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999149_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999149-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> He was granted a <a href="/wiki/State_funeral" title="State funeral">state funeral</a> at <a href="/wiki/St_Andrew%27s_Cathedral,_Sydney" title="St Andrew&#39;s Cathedral, Sydney">St Andrew's Cathedral</a>, with <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Cook" title="Joseph Cook">Joseph Cook</a>, <a href="/wiki/Albert_Gardiner" title="Albert Gardiner">Albert Gardiner</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Curtin" title="John Curtin">John Curtin</a> and <a href="/wiki/William_McKell" title="William McKell">William McKell</a> serving as pallbearers. His ashes were interred at the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Suburbs_Crematorium" class="mw-redirect" title="Northern Suburbs Crematorium">Northern Suburbs Crematorium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999151_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrassbyOrdonez1999151-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Evaluation_and_honours">Evaluation and honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chris_Watson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Evaluation and honours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chris_Watson_bust.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Chris_Watson_bust.jpg/170px-Chris_Watson_bust.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Chris_Watson_bust.jpg/255px-Chris_Watson_bust.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Chris_Watson_bust.jpg/340px-Chris_Watson_bust.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>Bust of Chris Watson by sculptor Wallace Anderson located in the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Ministers_Avenue" title="Prime Ministers Avenue">Prime Ministers Avenue</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ballarat_Botanical_Gardens" title="Ballarat Botanical Gardens">Ballarat Botanical Gardens</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Percival_Serle" title="Percival Serle">Percival Serle</a>, Watson "left a much greater impression on his time than this would suggest. He came at the right moment for his party, and nothing could have done it more good than the sincerity, courtesy and moderation which he always showed as a leader".<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Deakin" title="Alfred Deakin">Alfred Deakin</a> wrote of Watson: "The Labour section has much cause for gratitude to Mr Watson, the leader whose tact and judgement have enabled it to achieve many of its Parliamentary successes".<sup id="cite_ref-adb_12-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 2004 the Labor Party marked the centenary of the Watson government with a series of public events in <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a> and Melbourne, attended by then party leader <a href="/wiki/Mark_Latham" title="Mark Latham">Mark Latham</a> and former ALP Prime Ministers <a href="/wiki/Gough_Whitlam" title="Gough Whitlam">Gough Whitlam</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bob_Hawke" title="Bob Hawke">Bob Hawke</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_Keating" title="Paul Keating">Paul Keating</a>. Watson's daughter, Jacqueline Dunn, 77, was guest of honour at these functions.  The <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a> suburb <a href="/wiki/Watson,_Australian_Capital_Territory" title="Watson, Australian Capital Territory">Watson</a> and the federal electorate of <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Watson" title="Division of Watson">Watson</a> are named after him. In 1969 he was honoured on a <a href="/wiki/Postage_stamp" title="Postage stamp">postage stamp</a> bearing his portrait issued by <a href="/wiki/Australia_Post" title="Australia Post">Australia Post</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
