					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswitha&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Hereswitha">Hereswitha</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Northumbrian saint</div>
<p>
<b>Hereswith</b> or  <b>Hereswitha</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Hereswiþ</i></span>), also spelt <i>Hereswithe</i>, <i>Hereswyde</i> or <i>Haeresvid</i>, was a 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> saint. She married into the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">East Anglian</a> royal dynasty and afterwards retired to <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> to lead a religious life. Hereswith's sister was <a href="/wiki/Saint_Hilda" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Hilda">Saint Hilda</a> (or Hild), founder of the monastery at Whitby. Details of her life and identity come from <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Historia Ecclesiastica</a></i>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglian_collection" title="Anglian collection">Anglian collection</a></i> and the <i>Lives</i> of <a href="/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria" title="Edwin of Northumbria">Edwin of Northumbria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hilda_of_Whitby" title="Hilda of Whitby">Hilda of Whitby</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hereswith was the great-granddaughter of <a href="/wiki/Aelle_of_Deira" class="mw-redirect" title="Aelle of Deira">Ælle</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Deira" title="Deira">Deira</a> through her father Hereric,<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who was nephew of <a href="/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria" title="Edwin of Northumbria">Edwin of Northumbria</a>. Around 604 <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelfrith" title="Æthelfrith">Æthelfrith</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Bernicia" title="Bernicia">Bernicia</a> took control of Deira. Æthelfrith married Edwin's sister, <a href="/wiki/Acha_of_Deira" title="Acha of Deira">Acha of Deira</a>; Edwin and Hereric went into exile.
</p><p>Hereric was married to Beorhtswith/Breguswith and had two daughters, Hereswith and her younger sister Hild (born around 613).<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> By 610 Edwin was at the court of <a href="/wiki/Cearl_of_Mercia" title="Cearl of Mercia">Cearl of Mercia</a>, where he married Cearl's daughter, Cwenburh and by her had two sons, Osfrith and Eadfrith.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Hereric sought safety in the British kingdom of  <a href="/wiki/Elmet" title="Elmet">Elmet</a>, then ruled by <a href="/wiki/Ceretic_of_Elmet" title="Ceretic of Elmet">Ceretic</a>. While at Elmet, Hereric died by poisoning, perhaps at the instigation of Æthelfrith. Suspicion fell on his host.
</p><p>By around 616, Edwin had moved to the court of <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A6dwald_of_East_Anglia" title="Rædwald of East Anglia">Rædwald of East Anglia</a>. Multiple envoys from Æthelfrith offered bribes and then threats to try to persuade Rædwald to murder his guest or turn him over to them. Rædwald was inclined to do so until admonished by his pagan queen for acting in a manner dishonourable for a king by betraying a trust for fear or greed.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Once Æthelfrith's ambassadors had gone, Rædwald resolved on war.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Rædwald defeated Æthelfrith at <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A6dwald_of_East_Anglia#The_Battle_of_the_River_Idle" title="Rædwald of East Anglia">The Battle of the River Idle</a>. With Æthelfrith defeated, Rædwald installed Edwin as king of Northumbria.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Northumbria">Northumbria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Northumbria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With Edwin now king in Northumbria, Hereric's family became attached to his household. Edwin and <a href="/wiki/Eadbald_of_Kent" title="Eadbald of Kent">Eadbald of Kent</a> were allies at this time, and Edwin arranged to marry Eadbald's sister <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelburh_of_Kent" title="Æthelburh of Kent">Æthelburg</a>. Bede notes that Eadbald would agree to marry his sister to Edwin only if he converted to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-Yorke1990_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorke1990-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Edwin, a pagan, had encountered Christianity at Rædwald court. In 625, she married Edwin as his second wife.
</p><p>Edwin and his household, including Breguswith and her daughters Hereswith and Hild, were baptised by <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_York" title="Paulinus of York">Paulinus</a> at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> in the spring of 627. The family of Rædwald's brother <a href="/wiki/Eni_of_East_Anglia" title="Eni of East Anglia">Eni</a> began to assume power in East Anglia during this time.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="East_Anglia">East Anglia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: East Anglia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It was almost certainly in this period, and probably at Edwin's behest, that Hereswith was married to a son of Eni named Æthilric. It is suggested (but not certain) that Æthilric was the same person as <a href="/wiki/Ecgric_of_East_Anglia" title="Ecgric of East Anglia">Ecgric of East Anglia</a>, who ruled with <a href="/wiki/Sigeberht_of_East_Anglia" title="Sigeberht of East Anglia">Sigeberht of East Anglia</a> during the early 630s, when Christianity was restored to East Anglia. This royal alliance suggests that Æthilric was expected to rule and was either already Christian, or had accepted the faith in consequence of the marriage. Edwin was slain by Cadwallon in about 632: Ecgric and Sigeberht died together fighting the pagan <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercian</a> ruler <a href="/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia" title="Penda of Mercia">Penda</a>, probably in 636, and Ecgric succeeded by a Christian son of Eni named <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna</a>, who ruled until about 654.
</p>
<h2><span id="Hereswith.27s_son"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hereswith's_son">Hereswith's son</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hereswith&#039;s son">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hereswith and Æthilric's son <a href="/wiki/Ealdwulf_of_East_Anglia" title="Ealdwulf of East Anglia">Ealdwulf</a> was possibly born during the late 620s. Ealdwulf ruled East Anglia from 664 to 713, after two other sons of Eni, <a href="/wiki/Aethelhere_of_East_Anglia" class="mw-redirect" title="Aethelhere of East Anglia">Æthelhere</a> (reigned 654) and <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwold_of_East_Anglia" title="Æthelwold of East Anglia">Æthelwold</a> had ruled after Anna. Ealdwulf was therefore then seen as the legitimate heir of the <a href="/wiki/Wuffingas" title="Wuffingas">Wuffingas</a> household.
</p>
<h2><span id="Hereswith.27s_departure_for_Chelles"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hereswith's_departure_for_Chelles">Hereswith's departure for Chelles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Hereswith&#039;s departure for Chelles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the 640s, Hereswith's sister Hild received teaching from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Aidan_of_Lindisfarne" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Aidan of Lindisfarne">Saint Aidan of Lindisfarne</a>. About 647, she travelled to the court of Anna to join her sister Hereswith in East Anglia. However, Hereswith had already left to live a religious life and as there was then no nunnery in her kingdom she travelled to Gaul and (according to <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>) lived at <a href="/wiki/Chelles_Abbey" title="Chelles Abbey">Chelles Abbey</a>, where there was a royal oratory. Hereswith remained in Gaul for the rest of her life.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Confusions_of_identity">Confusions of identity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hereswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Confusions of identity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The identity of Hereswith's husband Æthilric is shown in the East Anglian dynastic tally known as the <i>Anglian collection</i> and in the list given in the <a href="/wiki/Historia_Brittonum" title="Historia Brittonum">Historia Brittonum</a>, since Æthilric is in both cases shown as the father of Ealdwulf and Bede states that Hild was Ealdwulf's aunt. It is unlikely that other versions which make her the wife of Æthelhere or of Anna can be correct, since her departure for the religious life in Gaul preceded their deaths. Æthilric was probably dead by 647, prompting Hereswith's retirement, and Ecgric is the only other ruler with whom this son of Eni might be identified. The <i>Anglian collection</i> also lists <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfwald_of_East_Anglia" title="Ælfwald of East Anglia">Ælfwald of East Anglia</a> as the son of Ealdwulf, and not of Athilric and Hereswith, as is sometimes stated.
</p><p>After staying for one year in East Anglia in 647, Hild returned to Northumbria to rule the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Hartlepool" title="Hartlepool">Hartlepool</a> and later founded the royal Northumbrian abbey and mausoleum of <a href="/wiki/Whitby_Abbey" title="Whitby Abbey">Whitby</a>, where Edwin was enshrined.
</p>
