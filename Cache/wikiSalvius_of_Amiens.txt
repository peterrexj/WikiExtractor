<p>Saint <b>Salvius of Amiens</b> (or <b>Sauve, Salin, Salinius, Salve, Salvinus, Sauflieu, Saulve, Sauvre</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;615</span>) was a 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Amiens" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Amiens">bishop of Amiens</a>. His feast day is 11 January.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_legacy">Life and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salvius_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Salvius was said to come from a wealthy family of <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESachy1770_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESachy1770-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>
He studied divinity from  his youth, and led a very pure life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
After his youth, he founded a monastery dedicated to the Virgin Mary in <a href="/wiki/Montreuil,_Pas-de-Calais" class="mw-redirect" title="Montreuil, Pas-de-Calais">Montreuil</a>, and became a monk and then abbot. 
Attracted by solitude, he wanted to retire to a cell, but instead was placed at the head of the diocese of Amiens.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESachy1770_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESachy1770-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
Salvius was Bishop of Amiens at the end of the 6th century.
He assiduously traveled through his diocese, proclaiming to all the word of eternal life, and did much to uproot the last vestiges of paganism from the hearts of his flock.
He built the first cathedral in the center of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_firmin2_amiens.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/St_firmin2_amiens.JPG/220px-St_firmin2_amiens.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/St_firmin2_amiens.JPG/330px-St_firmin2_amiens.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/St_firmin2_amiens.JPG/440px-St_firmin2_amiens.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="960" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_firmin2_amiens.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Discovery of St. Firmin's relics, sculpture in Notre-Dame d'Amiens</div></div></div>
<p>Salvius's body was transported to Montreuil, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Arras" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Arras">Diocese of Arras</a>, where he is still venerated.
The town of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Saulve" title="Saint-Saulve">Saint-Saulve</a> on the northern outskirts of <a href="/wiki/Valenciennes" title="Valenciennes">Valenciennes</a> is named after him.
A triptych in the parish church is dedicated to St Saulve, protector of cattle and crops.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Saulve,_Nominis-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
According to Catholic tradition, Salvius discovered the remains of the martyr <a href="/wiki/Fermin" title="Fermin">Fermin</a> in Abladène, on the outskirts of Amiens, and had them transferred to the city's cathedral.
Sculptures in the choir of the cathedral of Notre-Dame d'Amiens depict the events: Bishop Salvius at the pulpit asking the faithful to find Firmin's body, discovery of the relics at a place called Abladène on the road to <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a>, transport of the body which heals cripples and restores leaves to the wintry trees as it passes.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECathédrale_Notre-Dame_d&#39;Amiens_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECathédrale_Notre-Dame_d&#39;Amiens-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Baring-Gould.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Baring-Gould's_account">Baring-Gould's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salvius_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Baring-Gould&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a> (1834–1924) in his <i>Lives Of The Saints</i> wrote under January 11,
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>S. Salvius, of Amiens, B. C. (about 615.)<br />[Roman Martyrology. There are three bishops, Saints, of this name, one Bishop of Albi, one Bishop of Angouleme, and this one, Bishop of Amiens&#160;; they are often confounded by writers.]
</p><p>S. SALVIUS lived as a monk for many years, in what monastery is not known. He was afterwards elected abbot. Being chosen Bishop of Amiens, he ruled the diocese with prudence, but little or nothing is known of his acts. As he died in an ecstasy, a brilliant light is said to have illuminated his cell, and praying with extended arms, he surrendered his soul.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897160_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897160-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salvius_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a> wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921), 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Salvius (St.) Bp. (Jan 11)<br />(7th cent.) A Bishop of Amiens, in which See he succeeded St. <a href="/wiki/Honoratus_of_Amiens" title="Honoratus of Amiens">Honoratus</a>. He was famous for miracles and for his gift of supernatural prayer. He died A.D. 695<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>. Some years later, his body was transferred to <a href="/wiki/Montreuil,_Pas-de-Calais" class="mw-redirect" title="Montreuil, Pas-de-Calais">Montreuil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921237_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921237-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salvius_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_(d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest)_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg/220px-Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg/330px-Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg/440px-Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_%28d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest%29_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="960" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Amiens_cath%C3%A9drale_(d%C3%A9tail_portail_Ouest)_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Salvius, 3rd from the left, in St Firminius portal of <a href="/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral" title="Amiens Cathedral">Amiens Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> (1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i> under January 11, 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Salvius, or Sauve, Bishop of Amiens
</p><p>Famous for miracles, succeeded Ado in 672, and flourished in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theodoric III</a>. His relics rest at Montreuil, in <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>, in the Benedictin Abbey which bears his name, whither they were translated from the <a href="/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral" title="Amiens Cathedral">cathedral of Amiens</a>, several years after his death, as is related in his anonymous life, a piece of uncertain authority with regard to his actions. A relic of this saint was formerly kept with great veneration in the <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">cathedral of Canterbury</a>, mentioned in the history of that church, &amp;c. This saint must not be confounded with St. <a href="/wiki/Salvius_of_Albi" title="Salvius of Albi">Salvius of Albi</a>, nor with the martyr of this name in Africa, on whose festival <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">St. Austin</a> made a sermon. See his anonymous life in Bollandus; also Baillet, Gall. Christ. Nova, t. 10. p. 1154. This seems the day of his translation, and the 28th of October that of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1812144_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1812144-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
