					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other people named Palladius, see <a href="/wiki/Palladius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Palladius (disambiguation)">Palladius (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pallais_de_Saintes_(cropped).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG/170px-Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="297" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG/255px-Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG/340px-Pallais_de_Saintes_%28cropped%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1501" data-file-height="2621" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pallais_de_Saintes_(cropped).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sculpture of Palladius at the Church of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Palais-de-Phiolin" title="Saint-Palais-de-Phiolin">Saint-Palais-de-Phiolin</a></div></div></div>
<p><b>Palladius</b> or more often in French <b>Pallais</b> was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Saintes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Saintes">bishop of Saintes</a>. According to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, the family of Palladius was wealthy,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and had produced several bishops and teachers throughout the 5th century in Gaul.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Palladius plotted against the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Guntram" title="Guntram">Guntram</a>, whose territory included <a href="/wiki/Saintes,_Charente-Maritime" title="Saintes, Charente-Maritime">Saintes</a>, and sided with his rival <a href="/wiki/Gundoald" title="Gundoald">Gundoald</a>. He consecrated <a href="/w/index.php?title=Faustianus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Faustianus (page does not exist)">Faustianus</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Dax" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Dax">bishop of Dax</a> in 584, at Gundoald's behest.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was chastised by Guntram, who shunned having mass with him, and both the clergy under him and the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan bishop</a> withdrew their support.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_M%C3%A2con" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Mâcon">Second Council of Mâcon</a> in 585, the assembled bishops agreed to depose Faustianus, and fined Palladius and two other prelates an annual amount to support him. Two years later, Palladius plotted with <a href="/wiki/Fredegund" title="Fredegund">Fredegund</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Palladius worked effectively toward building churches and obtaining <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He procured relics of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>, built a church in his honor, and reported several healing miracles to have occurred within the first two or three months.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Palladius also promoted <a href="/wiki/Cult_of_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Cult of saints">saints' cults</a> for Eutropius, the first bishop of Saintes, and for the Abbot Martinus who may have studied with Martin of Tours.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 573, Palladius was a signatory to the <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canons</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Council_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Council of Paris">Fourth Council of Paris</a>, and hosted a council within his own diocese in 573. He is last mentioned in 595.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
