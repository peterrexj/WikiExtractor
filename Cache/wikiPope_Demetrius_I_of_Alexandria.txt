<p><b>Demetrius I</b> (died 22 October 232), 12th <a href="/wiki/List_of_Orthodox_Patriarchs_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Orthodox Patriarchs of Alexandria">Bishop and Patriarch of Alexandria</a>. <a href="/wiki/Sextus_Julius_Africanus" title="Sextus Julius Africanus">Sextus Julius Africanus</a>, who visited <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> in the Bishoprice of Demetrius, places his accession as eleventh bishop from <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Mark</a> in the tenth year of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Commodus" title="Commodus">Commodus</a>; <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius of Caesarea</a> places it in the tenth year of <a href="/wiki/Septimus_Severus" class="mw-redirect" title="Septimus Severus">Septimus Severus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Demetrius_I_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Demetrius was a farmer, who cohabited with his wife as celibates, for 47 years, until he was chosen Patriarch.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i>Coptic Synaxarium</i>, a biographical collection of the Church's saints, the ailing Patriarch Julian had a vision informing him that his successor would visit him, with a cluster of grapes, while out of season at that time of year. The next day, a farmer named Demetrius arrived with a cluster of grapes for the Bishop, asking for his blessings, and was announced next as Bishop Demetrius I, the twelfth bishop of Alexandria.
</p><p>Bishop Demetrius was eager to establish a fixed calendar for church fasts and feast days. He established a liturgical calendar by which fast dates were determined.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As bishop of the great metropolis, Demetrius was engaged in the controversy over the canonical <a href="/wiki/Computus" class="mw-redirect" title="Computus">calculation of Easter</a>. He was the first to apply the calculation method for determining the dates of Easter. His edict was approved by the Nicene Council (325 AD). The Oriental Orthodox churches continue to follow Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-stmark_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stmark-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> claimed that Demetrius sent <a href="/wiki/Pantaenus" title="Pantaenus">Pantaenus</a> on a mission to <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> it is likely that <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria" title="Clement of Alexandria">Clement</a> succeeded Pantaenus as head of the <a href="/wiki/Catechetical_School_of_Alexandria" title="Catechetical School of Alexandria">Catechetical School</a> before the patriarchy of Demetrius. When Clement left Alexandria (c. 203), Demetrius appointed <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>, who was in his eighteenth year, as Clement's successor.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Demetrius supported <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a> in the beginning of his career, it is said to have admired his scholarship. He dispatched <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a> to <a href="/wiki/Arabia_Petraea" title="Arabia Petraea">Arabia</a>, upon an invitation for his visit in letters to the prefect of Alexandria. When the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Caracalla" title="Caracalla">Caracalla</a> sacked Alexandria in 215 AD, Origen fled to <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Palaestina" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Palaestina">Caesarea</a>, where the Palestinean bishops requested him to give sermons. Demetrius was enraged and wrote to rebuke that his teaching was not canonical for him, as a layman. Bishops <a href="/wiki/Alexander_of_Jerusalem" title="Alexander of Jerusalem">Alexander of Jerusalem</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Caesarea">Theoctistus of Caesarea</a> wrote in his defense and mentioned precedents for laymen to give sermons, but despite their efforts Demetrius recalled Origen.<sup id="cite_ref-chapman_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chapman-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 230, Origen was asked to settle a dispute in <a href="/wiki/Achaea_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Achaea (province)">Achaea</a> which required his presence, so he set out by way of <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>. Origen was then ordained priest at Caesarea.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> When Demetrius learned of this, he considered it an act of emancipation, which deteriorated their relationship. Demetrius convened a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> in 232 that banished Origen, then sent a condemnation of Origen's behavior to all the churches. It is evident, it was personal jealousy not merely non ordination, that have been alleged by Demetrius for such a reaction.<sup id="cite_ref-chapman_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chapman-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Rome accepted the decision, but Caesarea, <a href="/wiki/Phoenicia" title="Phoenicia">Phoenicia</a>, Arabia, Achaea disputed it. From Caesarea Origen sent forth letters in his self defence, and attacked Demetrius.
</p><p>Demetrius then passed Catechetical School under the charge of <a href="/wiki/Heraclas_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Heraclas of Alexandria">Heraclas</a>, an assistant of Origen, who had long been his associate. This may have been Demetrius' final act as bishop. Demetrius governed the Church of Alexandria for forty-two years,<sup id="cite_ref-woods_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-woods-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and died at the age of 105.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
