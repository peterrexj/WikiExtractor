<p><b>David Lewis</b>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">S.J.</a> (1616 – 27 August 1679) was a <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">Catholic priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who was also known as <b>Charles Baker</b>. Lewis was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1970 as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> and is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 27 August.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=David_Lewis_(Jesuit_priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lewis, the youngest of nine children of <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a> Reverend Morgan Lewis, the headmaster of a grammar school, and Margaret Pritchard, a Catholic, was born at <a href="/wiki/Abergavenny" title="Abergavenny">Abergavenny</a>, <a href="/wiki/Monmouthshire_(historic)" title="Monmouthshire (historic)">Monmouthshire</a>, in 1616.<sup id="cite_ref-WBO_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WBO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His cousin was another future martyr – <a href="/wiki/John_Kemble_(martyr)" title="John Kemble (martyr)">John Kemble</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At 16 years of age, while visiting <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, he converted to <a href="/wiki/Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism">Catholicism</a> from Anglicanism and subsequently went to study at the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Rome" title="English College, Rome">English College, Rome</a>, where he assumed the alias "Charles Baker", a common practice among Catholic priests to avoid spies and informers in the employ of the Crown. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> on 20 July 1642. Three years later, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He returned to his native land  for a year. Then he was appointed spiritual director to the seminarians at the English College in Rome. After his mission in Rome had been completed, he came back to Monmouthshire and worked for 30 years in the apostolate paying much attention to the poor people and people in need.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Due to this, he was known as <i>Tad y Tlodion</i> ("Father of the Poor").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

</p>
</div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:282px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_David_Lewis.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Saint_David_Lewis.jpg/280px-Saint_David_Lewis.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="351" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Saint_David_Lewis.jpg/420px-Saint_David_Lewis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Saint_David_Lewis.jpg/560px-Saint_David_Lewis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1068" data-file-height="1340" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_David_Lewis.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Oil painting after the engraving, <a href="/wiki/Llantarnam_Abbey" title="Llantarnam Abbey">Llantarnam Abbey</a></div></div></div>
<p>He was arrested on 17 November 1678 at <a href="/wiki/St_Michael%27s_Church,_Llantarnam" title="St Michael&#39;s Church, Llantarnam">St Michael's Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Llantarnam" title="Llantarnam">Llantarnam</a>, then in Monmouthshire, and condemned at the <a href="/wiki/Assizes" title="Assizes">Assizes</a> in <a href="/wiki/Monmouth" title="Monmouth">Monmouth</a> in March 1679 as a Catholic priest and for saying Catholic <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a>. He was accused of attempting to kill Charles II and trying to restore the Catholic faith in Wales. He was betrayed by an apostate couple who had been promised an award of 50 pounds for the Jesuit's capture, and another sum of 200 pounds was promised by a Welsh magistrate to those who could help in his exposure.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Like <a href="/wiki/John_Wall_(priest_and_martyr)" title="John Wall (priest and martyr)">John Wall</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Kemble_(martyr)" title="John Kemble (martyr)">John Kemble</a>, he was then sent to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> to be examined by <a href="/wiki/Titus_Oates" title="Titus Oates">Titus Oates</a> (the originator of the <a href="/wiki/Popish_Plot" title="Popish Plot">Popish Plot</a>) and other informers.
</p><p>He was brought for <a href="/wiki/Trial" title="Trial">trial</a> at the Lenten Assizes in Monmouth on 16 March 1679, on a charge of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a> – for having become a Catholic priest and then remaining in England, contrary to the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits,_etc._Act_1584" title="Jesuits, etc. Act 1584">Jesuits, etc. Act 1584</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Roberts_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roberts-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He pled not guilty to the charge of being an <a href="/wiki/Accessory_(legal_term)" title="Accessory (legal term)">accessory</a> to the Popish Plot. However, five or six witnesses claimed they had seen him say Mass and perform other priestly duties. For this, Lewis was found guilty and sentenced to death by Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Atkyns_(judge)" title="Robert Atkyns (judge)">Robert Atkyns</a>. The condemned priest was brought to <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a> in London with John Kemble (Herefordshire) and questioned about the "plot". Oates and his fellow informers  <a href="/wiki/William_Bedloe" title="William Bedloe">William Bedloe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Dugdale" title="Stephen Dugdale">Stephen Dugdale</a> and  <a href="/wiki/Miles_Prance" title="Miles Prance">Miles Prance</a>  were unable to prove anything against him. <a href="/wiki/Lord_Shaftesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Shaftesbury">Lord Shaftesbury</a> advised him that if he gave evidence about the "plot" or renounced his <a href="/wiki/Catholic_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic faith">Catholic faith</a>, his life would be spared and he would be greatly rewarded. Lewis said in his dying speech, "discover the plot I could not, as I knew of none; and conform I would not, for it was against my conscience".<sup id="cite_ref-Roberts_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roberts-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> His last words before execution were:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I speak not as a murderer, thief or such-like malefactor, but as a Christian, and therefore am not ashamed.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:232px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/1/10/Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg/230px-Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg" decoding="async" width="230" height="307" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/1/10/Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg/345px-Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/1/10/Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg/460px-Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1944" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Grave_of_St_David_Lewis.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Grave of St David Lewis</div></div></div>
<p>He was brought back to <a href="/wiki/Usk" title="Usk">Usk</a> in Monmouthshire for his execution by <a href="/wiki/John_Arnold_of_Monmouthshire" title="John Arnold of Monmouthshire">John Arnold of Monmouthshire</a>, prayed at the <a href="/wiki/Gunter_Mansion" title="Gunter Mansion">Gunter Mansion</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Hanging" title="Hanging">hanged</a> on 27 August 1679 and then posthumously <a href="/wiki/Disembowelment" title="Disembowelment">disemboweled</a>. It was a tribute to the esteem in which he was held that the crowd, mainly Protestants, insisted that he be allowed to hang until he was dead and receive a proper burial.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The Sheriff, who knew and liked Lewis, refused to attend the execution, which he had postponed for as long as he could.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the Titus Oates affair (1679–80), the remaining Welsh-speaking Catholic clergy were either executed or exiled. Lewis was the last Welshman to become a Jesuit until 2001, more than 300 years later.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Recognition">Recognition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=David_Lewis_(Jesuit_priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Recognition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Together with John Wall, John Kemble and 37 other martyrs, David Lewis was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Paul_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul VI">Paul VI</a> in 1970 – the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. In November 2007, a plaque was erected on the spot where Lewis was arrested near <a href="/wiki/Llantarnam_Abbey" title="Llantarnam Abbey">Llantarnam Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic church in Usk is <a href="/wiki/St_David_Lewis_and_St_Francis_Xavier_Church,_Usk" title="St David Lewis and St Francis Xavier Church, Usk">St David Lewis and St Francis Xavier Church</a>.
</p>
