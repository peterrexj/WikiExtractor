<p><b>Ingrid of Sweden</b> (Ingrid Victoria Sofia Louise Margareta; 28 March 1910 – 7 November 2000) was <a href="/wiki/List_of_Danish_consorts" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Danish consorts">Queen of Denmark</a> from 1947 until 1972 as the wife of <a href="/wiki/King_Frederick_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="King Frederick IX">King Frederick IX</a>.
</p><p>Born into the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Bernadotte" title="House of Bernadotte">House of Bernadotte</a>, she was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf" title="Gustaf VI Adolf">King Gustaf VI Adolf of Sweden</a> and his first wife, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Margaret_of_Connaught" title="Princess Margaret of Connaught">Princess Margaret of Connaught</a>. In 1935, she married Crown Prince Frederick of Denmark and they had three daughters: <a href="/wiki/Margrethe_II_of_Denmark" title="Margrethe II of Denmark">Margrethe</a>, the current Queen of Denmark; <a href="/wiki/Princess_Benedikte_of_Denmark" title="Princess Benedikte of Denmark">Benedikte</a>, now a Princess of <a href="/wiki/Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg" title="Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg">Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Anne-Marie_of_Greece" title="Queen Anne-Marie of Greece">Anne-Marie</a>, the former <a href="/wiki/List_of_Greek_royal_consorts" title="List of Greek royal consorts">Queen of the Hellenes</a>.
</p><p>In 1947, her husband became king on his father's death. As queen, Ingrid reformed the traditions of Danish court life, abolished many old-fashioned customs at court and created a more relaxed atmosphere at official receptions. King Frederick IX died in 1972, and Ingrid's daughter Margrethe became queen.
</p><p>She was also a paternal aunt of the present Swedish monarch, <a href="/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf" title="Carl XVI Gustaf">King Carl XVI Gustaf</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Crown_Prince_Gustav_(VI)_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg/220px-Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg/330px-Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg/440px-Crown_Prince_Gustav_%28VI%29_Adolf_of_Sweden_w_fam_07729v.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1875" data-file-height="1914" /></a><figcaption>Princess Ingrid (far right) with her father, mother and three brothers in 1912.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Princess Ingrid was born on 28 March 1910, at the <a href="/wiki/Stockholm_Palace" title="Stockholm Palace">Royal Palace</a> in <a href="/wiki/Stockholm" title="Stockholm">Stockholm</a> as the third child and only daughter of <a href="/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="Gustaf VI Adolf of Sweden">Gustaf Adolf, Crown Prince of Sweden</a> and his first wife, Princess <a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Connaught" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret of Connaught">Margaret of Connaught</a>. Her mother was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Victoria" title="Queen Victoria">Queen Victoria</a>'s third son <a href="/wiki/Prince_Arthur,_Duke_of_Connaught_and_Strathearn" title="Prince Arthur, Duke of Connaught and Strathearn">Prince Arthur, Duke of Connaught and Strathearn</a> by his wife <a href="/wiki/Princess_Louise_Margaret_of_Prussia" title="Princess Louise Margaret of Prussia">Princess Louise Margaret of Prussia</a>.
</p><p>She was baptised Ingrid Victoria Sofia Louise Margareta in Slottskyrkan (the Royal Chapel) in Stockholm, Sweden on 5 May 1910. Her godparents were: the <a href="/wiki/Gustaf_V" title="Gustaf V">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Victoria_of_Baden" title="Victoria of Baden">Queen of Sweden</a> (her paternal grandparents); the <a href="/wiki/Sophia_of_Nassau" title="Sophia of Nassau">Dowager Queen of Sweden</a> (her paternal great-grandmother); the <a href="/wiki/Prince_Arthur,_Duke_of_Connaught_and_Strathearn" title="Prince Arthur, Duke of Connaught and Strathearn">Duke</a> and <a href="/wiki/Princess_Louise_Margaret_of_Prussia" title="Princess Louise Margaret of Prussia">Duchess</a> of Connaught and Strathearn (her maternal grandparents); the <a href="/wiki/Princess_Louise_of_Prussia" title="Princess Louise of Prussia">Dowager Grand Duchess of Baden</a> (her paternal great-grandmother); the <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_Feodorovna_(Alix_of_Hesse)" title="Alexandra Feodorovna (Alix of Hesse)">Empress of Russia</a> (her mother's paternal first cousin); <a href="/wiki/Princess_Alice,_Countess_of_Athlone" title="Princess Alice, Countess of Athlone">Princess Alexander of Teck</a> (her mother's paternal first cousin); the <a href="/wiki/George_V" title="George V">Prince of Wales</a> (her mother's paternal first cousin); <a href="/wiki/Prince_Adalbert_of_Prussia_(1884%E2%80%931948)" title="Prince Adalbert of Prussia (1884–1948)">Prince Adalbert of Prussia</a> (her maternal second cousin); the <a href="/wiki/Princess_Hilda_of_Nassau" title="Princess Hilda of Nassau">Grand Duchess of Baden</a> (her grandaunt); and the <a href="/wiki/Princess_Therese_of_Saxe-Altenburg" title="Princess Therese of Saxe-Altenburg">Dowager Duchess of Dalarna</a> (her great-grandaunt).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ingrid and her family lived in apartments in the Royal Palace in Stockholm, in a mansion at <a href="/wiki/Ulriksdal_Palace" title="Ulriksdal Palace">Ulriksdal</a>, near the capital, and in a summer residence, <a href="/wiki/Sofiero_Palace" title="Sofiero Palace">Sofiero Palace</a> in <a href="/wiki/Helsingborg_Municipality" title="Helsingborg Municipality">Helsingborg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scania" title="Scania">Scania</a> in southern Sweden.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Crown Princess Margaret founded a school for Ingrid with a small circle of Swedish noble girls. Ingrid was also given some domestic instruction as part of her education. As a child, she practiced cooking in her model cottage on the palace grounds and even washed the dishes after meals. The ability for a girl to cook, sew, and manage a household was seen as important at the time for royalty.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In 1920, when Ingrid was just ten years old, her mother died from <a href="/wiki/Sepsis" title="Sepsis">sepsis</a> while in the eighth month of her sixth pregnancy.<sup id="cite_ref-timesobit_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timesobit-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-The_Guardian_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-The_Guardian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After her mother's death, Ingrid spent several months of each year in the United Kingdom in the care of her grandfather.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Observers suggested that Ingrid's strong self-discipline was shaped as an effect of her mother's death.<sup id="cite_ref-The_Guardian_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-The_Guardian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her father remarried <a href="/wiki/Louise_Mountbatten" title="Louise Mountbatten">Lady Louise Mountbatten</a> three years later. Louise was a <a href="/wiki/Second_cousin" class="mw-redirect" title="Second cousin">second cousin</a> of Ingrid's. Only a <a href="/wiki/Stillborn" class="mw-redirect" title="Stillborn">stillborn</a> daughter resulted from her father's second marriage. Ingrid felt betrayed by her father when he remarried, and she was unkind to Crown Princess Louise. Ingrid and her father would not reconcile until many years later.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ingrid was taught <a href="/wiki/History" title="History">history</a>, <a href="/wiki/Art_history" title="Art history">art history</a>, <a href="/wiki/Political_science" title="Political science">political science</a>, and learned several languages. Her knowledge of art and culture was extended by long stays in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. Along with her father, stepmother and brother <a href="/wiki/Prince_Bertil,_Duke_of_Halland" title="Prince Bertil, Duke of Halland">Prince Bertil</a>, Ingrid took a five-month journey through the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a> in 1934 - 1935.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Ingrid interested in sports, especially <a href="/wiki/Horse-riding" class="mw-redirect" title="Horse-riding">horse-riding</a>, <a href="/wiki/Skiing" title="Skiing">skiing</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tennis" title="Tennis">tennis</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Ingrid made her debut at the opening of the <a href="/wiki/Swedish_Riksdag" class="mw-redirect" title="Swedish Riksdag">Swedish Riksdag</a> in 1928 when she was noted to be “smartly dressed.” She was also noted to be an accomplished linguist, horsewoman, skier and skater, and dancer. Ingrid often played tennis against her grandfather King <a href="/wiki/Gustav_V_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="Gustav V of Sweden">Gustav V</a>. During her young adulthood, Ingrid was often seen driving her two-seat car around Stockholm.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Besides gaining a reputation as a stylish young woman, Ingrid was known as being quite attractive. After her visit to the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> in 1939, Americans described Ingrid as "tall and very slender" with a "nicely modeled mouth and exquisite teeth."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg/220px-The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="189" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg/330px-The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/The_newly_married_royal_couple_running_through_the_city.jpg 2x" data-file-width="428" data-file-height="368" /></a><figcaption>The newly married royal couple at their arrival in <a href="/wiki/Copenhagen" title="Copenhagen">Copenhagen</a> in 1935</figcaption></figure>
<p>The question of Ingrid's marriage was a hot topic of conversation in the 1920s. She was matched with various foreign royalties and was seen by some as a possible wife for the heir-apparent to the British throne, the <a href="/wiki/Edward_VIII" title="Edward VIII">Prince of Wales</a>, who was her second cousin.<sup id="cite_ref-dfat_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dfat-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her mother, Margaret of Connaught, and the then-Prince of Wales' father, King George V, were first cousins, both being grandchildren of Queen Victoria. In 1928, Ingrid met the Prince of Wales in London. However, no engagement took place.<sup id="cite_ref-dfat2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dfat2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She was also considered as a match for <a href="/wiki/Prince_George,_Duke_of_Kent" title="Prince George, Duke of Kent">Prince George of the United Kingdom</a>, the fourth son of King George V. 
</p><p>On 15 March 1935, shortly before her 25th birthday, she was engaged to <a href="/wiki/Frederick_IX_of_Denmark" title="Frederick IX of Denmark">Frederick, Crown Prince of Denmark and Iceland</a>. They were related in several ways. As descendants of <a href="/wiki/Oscar_I_of_Sweden" title="Oscar I of Sweden">Oscar I of Sweden</a>, they were third cousins. Through <a href="/wiki/Leopold,_Grand_Duke_of_Baden" title="Leopold, Grand Duke of Baden">Leopold, Grand Duke of Baden</a>, they were third cousins. And finally through <a href="/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia" title="Paul I of Russia">Paul I of Russia</a>, Frederick was a fourth cousin of Ingrid's mother. They married in <a href="/wiki/Storkyrkan" title="Storkyrkan">Stockholm Cathedral</a> on 24 May 1935. Among the wedding guests were the <a href="/wiki/Christian_X_of_Denmark" title="Christian X of Denmark">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexandrine_of_Mecklenburg-Schwerin" title="Alexandrine of Mecklenburg-Schwerin">Queen of Denmark</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Leopold_III_of_Belgium" title="Leopold III of Belgium">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Astrid_of_Sweden" title="Astrid of Sweden">Queen of the Belgians</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Olav_V_of_Norway" title="Olav V of Norway">Crown Prince</a> and <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A4rtha_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="Märtha of Sweden">Crown Princess of Norway</a>. Her wedding was one of the greatest media events of the day in Sweden in 1935, and received so much attention that the media were criticised for it.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2012)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Ingrid also appeared on the radio in 1935 and read a poem, something which was also given much attention.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2012)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Crown_Princess">Crown Princess</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Crown Princess">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:May_9th_1945_(10536964834).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg/220px-May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="158" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg/330px-May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg/440px-May_9th_1945_%2810536964834%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="720" data-file-height="516" /></a><figcaption>Crown Prince Frederick and Crown Princess Ingrid on 9 May 1945, leaving <a href="/wiki/Christiansborg_Palace" title="Christiansborg Palace">Christiansborg Palace</a> in <a href="/wiki/Copenhagen" title="Copenhagen">Copenhagen</a> after the first <a href="/wiki/State_Opening_of_Parliament#Equivalents_in_other_countries" title="State Opening of Parliament">opening</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rigsdagen" title="Rigsdagen">Parliament</a> following the end of <a href="/wiki/German_occupation_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="German occupation of Denmark">Nazi Germany's occupation of Denmark</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>While she was Crown Princess, she was the official patron of the <a href="/wiki/Pigespejdernes_F%C3%A6llesr%C3%A5d_Danmark" title="Pigespejdernes Fællesråd Danmark">Girl Guides</a> (1936), after having taken, and passed, the same tests all applicants were given. In 1940, before the occupation, she was the leader of the <i>Danske Kvinders Beredskab</i> (The Danish Women's war-effort society).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> During the German occupation of Denmark in <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, Ingrid, with her personal courage and integrity, influenced the Danish Royal House and its conduct in relation to the occupation forces, and won great popularity as a symbol of silent resistance and public patriotic moral. She showed solidarity toward the Danish population, and could often be seen on her bicycle or with her baby carriage on the streets of Copenhagen during the war. Her open defiance of the occupation forces made her grandfather, King Gustav of Sweden, worry about the risks, and in 1941, he sent a demand to her to be more discreet "for the sake of the dynasty" and its safety, but she reacted with anger and refused to obey, and she had the support of her spouse, who shared her views. One display of defiance shown by Ingrid was her positioning of the flags of Denmark, Sweden and the United Kingdom in the window of the nursery at <a href="/wiki/Amalienborg" title="Amalienborg">Amalienborg</a>, the royal residence in the centre of Copenhagen.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Queen_consort">Queen consort</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Queen consort">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Kongeparret.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Kongeparret.jpg/220px-Kongeparret.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="151" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Kongeparret.jpg/330px-Kongeparret.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Kongeparret.jpg/440px-Kongeparret.jpg 2x" data-file-width="984" data-file-height="676" /></a><figcaption>King Frederick IX and Queen Ingrid in the 1950s</figcaption></figure>
<p>Upon her husband's accession to the throne on 20 April 1947, she became the Queen of Denmark. As such, she reformed the traditions of Danish court life, abolished many old-fashioned customs at court and created a more relaxed atmosphere at official receptions. She was interested in gardening and art, and renovated the <a href="/wiki/Gr%C3%A5sten_Palace" title="Gråsten Palace">Gråsten Slot</a> according to her own historical research about the palace's original appearance.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<p>In 1972, King Frederick IX died, and Ingrid was widowed at the age of 61. Her elder daughter, aged 31, became the new queen, and Ingrid now assumed a position as family matriarch. That same year, after having sworn to respect the Danish constitution, she was appointed <i>Rigsforstander</i> (formal Regent) and representative of the monarch whenever her daughter (and later her grandsons) were absent, a task she performed on many occasions. This was exceptional; since the constitution of 1871, only the Crown Prince had been allowed to act as regent in the absence of the monarch.
</p><p>She was patron of a long line of social organizations, positions which, one after another, she eventually left to Princess Benedikte as years passed: <i>Røde Kors</i>, <i>Ældre Sagen</i>, <i>Red Barnet</i>, <i>Løgum Klosters Refugium</i>, and <i>Fonden for Træer og Miljø</i>. She also founded the organizations <i>Kong Frederik og Dronning Ingrids fond til humanitære og kulturelle formål, Ingridfondet</i> for South Jutland, <i>Det kgl. Grønlandsfond</i>, and <i>Dronning Ingrids Romerske Fond til støtte af kulturelle og videnskabelige formål</i>. She was described as dutiful, well-prepared and energetic.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg/220px-FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg/330px-FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg/440px-FrederikIX-Gravsten.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>The grave of King Frederick IX and Queen Ingrid at Roskilde</figcaption></figure>
<p>Queen Ingrid died on 7 November 2000 at <a href="/wiki/Fredensborg_Palace" title="Fredensborg Palace">Fredensborg Palace</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fredensborg" title="Fredensborg">Fredensborg</a>, with her three daughters—<a href="/wiki/Margrethe_II_of_Denmark" title="Margrethe II of Denmark">Queen Margrethe II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Benedikte_of_Denmark" title="Princess Benedikte of Denmark">Princess Benedikte</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Anne-Marie_of_Greece" title="Queen Anne-Marie of Greece">Queen Anne-Marie of Greece</a>—and ten grandchildren at her bedside. Thousands gathered outside <a href="/wiki/Amalienborg_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Amalienborg Palace">Amalienborg Palace</a>, her official residence, after her death was announced; flowers were left, candles were lit and hymns were sung in her honour.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Her funeral took place on 14 November 2000, and Ingrid was interred next to her husband, <a href="/wiki/Frederick_IX_of_Denmark" title="Frederick IX of Denmark">King Frederick IX</a>, outside <a href="/wiki/Roskilde_Cathedral" title="Roskilde Cathedral">Roskilde Cathedral</a> near <a href="/wiki/Copenhagen" title="Copenhagen">Copenhagen</a>. The funeral was attended by many crowned heads of Europe and other heads of state, among them the <a href="/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf" title="Carl XVI Gustaf">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Silvia_of_Sweden" title="Queen Silvia of Sweden">Queen of Sweden</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Queen_Sofia_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Sofia of Spain">Queen of Spain</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands" title="Beatrix of the Netherlands">Queen of the Netherlands</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Harald_V_of_Norway" title="Harald V of Norway">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Sonja_of_Norway" title="Queen Sonja of Norway">Queen of Norway</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Albert_II_of_Belgium" title="Albert II of Belgium">King</a> and <a href="/wiki/Queen_Paola_of_Belgium" title="Queen Paola of Belgium">Queen of the Belgians</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Jean_of_Luxembourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duke Jean of Luxembourg">Grand Duke</a> and <a href="/wiki/Princess_Jos%C3%A9phine_Charlotte_of_Belgium" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Joséphine Charlotte of Belgium">Grand Duchess of Luxembourg</a>, the then <a href="/wiki/Charles_III" title="Charles III">Prince of Wales</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Albert_II_of_Monaco" class="mw-redirect" title="Albert II of Monaco">Hereditary Prince of Monaco</a>, the then <a href="/wiki/President_of_Iceland" title="President of Iceland">President of Iceland</a> <a href="/wiki/%C3%93lafur_Ragnar_Gr%C3%ADmsson" title="Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson">Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson</a> and former <a href="/wiki/President_of_Finland" title="President of Finland">President of Finland</a> <a href="/wiki/Mauno_Koivisto" title="Mauno Koivisto">Mauno Koivisto</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Issue">Issue</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingrid_of_Sweden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Issue">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Queen Ingrid and King Frederick IX have three daughters, ten grandchildren and twenty-one great-grandchildren.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Margrethe_II_of_Denmark" title="Margrethe II of Denmark">Margrethe II of Denmark</a> (born 16 April 1940) she married <a href="/wiki/Henrik,_Prince_Consort_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="Henrik, Prince Consort of Denmark">Henri de Laborde de Monpezat</a> on 10 June 1967. They have two sons and eight grandchildren.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Princess_Benedikte_of_Denmark" title="Princess Benedikte of Denmark">Princess Benedikte of Denmark</a> (born 29 April 1944) she married <a href="/wiki/Prince_Richard_of_Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Richard of Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg">Prince Richard of Sayn-Wittgenstein-Berleburg</a> on 3 February 1968. They have three children and five grandchildren.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Queen_Anne-Marie_of_Greece" title="Queen Anne-Marie of Greece">Queen Anne-Marie of Greece</a> (born 30 August 1946) she married <a href="/wiki/King_Constantine_II_of_Greece" class="mw-redirect" title="King Constantine II of Greece">King Constantine II of Greece</a> on 18 September 1964. They have five children and nine grandchildren.</li></ul>
