<p><b>Brocard</b> is said to have been one of the first leaders of <a href="/wiki/Hermits" class="mw-redirect" title="Hermits">hermits</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mount_Carmel" title="Mount Carmel">Mount Carmel</a>, and was perhaps the leader of the community on the death of <a href="/wiki/Berthold_of_Calabria" title="Berthold of Calabria">Berthold of Calabria</a> around 1195. Various details of his life are legendary.<sup id="cite_ref-staring_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staring-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Brocard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brocard (or Burchard, as he is sometimes called), was of French ancestry and a hermit monk at Mount Carmel. The superior of this community, Berthold, died about the year 1195 and Brocard was elected superior.<sup id="cite_ref-desert_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-desert-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1207 Brocard approached <a href="/wiki/Albert_Avogadro" class="mw-redirect" title="Albert Avogadro">Albert Avogadro</a>, the papal legate and <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem">Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem</a>, for assistance in developing a rule of common life for the monks on Carmel. Albert was a <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a> and composed a brief, 16 chapter rule, for the community.<sup id="cite_ref-desert_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-desert-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The document is addressed to a community member known only as 'B' (traditionally associated with Brocard, although no historical records exist that clearly identify this individual's full name). Receiving the Rule marks the origin of the <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> Order.
</p><p>Tradition says that Brocard was well-versed in scripture and that Albert planned to take him to the next Lateran Council, but was murdered before the Council took place.<sup id="cite_ref-desert_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-desert-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Brocard died around 1231. His cult was ordered by the general chapter of 1564. It was removed from the reformed breviary of 1585, but taken up again in 1609; and the proper lessons were approved by the S. Congregation of Rites in 1672. His feast has been again suppressed.<sup id="cite_ref-staring_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staring-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
