<p><b>Vincent Ferrer</b>, OP (<a href="/wiki/Valencian_language" title="Valencian language">Valencian</a>: <i lang="ca-valencia">Sant Vicent Ferrer</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="ca-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Catalan" title="Help:IPA/Catalan">[ˈsam viˈsɛm feˈreɾ]</a></span>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Vicente Ferrer</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Vincenzo Ferreri</i>, <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Sankt Vinzenz Ferrer</i>, <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Sint-Vincent Ferrer</i>, <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Vincent Ferrier</i>; 23 January 1350 – 5 April 1419) was a <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Valencia" title="Kingdom of Valencia">Valencian</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a>, who gained acclaim as a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Logician" class="mw-redirect" title="Logician">logician</a>. He is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and other churches of Catholic traditions.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg/170px-San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="127" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg/255px-San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg/340px-San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2848" data-file-height="2134" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Vicent_Casa_Natalicia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Inside the birthhouse of Vincent Ferrer, <a href="/wiki/Valencia" title="Valencia">Valencia</a></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_(Val%C3%A8ncia).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg/220px-207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg/330px-207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg/440px-207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_%28Val%C3%A8ncia%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2112" data-file-height="2816" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:207_Sant_Esteve_des_de_la_pl._Sant_Llu%C3%ADs_Bertran_(Val%C3%A8ncia).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Iglesia_de_San_Esteban,_Valencia" class="mw-redirect" title="Iglesia de San Esteban, Valencia">Iglesia de San Esteban</a> in Valencia, where Vincent Ferrer was baptized</div></div></div>
<p>Vincent was the fourth child of Guillem Ferrer, a notary from <a href="/wiki/Palam%C3%B3s" title="Palamós">Palamós</a>, and his wife, Constança Miquel, apparently from <a href="/wiki/Valencia" title="Valencia">Valencia</a> itself or <a href="/wiki/Girona" title="Girona">Girona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Legends surround Vincent's birth. It was said that his father was told in a dream by a Dominican friar that his son would be famous throughout the world. His mother is said never to have experienced pain when she gave birth to him. He was named after <a href="/wiki/Vincent_of_Saragossa" title="Vincent of Saragossa">Vincent Martyr</a>, the patron saint of Valencia.<sup id="cite_ref-minnaji_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-minnaji-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He would fast on Wednesdays and Fridays and distribute alms to the poor. He began his classical studies at the age of eight, and his study of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and philosophy at fourteen.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Four years later, at the age of eighteen, Ferrer entered the Order of Preachers,<sup id="cite_ref-cna_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> commonly called the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> (in England also known as "Black Friars" because of the black cloak they wear over their white habits<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>). As soon as he had entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Order, though, he experienced temptations urging him to leave. Even his parents pleaded with him to do so and become a <a href="/wiki/Secular_clergy" title="Secular clergy">secular priest</a>. He prayed and practiced penance to overcome these trials. Thus he succeeded in completing the year of probation and advancing to his <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">profession</a>.
</p><p>For a period of three years, he read solely <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Sacred Scripture</a> and eventually committed it to memory. He published a treatise on Dialectic Suppositions after his solemn profession, and in 1379 was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> at Barcelona. He eventually became a <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Sacred_Theology" title="Master of Sacred Theology">Master of Sacred Theology</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Commission_(document)" title="Commission (document)">commissioned</a> by the Order to deliver lectures on philosophy. He was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> and eventually to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Lleida" title="University of Lleida">University of Lleida</a>, where he earned his <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Theology" title="Doctor of Theology">doctorate in theology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn.com_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn.com-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent Ferrer is described as a man of medium height, with a lofty forehead and very distinct features. His hair was fair in color and tonsured. His eyes were very dark and expressive; his manner gentle. Pale was his ordinary color. His voice was strong and powerful, at times gentle, resonant, and vibrant.<sup id="cite_ref-minnaji_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-minnaji-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Western_Schism">Western Schism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Western Schism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_(Brech)_4240.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG/220px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="308" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG/330px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG/440px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_%28Brech%29_4240.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2626" data-file-height="3675" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_Saint-Andr%C3%A9_(Brech)_4240.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Vincent Ferrer, Église Saint-André (Brech)</div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> (1378–1417) divided Catholicism between two, then eventually three, claimants to the papacy. <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">Antipope</a> Clement VII lived at Avignon in France, and Pope Urban VI in Rome. Vincent was convinced that the election of Urban was invalid, although <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> was just as devoted a supporter of the Roman pope. In the service of Cardinal Pedro de Luna, Vincent worked to persuade Spaniards to follow Clement. When Clement died in 1394, Cardinal de Luna was elected as the second antipope successor to the <a href="/wiki/Avignon_papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Avignon papacy">Avignon papacy</a> and took the name Benedict XIII.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent and his brother Boniface, General of the Carthusians, were loyal to <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Benedict_XIII" title="Antipope Benedict XIII">Benedict XIII</a>, commonly known as "Papa Luna" in Castile and Aragon.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He worked for Benedict XIII as apostolic penitentiary and Master of the Sacred Palace.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Nonetheless Vincent labored to have Benedict XIII end the schism.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn.com_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn.com-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> When Benedict XIII did not resign as intended at either the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Pisa" title="Council of Pisa">Council of Pisa</a> (1409) or the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Constance" title="Council of Constance">Council of Constance</a> (1414–1418), he lost the support of the French king and of most of his cardinals, and was excommunicated as a schismatic in 1417.
</p><p>Vincent later claimed that the Western Schism had had such a depressing effect on his mind that it caused him to be seriously ill.<sup id="cite_ref-Hogan_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hogan-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_gifts_and_missionary_work">Religious gifts and missionary work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious gifts and missionary work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>For twenty-one years he was said to have traveled to <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aragon" title="Aragon">Aragon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Castile_(historical_region)" title="Castile (historical region)">Castile</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, preaching the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> and converting many. Many biographers believe that he could speak only <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>, but was endowed with the <a href="/wiki/Gift_of_tongues" class="mw-redirect" title="Gift of tongues">gift of tongues</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was a noted preacher. Though he himself was an intellectual, his preaching style has been described as "innovative in that it incorporated a popular tone and rhetorical directness into the (by then traditional) Scholastic, thematic sermon structure".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He preached to <a href="/wiki/Colette_of_Corbie" title="Colette of Corbie">Colette of Corbie</a> and her <a href="/wiki/Nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuns">nuns</a>, and it was she who told him that he would die in France. Too ill to return to Spain, he did, indeed, die in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> in 1419. Breton fishermen still invoke his aid in storms, and in Spain he is the patron of orphanages.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion_of_Jews_and_controversy">Conversion of Jews and controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Conversion of Jews and controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent is said to have been responsible for the conversion of many <a href="/wiki/Jews" title="Jews">Jews</a> to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>, often by questionable means according to the Jewish Encyclopedia; for instance, he is said to have made their lives difficult until they converted and to have "dedicated" synagogues as churches on the basis of his own authority.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> One of his converts, a former <a href="/wiki/Rabbi" title="Rabbi">rabbi</a> by the name of <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Burgos" title="Paul of Burgos">Solomon ha-Levi</a>, went on to become the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cartagena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cartagena">Bishop of Cartagena</a> and later the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Burgos" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Burgos">Archbishop of Burgos</a>. Vincent is alleged to have contributed to <a href="/wiki/Antisemitism_in_Spain" title="Antisemitism in Spain">anti-Semitism in Spain</a>, as violence accompanied his visits to towns that had Jewish communities.<sup id="cite_ref-books.google.com_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-books.google.com-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Because of the Spanish's <a href="/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Spain#1391.E2.80.931492" title="History of the Jews in Spain">methods of converting Jews at the time</a>, the means which Vincent had at his disposal were either baptism or <a href="/wiki/Looting" title="Looting">spoliation</a>. According to the Catholic Encyclopedia, he won them over by his preaching, estimated at 25,000.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sources are contradictory concerning Vincent's achievement in converting a synagogue in <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a>, Spain, into the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Blanca" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa María la Blanca">Santa María la Blanca</a>. One source says he preached to the mobs whose riots led to the appropriation of the synagogue and its transformation into a church in 1391;<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> a second source says he converted the Jews of the city who then changed the synagogue to a church after they embraced the Faith, but hints at the year 1411.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn.com_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn.com-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> A third source identifies two distinct incidents, one in Valencia in 1391 and one in Toledo at a later date, but says that Vincent put down an uprising against Jews in one place and defused a persecution against them in the other.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Vincent also attended the <a href="/wiki/Disputation_of_Tortosa" title="Disputation of Tortosa">Disputation of Tortosa</a> (1413–14), called by Avignon Pope Benedict XIII in an effort to convert Jews to Catholicism after a debate among scholars of both religions.<sup id="cite_ref-books.google.com_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-books.google.com-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Compromise_of_Caspe">Compromise of Caspe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Compromise of Caspe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent participated in the management of a significant political crisis in his homeland. King <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Aragon" title="Martin of Aragon">Martin of Aragon</a> died in 1410 without a legitimate heir, and five potential candidates came forth to claim the throne, all with royal bloodlines. It was determined that a committee of nine respected figures, three each from Aragon, Catalonia, and Valencia (the realms comprising the <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon" title="Crown of Aragon">Crown of Aragon</a>), would review the qualifications and select the next king. Vincent was chosen as one of the representatives of Valencia, and he voted for the Castilian prince <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_of_Antequera" class="mw-redirect" title="Ferdinand of Antequera">Ferdinand of Antequera</a>, who became the next King of Aragon.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> On 28 June, Ferrer publicly proclaimed <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Aragon" title="Ferdinand I of Aragon">Ferdinand of Castile</a> as king of Aragon.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The process by which Ferdinand was determined to be the next king is known as the <a href="/wiki/Compromise_of_Caspe" title="Compromise of Caspe">Compromise of Caspe</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent died on 5 April 1419 at <a href="/wiki/Vannes" title="Vannes">Vannes</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, at the age of 69,<sup id="cite_ref-cna_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in <a href="/wiki/Vannes_Cathedral" title="Vannes Cathedral">Vannes Cathedral</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Calixtus_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Calixtus III">Pope Calixtus III</a> on 3 June 1455.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 5 April.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Entities named after him include a pontifical religious institute, the <a href="/wiki/Fraternity_of_Saint_Vincent_Ferrer" title="Fraternity of Saint Vincent Ferrer">Fraternity of Saint Vincent Ferrer</a>, and two Brazilian municipalities, <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente_Ferrer,_Maranh%C3%A3o" title="São Vicente Ferrer, Maranhão">São Vicente Ferrer, Maranhão</a>, and <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente_Ferrer,_Pernambuco" title="São Vicente Ferrer, Pernambuco">São Vicente Ferrer, Pernambuco</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A 50-metre (164-foot) <a href="/wiki/St._Vincent_Ferrer_Statue" title="St. Vincent Ferrer Statue">statue of Ferrer</a> was erected in <a href="/wiki/Bayambang" title="Bayambang">Bayambang</a>, Philippines, in 2019.
</p>
