					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Peter of Atroa</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">AD 773</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">AD 837<br />Atroa, <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a><br />(modern-day <a href="/wiki/Anatolia" title="Anatolia">Anatolia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Peter of Atroa</b> (773–837) was an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> who was later <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Atroa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born the eldest of three children.  His given name was <b>Theophylact</b>.  At eighteen, he determined to become a monk and joined <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_the_Hesychast&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paul the Hesychast (page does not exist)">Paul the Hesychast</a> at his <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermitage</a> in <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a>, where he took the religious name "Peter".
</p><p>The two of them started a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, but, reportedly because of a vision, they went instead to <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a>. There Paul established a monastery at the church of St. Zachary in the area of Atroa. The monastery grew quickly, and Paul named Peter as his successor on the former's death in 805.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Leo_V_the_Armenian" title="Leo V the Armenian">Leo V the Armenian</a> was in power at the time, and supported the <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">Iconoclasts</a> in their efforts to destroy religious imagery. Peter was forced to disband the monastery for the safety of the monks during these persecutions, himself travelling first to <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a> and later <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>. His reputation as a <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">thaumaturge</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a> had become widespread by this time, and he was rarely left to pursue the secluded life he sought in peace. He wandered throughout the area in his efforts to find seclusion, ultimately finding some privacy in the area of <a href="/wiki/Coracesium" class="mw-redirect" title="Coracesium">Coracesium</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a>.
</p><p>Upon the death of Leo V, the iconoclast persecution dissipated, permitting internal struggles within the church to rise to the forefront. One of these involved accusations by several bishops and abbots that Peter <a href="/wiki/Exorcism" title="Exorcism">exorcised</a> <a href="/wiki/Demons" class="mw-redirect" title="Demons">demons</a> not by the power of God, but rather the power of <a href="/wiki/Beelzebub" title="Beelzebub">Beelzebub</a>. Peter tried to convince them otherwise, unsuccessfully, and ultimately had to seek the help of <a href="/wiki/Theodore_the_Studite" title="Theodore the Studite">Theodore the Studite</a> in persuading them. Theodore wrote a letter to all the monks of the area, telling them that Peter was in fact above reproach in conduct, teachings, and beliefs, and was as good a monk as could be found. This letter survives to this day.
</p><p>Peter then returned to St. Zachary's and helped to reorganize two other monasteries he had established. During this time, he himself resided at a hermitage in Atroa. Iconoclastic attacks broke out again, more virulent than before, and Peter again found himself having to disperse his monks, successfully doing so just barely before the local bishop arrived to forcibly remove them. Peter himself left to reside with a locally famous recluse named James. While living with him, Peter cured <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_of_Prusias&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paul of Prusias (page does not exist)">Paul of Prusias</a> of a fever. This was ascribed as a miracle at the time, but was apparently accomplished by doing nothing more than giving Paul a good, nutritious meal.
</p><p>Iconoclast persecution increased in the area, obliging Peter and James to leave for the safety of the monastery of St. Porphyrious on the <a href="/wiki/Hellespont" class="mw-redirect" title="Hellespont">Hellespont</a>. Peter later left for the <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A2lea_Lake" title="Bâlea Lake">Bâlea Lake</a> area, where he visited his friend and fellow opponent of iconoclasm <a href="/wiki/Joannicius_the_Great" title="Joannicius the Great">Joannicius</a> before returning again to St. Zachary's. After delivering a final statement of farewell to the assembly, he died in the <a href="/wiki/Choir_(architecture)" title="Choir (architecture)">choir</a> while praying the evening office on 1 January 837.
</p>
