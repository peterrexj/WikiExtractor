<p><b>John of Tobolsk</b> (1651–1715) was born as <b>Ioann Maksimovich Vasilkovskiy</b> in <a href="/wiki/Nizhyn" title="Nizhyn">Nieżyn</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Czernihow_Voivodeship" class="mw-redirect" title="Czernihow Voivodeship">Czernihow Voivodeship</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth" title="Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth">Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a>. He was the only one of the seven sons of Maksym Wasylkowski Maksymowicz to enter the service of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, in which he was appointed <i><a href="/wiki/Oeconomus" class="mw-redirect" title="Oeconomus">manager</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Kiev_Pechersk_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev Pechersk Lavra">Kiev Pechersk Lavra</a> by 1678. As <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bishop_Theodore_of_Uglich&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bishop Theodore of Uglich (page does not exist)">Bishop Theodore of Uglich</a> wanted someone to succeed him of <a href="/wiki/Chernihiv" title="Chernihiv">Chernigov</a>, he appointed John as <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">Archimandrite</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Eletsky_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Eletsky Monastery">Eletsky Monastery</a> in 1695.  Bishop Theodore of <a href="/wiki/Uglich" title="Uglich">Uglich</a> reposed in 1696 and John became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of Chernigov.
</p><p>During his pastorate in Chernigov, John distinguished himself by operating a spiritual academy, writing prose and poetry inspired by faith, and inspiring faith in others. His most famous work is "Iliotropion", which he translated and adapted into <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic" title="Church Slavonic">Slavonic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a> from the original <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> of the German <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuit</a> priest <a href="/wiki/Jeremias_Drexel" title="Jeremias Drexel">Jeremias Drexel</a>. In the early 21st century, it remains the standard work on <a href="/wiki/Theodicy" title="Theodicy">theodicy</a> among the Eastern Orthodox.
</p><p>In 1711, he was made <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberian</a> city of <a href="/wiki/Tobolsk" title="Tobolsk">Tobolsk</a>, taking the place of Metropolitan Philotheos who wished to carry out <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> work among <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> tribes in remote places.
</p><p>John reposed peacefully in 1715, inside his quarters while at prayer. John was honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in Siberia by longstanding local veneration. In 1916 the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> officially <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">glorified</a> (canonized) him for veneration throughout the church. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is June 10, the anniversary of his repose.
</p><p>John of Tobolsk is the namesake of <a href="/wiki/John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco" title="John of Shanghai and San Francisco">John of Shanghai and San Francisco</a>.
</p>
