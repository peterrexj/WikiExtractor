<p><b>Saint Maturinus</b>, or <b>Mathurin</b><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (died ca. 300 AD<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) was a French <a href="/wiki/Exorcist" title="Exorcist">exorcist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p><p>The first source to mention Maturinus is the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Usuard" title="Martyrology of Usuard">Martyrology of Usuard</a></i>, written in 875.<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In the next century, a biography of Maturinus was composed. According to his legend, Maturinus was born in <i>Liricantus</i> (now <a href="/wiki/Larchant" title="Larchant">Larchant</a>). His parents, Marinus and Euphemia, were <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a>. His father was entrusted by <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> with the task of exterminating the Christians of the region.
</p><p>His son Maturinus, however, was secretly <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Polycarp" title="Polycarp">Polycarp</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> when the boy was twelve. According to legend, he began to perform <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a>, drive out <a href="/wiki/Demon" title="Demon">demons</a>, and calm rowdy or riotous individuals. He eventually converted his parents to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-ab_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ab-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He became a priest at the age of twenty, and took care of the diocese when the local bishop went to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-ab_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ab-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His fame grew, and emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> himself requested that he come to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> so that his daughter-in-law <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Maximiana_Theodora" title="Flavia Maximiana Theodora">Flavia Maximiana Theodora</a>, who had been possessed by an evil spirit, could be cured by the saint. The demon in Theodora's body was expelled. Maturinus lived for three more years in Rome, interceding with the emperor on behalf of persecuted Christians. He died on November 1, and was buried in a Roman cemetery. However, Maturinus miraculously indicated that he wished to be buried in Larchant; his body was taken to <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a> and then to Larchant.<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maturinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg/150px-Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="148" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg/225px-Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/08/Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg/300px-Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2354" data-file-height="2325" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Larchant_-_Basilique_Saint-Mathurin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Basilica of Saint Mathurin in Larchant</div></div></div>
<p>Maturinus' relics were kept at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Mathurin" title="Basilica of Saint Mathurin">Basilica of Saint-Mathurin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Larchant" title="Larchant">Larchant</a>, as well as in the church of Saint-Mathurin in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, situated in the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Quarter" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Quarter">Latin Quarter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Saint-Mathurin de Larchant, a property of the chapter of <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris" title="Notre-Dame de Paris">Notre-Dame de Paris</a> since 1005,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  was rebuilt beginning in 1153, and the church became a popular <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> site, which Harry Bailey, host of the Tabard Inn of Chaucer's <i><a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Tales" class="mw-redirect" title="Canterbury Tales">Canterbury Tales</a></i> was recalling, when he swore "by that precious corpus Madrian".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Both <a href="/wiki/Louis_XI" title="Louis XI">Louis XI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France" title="Henry IV of France">Henry IV</a> visited the church.<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Patricia_Highsmith" title="Patricia Highsmith">Patricia Highsmith</a>'s novel <i><a href="/wiki/The_Boy_Who_Followed_Ripley" title="The Boy Who Followed Ripley">The Boy Who Followed Ripley</a></i> (1980), the two principal characters visit the basilica en route to an outing in the <a href="/wiki/Forest_of_Fontainebleau" title="Forest of Fontainebleau">Forest of Fontainebleau</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maturinus’ cult became popular during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. He was invoked against <a href="/wiki/Mental_illness" class="mw-redirect" title="Mental illness">mental illness</a> and <a href="/wiki/Infertility" title="Infertility">infertility</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and the faculty of medicine of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> kept their great seal safely in the Church of Saint-Mathurin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Due to his association with madness and mental illness, he also became the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of comic actors, <a href="/wiki/Jester" title="Jester">jesters</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Clown" title="Clown">clowns</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was also the patron saint of sailors (in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>), of tinmen (in Paris),<sup id="cite_ref-ppo_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ppo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and of plumbers.<sup id="cite_ref-sb_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sb-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarian</a> friars were known in France as “Mathurins”; this is because the Trinitarians were based in the church of Saint-Mathurin in Paris from 1228 onwards.<sup id="cite_ref-ab_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ab-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His relics were destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sb_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sb-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
