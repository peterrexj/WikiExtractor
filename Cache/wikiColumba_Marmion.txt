<p><b>Columba Marmion, OSB</b>, born <b>Joseph Aloysius Marmion</b> (April 1, 1858 &#8211; January 30, 1923) was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> Irish monk and the third <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Maredsous_Abbey" title="Maredsous Abbey">Maredsous Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. Beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on September 3, 2000, Columba was one of the most popular<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and influential<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Catholic authors of the 20th century. His books are considered spiritual classics.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Early_Years_.281858.E2.80.931886.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_Years_(1858–1886)">Early Years (1858&#8211;1886)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early Years (1858–1886)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Columba was born in Queen Street, <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> on April 1, 1858, into a large and very religious family; three of his sisters became nuns. His father, William Marmion was from Clane, County Kildare. His mother, Herminie Cordier was French, prompting his biographer, Dom Raymond Thibaut to remark: "He owes to his Celtic origin his penetrating intelligence, his lively imagination, his sensibility, his exuberance and his youthful spirit. The French blood which ran in his veins contributes to his clearness of mind, his habit of clear perception, his ease of exposition, and his uprightness of character. From the combination of the two he derives his constant gaiety and his generosity of heart with all the strength, devotion, and fine feeling which this noble quality implies."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was baptised with the name "Joseph Aloysius". From a very early age he was seemingly "consumed with some kind of inner fire or enthusiasm for the things of God."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was educated at the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Belvedere_College" title="Belvedere College">Belvedere College</a> in Dublin.
</p><p>He entered the seminary at the age of 16.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  At the time he entered the seminary, his "faith<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> was very strong";<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThibaut194916_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThibaut194916-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> he perceived "something more than simple theoretical theses"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThibaut194916_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThibaut194916-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> in Catholic doctrine, in particular "that a man's love for God is measured by his love for his neighbor."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThibaut194919_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThibaut194919-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd>One day during a vacation [at about the age of 17] he learnt that a poor old woman, well known to his family, was threatened with being summoned before the magistrates by an exacting creditor who claimed the payment of a somewhat large debt. The young seminarian possessed an equivalent amount saved up little by little for a trip he had promised himself. A struggle went on in his heart between his generosity and the legitimate desire to enjoy the fruit of his economies. This struggled lasted all night. In the morning charity had gained the day; with his father's consent he generously made over his savings in favor of the poor woman.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThibaut194919_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThibaut194919-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>A "very important moment in Dom Marmion's inner life"<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> occurred while he was still in seminary.
</p>
<dl><dd>It seems that one day when returning to the study hall he had all at once, to use his own words, "a light on <a href="/wiki/God_in_Christianity" title="God in Christianity">God's Infinity</a>." While this "light" only lasted for an instant, it was so clear and strong that it left an indelible impression on him, so that... "he referred to this not without emotion and thanksgiving during the last days of his life."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>He completed his studies in Rome at the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Irish_College" title="Pontifical Irish College">Pontifical Irish College</a> and was ordained in 1881.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On his journey back to Ireland, he passed through <a href="/wiki/Maredsous_Abbey" title="Maredsous Abbey">Maredsous</a>, Belgium &#8211; a young and dynamic <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">monastery</a> founded 9 years before (in 1872) by <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monks" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monks">Benedictine monks</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Beuron_Archabbey" title="Beuron Archabbey">Abbey of Beuron</a>, Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  He wished very much to join the community there.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  But his archbishop in Ireland refused his request to do so and appointed him as curate at <a href="/wiki/Dundrum,_Dublin" title="Dundrum, Dublin">Dundrum</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> a parish in the south of Dublin. After a year, he was appointed Professor of <a href="/wiki/Metaphysics" title="Metaphysics">Metaphysics</a> at <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_College_(Dublin)" title="Holy Cross College (Dublin)">Holy Cross College at Clonliffe</a>, the Dublin diocesan seminary where Marmion himself had studied.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> For the next four years (1882–1886) he embarked on the education and spiritual direction of others, including an appointment as chaplain to a nearby convent.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Parish_priest">Parish priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Parish priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Marmion's work as a parish priest "daily brought him into contact with a cross-section of humanity," and he was "called upon to advise, teach, console and give every kind of spiritual and material aid."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Biography&#39;&#39;25_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Biography&#39;&#39;25-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  He "possessed an extraordinary facility for adapting himself to other people," and above all "in comforting others and putting them at their ease."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Biography&#39;&#39;25_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Biography&#39;&#39;25-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> During this period he began to learn "the delicate art of <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual direction</a> in which he was later to excel."<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His four years as professor at Clonliffe (1882–1886) "helped to complete his intellectual and spiritual formation. Thrown into the atmosphere of college life, he soon found himself in his native element."<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Maredsous_.281886.E2.80.931899.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Maredsous_(1886–1899)">Maredsous (1886&#8211;1899)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Maredsous (1886–1899)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marmion joined the monastic community at <a href="/wiki/Maredsous_Abbey" title="Maredsous Abbey">Maredsous</a> in 1886, having received his archbishop's approval.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  At first, it was very hard for him, even "traumatic."<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>  He was 27 years old, a respected priest and professor. In Maredsous he was a novice, and had as well to learn a language (French) and <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">monastic disciplines</a> that were foreign to him.
</p><p>After his <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">Solemn Profession</a> on February 10, 1891, Columba<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> (as he was now called) was appointed to act as assistant to the Novice Master &#8211; with whom he got on rather badly<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> &#8211; and in addition to preach at parishes in the vicinity of the Abbey.
</p><p>"There was an element of the dramatic in his initiation into pastoral work. A neighboring parish priest, whose preacher had unexpectedly failed him on the eve of a great feast, came to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictines</a> to ask their help in his difficulty. The superior was very sorry, but he had no one to offer him except a young Irish monk whose French was far from perfect. 'I will take him all the same,' said the parish priest, and he brought off Dom Columba. Three days later he brought him back to the Abbey saying: 'We have never had such a preacher before in my parish.' And soon the other parish priests were competing with each other for 'the Irish father.'"<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_formation">Monastic formation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monastic formation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During a season of "monastic calm" from 1891 to 1899,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEarl_of_Wicklow194919_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEarl_of_Wicklow194919-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Marmion's spiritual life came "to full maturity"<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> as he attended to "the various <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">duties of the monastic state</a>, the life of silence and recollection, of constant fidelity to the liturgy."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEarl_of_Wicklow194919_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEarl_of_Wicklow194919-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Of particular importance to him were developing a spirit of obedience,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Contrition" title="Contrition">compunction</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Humility" title="Humility">humility</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> as well as continued growth in the fundamental matters of faith,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Hope_(virtue)" title="Hope (virtue)">hope</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Charity_(virtue)" class="mw-redirect" title="Charity (virtue)">charity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Above all, his spiritual life became more and more centered on <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Christ</a>.
</p>
<dl><dd><i><b>1887:</b></i> After breakfast, while walking in the garden, I read the eighth chapter of <a href="/wiki/The_Imitation_of_Christ_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="The Imitation of Christ (book)"><i>The Imitation of Christ</i></a> and I felt strongly impelled to take Jesus as my one friend. I realized that, in spite of my great weakness and unfaithfulness, Jesus desired to be my friend above all others. The text: "My delights are to be with the children of men" [Proverbs 8:31], gripped me and compelled me irresistibly to respond with all my heart to this desire of Jesus. In the course of this meditation I felt the near presence of Jesus and a great desire to do all things before His eyes.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<dl><dd><i><b>1895:</b></i> We are <i>infinitely</i> rich in Jesus Christ and God's mercies are to our miseries what the ocean is to a drop of water. We never glorify God more than when despite the sight of our sins and unworthiness we are so filled with confidence in His mercy and in the infinite merits of Jesus Christ that we throw ourselves on His bosom full of confidence and love, <i>sure</i> that He cannot repel us: "a humble and contrite heart, Oh God, Thou wilt not despise."<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<dl><dd><i><b>1896:</b></i> Oh, my dear child, I would wish to engrave on your heart in letters of gold this truth, that no matter how great our misery, <i>we are infinitely rich in Jesus Christ,</i> if we unite with Him, if we lean on Him, if we realize constantly by a firm living faith that all the value of our prayer, and of all that we do comes from His merits in us.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<h2><span id="Prior_of_Mont_C.C3.A9sar.2C_Louvain_.281899.E2.80.931909.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Prior_of_Mont_César,_Louvain_(1899–1909)">Prior of Mont César, Louvain (1899&#8211;1909)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Prior of Mont César, Louvain (1899–1909)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1899, Dom Columba helped to found the <a href="/wiki/Keizersberg_Abbey" title="Keizersberg Abbey">Abbey of Mont César</a>, Louvain, Belgium, and became its first <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">Prior</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>  He was invested with heavy responsibilities: Director of Studies for the young monks; Professor of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">Theology</a>; <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> of <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelite nuns</a>, all in addition to being Prior.  He gave <a href="/wiki/Retreat_(spiritual)" title="Retreat (spiritual)">retreats</a> in Belgium and the United Kingdom.  He also became confessor to the future <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9-Joseph_Mercier" title="Désiré-Joseph Mercier">Cardinal Mercier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marmion_the_teacher">Marmion the teacher</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Marmion the teacher">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Marmion's great gift for teaching came into full bloom during this period.  His lectures were distinguished by, "on the one hand, his extreme clearness, and on the other his happy and fluent application of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic theology">doctrine</a> to the inner life."<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>  Rather than presenting "revealed truths like mere theorems of geometry having no bearing on the interior life,"<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Marmion sought to inspire his students to "live in and by the mysteries he set forth to them."<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd>The fruitful years in Mont César enabled him to attain an unrivalled mastery of his subjects. Others may surpass him in the detailed documentation of their learning; but when Dom Columba discusses one of the major theses in which dogma approaches the highest mysteries of God... his teaching has a breadth which approaches the infinite. The vast repercussions of his thought, the fruit of long contemplation, throw light on a whole world of secondary conclusions. His trenchant summaries unite with an unusual power of synthesis in one beam of light the diverse aspects of a problem hidden at the first approach in its complexity. The central point stands out in brilliant relief and the whole assembly of connected truths is illuminated by the light of a governing principle which is the key to the whole problem. As a master of synthesis he is unrivalled.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<h2><span id="Abbot_of_Maredsous_.281909.E2.80.931923.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Maredsous_(1909–1923)">Abbot of Maredsous (1909&#8211;1923)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Abbot of Maredsous (1909–1923)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1893, Dom <a href="/wiki/Hildebrand_de_Hemptinne" title="Hildebrand de Hemptinne">Hildebrand de Hemptinne</a>, Second Abbot of Maredsous, was appointed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> as the first Abbot Primate of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine Order</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>  At the request of the Pope, Dom Hildebrand continued as Abbot of Maredsous, but relinquished that office in 1909.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In that year, at the age of 51, "at the height of his powers, both physical and intellectual,"<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Dom Marmion was elected Third <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> of Maredsous. A community consisting of a hundred <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> it ran two schools<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> and was a publisher, in particular of <i>La Revue bénédictine</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>  Marmion adopted as his motto <i>Magis prodesse quam praesse,</i> "To serve rather than be served," a maxim taken from the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Rule</a> of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">St. Benedict</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> The monastery had great spiritual and intellectual influence under his leadership.  Vocations abounded.  But Dom Marmion was not indifferent to temporal matters.  Thus he had the Abbey equipped with electricity and central heating, facilities rarely to be found in monasteries at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd>"Gathering up all he had learnt during his <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a> of nearly thirty years and concentrating in his mind the treasures of theological science accumulated during as many years of study and teaching, a consummate master in <a href="/wiki/Dogmatics" class="mw-redirect" title="Dogmatics">dogmatics</a> and asceticism, an experienced spiritual director, and a contemplative who constantly searched into the mysteries of God, Dom Marmion was now about to give the matured fruits of these years and to be above all among his own monks, the exponent of the Christian and monastic life in its fullness."<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Maredsous_and_other_communities">Maredsous and other communities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Maredsous and other communities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1909, the government of Belgium asked Maredsous to consider founding a Benedictine monastery in <a href="/wiki/State_of_Katanga" title="State of Katanga">Katanga</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Belgian_Congo" title="Belgian Congo">Belgian Congo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>  Doubtless Dom Marmion's missionary spirit would not have hesitated; but the Community preferred to devote itself to research and to promotion of sources of the faith, rather than to launch out into direct evangelization.  However, Marmion lent effective aid to this mission, which was taken on by the <a href="/wiki/St._Andrew%27s_Abbey_(Bruges)" title="St. Andrew&#39;s Abbey (Bruges)">Abbey of Saint André</a> at Bruges.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A few years later, Marmion gave help and support to the conversion to Catholicism of Anglican communities in Wales (<a href="/wiki/Caldey_Abbey" title="Caldey Abbey">Caldey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Curzon_Park_Abbey" title="Curzon Park Abbey">Milford Haven</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="First_World_War_.281914.E2.80.931918.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="First_World_War_(1914–1918)">First World War (1914&#8211;1918)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: First World War (1914–1918)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When war broke out in 1914 Dom Marmion, fearing that his young novices might be called up, sent them to Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>  This involved Marmion traveling, disguised as a cattle dealer, through the war zone from Belgium to England, "without passport or papers of any kind."<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> During the war years Marmion continued his activities as preacher and spiritual director.  In 1915 he wrote to a young man preparing for <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordination</a>: "The <i>best</i> of all preparations for the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a> is to live each day with love, wherever obedience and Providence place us."<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the Irish house, established at Edermine, did not give him entire satisfaction; the attitude of the young novices grieved him: "I have tried to win them by constancy and prayer, but so far without success.  They are good, but full of confidence in themselves... They oppose the letter of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">Canon Law</a> to the spirit of <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">the Holy Rule</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>  The Edermine house was closed in 1920.<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_episode_of_the_Dormition_Monastery">The episode of the Dormition Monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: The episode of the Dormition Monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the war, the need to provide replacements for the German monks of the Beuronese congregation who had been expelled from the Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Maria_Sion_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagia Maria Sion Abbey">Monastery of the Dormition</a>, on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Zion" title="Mount Zion">Mount Zion</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, made Marmion dream of there being a foundation from Maredsous in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.
</p><p>Despite his efforts and the support they gained, this dream was not realized and the German monks returned to the Dormition.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="His_writings">His writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: His writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1895, Marmion gave a retreat for a small group of nuns. The notes for those talks contained in kernel an idea he would develop during the next 20 years &#8211; meditating upon it in prayer, and refining and polishing it in the many talks he gave as a popular retreat master.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup>  In its finished form it became <i>Christ, the Life of the Soul</i> (1917) &#8211; a book that was first published privately, but then rapidly, unexpectedly, became an "overwhelming success"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECapelle194810_62-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECapelle194810-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> in the Catholic world.
</p><p>At the time of its publication, much Catholic literature was a mere "rehash... of pious thoughts,"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Herlihy1948107_63-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Herlihy1948107-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup> marked by a "sentimental emphasis," and a tendency towards a sterile "refinement of interior analysis."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28_64-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> "Little attention was paid to the Bible, the <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Fathers</a> and the great masters of the spiritual life."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28_64-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> In this atmosphere, Marmion's work seemed like "something new,"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28_64-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;More_About_Dom_Marmion&#39;&#39;28-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> even "revolutionary."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Herlihy1948107_63-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Herlihy1948107-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup>  "It was as if the desert had received its long-awaited rain."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECapelle194810_62-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECapelle194810-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup>  His books "initiated a profound spiritual revival the influence of which... permeated the whole Catholic world."<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Yet there was essentially "nothing new" in Marmion's work.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Josephinum&#39;&#39;144_66-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Josephinum&#39;&#39;144-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup>  Rather, his "revolution" was effected by "a return to what was fundamental,"<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup> specifically his restoration of "Christ as the center of all... spiritual thinking."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Josephinum&#39;&#39;144_66-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETierney,_&#39;&#39;Josephinum&#39;&#39;144-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A second major theme of his work is the doctrine of <a href="/wiki/Divine_filiation" title="Divine filiation">divine adoption</a> in Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup>  Again, this idea<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup> was not original with Marmion; it is clearly set forth in the New Testament, particularly in the writings of <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">St. Paul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">&#91;70&#93;</a></sup> But although the doctrine had been addressed by many spiritual writers before him, "it would be difficult to find another who had given the mystery such preeminence, making it, as he does, the beginning and the end of the spiritual life. And with Dom Marmion it is not so much a theory or a system, as a living truth that acts directly on the soul."<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">&#91;71&#93;</a></sup> Some believe the Catholic Church will one day formally declare Marmion the <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor</a> of Divine Adoption.<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">&#91;72&#93;</a></sup>
Sources for Marmion's thought include, preeminently, the Bible (especially <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">St. Paul</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_the_Apostle" title="John the Apostle">St. John</a>), the <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Fathers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">St. Thomas Aquinas</a>, and the Liturgy (i.e., the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church" title="Sacraments of the Catholic Church">sacraments</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">&#91;73&#93;</a></sup> as well as <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">St. Francis de Sales</a> (1567–1622) and Msgr. Charles Gay (1815–1880).<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">&#91;74&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a 20th-century writer, Marmion is notable, perhaps unique, in the several formal and informal endorsements his works have received from the popes of the 20th century, including <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Benedict XV</a> (1914–1922),<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pius XI</a> (1922–1939),<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">&#91;76&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pius XII</a> (1939–1958),<sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">&#91;77&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Paul VI</a> (1963–1978),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEToups2006160,_note_4_78-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEToups2006160,_note_4-78">&#91;78&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a> (1978–2005).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEToups2006160,_note_4_78-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEToups2006160,_note_4-78">&#91;78&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="His_last_years">His last years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: His last years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>With <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9-Joseph_Mercier" title="Désiré-Joseph Mercier">Cardinal Mercier</a>, his friend and confidant, Marmion was a spiritually dominant figure on the Belgian and international scene.  The publication of his books had met with "immediate and overwhelming success,"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECapelle194810_62-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECapelle194810-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> and they were rapidly being translated into a number of languages, including Korean and Japanese.<sup id="cite_ref-79" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-79">&#91;79&#93;</a></sup>  His influence was at its height, despite his fatigue and a precarious state of health.
</p><p>In September 1922, he took the place of the Bishop of Namur as leader of the diocesan pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Our Lady of Lourdes">Lourdes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-80" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-80">&#91;80&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In October of the same year, he presided at the celebration of the 50th anniversary of the foundation of Maredsous Abbey (which he had governed as abbot for 14 years).<sup id="cite_ref-81" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-81">&#91;81&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marmion was struck during a flu epidemic, and succumbed to bronchial pneumonia on January 30, 1923.<sup id="cite_ref-82" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-82">&#91;82&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_Marmion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rapidly, favors and <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">miracles</a> were attributed to him; justifying the transfer, in 1963, of his body from the monks' cemetery to the abbatial church (his body was found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>, after more than 40 years).<sup id="cite_ref-83" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-83">&#91;83&#93;</a></sup>  A cure from cancer obtained after a woman from St. Cloud, Minnesota, visited his tomb in 1966 was investigated by the Church and recognized as miraculous in 2000,<sup id="cite_ref-84" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-84">&#91;84&#93;</a></sup> leading to his beatification in that year.
</p><p>Dom Columba Marmion was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on September 3, 2000 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, on the same occasion as:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>, who died in 1963</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>, who died in 1878</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Tommaso_Reggio" title="Tommaso Reggio">Tommaso Reggio</a>, Archbishop of Genoa, who died in 1901</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/William_Joseph_Chaminade" title="William Joseph Chaminade">William Chaminade</a>, who died in 1850</li></ul>
<p>At the Beatification ceremony Pope John Paul II declared:
</p>
<dl><dd>He bequeathed to us an authentic treasury of spiritual teaching for the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Church</a> of our time.  In his writings he teaches a way of holiness, simple and yet demanding, for all the faithful, whom God, through love, has destined to be his adopted children in Christ Jesus...  May a wide rediscovery of the spiritual writings of Blessed Columba Marmion help <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priests</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catholic_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic religious order">religious</a> and <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">laity</a> to grow in <a href="/wiki/Divinization_(Christian)" title="Divinization (Christian)">union with Christ</a> and bear faithful witness to Him through ardent love of God and generous service to their brothers and sisters.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>May Blessed Columba Marmion help us to live ever more intensely, to understand ever more deeply, our membership in the Church, the <a href="/wiki/Mystici_corporis_Christi" class="mw-redirect" title="Mystici corporis Christi">Mystical Body of Christ</a>!<sup id="cite_ref-85" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-85">&#91;85&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatification</a>, Dom Marmion's <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Cause for Canonization</a> has been opened and is very active.  Recently (2009) the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Vancouver" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Vancouver">Archdiocese of Vancouver</a>, Canada, began a <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">canonical investigation</a> into the cure of a man ravaged by a <a href="/wiki/Necrotizing_fasciitis" title="Necrotizing fasciitis">necrotizing fasciitis</a>.  He had been expected to die within hours.<sup id="cite_ref-86" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-86">&#91;86&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both Marmion Abbey and Marmion Academy (established 1933) in Aurora, Illinois are named in his honor.
</p>
