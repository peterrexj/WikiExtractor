<p><b>Serenicus</b> (French: <i lang="fr">Céneri</i> or <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Sérène</i></span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;620</span>&#160;– c.<span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;669</span>) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a>. He was an early evangelist in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, and founded a monastery and a chapel in a village in <a href="/wiki/Orne" title="Orne">Orne</a> that later took the name of <a href="/wiki/Saint-C%C3%A9neri-le-G%C3%A9rei" title="Saint-Céneri-le-Gérei">Saint-Céneri-le-Gérei</a>.
</p><p>Serenicus is venerated as a saint. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on May 7 with his brother <a href="/wiki/Serenidus_of_Saulges" title="Serenidus of Saulges">Serenidus of Saulges</a>, or locally on August 16. He is a patron against skin diseases, colic, and infertility.<sup id="cite_ref-Bargellini_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bargellini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serenicus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born into a noble family in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a> around 620, Serenicus travelled to the <a href="/wiki/Maine_(province)" title="Maine (province)">province of Maine</a> in 649 during the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> with his brother, <a href="/wiki/Serenidus_of_Saulges" title="Serenidus of Saulges">Serenidus</a>, to live a life of contemplation and penance.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At first, he lived with Serenidus as a hermit and an ascetic near <a href="/wiki/Saulges" title="Saulges">Saulges</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Le Mans">diocese of Le Mans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-City_Desert_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-City_Desert-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At some point, Serenicus departed Saulges and began to live near a <a href="/wiki/Saint-C%C3%A9neri-le-G%C3%A9rei" title="Saint-Céneri-le-Gérei">village</a> in <a href="/wiki/Orne" title="Orne">Orne</a> near the <a href="/wiki/Sarthe_(river)" title="Sarthe (river)">Sarthe river</a>. He started accepting disciples and found a church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a> and an accompanying monastery. He ended up accepting a few disciples and built a church dedicated to Saint Martin of Tours and a monastic establishment.<sup id="cite_ref-City_Desert_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-City_Desert-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The church was completed by Bishop, and saint, Milehard de Sées.<sup id="cite_ref-Bargellini_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bargellini-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is said that after a long journey Serenicus settled in Orne, where he experienced a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> in answer to his <a href="/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer">prayer</a> for water to quench his thirst. According to legend a spring, located near the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Sarthe_(river)" title="Sarthe (river)">Sarthe</a>, sprang up in answer to his prayer. It is believed that the <a href="/wiki/Water" title="Water">water</a> from the spring has the ability to cure <a href="/wiki/Eye" title="Eye">eye</a> problems.<sup id="cite_ref-tourisme_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tourisme-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Serenicus is thought to have died around 669.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
