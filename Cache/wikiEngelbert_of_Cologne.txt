<p><b>Count Engelbert II of Berg</b>, also known as <b>Saint Engelbert</b>, <b>Engelbert of Cologne</b>, <b>Engelbert I, Archbishop of Cologne</b> or <b>Engelbert I of Berg, Archbishop of Cologne</b> (1185 or 1186, <a href="/wiki/Schloss_Burg_(Solingen)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schloss Burg (Solingen)">Schloss Burg</a> &#8211; 7 November 1225, <a href="/wiki/Gevelsberg" title="Gevelsberg">Gevelsberg</a>) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">archbishop of Cologne</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> he was notoriously murdered by a member of his own family.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engelbert_II_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Engelbert was born in 1185 or 1186 in <a href="/wiki/Schloss_Burg_(Solingen)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schloss Burg (Solingen)">Schloss Burg</a> (present <a href="/wiki/Burg_an_der_Wupper" class="mw-redirect" title="Burg an der Wupper">Burg an der Wupper</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burg_an_der_Wupper" class="extiw" title="de:Burg an der Wupper">de</a>&#93;</span>), the younger son of Count <a href="/wiki/Engelbert_I_of_Berg_(count)" class="mw-redirect" title="Engelbert I of Berg (count)">Engelbert I of Berg</a> and his wife Margarete of <a href="/wiki/Guelders" class="mw-redirect" title="Guelders">Guelders</a>. He was educated at the cathedral school in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>. From 1198 (at the age of twelve or thirteen) he held the office of <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> of St. George in Cologne and from 1199 to 1216 he also held the office of cathedral provost at <a href="/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral" title="Cologne Cathedral">Cologne Cathedral</a>. He further acquired at various times a number of other provostships: in Cologne, <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Deventer" title="Deventer">Deventer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Zutphen" title="Zutphen">Zutphen</a>. He was elected <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_M%C3%BCnster" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Münster">Bishop of Münster</a> in 1203 but he declined because of his age.
</p><p>Engelbert was <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> in 1206, on account of his support for his cousin <a href="/wiki/Adolf_of_Altena" title="Adolf of Altena">Adolf of Altena</a>, archbishop of Cologne, in the interests of <a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Swabia" title="Philip of Swabia">Philip of Swabia</a> against <a href="/wiki/Otto_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto of Brunswick</a> but was pardoned in 1208. In 1212, as an act of <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> for his earlier rebellion, he took part in the <a href="/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade" title="Albigensian Crusade">Albigensian Crusade</a>. He gave his allegiance to the future <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Bouvines" title="Battle of Bouvines">Battle of Bouvines</a> in 1214.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Cologne_and_after">Archbishop of Cologne and after</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engelbert_II_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Cologne and after">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg/220px-Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="254" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg/330px-Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg/440px-Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3188" data-file-height="3674" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Solingen_-_Schloss_Burg_-_Engelbert_01_ies.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Archbishop Engelbert at Schloss Burg (sculptor <a href="/wiki/Paul_Wynand" title="Paul Wynand">Paul Wynand</a>)</div></div></div>
<p>Engelbert was elected Archbishop of Cologne as Engelbert I on 29 February 1216 and was consecrated on 24 September 1217, in which office he remained until his violent death.
</p><p>Engelbert came to enjoy the trust of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, becoming imperial regent (<i><a href="/wiki/Reichsverweser" title="Reichsverweser">Reichsverweser</a></i>) in 1220 and guardian of the Emperor's son <a href="/wiki/Henry_(VII)_of_Germany" title="Henry (VII) of Germany">Henry</a>. In 1222, Engelbert crowned twelve-years-old Henry as <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Romans" title="King of the Romans">King of the Romans</a> in <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>. Engelbert remained Henry's tutor and guardian until his death.
</p><p>It is not clear to what extent Engelbert was personally involved with the <i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Confoederatio_cum_principibus_ecclesiasticis" title="Confoederatio cum principibus ecclesiasticis">Confoederatio cum principibus ecclesiasticis</a></i></span></i>, a treaty with the ecclesiastical princes, which Frederick signed on 26 April 1220, although as Administrator of the German Kingdom (<i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Gubernator Regni Teutonici</i></span></i>) he must have had at least some input. Clearly, however, in the increased powers it gave to all ecclesiastical princes it was of benefit also to the archbishops of Cologne, and the establishment and development of the new powers was part of Engelbert's archiepiscopal strategy.
</p><p>When Engelbert succeeded, the rights and territories of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese">archdiocese</a> were in bad order, following a long period of civil unrest in Germany. He engaged himself at once in a series of campaigns and strategies to win them back and safeguard them, principally against the <a href="/wiki/Limburg_(county)" class="mw-redirect" title="Limburg (county)">Dukes of Limburg</a> and their allies the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Cleves" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Cleves">County of Cleves</a>. Engelbert in turn set up alliances with <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant" title="Duchy of Brabant">Brabant</a> and <a href="/wiki/Namur_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Namur (province)">Namur</a>.
</p><p>Engelbert also defended his personal inheritance as Count of Berg against Duke <a href="/wiki/Waleran_III_of_Limburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Waleran III of Limburg">Waleran III of Limburg</a>. In 1218 Engelbert's elder brother Count <a href="/wiki/Adolf_VI_of_Berg" class="mw-redirect" title="Adolf VI of Berg">Adolf VI of Berg</a> died on the <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Crusade" title="Fifth Crusade">Fifth Crusade</a> without a male heir. Waleran considered himself entitled to inherit the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Berg" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Berg">County of Berg</a> because his son <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_Limburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry IV of Limburg">Henry</a> was married to <a href="/wiki/Irmgard_of_Berg" title="Irmgard of Berg">Irmgard of Berg</a>, Adolf's only daughter. According to the <a href="/wiki/Salic_law" title="Salic law">Salic law</a>, however, Engelbert was the heir. He won the dispute in two feuds. In 1220 a peace was concluded and Waleran's claim settled by the payment of a year's revenues.
</p><p>Engelbert granted <a href="/wiki/Town_privileges" title="Town privileges">town privileges</a> to many places, including <a href="/wiki/Wipperf%C3%BCrth" title="Wipperfürth">Wipperfürth</a>, <a href="/wiki/Attendorn" title="Attendorn">Attendorn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brilon" title="Brilon">Brilon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siegen" title="Siegen">Siegen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Werl" title="Werl">Werl</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herford" title="Herford">Herford</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vianden" title="Vianden">Vianden</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hamm" title="Hamm">Hamm</a>, <a href="/wiki/Neuerburg" title="Neuerburg">Neuerburg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Manderscheid,_Bernkastel-Wittlich" title="Manderscheid, Bernkastel-Wittlich">Manderscheid</a>.
</p><p>During his incumbency as archbishop, Engelbert continued to fight for the re-establishment and security of the Archdiocese of Cologne both as an ecclesiastical authority and also as a secular territory. (It was said of him that despite his personal piety he was more of a monarch than a churchman). Not only did he constantly battle, by all means necessary, for the secular well-being of the lands of the archdiocese, of which he may be counted the <i><a href="/wiki/De_facto" title="De facto">de facto</a></i> founder as a significant state; he also took energetic measures for the effective regulation of the City of Cologne itself; and he was a zealous champion of the religious throughout his archdiocese.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engelbert_II_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<table align="right">
<tbody><tr>
<td><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg/220px-Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg/330px-Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg/440px-Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Denkmal_Engelbert01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Memorial to the assassinated Archbishop in <a href="/wiki/Gevelsberg" title="Gevelsberg">Gevelsberg</a></div></div></div>
</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Engelbert earned the respect and affection of his subjects through his devotion to justice and his energy in maintaining law, and took great pains to ensure the well-being of the religious within his authority. However, his effectiveness in achieving his goals by all means necessary, including military action, his allegiance to the pope and the emperor, and his uncompromising defence of the law and the rights of religious persons and bodies, brought him into conflict with the nobility, including his own family, and this led to his death.
</p><p>His cousin Count <a href="/wiki/Frederick_of_Isenberg" title="Frederick of Isenberg">Frederick of Isenberg</a> was <i><a href="/wiki/Vogt" class="mw-redirect" title="Vogt">vogt</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Essen_Abbey" title="Essen Abbey">Essen Abbey</a>, and abused his position by defrauding the <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>. Engelbert was determined to protect the nuns' interests and sought to bring Frederick to justice. On 7 November 1225 while they travelled together to Cologne from a judicial hearing in <a href="/wiki/Soest,_Germany" title="Soest, Germany">Soest</a>, Engelbert was killed, possibly by Frederick, in a <a href="/wiki/Defile_(geography)" title="Defile (geography)">defile</a> near present-day <a href="/wiki/Gevelsberg" title="Gevelsberg">Gevelsberg</a> near <a href="/wiki/Schwelm" title="Schwelm">Schwelm</a>.
</p><p>It seems probable that a group of disaffected nobility was behind the attack which may have been intended to take Engelbert captive rather than kill him.
</p><p>Engelbert's body was taken to Cologne on a dung-cart, and when examined, found to have forty-seven wounds.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engelbert_II_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Engelbert's body was buried in Cologne Cathedral on 24 February 1226 by order of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Conrad_of_Urach" title="Conrad of Urach">Conrad of Urach</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a>, who declared him a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, though a formal canonization did not take place.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His remains are preserved today in a <a href="/wiki/Baroque_architecture" title="Baroque architecture">Baroque</a> shrine prepared on the authority of <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_of_Bavaria_(bishop)" title="Ferdinand of Bavaria (bishop)">Ferdinand of Bavaria</a>, archbishop of Cologne, who in 1618 also ordered the celebration of his feast on 7 November.
</p>
