<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Onuphrius,_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg/220px-Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="276" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg/330px-Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg/440px-Onuphrius%2C_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg 2x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="565" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Onuphrius,_Stylianos_and_Simeon_Stylites.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Simeon_Stylites" title="Simeon Stylites">Simeon Stylites</a>, Stylianos and <a href="/wiki/Onuphrius" title="Onuphrius">Onuphrius</a></div></div></div>
<p>Stylianos of Paphlagonia was born in <a href="/wiki/Hadrianopolis_in_Paphlagonia" title="Hadrianopolis in Paphlagonia">Adrianopolis</a> sometime between 400 AD and 500 AD. He distributed his inheritance among the poor, and left the city to live in a monastery. His zealous devotion and asceticism provoked jealousy on the part of other monks, so he left the monastery to live as a hermit in a cave in the wilderness, where he spent his time in prayer and fasting.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There, in the peace of the desert, the Stylianos had time to observe creation and meditate upon it, and he saw the Creator in all things. His holiness evident to the people of the surrounding area, and they came to listen to his teaching, or to be cured by through his prayers. He knew how to calm troubled souls; other ascetics came to join him.<sup id="cite_ref-pemptousia_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pemptousia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stylianos is known for his smiling countenance and cheerful disposition. He would periodically leave his hermitage and make pastoral visits to neighboring villages.
</p><p>Stylianos was also known for his love of children. He believed that for a person to be saved, they needed to have their soul like that of a little child. Sometimes parents would leave their children with for a period of time in order for them to receive some spiritual guidance.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>He is celebrated for the gift of healing children by his prayers. Parents would travel great distances seeking a cure for their offspring, which Stylianos attributed to the holy name of God. He also acquired the reputation of a wonder-worker because his prayers seemed to help childless couples have a child.<sup id="cite_ref-pemptousia_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pemptousia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Even after his death, the people of Paphlagonia believed that he could cure their children. Whenever a child became sick, an icon of Saint Stylianos was painted and hung over the child’s bed.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stylianos_of_Paphlagonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Stylianos is commemorated on 26 November.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stylianos_of_Paphlagonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Stylianos is known as a protector of children, especially orphans.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Pious Christians invoke him to help and protect their children; childless women entreat his intercession so that they might have children.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stylianos_of_Paphlagonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Stylianos is depicted in iconography holding an swaddled infant in his arms.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stylianos_of_Paphlagonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> argued that Stylianos never existed, but was a confusion with <a href="/wiki/Alypios_the_Stylite" class="mw-redirect" title="Alypios the Stylite">Alypios the Stylite</a>, who shares a saint's day and city of birth, with the attribute 'stylite' being confused with a <a href="/wiki/Cognomen" title="Cognomen">cognomen</a> Στυλιανός (both derived from στύλος 'pillar').<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
