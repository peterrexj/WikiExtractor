<p><b>Amandus</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 584 – 679), commonly called <b>Saint Amand</b>, was a bishop of <a href="/wiki/List_of_bishops_and_prince-bishops_of_Li%C3%A8ge" title="List of bishops and prince-bishops of Liège">Tongeren-Maastricht</a> and one of the great <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionaries</a> of <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, particularly in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amandus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The chief source of details of his life is the <i>Vita Sancti Amandi</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> an eighth-century text attributed to Beaudemond (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Baudemundus</i>). The vita was expanded by Philippe, abbot of Aumône. According to this biography, Amand was born in Lower <a href="/wiki/Poitou" title="Poitou">Poitou</a>. He was of noble birth but at the age of twenty, he became a monk on the <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele_d%27Yeu" title="Île d&#39;Yeu">Île d'Yeu</a>, against the wishes of his family. His father threatened to disinherit him if he did not return home.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> From there Amandus went to <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a> and became a pupil of bishop <a href="/wiki/Austregisilus" title="Austregisilus">Austregisilus</a>. There he lived in solitude in a cell for fifteen years, living on no more than bread and water.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Humbert_of_Maroilles" title="Humbert of Maroilles">Humbert of Maroilles</a> was of a noble family and trained as a monk in <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a>. However, upon the death of his parents, he returned to his estates in Mézières sur Oise to settle some inheritance issues and found fine food, servants, and various conveniences sufficiently distracting that he gave up any thought of the monastic life, until one day Amandus took him on a pilgrimage to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Humbert became his disciple and companion.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="326" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/Archive-ugent-be-E336B140-751E-11E6-9B48-3FC1D43445F2_DS-55_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="356" data-file-height="527" /></a><figcaption>Excerpt from the Vita St. Amandi manuscript. Manufactured in Ghent, ca. 10th century. Preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Ghent_University_Library" title="Ghent University Library">Ghent University Library</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>After the pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Amandus was made a missionary bishop in France in 628, without a fixed diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-Smail_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smail-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> At the request of <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire II">Clotaire II</a>, he evangelized the pagan inhabitants of <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, later extending his field of operations to all of Flanders. Initially, he had little success, suffering persecution and undergoing great hardships. However, after allegedly performing a miracle (bringing back to life a hanged criminal)<sup id="cite_ref-Smail_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smail-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the attitude of the people changed and he made many converts.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  He founded a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Amand_Abbey" title="Saint-Amand Abbey">Elnon</a> where he served as abbot for four years. Amandus was made a bishop in 628.<sup id="cite_ref-treasures_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-treasures-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saintamandus.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Saintamandus.jpg/220px-Saintamandus.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="282" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Saintamandus.jpg/330px-Saintamandus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4b/Saintamandus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="411" data-file-height="527" /></a><figcaption>St. Amandus and the serpent, from a 14th-century manuscript</figcaption></figure>
<p>He returned to France in 630. Amandus was a close friend of <a href="/wiki/Adalbard" title="Adalbard">Adalbard of Ostrevent</a>, whom he advised on the founding <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-athent_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-athent-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His disciple, <a href="/wiki/Jonatus" title="Jonatus">Jonatus</a>, was made abbot of the new house.<sup id="cite_ref-KU_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KU-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Amandus, however, angered <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> by attempting to have the king amend his life. In spite of the intervention of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Acarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Acarius">Saint Acarius</a>, Amand was expelled from the kingdom and went to Gascony.<sup id="cite_ref-athent_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-athent-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later Dagobert asked him to return and tutor the heir to the throne. Amand however declined. In 633, Amandus founded two monasteries in Ghent; one at <a href="/wiki/Blandijnberg" title="Blandijnberg">Blandinberg</a>, and the other named for St. <a href="/wiki/Bavo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bavo">Bavo</a>, who gave his estate for its foundation.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His next missionary task was among the <a href="/wiki/Slavic_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavic people">Slavic people</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> valley in present-day <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a> but this was unsuccessful. Amand went to Rome and reported to the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>While returning to France, Amandus is said to have calmed a storm at sea. In 639, he built an abbey near Tournay.
</p><p>From 647 till 650, Amand briefly served as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Maastricht" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Maastricht">Bishop of Maastricht</a>. The pope gave him some advice on how to deal with disobedient clerics and warned him about the <a href="/wiki/Monothelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Monothelite">Monothelite</a> heresy, at that time prevalent in the East. Amand was commissioned by the pope to organize church councils in <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> in order to pass on the various decrees from Rome. The bishops asked Amand to transmit the proceedings of the church councils to the pope. He resigned the see to St. Remaclus, to resume his missionary work.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around this time, Amand established contact with the family of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Landen" title="Pepin of Landen">Pepin of Landen</a> and helped <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude of Nivelles</a> and her mother <a href="/wiki/Itta" class="mw-redirect" title="Itta">Itta</a> establishing the famous monastery of <a href="/wiki/Nivelles" title="Nivelles">Nivelles</a>. At the same time, he was now 70 years old, the inhabitants of the <a href="/wiki/Basque_Country_(historical_territory)" class="mw-redirect" title="Basque Country (historical territory)">Basque country</a> asked him to return to their country to evangelize, although 30 years earlier he had preached there in vain. Returning home, he founded several more monasteries in present-day Belgium with the help of king Dagobert.
</p><p>Amand died in <a href="/wiki/Elnone_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Elnone Abbey">Elnone Abbey</a> (later Saint-Amand Abbey, in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Amand-les-Eaux" title="Saint-Amand-les-Eaux">Saint-Amand-les-Eaux</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a>) at the age of ninety.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Vita</i> of St. <a href="/wiki/Aldegonde" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldegonde">Aldegonde</a> recounts that on the day of his death, Aldegonde was shown a vision of the missionary ascending to heaven. This account did much to further the cult of Amandus.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p><br />
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amandus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Known for his hospitality, Saint Amand is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_Saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron Saint">patron saint</a> of all who produce <a href="/wiki/Beer" title="Beer">beer</a>: brewers, innkeepers and bartenders.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He is also the patron of vine growers, <a href="/wiki/Vintner" class="mw-redirect" title="Vintner">vintners</a> and merchants, and of <a href="/wiki/Scouting" title="Scouting">Boy Scouts</a>. 
</p><p>His feast day is 6 February. Although mostly revered in Flanders and <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>, he is also venerated in England, where at least one private chapel (at <a href="/wiki/East_Hendred" title="East Hendred">East Hendred</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a>) is dedicated to him.
</p>
