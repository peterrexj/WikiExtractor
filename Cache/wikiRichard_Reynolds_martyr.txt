<p><b>Richard Reynolds</b>, OSsS (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr>1492 – 4 May 1535) was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Bridgettine" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridgettine">Bridgettine</a> monk executed in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> for refusing the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> to King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1970, among the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Reynolds_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Richard Reynolds was a Bridgettine monk of the <a href="/wiki/Syon_Abbey" title="Syon Abbey">Syon Abbey</a>, founded in <a href="/wiki/Twickenham" title="Twickenham">Twickenham</a> by Henry V. He was born in Devon in 1492, educated at <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge" title="Corpus Christi College, Cambridge">Corpus Christi College, Cambridge</a>, and joined the Abbey in 1513.<sup id="cite_ref-college_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-college-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Pole" title="Reginald Pole">Reginald Pole</a> is quoted as saying that Reynolds was the only English monk well-versed in the three principal languages of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hamilton_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hamilton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dom Hamilton is of the opinion that as Reynolds was the most renowned spiritual counsellor of the <a href="/wiki/Syon_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Syon Monastery">Syon</a> community, he would have likely been consulted by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Barton" title="Elizabeth Barton">Elizabeth Barton</a>, the Holy Maid of Kent, who had been executed at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> almost a year prior for speaking out against the king's marriage to <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a>. Reynolds had previously arranged a meeting between <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Barton" title="Elizabeth Barton">Elizabeth Barton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a>. It was his connection to Barton that particularly compromised Reynolds in the view of the Crown officers.<sup id="cite_ref-hamilton_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hamilton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Reynolds was arrested and imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> around the middle of April 1535, along with the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> priors <a href="/wiki/John_Houghton_(martyr)" title="John Houghton (martyr)">John Houghton</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a> (a monk of <a href="/wiki/Sheen_Priory" title="Sheen Priory">Sheen Priory</a> in <a href="/wiki/Richmond,_London" title="Richmond, London">Richmond</a>). All four were tried for the denial of the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Supremacy" title="Acts of Supremacy">royal supremacy</a>.
</p><p>Against Reynolds, there was the additional charge of attempting to dissuade people from submitting to the king's authority. A witness claimed that Reynolds had stated that the "Dowager Princess" (<a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon" title="Catherine of Aragon">Queen Catherine</a>) was the true queen. Reynolds denied that he had declared an opinion against the king, except in confession, as compelled thereto. The practice of suborning penitents to accuse their confessors was in vogue at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-hamilton_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hamilton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All four were executed on 4 May 1535 by <a href="/wiki/Drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Drawing and quartering">drawing and quartering</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn_Tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn Tree">Tyburn Tree</a> in London after being dragged through the streets. Also convicted of treason for speaking against the King's marriage to Anne Boleyn, and martyred with them on that day, was <a href="/wiki/John_Haile" title="John Haile">John Haile</a>, the parish priest of <a href="/wiki/Isleworth" title="Isleworth">Isleworth</a> where Syon Abbey lay. The quarters of the body of Reynolds – the first man to refuse the oath – were chopped to pieces and hung in different parts of London, including the gate of Syon Abbey.
</p><p>According to <i>The Life of Sir Thomas More</i>, by <a href="/wiki/William_Roper" title="William Roper">William Roper</a>, More witnessed Reynolds and his three companions going to execution from his window in the Tower of London.  Roper reports the event thus:  "As Sir Thomas More in the Tower chanced on a time, looking out of his window, to behold one Master Reynolds, a religious, learned, and virtuous father of Sion and three monks of the Charterhouse, for the matters of the matrimony and Supremacy, going out of the Tower to execution - he, as one longing in that journey to have accompanied them, said unto my wife, then standing besides him: 'Lo, dost thou not see, <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Roper" title="Margaret Roper">Meg</a>, that these blessed fathers be now as cheerfully going to their deaths as bridegrooms to their marriage? Wherefore thereby mayst thou see, mine own good daughter, what a great difference there is between such as have in effect spent all their days in a strait, hard, penitential, and painful life religiously, and such as have in the world, like worldly wretches, as thy poor father hath done, consumed all their time in pleasure and ease licentiously. For God, considering their long-continued life in most sore and grievous penance, will no longer suffer them to remain here in this vale of misery and iniquity, but speedily hence taketh them to the fruition of his everlasting deity.'"<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the book "The Angel of Syon", Reynolds said before his execution, "... after a sharp breakfast, they should have a sweet supper." Coincidentally, <a href="/wiki/John_Sugar" title="John Sugar">Blessed John Sugar</a> is recorded having said the same phrase before his execution in 1604, but the author of the book, Dom Adam Hamilton, is uncertain if it originated with Reynolds.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Reynolds_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was beatified in 1886 and canonised by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> on 25 October 1970. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 4 May.
</p><p>A statue commemorating Saint Richard Reynolds can be found in Our Lady of Sorrows and St Bridget Roman Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Isleworth" title="Isleworth">Isleworth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A painting of the martyr is housed in the Church of St. Bridget in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a stained glass window of the martyr in <a href="/wiki/St_Etheldreda%27s_Church,_Ely" title="St Etheldreda&#39;s Church, Ely">St Etheldreda's Church</a> in Ely Place. He is the figure on the right.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A section of the dissolved <a href="/wiki/Syon_Abbey" title="Syon Abbey">Syon Abbey</a> is considered a relic of Reynolds,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> due to the fact that it supported the quartered remains of the martyr after his execution. The nuns of the Abbey took this section after the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries" title="Dissolution of the monasteries">dissolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is the patron of <a href="/wiki/St_Richard_Reynolds_Catholic_College" title="St Richard Reynolds Catholic College">St Richard Reynolds Catholic College</a> in Twickenham. The college is the federation of St Richard Reynolds Catholic High School and the new St Richard Reynolds Catholic Primary School for pupils aged 4 – 18.<sup id="cite_ref-college_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-college-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
