<p><b>Gerard</b> or <b>Gerard Sagredo</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Gellért</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Gerardo di Sagredo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gerardus</i>; 23 April 977/1000 – 29 August 1046) was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Csan%C3%A1d" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Csanád">bishop of Csanád</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> from around 1030 to his death. Most information about his life was preserved in his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">legends</a> which contain most conventional elements of medieval biographies of saints. He was born in a Venetian noble family, associated with the <a href="/wiki/Sagredo_family" title="Sagredo family">Sagredos</a> or <a href="/wiki/Morosini_family" title="Morosini family">Morosinis</a> in sources written centuries later. After a serious illness, he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_Monastery" title="San Giorgio Monastery">San Giorgio Monastery</a> at the age of five. He received excellent monastic education and also learnt grammar, music, philosophy and law.
</p><p>He left Venice for a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> around 1020, but a storm compelled him to break his journey near <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a>. He decided to visit the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurus_of_P%C3%A9cs" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Maurus of Pécs">Maurus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_P%C3%A9cs" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Pécs">bishop of Pécs</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a> convinced him not to continue his pilgrimage, emphasizing that Gerard's preachings could accelerate the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Hungarians" title="Hungarians">Hungarians</a>. Gerard was made the tutor of the king's son and heir, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary" title="Saint Emeric of Hungary">Emeric</a>. Before long, Gerard went to the <a href="/wiki/Bakony_Hills" class="mw-redirect" title="Bakony Hills">Bakony Hills</a> to live as a hermit near <a href="/wiki/Bakonyb%C3%A9l" title="Bakonybél">Bakonybél</a>. Stephen I made him bishop of the newly established <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Csan%C3%A1d" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Csanád">Diocese of Csanád</a> (encompassing present-day <a href="/wiki/Banat" title="Banat">Banat</a> in <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>) around 1030. Benedictine monks who could speak <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a> helped him to preach among the local inhabitants.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Most information of Gerard was not preserved in impartial sources, but in his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200623_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200623-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Short Life of Saint Gerard</i>, which was composed around 1100, is an <a href="/wiki/Abridgement" title="Abridgement">abridgement</a> of an earlier biography.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953152–153_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953152–153-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The earlier biography did not survive.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Short Life</i> primarily presents Gerard as a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200623_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200623-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The majority of scholars regard the <i>Short Life</i> the most reliable source of Gerard's life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Long_Life_of_Saint_Gerard" title="Long Life of Saint Gerard">Long Life of Saint Gerard</a></i> is a compilation of multiple sources, including the biography that the author of the shorter legend had also utilized.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200625_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200625-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Long Life</i> was completed in the late 13th century or in the 14th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200625_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200625-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It was regarded as a source of absolute reliability for centuries, but this view radically changed in the 20th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_Gy%C3%B6rffy" title="György Györffy">György Györffy</a> even stated that the <i>Long Life</i> was a forgery.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Historian Gábor Klaniczay also emphasizes that the longer legend contains obviously anachronistic elements.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200624,_122_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200624,_122-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> On the other hand, <a href="/wiki/Carlile_Aylmer_Macartney" title="Carlile Aylmer Macartney">Carlile Aylmer Macartney</a> says that the <i>Long Life</i> preserved the original form of Gerard's earliest (now lost) biography.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953156_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953156-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard's own work, the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Deliberatio_supra_hymnum_trium_puerorum" title="Deliberatio supra hymnum trium puerorum">Deliberatio supra hymnum trium puerorum</a></i></span> also contain references to his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Simon_of_K%C3%A9za" title="Simon of Kéza">Simon of Kéza</a>'s chronicle and the <i><a href="/wiki/Illuminated_Chronicle" class="mw-redirect" title="Illuminated Chronicle">Illuminated Chronicle</a></i> preserved fragments from the common source of Gerard's two <i>Lives</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200627_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200627-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> A 13th-century rhymed version (or <a href="/wiki/Chant" title="Chant">chant</a>) of Gerard's legend was also preserved, but it does not contain more information than the <i>Short Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199656_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199656-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_and_youth">Family and youth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family and youth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gerard's <i>Long Life</i> dedicates two chapters to his family and childhood.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Conventional elements of medieval hagiographies abound in both chapters, suggesting that the author borrowed many motives from other legends, especially from the <i><a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Life of Saint Adalbert of Prague</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gerard was born in Venice in a noble family.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The noble origin of saintly hermits was often emphasized in their legends.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The identification of Gerard's family is uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> An expanded version of <a href="/wiki/Petrus_de_Natalibus" title="Petrus de Natalibus">Petrus de Natalibus</a>'s <i>Catalogue of Saints</i>, which was published in 1516, identified Gerard as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Sagredo_family" title="Sagredo family">Sagredo family</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Although the family was granted Venetian nobility only in the 14th century, some scholars (including Fabio Banfi) accept the Sagredos' claim to their kinship with Saint Gerard.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Historian László Szegfű says that Gerard was actually a <a href="/wiki/Morosini_family" title="Morosini family">Morosini</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard's father, who was also named Gerard, and mother, Catherine, had awaited his birth for three years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> They baptised their son George because he was born on the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a> (23 April).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The year of his birth is unknown, but he was born between around 977 and 1000.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636_10-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200636-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He was renamed in the memory of his father who died during a pilgrimage or journey (anachronistically mentioned in Gerard's <i>Long Life</i> as a crusade).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637,_128_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637,_128-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesiastic_career">Ecclesiastic career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ecclesiastic career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Benedictine_monk">Benedictine monk</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Benedictine monk">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the age of five, Gerard was taken seriously ill.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His recovery was attributed to the prayers of the Benedictine monks of the <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_Monastery" title="San Giorgio Monastery">San Giorgio Monastery</a> in Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His family soon sent him to the monastery, offering him to spiritual life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200637-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Gerard took the "religious cloth" and was educated in the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș2006127-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He could read and write and knew the basic elements of arithmetic.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200638_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200638-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> His <i>Long Life</i> emphasizes that Gerard strictly observed the rules of monastic life and wore coarse cloths to "mortify his body".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> He also studied the "words of the prophets and the speeches of the Orthodox apostles".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The use of certain expressions (including <i>dux verbi</i>, or "leader of the <a href="/wiki/Logos_(Christianity)" title="Logos (Christianity)">Word</a>") suggests that Gerard read <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Dionysius_the_Areopagite" title="Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite">Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite</a> in Greek.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199658_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199658-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the founding <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the monastery, <a href="/wiki/John_Morosini" title="John Morosini">John Morosini</a>, died in 1012, Gerard was appointed <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> to administer the monastery until the new abbot, Guglielmo, was elected.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Guglielmo sent Gerard to "<a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>" to study grammar, music, philosophy and law.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639,_129_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639,_129-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Gerard mentioned his stay in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, where he read <a href="/wiki/Plato" title="Plato">Plato</a>, suggesting that the original version of the <i>Long Life</i> or its source referred to his studies in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy" title="Kingdom of Burgundy">Burgundy</a> instead of Bologna.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199659_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199659-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Gerard returned to the San Giorgo Monastery five years later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> His <i>Long Life</i> writes that Gerard was made abbot although he had been opposed to his election.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> No information about his activities as abbot was preserved in the sources, implying that he actually never held that office.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas1995221-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJékely2004304-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639_17-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200639-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard left for a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> According to his <i>Long Life</i>, he wanted to follow the example of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jerome">Saint Jerome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Modern historians say that internal strifes (armed conflicts between the <a href="/wiki/Orseolo" title="Orseolo">Orseolos</a> and their opponents) compelled him to leave the town around 1018 or 1021.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> A storm forced him to seek refuge in a Benedictine monastery on an island near <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640_21-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In the monastery, he met one Rasina.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640_21-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200640-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Historian <a href="/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_Gy%C3%B6rffy" title="György Györffy">György Györffy</a> associates Rasina with <a href="/wiki/Radla" title="Radla">Radla</a>, a close companion of <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Adalbert of Prague</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994105_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994105-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> László Mezey writes that Rasina was the abbot of the Saint Martin Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Lo%C5%A1inj" title="Lošinj">Lošinj</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199662_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199662-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Rasina persuaded Gerard to accompany him to Hungary, saying that "nowhere else in the world could one find today a more suitable place to win souls for the Lord".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200641_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200641-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Hungarians" title="Hungarians">Hungarians</a> had started in the 970s, but it accelerated only around 1000.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200616_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200616-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The systematic organization of the Church began during the reign of the first <a href="/wiki/King_of_Hungary" title="King of Hungary">king of Hungary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a>, who was crowned on the first day the new millennium.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200617_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200617-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Royal_tutor_and_hermit">Royal tutor and hermit</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Royal tutor and hermit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Bakonyb%C3%A9l_Abbey" title="Bakonybél Abbey">Bakonybél Abbey</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Portrayal_of_Stephen_I,_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="A bearded crowned man wearing a lance and the orb and cross" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg/220px-Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="295" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg/330px-Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg/440px-Portrayal_of_Stephen_I%2C_King_of_Hungary_on_the_coronation_pall.jpg 2x" data-file-width="953" data-file-height="1280" /></a><figcaption>Portrayal of <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a> who convinced Gerard to stay in Hungary instead of travelling to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Gerard and Rasina visited <a href="/wiki/Zadar" title="Zadar">Zadar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Knin" title="Knin">Knin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Senj" title="Senj">Senj</a> before reaching <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9cs" title="Pécs">Pécs</a> in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Gerard met <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurus_of_P%C3%A9cs" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Maurus of Pécs">Maurus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_P%C3%A9cs" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Pécs">bishop of Pécs</a>, and Anastasius, <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9csv%C3%A1rad_Abbey" title="Pécsvárad Abbey">Abbot of Pécsvárad</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199662_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199662-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> in Pécs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> The two prelates wanted to persuade Gerard to stay in Hungary, stating that "God's will" had brought him to the country.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200644-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> After Gerard gave sermons in their presence, Maurus and Anastasius stated that he was a "master of the word", declaring that such a cleric had never visited Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maurus and Anastasius convinced Gerard, who wanted to continue his pilgrimage to the Holy Land, to meet King Stephen I in <a href="/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r" title="Székesfehérvár">Székesfehérvár</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> During their meeting, the king emphasized that his realm was the most suitable place for Gerard "to serve God", promising that he would authorize Gerard to preach anywhere in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Stephen I even threatened Gerard that he would not allow him to continue his journey to Jerusalem, but also alluding that he would make Gerard bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645–46_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200645–46-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Finally, Gerard accepted Stephen's proposal and decided to stay in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200646_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200646-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Before long, on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Assumption" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Assumption">Feast of the Assumption</a> (15 August), Gerard gave a sermon in honor of the "<a href="/wiki/Woman_of_the_Apocalypse" title="Woman of the Apocalypse">Woman clothed with the Sun</a>", which was the first recorded sign of the cult of <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Virgin Mary</a> in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200646_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200646-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> According to Macartney, the description of Gerard's journey to Hungary and his meetings with the two prelates and the king were incorporated into the <i>Long Life</i> based on a nearly contemporaneous report, but they contain evidently imaginary details, such as the conversations between Gerard and Stephen I.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard was made the tutor of Stephen's son and heir, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary" title="Saint Emeric of Hungary">Emeric</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200650_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200650-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Gerard's role as the crown prince's tutor was only mentioned in the <i>Long Life</i>, implying that this was only an invention by the hagiographer who wanted to create a strong connection between the three most important saints of the early Kingdom of Hungary, but the story is not surely invented.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200650_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200650-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacartney1953157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Szegfű writes that Gerard may have influenced Stephen's <i><a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary#Legacy#Admonitions" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Admonitions</a></i> to Emeric.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> László Mezey proposes that Gerard was only responsible for the spiritual education of Emeric.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199661_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199661-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Emeric's education was completed, Gerard settled in the <a href="/wiki/Bakony_Hills" class="mw-redirect" title="Bakony Hills">Bakony Hills</a> to live as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> near <a href="/wiki/Bakonyb%C3%A9l" title="Bakonybél">Bakonybél</a>, at a place where the saintly <a href="/wiki/Gunther_of_Bohemia" title="Gunther of Bohemia">Gunther of Bohemia</a> had lived.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200651_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200651-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994122_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994122-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Szegfű says that Gerard's withdrawal from the royal court was the consequence of the arrival of the family of the Doge <a href="/wiki/Otto_Orseolo" title="Otto Orseolo">Otto Orseolo</a> to Hungary around 1024.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> During the following years, Gerard built a chapel at the foot of a hill,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994153_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994153-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> and wrote theological studies and <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homilies</a> (which were later lost).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231_12-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESzegfű1994231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He referred to the commentaries that he had written to the <i><a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews" title="Epistle to the Hebrews">Epistle to the Hebrews</a></i> and to the Prologue to the <i><a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">Gospel of John</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199661_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199661-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Gerard lived as a hermit for seven years, which suggests that he must have spent several years in the Bakony Hills even if the author of his legend only invented the symbolic number seven.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200651_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200651-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Bishop_of_Csan.C3.A1d"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Csanád">Bishop of Csanád</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Bishop of Csanád">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A powerful chieftain, <a href="/wiki/Ajtony" title="Ajtony">Ajtony</a>, ruled the region near the rivers <a href="/wiki/Tisza" title="Tisza">Tisza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mure%C8%99_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Mureș River">Mureș</a> in the early 11th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200652_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200652-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> He was baptised according to the "Greek rite" and settled "Greek" (or <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a>) monks in his seat on the Mureș.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200653_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200653-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> After Ajtony began taxing the salt carried on the Mureș, Stephen I of Hungary sent the royal army against him under the command of <a href="/wiki/Csan%C3%A1d" title="Csanád">Csanád</a>, who had previously been Ajtony's commander.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> Csanád defeated and killed Ajtony whose domain was transformed into a <a href="/wiki/County_(Kingdom_of_Hungary)" class="mw-redirect" title="County (Kingdom of Hungary)">county</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> Ajtony's seat was renamed for Csanád.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the conquest of Ajtony's territory, Stephen I summoned Gerard from his hermitage and made him bishop of the newly established <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Csan%C3%A1d" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Csanád">Diocese of Csanád</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThoroczkay200162_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThoroczkay200162-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200659_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200659-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> László Mezey says that the king appointed Gerard to administer the diocese because Gerard's knowledge of the Greek language and the Eastern Orthodox theological ideas enabled him to preach in a territory where Greek priests had up to that time proselytized.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199661_32-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199661-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Annales_Posonienses" title="Annales Posonienses">Annales Posonienses</a></i> recorded that "Gerard was consecrated bishop" in 1030,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThoroczkay200162_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThoroczkay200162-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> but the reliability of this date was not accepted by all historians.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200657_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200657-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> The king appointed twelve monks from the Benedictine monasteries in Hungary to accompany Gerard to his see.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200659–60_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200659–60-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> Seven of the twelve monks who could speak <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a> were tasked with interpreting for Gerard among Ajtony's former subjects.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200661_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200661-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> The Greek monks who had arrived during Ajtony's rule were transferred from Csanád to a monastery newly established at Oroszlámos (present-day <a href="/wiki/Banatsko_Aran%C4%91elovo" title="Banatsko Aranđelovo">Banatsko Aranđelovo</a> in Serbia), and their former monastery was granted to the Benedictines.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200660_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200660-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994154_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994154-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard and the Benedictine monks shared a house and he forbade them to leave it without his authorization.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200661–62_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200661–62-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> The monks were required to be present for the morning service and to wear monastic costume.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Gerard continued to wear the habits of a hermit (cilice or goat skins) and spent days in solitude in the forests near his see.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200664_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200664-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> His legend also writes that he often "took the axe" to cut woods to "mortify his flesh" and to help to "those who had to do this work".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200665_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200665-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard was a missionary bishop, tasked with the conversion of the pagan inhabitants of his diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662_47-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> His <i>Long Life</i> writes that people came to Gerard, "noblemen and commoners, rich and poor", asking him to baptize them "in the name of the Holy Trinity".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662_47-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200662-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> They brought horses, cattle, sheep, carpets, rings and necklaces to give them to the bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó200130_37-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó200130-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Long Life</i> credits Gerard with the building of churches "for every city" in his diocese to serve the growing number of believers.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200663_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200663-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> Although the <i>Long Life</i> attributes the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeaconries</a> of Gerard's diocese to him, most scholars regard this statement as a clear anachronism.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMezey199661_32-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMezey199661-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Gerard regularly visited Stephen.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200669_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200669-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> During a travel from Csanád to the royal court in <a href="/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r" title="Székesfehérvár">Székesfehérvár</a> or <a href="/wiki/Esztergom" title="Esztergom">Esztergom</a>, he and one of his clerics, Walther, stayed in a manor where a slave woman was singing while making flour on a grinder.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996103_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996103-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> Gerard referred to the music as the <i>"symphonia Ungarorum"</i> (or "drum of the Hungarians"), associating the sound of the grinder with a drum roll.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996103_52-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996103-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> Being touched by her cheerfulness while making a hard work, Gerard gave the woman precious gifts.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996105_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMészáros1996105-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stephen I died on 15 August 1038.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994170_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994170-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup> His nephew, the Venetian <a href="/wiki/Peter_Orseolo" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter Orseolo">Peter Orseolo</a> mounted the throne, but he was dethroned in 1041.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994170_54-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGyörffy1994170-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200674_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200674-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> Peter's successor, <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Aba" title="Samuel Aba">Samuel Aba</a>, had many lords executed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> He visited Csanád, asking Gerard to put a crown on his head during the mass on <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_Sunday" class="mw-redirect" title="Resurrection Sunday">Resurrection Sunday</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> He refused Aba, but the bishops who accompanied the king to Csanád, performed the coronation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> Gerard went to the pulpit, declaring that the "sword of vengeance will descend" upon Aba's head in three years, because he had gained the kingdom by deceit.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> The credibility of the report of the <i>Long Life</i> of Aba's visit in Gerard's see is subject to scholarly debates.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SzentGellert68.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/SzentGellert68.jpg/220px-SzentGellert68.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/SzentGellert68.jpg/330px-SzentGellert68.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/SzentGellert68.jpg/440px-SzentGellert68.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1096" data-file-height="1524" /></a><figcaption>A <a href="/wiki/Miniature_(illuminated_manuscript)" title="Miniature (illuminated manuscript)">miniature</a> from the <i><a href="/wiki/Anjou_Legendarium" title="Anjou Legendarium">Anjou Legendarium</a></i> showing St. Gerard falling to his death (1330)</figcaption></figure>
<p>The Holy Roman Emperor, <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry III</a>, invaded Hungary and defeated Aba in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_M%C3%A9nf%C5%91" title="Battle of Ménfő">Battle of Ménfő</a> in 1044.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> Peter Orseolo was restored, but his rule was unpopular, because he favored his German and Italian retaineers.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675_56-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETurcuș200675-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gerard's <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a> took place on 24 September 1046, during the <a href="/wiki/Vata_pagan_uprising" title="Vata pagan uprising">Vata pagan uprising</a>. His co-martyrs were <a href="/wiki/Bystrik" class="mw-redirect" title="Bystrik">Bystrik</a> and Buldus. There are various accounts of his death. According to one, he was stoned, pierced with a lance, and his body thrown from the <a href="/wiki/Gell%C3%A9rt_Hill" title="Gellért Hill">Blocksberg cliff</a> into the Danube.<sup id="cite_ref-courier_57-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-courier-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup> An alternate account claims that he was placed on a two-wheel cart, hauled to the hilltop and rolled down a hill of <a href="/wiki/Budapest" title="Budapest">Buda</a>, now named <a href="/wiki/Gellert_Hill" class="mw-redirect" title="Gellert Hill">Gellert Hill</a>, then still being alive at the bottom, was beaten to death. Other unverified tales report him as being put into a spiked barrel and rolled down the hill during a mass revolt of pagans.
</p><p>Canonized in 1083, along with St. Stephen and St. Emeric, Gerard is currently one of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of Hungary. His feast day is 24 September.<sup id="cite_ref-courier_57-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-courier-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
