					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Helen Enselmini</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Elena Enselmini</i>), also known as the <b>Blessed Helen of Arcella</b> (1207&#8211;1231/1242), was an <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> who has been <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helen_Enselmini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Enselmini was born in <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, from an impoverished noble family of the region. About 1220, <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, recently returned from the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a>, went to that city to supervise the opening of a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> on land donated in the nearby village of <a href="/wiki/Arcella_(Padua)" title="Arcella (Padua)">Arcella</a>, to which was attached a small friary of the <a href="/wiki/Friars_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Friars Minor">Friars Minor</a> he had founded. Moved by a sermon she heard him give on the streets of Padua, Enselmini was inspired to become a Poor Clare at the age of 12, according to tradition receiving the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> from Francis himself.
</p><p>Although she was always in frail health, Enselmini spent the rest of her life living the full rigor of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Clare">Rule of St. Clare</a>, including frequent <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a>, poverty of life and manual labor, without complaint. She was said to have had the gift of <a href="/wiki/Inedia" title="Inedia">inedia</a>, living solely off the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a> for months. Her health declined severely later in life, however, and she became both blind and mute in her last years, which she accepted with patience.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She is said to have been advised and comforted during this period by <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">Anthony of Padua</a>. She died on 4 November 1231 (or 1242) of natural causes.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helen_Enselmini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After her death, Enselmini was honored as a saint by Franciscan Order as well as by the people of Padua and the surrounding region. Her body was described as still lifelike for a considerable period and miracles were reported by visitors to her tomb. A century later, <a href="/wiki/Giusto_de%27_Menabuoi" title="Giusto de&#39; Menabuoi">Giusto de' Menabuoi</a> included her in his mural of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of the city in the <a href="/wiki/Baptistry" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptistry">baptistry</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Padua_Cathedral" title="Padua Cathedral">Cathedral of Padua</a>. In the 15th century, the monastery was re-dedicated to her as the Monastery of St. Helen outside the Walls, with an <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a> granted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a> in 1443 to all those who contributed to its rebuilding.
</p><p>In 1693 a delegation representing the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Conventual" title="Order of Friars Minor Conventual">Order of Friars Minor Conventual</a>, as well as <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Barbarigo" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory Barbarigo">Gregory Barbarigo</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Padua">Bishop of Padua</a>, and various other Church authorities, went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to seek Enselmini's beatification. She was beatified on 29 October 1695 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_X" title="Pope Leo X">Pope Leo X</a>, later confirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today her remains are encased in a glass coffin in the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_dell%27Arcella,_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Sanctuary dell&#39;Arcella, Padua">Sanctuary of Arcella</a>, now part of the City of Padua.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
