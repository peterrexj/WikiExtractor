<p><b>Roseline</b> or <b>Rossolina de Villeneuve</b> (1263 – January 17, 1329) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. She is regarded as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roseline_de_Villeneuve&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Roseline was born to an aristocratic family, at the <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau" title="Château">château</a> of <a href="/wiki/Les_Arcs-sur-Argens" class="mw-redirect" title="Les Arcs-sur-Argens">Les Arcs-sur-Argens</a>, <a href="/wiki/Var_(department)" title="Var (department)">Var</a>, in eastern <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Draguignan" title="Draguignan">Draguignan</a>. As a child, she discreetly distributed food from the chateau larder to the local poor people. A similar <a href="/wiki/Miracle_of_the_roses" title="Miracle of the roses">Miracle of the roses</a> is told of her, as is attributed to several other saints.
</p><p>Having overcome her father's opposition, Roseline became a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> at Bertaud in the <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alps</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9" title="Dauphiné">Dauphiné</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">consecration</a> took place in 1288, and in about 1330 she succeeded her aunt, Jeanne (Diane) de Villeneuve, as <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prioress</a> of La Celle-Robaud in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Fr%C3%A9jus" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Fréjus">Diocese of Fréjus</a> near her home. In 1320 her brother <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A9lion_de_Villeneuve" title="Hélion de Villeneuve">Hélion de Villeneuve</a>, Grand Master (1319–46) of the <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_St._John" class="mw-redirect" title="Knights of St. John">Knights of St. John</a>, restored the monastery, and in 1323 and 1328 <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a>, formerly Bishop of Fréjus, increased its revenue, granting <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgences</a> for the anniversary of the dedication of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Many visions together with extraordinary austerities and great power over demons are ascribed to her.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Roseline obtained leave to resign her office before her death in 1329 at the age of sixty-six.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roseline_de_Villeneuve&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her feast was given in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i> on 11 June, the day of the first <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation of her relics</a> in 1334 by her brother, Elzéar de Villeneuve, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Digne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Digne">Bishop of Digne</a>. It is now observed on her day of death, 17 January. A plenary indulgence has been granted to all those who visit a Carthusian church on that day.<sup id="cite_ref-dft_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dft-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There has always been a local cultus and this was confirmed for the Diocese of Fréjus by a Decree of 1851, for the Carthusian Order in 1857. Roseline is usually represented with a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> containing two eyes, recalling the fact that her eyes were removed and preserved apart. This relic was still extant at Arcs in 1882. There is no ancient life of the saint, but that given in the <i>Acta Sanctorum</i>, 2 June, 489 sq., was constructed by <a href="/wiki/Papebroch" class="mw-redirect" title="Papebroch">Papebroch</a> from ancient documents.
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a>, situated at Les Arcs-sur-Argens near Draguignan, has been for six centuries a place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. The Roseline Chapel near the Château Sainte Roseline is located in a private vineyard. It contains the body of Roseline in a glass reliquiary. According to legend, one day Rosaline was so caught up in contemplation that she failed to make dinner for the nuns. Angels came down, prepared the dinner and set the table. This story is depicted at the chapel in a wall <a href="/wiki/Mosaic" title="Mosaic">mosaic</a> by <a href="/wiki/Chagall" class="mw-redirect" title="Chagall">Chagall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Roseline de Villeneuve is a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Fr%C3%A9jus-Toulon" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Fréjus-Toulon">Diocese of Fréjus-Toulon</a> and of sailors.<sup id="cite_ref-dft_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dft-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
