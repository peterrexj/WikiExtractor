<p><b>Saints Simplicius, Faustinus and Beatrix</b> (or <i>Beatrice, Viatrix</i>) were siblings martyred in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution (302 or 303).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simplicius,_Faustinus_and_Beatrix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The legend about them is that the brothers Simplicius and Faustinus were cruelly tortured on account of their Christian faith, beaten with <a href="/wiki/Club_(weapon)" title="Club (weapon)">clubs</a>, and finally <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>; their bodies were thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a> (according to another version a stone was tied to them and they were drowned). Their sister Beatrix had the bodies drawn out of the water and buried.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Beatrix is thought to be a manuscript corruption of the name "Viatrix".<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then for seven months she lived with a pious woman named Lucina and together they secretly helped persecuted Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Finally she was discovered and arrested. Her accuser was Lucretius, a neighboring kinsman,  who desired to obtain possession of her lands.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She asserted before the judge that she would never sacrifice to demons, because she was a Christian. As punishment, she was strangled in prison. Her friend Lucina buried her with her brothers in the cemetery <i>ad Ursum Pileatum</i> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Portuensis" title="Via Portuensis">Via Portuensis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Divine punishment soon overtook the accuser Lucretius, who at a feast was mocking the folly of the martyrs. A small child cried out, "Thou hast committed murder and hast taken unjust possession of land. Thou art a slave of the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devil</a>". The angry citizens grabbed and tortured Lucretius for three hours, eventually throwing him into a bottomless pit.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The terror of those present was so great that they became Christians. This is the story of the legend. Trustworthy Acts concerning the history of the two brothers and sister are no longer in existence.<sup id="cite_ref-Loffler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simplicius,_Faustinus_and_Beatrix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of these martyrs, apart from their names, nothing is known except that they were buried on July 29 in the Cemetery of Generosa on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Portuensis" title="Via Portuensis">Via Portuensis</a>. Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is thus July 29.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Because of the extremely limited knowledge about them, they are no longer included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but, since they are included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, they continue to be officially <a href="/wiki/Venerate" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerate">venerated</a> and may be included in local liturgical calendars. From the time of the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> until 1969, the General Roman Calendar included a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> of them within the liturgy of <a href="/wiki/Martha" title="Martha">Saint Martha</a> on July 29. 
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_II" title="Pope Leo II">Pope Leo II</a> (682-683) <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> their relics to a church which he had built in Rome in honor of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Bibiana" title="Santa Bibiana">Santa Bibiana</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Later the greater part of the relics of the martyrs were taken to the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Maggiore" title="Santa Maria Maggiore">Santa Maria Maggiore</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Sisters_of_Perpetual_Adoration" title="Benedictine Sisters of Perpetual Adoration">Benedictine Sisters of Perpetual Adoration</a> have relics purported to be those of Saint Beatrice at their monastery in <a href="/wiki/Clyde,_Missouri" title="Clyde, Missouri">Clyde, Missouri</a>, where a wax effigy of the saint lies in a glass case.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:183px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wappen_Lauterbach.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/Wappen_Lauterbach.png" decoding="async" width="181" height="195" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="181" data-file-height="195" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wappen_Lauterbach.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Lauterbach, Coat of Arms</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simplicius,_Faustinus_and_Beatrix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Simplicius is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Lauterbach,_Hesse" title="Lauterbach, Hesse">Lauterbach</a> and one of the patrons of the city of <a href="/wiki/Fulda" title="Fulda">Fulda</a>. Images of Simplicius can be found on monuments, house facades and as a work of art throughout Lauterbach.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simplicius,_Faustinus_and_Beatrix&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Simplicius is represented in art with a pennant, on the shield of which are three lilies, called <i>the crest of Simplicius</i>; the lilies are a symbol of purity of heart. Saint Beatrix is portrayed with a cord in her hand, because she was strangled.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Their feast day is July 29.
</p>
