<p><b>Agatha</b><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> <b>of Sicily</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;231 – 251 AD</span>) is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">Christian saint</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast</a> is on 5 February. Agatha was born in <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a>, part of the <a href="/wiki/Sicilia_(Roman_province)" title="Sicilia (Roman province)">Roman Province of Sicily</a>, and was martyred <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;251</span>. She is one of several virgin martyrs who are commemorated by name in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agatha is the patron saint of Catania, <a href="/wiki/Molise" title="Molise">Molise</a>, <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_Museum_of_Gallipoli" title="Diocesan Museum of Gallipoli">Gallipoli in Apulia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Zamarramala" title="Zamarramala">Zamarramala</a>, a municipality of the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Segovia" title="Province of Segovia">Province of Segovia</a> in Spain. She is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Breast_cancer" title="Breast cancer">breast cancer</a> patients, rape victims,
<a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wet_nurse" title="Wet nurse">wet nurses</a>, bell-founders, and <a href="/wiki/Baker" title="Baker">bakers</a>, and is invoked against fire, earthquakes, and eruptions of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Etna" title="Mount Etna">Mount Etna</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_history">Early history</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early history">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agatha is buried at the Badia di Sant'Agata, Catania.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> She is listed in the late 6th-century <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i></span> associated with <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and the <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn"><a href="/wiki/Synaxarion" class="mw-redirect" title="Synaxarion">Synaxarion</a></i></span>, the calendar of the church of Carthage, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;530</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Agatha also appears in one of the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">carmina</i></span> of <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two early churches were dedicated to her in Rome; Sant'Agata in via della Lugaretta, <a href="/wiki/Trastevere" title="Trastevere">Trastevere</a>, and notably the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Sant%27Agata_dei_Goti" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Sant&#39;Agata dei Goti">Church of Sant'Agata dei Goti</a> in Via Mazzarino,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Disputed_statement" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:Disputed statement"><span title="This claim has reliable sources with contradicting facts (February 2023)">disputed</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Agatha_of_Sicily" title="Talk:Agatha of Sicily">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> a <a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church">titular church</a> with apse mosaics of <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;460</span> and traces of a fresco cycle,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> overpainted by Gismondo Cerrini in 1630. In the 6th century AD, the church was adapted to <a href="/wiki/Arian_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Arian Christianity">Arianism</a>, hence its name "Saint Agatha of <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Goths</a>", and later reconsecrated by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, who confirmed her traditional sainthood.
</p><p>Agatha is also depicted in the mosaics of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Apollinare_Nuovo" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant&#39;Apollinare Nuovo">Sant'Apollinare Nuovo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, where she appears, richly dressed, in the <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">procession</a> of female martyrs along the north wall. Her image forms an initial 'I' in the Sacramentary of Gellone, which dates from the end of the 8th century.
</p>

<p>One of the most highly venerated virgin martyrs of Christian antiquity, Agatha was put to death during the <a href="/wiki/Decian_persecution" title="Decian persecution">Decian persecution</a> (250–253) in Catania, Sicily, for her determined profession of faith.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her written legend<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> comprises "straightforward accounts of interrogation, torture, resistance, and triumph which constitute some of the earliest <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic literature</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and are reflected in later recensions, the earliest surviving one being an illustrated late 10th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">passio</a></i> bound into a composite volume<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> in the <a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_nationale_de_France" title="Bibliothèque nationale de France">Bibliothèque nationale de France</a>, originating probably in <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>, Burgundy; in its margin illustrations Magdalena Carrasco detected Carolingian or Late Antique <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconographic traditions</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg/220px-Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="239" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg/330px-Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg/440px-Saint-L%C3%B4_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_Vitrail_Baie_17_Jugement_de_sainte_Agathe_2019_08_19.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2537" data-file-height="2757" /></a><figcaption>Agatha in front of the judge as depicted in a stained glass window from 1515 in Notre-Dame, <a href="/wiki/Saint-L%C3%B4" title="Saint-Lô">Saint-Lô</a><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to the 13th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> (III.15) by <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>, 15-year-old Agatha, from a rich and noble family, made a <a href="/wiki/Bride_of_Christ" title="Bride of Christ">vow of virginity</a> and rejected the amorous advances of the Roman prefect Quintianus, who thought he could force her to turn away from her vow and marry him. His persistent proposals were consistently spurned by Agatha. This was during the persecutions of Decius, so Quintianus, knowing she was a Christian, reported her to the authorities. Quintianus himself was governor of the district.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Quintianus expected Agatha to give in to his demands when faced with torture and possible death, but Agatha simply reaffirmed her belief in God by praying: "Jesus Christ, Lord of all, you see my heart, you know my desires. Possess all that I am. I am your sheep: make me worthy to overcome the devil." To force her to change her mind, Quintianus sent Agatha to Aphrodisia, the keeper of a brothel, and had her imprisoned there; however, the punishment failed, with Agatha remaining a Christian.<sup id="cite_ref-italymag_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-italymag-24">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
Quintianus sent for Agatha again, arguing with her and threatening her, before finally having her imprisoned and tortured. She was stretched on a rack to be torn with iron hooks, burned with torches, and whipped. Her breasts were torn off with tongs. </p><figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg/220px-AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="421" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg/330px-AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg/440px-AgatheRouenCathedral.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1983" data-file-height="3792" /></a><figcaption>St Agatha as depicted in a stained glass window in <a href="/wiki/Rouen_Cathedral" title="Rouen Cathedral">Rouen Cathedral</a></figcaption></figure><p> After further dramatic confrontations with Quintianus, represented in a sequence of dialogues in her <i>passio</i> that document her fortitude and steadfast devotion, Agatha was then sentenced to be <a href="/wiki/Burnt_at_the_stake" class="mw-redirect" title="Burnt at the stake">burnt at the stake</a>; however, an earthquake prevented this from happening, and she was instead sent to prison, where St. <a href="/wiki/Peter_the_Apostle" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter the Apostle">Peter the Apostle</a> appeared to her and healed her wounds.<sup id="cite_ref-stracke_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stracke-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agatha died in prison, probably in the year 251 according to the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Legenda Aurea</i></span>. Although the martyrdom of Agatha is authenticated, and her veneration as a saint had spread beyond her native place even in antiquity, there is no reliable information concerning the details of her death.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Osbern_Bokenam" title="Osbern Bokenam">Osbern Bokenam</a>, <i>A Legend of Holy Women</i>, written in the 1440s, offers some further detail.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Maltese tradition, during the persecution of Roman Emperor Decius (AD 249–251), Agatha, together with some of her friends, fled from Sicily and took refuge in Malta. Some historians believe that her stay on the island was rather short, and she spent her days in a rock-hewn crypt at <a href="/wiki/Rabat,_Malta" title="Rabat, Malta">Rabat</a>, praying and teaching Christianity to children. After some time, Agatha returned to Sicily, where she faced martyrdom. Agatha was arrested and brought before Quintianus, praetor of Catania, who condemned her to torture and imprisonment.
</p><p>The crypt of St. Agatha is an underground basilica, which from early ages was venerated by the Maltese. At the time of St. Agatha's stay, the crypt was a small natural cave, which, later on, during the 4th or 5th century, was enlarged and embellished.<sup id="cite_ref-malta_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-malta-27">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the Reformation era, Agatha was retained in the calendar of the Church of England's <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer" title="Book of Common Prayer">Book of Common Prayer</a> with her feast on 5 February. Several Church of England parish churches are dedicated to her.
</p><p>A feast day to honor Agatha on 5 February was given final authorization in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> in 2022.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The translation of her relics is commemorated on 10 March and 17 August.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Festival_of_Saint_Agatha_in_Catania">Festival of Saint Agatha in Catania</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Festival of Saint Agatha in Catania">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Festival_of_Saint_Agatha_(Catania)" title="Festival of Saint Agatha (Catania)">Festival of Saint Agatha (Catania)</a></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Festival_of_Saint_Agatha_(Catania)" title="Festival of Saint Agatha (Catania)">Festival of Saint Agatha</a> in <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a> is a major festival in the region, it takes place in the first five days of February. The <a href="/wiki/Catania_Cathedral" title="Catania Cathedral">Catania Cathedral</a> (also known as <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Cattedrale di Sant'Agata</i></span>) is dedicated to her.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG/220px-Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG/330px-Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG/440px-Mons_Ste_Agathe_919.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>Saint Agatha's breasts sculpted in the fortification walls at <a href="/wiki/Mons,_Var" title="Mons, Var">Mons, Var</a> in the south of France</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Agatha is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of rape victims, breast cancer patients, wet nurses, and bellfounders (due to the shape of her severed breasts). She is also considered to be a powerful intercessor when people suffer from fires. Her feast day is celebrated on 5 February.
</p><p>She is also a patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a>, where in 1551 her intercession through a reported apparition to a Benedictine nun is said to have saved Malta from <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_(1565)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Malta (1565)">Turkish invasion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-malta_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-malta-27">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She became the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">Republic of San Marino</a> after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> restored the independence of the state on her feast day of 5 February 1740.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sorihuela_del_Guadalimar" title="Sorihuela del Guadalimar">Sorihuela del Guadalimar</a> (Spain), <a href="/wiki/Molise" title="Molise">Molise</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kalsa" title="Kalsa">Kalsa</a>, a historical quarter of <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>.
</p><p>She is claimed as the patroness of <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>. The year after her death, the stilling of an eruption of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Etna" title="Mount Etna">Mount Etna</a> was attributed to her intercession. As a result, apparently, people continued to ask her prayers for protection against fire.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-31">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, Agatha is considered the patron saint of fire services.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>, Agatha is the patron saint of bell ringers in service of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales" title="Catholic Church in England and Wales">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg/220px-Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="275" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg/330px-Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg/440px-Minnuzze_di_sant%27aita.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1354" data-file-height="1693" /></a><figcaption><span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Minne di Sant'Agata</i></span>, a typical Sicilian sweet shaped as a breast, representing the cut breasts of Saint Agatha</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Agatha is often depicted <a href="/wiki/Saint_symbology" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint symbology">iconographically</a> carrying her excised breasts on a platter, as in <a href="/wiki/Bernardino_Luini" title="Bernardino Luini">Bernardino Luini</a>'s <i>Saint Agatha</i> (1510–1515) in the Galleria Borghese, Rome, in which Agatha contemplates the breasts on a standing <a href="/wiki/Salver" title="Salver">salver</a> held in her hand.
</p><p>The tradition of making <a href="/wiki/Gebildbrot" title="Gebildbrot">shaped pastry</a> on the feast of St. Agatha, such as Agatha bread or buns, or so-called <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it"><a href="/wiki/Minne_di_Sant%27Agata" class="mw-redirect" title="Minne di Sant&#39;Agata">Minne di Sant'Agata</a></i></span> ("Breasts of St. Agatha") or <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Minni di Virgini</i></span> ("Breasts of the virgin"), is found in many countries.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_of_Sicily&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Basque_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Basque people">Basque people</a> have a tradition of gathering on Saint Agatha's Eve (<a href="/wiki/Basque_language" title="Basque language">Basque</a>: <i lang="eu">Santa Ageda bezpera</i>) and going round the village. Homeowners can choose to hear a song about her life, accompanied by the beats of their walking sticks on the floor or a prayer for the household's deceased. After that, the homeowner donates food to the chorus.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> This song has varying lyrics according to the local tradition and the <a href="/wiki/Basque_language" title="Basque language">Basque language</a>. An exceptional case was that of 1937, during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>, when a version appeared that in the Spanish language praised the Soviet ship <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Komsomol_(ship)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Komsomol (ship) (page does not exist)">Komsomol</a></i>, which had sunk while carrying Soviet weapons to the <a href="/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic" title="Second Spanish Republic">Second Spanish Republic</a>.
</p><p>An <a href="/wiki/Festival_of_Saint_Agatha_(Catania)" title="Festival of Saint Agatha (Catania)">annual festival</a> to commemorate the life of Saint Agatha takes place in <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, from 3 to 5 February. The festival culminates in an all-night procession through the city.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Agatha%27s_Tower" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Agatha&#39;s Tower">St. Agatha's Tower</a> is a former Knight's stronghold located in the north west of Malta. The seventeenth-century tower served as a military base during both World Wars and was used as a radar station by the Maltese army.<sup id="cite_ref-malta_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-malta-27">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Agatha is also commemorated in literature. The Italian poet Martha Marchina wrote an epigram in <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Marthae_Marchinae_Virginis_Neapolitanae_Musa_Postuma" title="Marthae Marchinae Virginis Neapolitanae Musa Postuma">Musa Posthuma</a></i></span> that commemorates her martyrdom. In it, Marchina characterizes Agatha as powerful and she reclaims that power because she has become more beautiful through her wounds.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agatha of Sicily is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">honored</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Feasts_and_Fasts" class="mw-redirect" title="Lesser Feasts and Fasts">Lesser Feast</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">liturgical calendar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church in the United States of America</a><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> on <a href="/wiki/5_February" class="mw-redirect" title="5 February">5 February</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li id="cite_note-6"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-6">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">'Agatha' is the <a href="/wiki/Latinisation_of_names" title="Latinisation of names">Latinized</a> form of the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a> <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἀγαθή</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Agathe</i></span>), derived from the Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">ἀγαθός</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">agathos</i></span>, meaning "good";<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Jacobus de Voragine, taking <a href="/wiki/Etymology" title="Etymology">etymology</a> in the Classical tradition, as a text for a creative <a href="/wiki/Excursus" title="Excursus">excursus</a>, made of 'Agatha' one symbolic origin in <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">ἅγιος</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">agios</i></span>), "sacred", and <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Θεός</span></span> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Theos</i></span>), "God", and another in <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">a-geos</i></span>, "without Earth", meaning virginally untainted by earthly desires.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-9"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-9">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The relics of St. Agatha, in particular her breasts, were stolen, on orders of the saint herself, and brought to Gallipoli in 1126. She is the patron of the diocese of Gallipoli, the cathedral of Gallipoli, and of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-11"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-11">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The present rebuilding of the ancient foundation was by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Vaccarini" title="Giovanni Battista Vaccarini">Giovanni Battista Vaccarini</a> (1767).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-17"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-17">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The date of 460 appears in TCI, <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Roma e dintorni</i></span>; a letter from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hadrian_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Hadrian I">Pope Hadrian I</a> (died 795) to <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> remarks that Gregory (died 604) ordered the church adorned with mosaics and frescoes.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-20"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-20">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The volume comprising texts of various places and dates was probably compiled when it was in the collection of <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Colbert" title="Jean-Baptiste Colbert">Jean-Baptiste Colbert</a> from which it entered the French royal collection.</span>
</li>
