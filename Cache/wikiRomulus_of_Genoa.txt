<p><b>Saint Romulus</b> (or <b>Remo</b>) <b>of Genoa</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Romolo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Ligurian_(Romance_language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ligurian (Romance language)">Ligurian</a>: <i lang="lij">Reumo(l)o</i>) was an early <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Genoa" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Genoa">Bishop of Genoa</a>, around the time of <a href="/wiki/Syrus_of_Genoa" title="Syrus of Genoa">Saint Syrus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His dates are uncertain: since <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> traditional lists compiled from local liturgies generally place his bishopric fourth in a largely legendary list.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He fled from Genoa and never returned<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He died in the cave he inhabited at Villa Matutiae,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> a town on the <a href="/wiki/Italian_Riviera" title="Italian Riviera">Italian Riviera</a> which later adopted his name, becoming San Remo (from 15th century until the first half of the 20th century), and later <a href="/wiki/Sanremo" title="Sanremo">Sanremo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romulus_of_Genoa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 876 the bishop Sabbatinus brought his remains to <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, to the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Siro_(Genoa)" title="San Siro (Genoa)">San Siro</a>, where a new structure was consecrated in 1023.
</p><p>Since he was invoked in defence of Villa Matutiæ from its inhabitants during enemy attack, the saint is depicted with episcopal dress and a sword in hand.
</p><p>St Romulus' <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> had been kept on October 13, the traditional date of his death, as well as on December 22. In the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Genoa" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Genoa">Archdiocese of Genoa</a> his feast day is now celebrated on November 6, together with two more of its early bishops: <a href="/w/index.php?title=Valentine_of_Genoa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Valentine of Genoa (page does not exist)">Saint Valentine of Genoa</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Genoa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Felix of Genoa (page does not exist)">Saint Felix of Genoa</a>.
</p>
