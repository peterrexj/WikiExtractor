<p><b>Luigi Giovanni Orione</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> priest who was active in answering the social needs of his nation as it faced the social upheavals of the late 19th century. To this end, he founded a <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> of men. He has been declared a saint by the Catholic Church.

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Orione was born into a poor family at <a href="/wiki/Pontecurone" title="Pontecurone">Pontecurone</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Alessandria" title="Province of Alessandria">Province of Alessandria</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of Italy, on the <a href="/wiki/Vigil" title="Vigil">vigil</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_St._John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="Nativity of St. John the Baptist">Nativity of St. John the Baptist</a>. He was named after <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> the next day by Michele Cattaneo, the <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> of the town. His father, Vittorio, was a street paver of few words and his mother, Carolina, was an energetic, pious, thrifty homemaker.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At thirteen years of age he entered the Franciscan Friary of Voghera (Pavia), but he left after one year owing to poor health.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> From 1886 to 1889 Orione was a student at the Valdocco <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco#Adulthood" title="John Bosco">Oratory</a> in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> operated by the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Clissold_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clissold-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> There he gained the attention of <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a>, the founder, who numbered him among his favorite pupils. From the age of 13, Luigi began to suffer health problems. However, three years later, in 1888, he was present at Bosco's death in Turin in 1888. At that moment his ailments were miraculously cured.<sup id="cite_ref-Clissold_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clissold-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Orione was determined to become a priest and entered the seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Turin" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Turin">Archdiocese of Turin</a>. He became a member of both the San Marziano Society for Mutual Help and the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Society of Saint Vincent de Paul">Society of Saint Vincent de Paul</a>. In 1892, inspired by the education he had received from the followers of John Bosco, the 20-year-old seminarian opened his own oratory to educate the poor boys of the city, and the following year he started a boarding school for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest on April 13, 1895.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Starting in 1899, Orione started to gather a group of priests and clerics that were to become <i>Piccola Opera della Divina Provvidenza</i> (Little Work of Divine Providence). In 1903 the group received the full authorization of the bishop as a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> called the <a href="/wiki/Sons_of_Divine_Providence" title="Sons of Divine Providence">Sons of Divine Providence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of the priests who was in his inner circle was <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_Perosi" title="Lorenzo Perosi">Lorenzo Perosi</a>, who later became Perpetual Director of the <a href="/wiki/Sistine_Chapel_Choir" title="Sistine Chapel Choir">Sistine Chapel Choir</a> and one of the most famous composers of sacred music. Perosi was born in the same year and the same region as Orione; they remained lifelong friends.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG/170px-MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="240" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG/255px-MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG/340px-MadonnaDellaGuardia.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1802" data-file-height="2547" /></a><figcaption>Don Orione was buried in the Santuario di Nostra Signora della Guardia in Tortona, the church that he himself built in 1931, with the help of priests and <a href="/wiki/Acolyte" title="Acolyte">acolytes</a>. It was built in gratitude to the <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Madonna</a> for ending the hostilities of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>. It is the most important pilgrimage site in the world for Orione's followers.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1908 Orione went to <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Reggio_Calabria" title="Reggio Calabria">Reggio Calabria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, both of which had been devastated by earthquakes. He dedicated three years to help those in need, most especially the caring of orphans. In 1915 he went to <a href="/wiki/Marsica" title="Marsica">Marsica</a> when that region had a similarly devastating earthquake. That same year he founded the Little Missionary Sisters of Charity.
</p><p>At the end of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, Orione began to expand his work. He founded schools, farming colonies, charity organizations and nursing homes—always with a special emphasis on helping orphans and the poor. Over the next two decades, he started foundations throughout Italy and the Americas.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrine_of_the_Madonna_della_Guardia">Shrine of the Madonna della Guardia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Shrine of the Madonna della Guardia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1931, he founded the Shrine of the Madonna della Guardia in Tortona, which to this day is the principal church in the world for the Orionine order. It is also a center for annual music festivals in honor of Orione's friend, the hitherto mentioned Perosi.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_illness">Final illness</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Final illness">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the winter of 1940, Orione started to suffer serious cardiac and pulmonary ailments. He went to <a href="/wiki/Sanremo" title="Sanremo">Sanremo</a> to recuperate, but not without a tinge of regret. On March 8, 1940, on the eve of his departure for Sanremo, Don Orione is recorded as saying, "It is not among the palm trees that I would like to die," he said, "but amongst the poor who are Jesus Christ."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Four days later, surrounded by fellow priests of his Orionine order, Luigi Orione died. His last words were, "Jesus, Jesus! Jesus! I am going..."<sup id="cite_ref-B95_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B95-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Now he is the patron saint of the abandoned.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg/280px-Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="178" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg/420px-Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg/560px-Saint_luigi_orione_body.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1019" /></a><figcaption>Saint Luigi Orione's body in <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary" title="Sanctuary">Sanctuary</a> of Nostra Signora della Guardia, in <a href="/wiki/Tortona" title="Tortona">Tortona</a>, Italy</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Orione&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Orione's mortal remains have rested in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> of the Shrine of La Madonna della Guardia in Tortona, which he himself founded, since his burial on March 19, 1940<sup id="cite_ref-B95_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-B95-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His body was later <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> in 1965 for examination. On October 26, 1980, he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. Nearly 24 years later, he was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by that same pope, on May 16, 2004.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today the charitable organizations begun by Orione are still operating in abundance throughout the world. In the United States, the national shrine and headquarters of the Sons of Divine Providence is located on a well-known hill in <a href="/wiki/East_Boston" title="East Boston">East Boston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Massachusetts" title="Massachusetts">Massachusetts</a>, known as <a href="/wiki/Orient_Heights" title="Orient Heights">Orient Heights</a>.
</p>
