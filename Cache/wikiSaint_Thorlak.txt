<p><b>Thorlak Thorhallsson</b> (<a href="/wiki/Icelandic_language" title="Icelandic language">Icelandic</a>: <i>Þorlákur Þórhallsson</i>; 1133 – 23 December 1193) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Iceland</a>. He was Bishop of Skálholt from 1178 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Thorlak's relics were translated to the <a href="/wiki/Sk%C3%A1lholt_Cathedral" title="Skálholt Cathedral">Cathedral of Skalholt</a> in 1198, not long after his successor, <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A1ll_J%C3%B3nsson" title="Páll Jónsson">Páll Jónsson</a>, announced at the <a href="/wiki/Althing" title="Althing">Althing</a> that vows could be made to Thorlak. 
</p><p>His status as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> did not receive official recognition from the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> until 14 January 1984, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a> canonized him and declared him the patron saint of Iceland.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 23 December, when Thorlac's Mass is celebrated in Iceland.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Thorlak&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in 1133 at <a href="/wiki/Hl%C3%AD%C3%B0arendi" title="Hlíðarendi">Hlíðarendi</a> in the see of Skálholt in southern Iceland,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Thorlak was from an agrarian family.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> before he was fifteen and a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Priesthood in the Catholic Church">priest</a> at the age of eighteen. He studied abroad at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> with the <a href="/wiki/School_of_Saint_Victor" title="School of Saint Victor">Victorines</a>, where he learned the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Rule of Saint Augustine">Rule of Saint Augustine</a> from roughly 1153 to 1159, and then studied <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lincoln,_Lincolnshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Lincoln, Lincolnshire">Lincoln</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201019-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning to Iceland in 1165, Thorlak founded a monastery of <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Canons Regular</a> at <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Eykkvab%C3%A6r" class="mw-redirect" title="Þykkvabær">Þykkvabær</a> after refusing to marry a rich widow. There he devoted himself to a strictly religious life, refusing to marry (many other Icelandic priests were married) and devoting himself to reciting the <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Our Father</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">Creed</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a>, as well as fifty <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a>.
</p><p>Thorlak was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> a bishop by <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Nidaros" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustine of Nidaros">Augustine of Nidaros</a> and worked to regulate the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian Rule</a> in Iceland, as well as eradicate <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a>, lay patronage, and clerical incontinency.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Thorlak&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thorlak's life and dozens of his miracles are described in great detail in the Icelandic saga <i><a href="/wiki/%C3%9Eorl%C3%A1ks_saga_helga" title="Þorláks saga helga">Þorláks saga helga</a></i> (the Saga of Saint Thorlak), republished in Icelandic on the occasion of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a>'s visit to Iceland in 1989.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It seems likely that Thorlak's informal <a href="/wiki/Sanctification" class="mw-redirect" title="Sanctification">sanctification</a> in the Church in Iceland, promoted by Latin texts on which this was based, "was arranged in Icelandic ecclesiastical circles, clerics of both dioceses being conspicuous in reports of early miracles".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020–21_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020–21-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thorlak was officially recognised as a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> on 14 January 1984, when John Paul II canonized him and declared him the patron saint of Iceland.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahnGottskálk201020-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sacred reliquary of Thorlak was maintained in the Diocese of <a href="/wiki/Sk%C3%A1lholt" title="Skálholt">Skálholt</a> until it was destroyed in the <a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a>, and his mortal remains were strewn about the cathedral grounds. The only known remaining relic of Thorlak is a bone fragment contained with other saints' relics in a lead box in sanctuary's end wall ("The Golden Locker") of the St. <a href="/wiki/Magnus_Cathedral" title="Magnus Cathedral">Magnus Cathedral</a>, <a href="/wiki/Faroe_Islands" title="Faroe Islands">Faroe Islands</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Novena">Novena</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Thorlak&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Novena">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A novena, or nine-day devotional prayer, in honor of Thorlac was approved in May 2018, by the Bishop of Reykjavík, Iceland for use by all faithful.
</p>
<h2><span id="Thorlac.27s_mass"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Thorlac's_mass">Thorlac's mass</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Thorlak&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Thorlac&#039;s mass">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <b>Mass of St. Thorlac</b> (<b>Þorláksmessa</b>; <small>Icelandic pronunciation:&#160;&#8203;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="is-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Icelandic" title="Help:IPA/Icelandic">[ˈθɔrˌlauksˌmɛssa]</a></span>) is an <a href="/wiki/Iceland" title="Iceland">Icelandic</a> holiday celebrated every December 23. The celebration honors Saint Thorlak. The day is also celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Faroe_Islands" title="Faroe Islands">Faroe Islands</a>, where it's called <i>Tollaksmessa</i> (<small>Faroese pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fo-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA" title="Help:IPA">[ˈtʰɔtlaksˌmɛsːa]</a></span>).
</p><p>In modern times, Þorláksmessa became part of <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a>, or the last day of preparations before <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Many people buy Christmas presents and finish decorating their houses and Christmas tree. On Þorláksmessa evening in <a href="/wiki/Reykjav%C3%ADk" title="Reykjavík">Reykjavík</a>, many stroll down <a href="/wiki/Laugavegur_(Reykjav%C3%ADk)" title="Laugavegur (Reykjavík)">Laugavegur</a>.
</p><p>Fish was usually eaten on Þorláksmessa since 23 December was the last day of the Catholic Christmas fast. In west fjords in Iceland, it is customary to eat buried and fermented <a href="/wiki/Skate_(fish)" title="Skate (fish)">skate</a> along with potatoes on Þorláksmessa. The <a href="/wiki/Ammonia" title="Ammonia">ammonia</a>-infused odor of fermented skate is quite strong, similar to that of <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A1karl" title="Hákarl">hákarl</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This pungent dish is eaten as a continuation of tradition. The skate is usually served with boiled or <a href="/wiki/Mashed_potatoes" class="mw-redirect" title="Mashed potatoes">mashed potatoes</a>, accompanied by a shot of <a href="/wiki/Brenniv%C3%ADn" title="Brennivín">brennivín</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other">Other</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Thorlak&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Other">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A group based in the <a href="/wiki/New_York_(state)" title="New York (state)">state of New York</a> has advocated for Thorlak becoming the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of people with <a href="/wiki/Autism" class="mw-redirect" title="Autism">autism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Autism Consecrated, a blog written by the autistic <a href="/wiki/Self-advocacy" title="Self-advocacy">self-advocate</a> Aimee O'Connell, promotes Saint Thorlak as a role model for autistic Catholics.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
