<p><b>Saint Fulrad</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Fulrade</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Fulradus</i>) was born in 710 into a wealthy family, and died on July 16, 784 as the <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Saint-Denis" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Saint-Denis">Abbot of Saint-Denis</a><sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was the counselor of both <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pippin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Historians see Fulrad as important due to his significance in the rise of the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Kingdom">Frankish Kingdom</a>, and the insight he gives into early <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> society.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He was noted to have been always on the side on <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, especially during the attack from the <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a> on Regnum Francorum (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> for <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankia</a>), and the Royal Mandatum (a royal official of the Carolingian administrative <a href="/wiki/Hierarchy" title="Hierarchy">hierarchy</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Other historians have taken a closer look at Fulrad's interactions with the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a>. When Fulrad was the counselor of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pepin</a> he was closely in contact with the papacy to gain approval for Pepin's appointment as King of the Franks.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  During his time under Charlemagne, he had dealings with the papacy again for different reasons.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  When he became Abbot of Saint-Denis in the mid-eighth century, Fulrad's life became important in the lives of distinct historical figures in various ways.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Saint Fulrad's <a href="/wiki/Feast_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast Day">Feast Day</a> is on July 16.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 710 Fulrad was born in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>, <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  He joined the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Denis" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Denis">abbey of Saint-Denis</a>, and in 750 he was elected its abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  With his new position, Fulrad increased the size of the abbey with his inheritance from his parents; as well he retook the land stolen from the abbey by <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad took full advantage of his position to increase his “reach as a leader”.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad founded new monasteries and operated them himself in the beginning; the monasteries were located in <a href="/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Alsace-Lorraine">Alsace-Lorraine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alemannia" class="mw-redirect" title="Alemannia">Alemannia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Each monastery had <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, which made them <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> destinations; Fulrad profited from them with the taking some of the money that travelers placed on the collection near the saint's relic;<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  however “as abbot of Saint-Denis, Fulrad’s personal property also belonged to the monastery he led”.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  With the new monasteries and them being popular pilgrimage destinations, Fulrad asked Pope Stephan II to have a “cloister” bishop in order to provide the pastoral services he was now unable to do at his other monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  As abbot, his main priority was to take care and be at the head of Saint-Denis abbey; that is why he was granted the bishop to take care of his other monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  With the position Fulrad had, he advised Frankish bishops; and would go on to have a higher role in the church<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  as a “quasi-bishop”.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick3_8-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad never became bishop, however he did climb up the ladder of the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> hierarchy, becoming a “capellanus”;<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  he was first <a href="/wiki/Pippin_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin III">Pippin III</a> and then Charlemagne's “arch-Chaplain”;<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick2_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  he would later become their counselor.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad had a close relation with the royal family, since he held office under both Pepin III and then <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  He also had great significance in shaping <a href="/wiki/Western_European" class="mw-redirect" title="Western European">Western European</a> history.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  For instance, he motivated the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> in siding with the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">Papacy</a>, rather than the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantines</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  this was a strategic diplomatic initiative that would end with Charlemagne becoming the Emperor from 800-14; but Fulrad would not see the result of his actions, as it occurred eleven years after his death in 784.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Fulrad.27s_Testament.5B3.5D"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Fulrad's_Testament[3]">Fulrad's Testament<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Fulrad&#039;s Testament[3]">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fulrad's Testament (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: Fulrad's <a href="/wiki/Testimonium" class="mw-redirect" title="Testimonium">Testimonium</a>), gives an account of early <a href="/wiki/Carolinian_people" title="Carolinian people">Carolinian</a> society.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  In the “testament”, Fulrad presents a survey of certain places and gave a detailed account of the <a href="/wiki/Religious" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious">religious</a>, <a href="/wiki/Political" class="mw-redirect" title="Political">political</a> and <a href="/wiki/Economic" class="mw-redirect" title="Economic">economic</a> differences between the towns.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Some Historians have seen the importance of Fulrad's account of early Carolingian history; the places he placed under the royal abbey were used as a defense against Eastern Nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  However, the eastern lands were economically important, and the abbey of Saint-Denis could benefit from expanding into that region.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad's testament is also seen as an important look into the lives of Carolingian characters as he identifies certain people and traces their lineage.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  This <a href="/wiki/Lineage_(anthropology)" title="Lineage (anthropology)">lineage</a> part of the testament was and is considered so important that Alain Stoclet's who wrote “Autour de Fulrad de Saint-Denis (v. 710-784)”,<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  devoted a whole chapter on the “genealogica et prosopographica”<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  of Abbot Fulrad.<sup id="cite_ref-deleeuw_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deleeuw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Fulrad_and_Pippin_III">Fulrad and Pippin III</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Fulrad and Pippin III">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abbot Fulrad was the counselor of <a href="/wiki/Pippin_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin III">Pippin III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick2_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  The connection between these two figures goes beyond just friendship, but as two strong figures that helped each other and had great respect for each other's service.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick6_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick6-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  Historians have written about this friendship, and examples of it are seen in distinctively in certain dealings with the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> and with Pippin's burial.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick6_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick6-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Dealings_with_the_papacy">Dealings with the papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Dealings with the papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The reason for this meeting with the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> was to make clear and make sure that <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pippin</a>, and not <a href="/wiki/Childeric_III" title="Childeric III">Childeric</a> should be king of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  Even before this, in 750, Fulrad was a servant of Pippin, being his consular and “Arch-Chaplain”.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick2_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  In that, Pippin III already had respect and trust in Fulrad; thus giving him the “highly important mission”<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  of going to “win over…[pope] [Zachary]”<sup id="cite_ref-boussard8_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard8-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  on the matter of “changing government”.<sup id="cite_ref-boussard8_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard8-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  The <a href="/wiki/Codex_Carolinus" title="Codex Carolinus">Codex Carolinus</a>, contains a summary of the response of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zachary" title="Pope Zachary">Pope Zachary</a> to Fulrad, which read, “it was better to call the person who has the royal power king rather than he who does not have it”.<sup id="cite_ref-boussard8_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard8-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  Pippin in 751 forced Childric to a monastery and made himself king.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Pippin.E2.80.99s_Charter_of_755"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Pippin’s_Charter_of_755">Pippin’s Charter of 755</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Pippin’s Charter of 755">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to a papal biographer, with Pepin as King, Pippin III “ordered a charter to be drawn up”.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen4_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen4-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  This charter gave all the land of: the Lombard king, donations of St. Peter to all future popes; which included such towns as <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gubbio" title="Gubbio">Gubbio</a> and Northern <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen4_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen4-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  Each town welcomed Fulrad, and after this, Fulrad went himself to place the “keys of the ceded cities on the tomb of [St. Peter]…with the official charter…drawn up in Pippin’s name”.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen4_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen4-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  The charter is somewhat unclear—according to church writings of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Pope Stephen II</a>—however, the papacy did profit for the supposed vague charter that gave Fulrad these regions: <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cesena" title="Cesena">Cesena</a>, <a href="/wiki/Comacchio" title="Comacchio">Comacchio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fano" title="Fano">Fano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro">Pesaro</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Forlimpopoli" title="Forlimpopoli">Forlimpopoli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Urbino" title="Urbino">Urbino</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Montefeltro" title="Montefeltro">Montefeltro</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen4_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen4-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  By the time this all occurred Pippin had left <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, feeling confident and with a clear conscience since the town were under Fulrad's care, so the papacy would be able to “take care of itself”.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Burial_of_Pippin_III">Burial of Pippin III</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Burial of Pippin III">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Abbot Fulrad had such an impact on <a href="/wiki/Pepin_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin III">Pepin III</a>, that the latter's final wishes and resting place were all connected to Fulrad and his abbey of Saint-Denis.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick1_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick1-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  Pippin became very ill at his campaign of <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a> in the city of <a href="/wiki/Saintes,_Charente-Maritime" title="Saintes, Charente-Maritime">Saintes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick6_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick6-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  He traveled to Saint-Denis where he would be buried.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick6_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick6-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  Pippin, at his own request asked to be buried at Saint-Denis next to the saint; also he wanted to be buried at the abbey where Fulrad, his Arch-Chaplin and “one of his staunchest political [allies]”<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick1_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick1-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  was the Abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick1_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick1-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Fulrad_and_Charlemagne">Fulrad and Charlemagne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Fulrad and Charlemagne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fulrad was also the Arch-Chaplin and Counselor to <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>  He was at the side or of great help for Charlemagne with Regnum Francorum (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> for <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankia</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  war with the <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a>, dealing with the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> with Charlemagne's promise of a “Donation” [and] royal Mandatum”,<sup id="cite_ref-boussard_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  and with the exchange with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hadrian" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Hadrian">Pope Hadrian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id=".E2.80.9CRagnum_Francorum.E2.80.9D.5B23.5D"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="“Ragnum_Francorum”[23]">“Ragnum Francorum”<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: “Ragnum Francorum”[23]">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> awarded the “estates in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Almania&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Almania (page does not exist)">Almania</a>”<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  to the abbey of Saint-Denis; Fulrad already had expanded the interest of Saint-Denis in these areas.<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  Charlemagne gave Fulrad's abbey many riches and more land—the “valuable royal villa of <a href="/wiki/Herbrechtingen" title="Herbrechtingen">Herbrechtingen</a>”;<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  and built up the area of the abbey in order to support armies on campaign.<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  This was Charlemagne's way of not having to rely solely on the loyalty of local officials, as Charlemagne could rely above all on Fulrad, his personal arch-chaplain and counselor.<sup id="cite_ref-boussard7_23-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard7-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fulrad_and_the_papacy">Fulrad and the papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Fulrad and the papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id="Missus.2C_.E2.80.9C.E2.80.98Donation.E2.80.99_.5Band.5D_.E2.80.98Royal_Mandatum.E2.80.99.E2.80.9D.5B21.5D"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Missus,_“‘Donation’_[and]_‘Royal_Mandatum’”[21]">Missus, “‘Donation’ [and] ‘Royal Mandatum’”<sup id="cite_ref-boussard_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Missus, “‘Donation’ [and] ‘Royal Mandatum’”[21]">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In the “Donation”,<sup id="cite_ref-boussard_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-boussard-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  the pope declared that there would be a <a href="/wiki/Missus_dominicus" title="Missus dominicus">Missus</a>, some one who would deal with the problems that the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> had identified in his letters in the Donation.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  In the letter, the pope stated that Charlemagne promised to dispatch someone with a “Royal Mandatum”<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  to act with the power of the king in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  As Fulrad was the Carolingian most senior expert on Italian matters, as one-historian notes, the abbot of Saint-Denis became the Missus, however Fulrad was still the Abbot of Saint-Denis at this time.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Fulrad_and_the_exchange_between_Charlemagne_and_Pope_Hadrian">Fulrad and the exchange between Charlemagne and Pope Hadrian</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Fulrad and the exchange between Charlemagne and Pope Hadrian">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Fulrad was at the side of Charlemagne from the beginning to the end of this exchange as his adviser.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach5_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach5-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  From autumn 774 through the summer of 775, Fulrad stayed and supported Charlemagne at court until Charlemagne left to invade <a href="/wiki/Saxony" title="Saxony">Saxony</a> in the summer of 775.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach5_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach5-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  During his time at the courts, Charlemagne authorized many charters in favor of Fulrad's abbey of Saint-Denis.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach5_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach5-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  The charters were granted because Fulrad successfully guiding Carolingian policy in Italian affairs;<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach5_24-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach5-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  however it is to be “understood as [an] illustrative of royal policies involving the monastery of Saint-Denis as a major cog in Charlemagne’s kingdom-side administration”.<sup id="cite_ref-bachrach5_24-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bachrach5-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Francia">Francia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Francia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fulrad is viewed as the figure that truly helped develop the growing kingdom of Francia.<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  One historian notes that Fulrad was one of the few “Neustrian figures”,<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  to come and support the cause and be in the service of the new rising dynasty.<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  The monastery of Saint-Denis, which Fulrad was the abbot, became a burial place for <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monarchy" title="Monarchy">monarchy</a>; Abbot Fulrad's Monastery of Saint-Denis became the “royal mausoleum” of the Frankish kings.<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  Abbot Fulrad and the First Carolingian kings destroyed “the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> church of Saint-Denis.<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  Fulrad and the Carolingian kings would build a basilica, helping to aerate the “first actual Carolingian church”.<sup id="cite_ref-lifshitz_25-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lifshitz-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="New_Basilica_of_Saint-Denis">New Basilica of Saint-Denis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: New Basilica of Saint-Denis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abbot Fulrad oversaw the new construction of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint-Denis" title="Basilica of Saint-Denis">basilica of Saint-Denis</a>. Construction began in 754 and was completed under Charlemagne, who was present at its consecration in 775. Much of what is now known about the Carolingian church at St Denis resulted from a lengthy series of excavations begun under the American art historian <a href="/wiki/Sumner_McKnight_Crosby" title="Sumner McKnight Crosby">Sumner McKnight Crosby</a> in 1937.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> The structure was about eighty meters long, with an imposing <a href="/wiki/Westwork" title="Westwork">facade</a>, a nave divided into three sections by two rows of marble columns, a transept, and <a href="/wiki/Apse" title="Apse">apse</a> at the east end. Fulrad's church had smaller elements such as <a href="/wiki/Arch" title="Arch">arches</a> and <a href="/wiki/Transepts" class="mw-redirect" title="Transepts">transepts</a>, it did however have classical elements had its design, strong <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> influence and metal work throughout the Basilica. During important religious celebrations, the interior of the church was lit with 1250 lamps.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>This same basilica is where many of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> monarchs were buried in the crypts.<sup id="cite_ref-mckitterick1_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mckitterick1-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  In 1137, <a href="/wiki/Abbot_Suger" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot Suger">Abbot Suger</a> (1081-1155) commissioned to have the old Church of Fulrad rebuilt.<sup id="cite_ref-halsall_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halsall-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>  He believed the old design from 775 “ would have been viewed with extreme suspicion by a…building inspector”.<sup id="cite_ref-halsall_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halsall-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>  The new church was completed in 1144 demonstrating a major architectural achievement;<sup id="cite_ref-halsall_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halsall-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>  “<a href="/wiki/Gothic_architecture" title="Gothic architecture">Gothic</a> was born”.<sup id="cite_ref-halsall_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halsall-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_successors">Death and successors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fulrad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Death and successors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abbot Fulrad died on July 16, 784.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>  When Fulrad died, <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> chose Angilram, the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hildebald" class="mw-redirect" title="Hildebald">Hildebald</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> to be the new abbots of Saint-Denis to carry on the work of abbot Fulrad.<sup id="cite_ref-halphen_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-halphen-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
