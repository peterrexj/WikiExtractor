<p><b>Lawrence Humphrey</b> (or <b>Laurence Humfrey</b>) <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity" title="Doctor of Divinity">DD</a> (1525/7? – 1 February 1589)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an English theologian, who was <a href="/wiki/President_(college)" class="mw-redirect" title="President (college)">President</a> of <a href="/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford" title="Magdalen College, Oxford">Magdalen College, Oxford</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Dean_(Christianity)" title="Dean (Christianity)">Dean</a> successively of <a href="/wiki/Gloucester_Cathedral" title="Gloucester Cathedral">Gloucester</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winchester_Cathedral" title="Winchester Cathedral">Winchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dbn_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dbn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_Humphrey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Humphrey was born at <a href="/wiki/Newport_Pagnell" title="Newport Pagnell">Newport Pagnell</a>, Buckinghamshire, England. He was first educated at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cambridge" title="University of Cambridge">University of Cambridge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-biblestudytools_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biblestudytools-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was elected to a <a href="/wiki/Demyship" title="Demyship">demy</a> at Magdalen College in 1546 and <a href="/wiki/Fellow_(college)" class="mw-redirect" title="Fellow (college)">Fellow</a> in 1548. He graduated <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts" title="Bachelor of Arts">BA</a> in. 1549, <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts" title="Master of Arts">MA</a> in 1552, and <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Divinity" title="Bachelor of Divinity">BD</a> and <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity" title="Doctor of Divinity">DD</a> in 1562. He was noted as one of the most promising pupils of <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Martire_Vermigli" class="mw-redirect" title="Pietro Martire Vermigli">Pietro Martire Vermigli</a>, and on <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Mary</a>'s accession obtained leave from his college to travel abroad. He lived at <a href="/wiki/Basel" title="Basel">Basel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich" title="Zürich">Zürich</a>, Frankfurt and <a href="/wiki/Geneva" title="Geneva">Geneva</a>, making the acquaintance of the leading Swiss divines, whose ecclesiastical views he adopted. His leave of absence having expired in 1556, he ceased to be fellow of Magdalen.
</p><p>Humphrey returned to England at <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a>'s accession, was appointed regius professor of divinity at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">Oxford</a> in 1560, and was recommended by <a href="/wiki/Matthew_Parker" title="Matthew Parker">Archbishop Parker</a> and others for election as President of Magdalen College. The fellows refused at first to elect so pronounced a reformer, but they yielded in 1561, and Humphrey gradually converted the college into a stronghold of <a href="/wiki/Puritan" class="mw-redirect" title="Puritan">Puritanism</a>.
</p><p>In 1564, Humphrey and his friend <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Sampson" title="Thomas Sampson">Thomas Sampson</a>, Dean of <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>, were called before Parker for refusing to wear the prescribed ecclesiastical vestments; and a prolonged struggle, the <a href="/wiki/Vestments_controversy" title="Vestments controversy">vestments controversy</a>, broke out, in which <a href="/wiki/Heinrich_Bullinger" title="Heinrich Bullinger">Bullinger</a> and other foreign theologians took part as well as most of the leading divines in England. In spite of Bullinger's advice, Humphrey refused to conform; and Parker wished to deprive him as well as Sampson. But the presidency of Magdalen was elective and the visitor of the college was not Parker but the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>; and Humphrey escaped with temporary retirement. Parker, in fact, was not supported by the council; in 1566 Humphrey was selected to preach at <a href="/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cross" title="St Paul&#39;s Cross">St Paul's Cross</a>, and was allowed to do so without the vestments.
</p><p>In the same year, Humphrey took a prominent part in the ceremonies connected with Elizabeth's visit to Oxford. On this occasion he wore his doctor's gown and habit, which the queen told him became him very well; and his resistance now began to weaken. He yielded on the point before 1571 when he was made <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_Gloucester" title="Dean of Gloucester">dean of Gloucester</a>. In 1578 he was one of the divines selected to attend a diet at Schmalkalde to discuss the project of a theological accommodation between the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran">Lutheran</a> and <a href="/wiki/Reformed_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Reformed church">Reformed churches</a>; and in 1580 he was made <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_Winchester" title="Dean of Winchester">Dean of Winchester</a>. In 1585 he was persuaded by his bishop, Cooper, to restore the use of <a href="/wiki/Surplice" title="Surplice">surplices</a> in Magdalen College chapel. He died on 1 February 1590 and was buried in the college chapel, where there is a mural monument to his memory; a portrait is in Magdalen College school.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_Humphrey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Humphrey was a prolific writer on theological and other subjects. At Parker's request, he wrote a life of his friend and patron <a href="/wiki/John_Jewel" title="John Jewel">Bishop Jewel</a>, which was published in 1573 and was also prefixed to the edition of Jewel's works issued in 1600. One of his books against the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> was included in vol. iii. of the <i>Doctrina Jesuitarum per van os authores</i>, published at <a href="/wiki/La_Rochelle" title="La Rochelle">La Rochelle</a> (6 volumes, 1585–1586).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_Humphrey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>About the beginning of the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a>, Lawrence married Joan Inkfordby, daughter of Andrew Inkfordby of <a href="/wiki/Ipswich" title="Ipswich">Ipswich</a>. By her he had seven sons and five daughters.  Joan died 27 August 1611 "aged 74" and was buried at the church of <a href="/wiki/Steeple_Barton" title="Steeple Barton">Steeple Barton</a> in Oxfordshire.  Her eldest daughter Justina Dormer, wife of Caspar Dormer, esquire, erected a monument to her memory there.  Her third daughter Judith was the third wife of Sir <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Carey" title="Edmund Carey">Edmund Carey</a> (died 1637), brother of the <a href="/wiki/Robert_Carey,_1st_Earl_of_Monmouth" title="Robert Carey, 1st Earl of Monmouth">Earl of Monmouth</a>.
</p>
