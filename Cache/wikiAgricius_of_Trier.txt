<p><b>Saint Agricius</b>, also <b>Agritius</b> (c. 260 – c. 335) was the first historically documented <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trier">bishop of Trier</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agricius_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From the time of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>'s reorganization of the divisions of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">empire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Augusta_Treverorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Augusta Treverorum">Augusta Treverorum</a>, now Trier, was the capital of <a href="/wiki/Belgica_Prima" class="mw-redirect" title="Belgica Prima">Belgica Prima</a>, the chief city of Gaul, and frequently the residence of the emperors. There were Christians among its population as early as the second century, and probably as early as the third century there was a bishop at Trier, which is the oldest episcopal see in Germany. The first clearly authenticated bishop is Agricius, who took part in the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Arles_(314)" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Arles (314)">Council of Arles in 314</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agricius_of_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little else is known about Agricius. There are stories about him, but these are based in part on Altmann's dubious <i>Vita Helenae</i> of about 850.<sup id="cite_ref-Jan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agricius was born about the year 260,<sup id="cite_ref-Oslo_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in Syria.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>An 11th-century tradition states that he had been a priest of <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, and that he was moved to the See of Trier by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_I" title="Pope Sylvester I">Pope Sylvester I</a> at the request of the <a href="/wiki/Helena_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Helena of Constantinople">Empress Helena</a>. He was present at the Council of Arles in 314, where he signed the acts immediately after the presiding bishop of that diocese. This indicated that, at least in the fourth century, Trier laid claim to the primacy of <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> and <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, a claim his successor <a href="/wiki/Maximin_of_Trier" title="Maximin of Trier">Saint Maximin</a> reinforced.<sup id="cite_ref-Grey_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grey-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This story seems to have developed in order to promote the primacy of Trier over other sees in Gaul and Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-Jan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> "Saint Agritius laboured zealously and successfully during twenty years at the conversion of Gaul and of Western Germany."<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Medieval tradition dates to Agricius' time the construction of the first <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Trier">cathedral in Trier</a>, which was said to have been built over the Empress Helena's palace which she gave for that purpose.<sup id="cite_ref-Oslo_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Heiliger Rock</i> (the "Holy Gown") is said to be the robe (the "seamless garment") which Jesus wore before His crucifixion and for which, according to the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_St._John" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospel of St. John">Gospel of St. John</a>, the Roman soldiers cast lots. Part of this robe is said to have been found by Helena, mother of Constantine the Great, who gave it to Agricius.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Athanasius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Athanasius">Saint Athanasius</a>, who came as an exile to Trier in 335 or 336, speaks of the large numbers of faithful whom he found there and the number of churches in the course of being built. The famous relics of Trier - the above-named "Heiliger Rock", the Nail of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>, and the body of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Matthias" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Matthias">Matthias</a> the Apostle - are all said to have been brought there by Agricius.<sup id="cite_ref-Grey_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grey-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
