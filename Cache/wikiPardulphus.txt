<p><b>Saint Pardulphus</b> (<i>Pardulf, Pardoux</i>) (657 – <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 737 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> saint and <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>.  The <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Pardulfi" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Pardulfi">Vita Pardulfi</a></i>, was written by an anonymous monk around the middle of the eighth century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is notable for the insight it provides into life in <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a> at the time.
</p><p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Sardent" title="Sardent">Sardent</a>, from a family of <a href="/wiki/Peasants" class="mw-redirect" title="Peasants">peasants</a>.  His legend states that he was a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd" title="Shepherd">shepherd</a> who decided to live as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> after experiencing a terrible storm.  Lantarius, the count of <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a>, had built a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Gu%C3%A9ret" title="Guéret">Guéret</a>.  Pardulphus joined this monastery, later serving as its abbot.  He followed strict <a href="/wiki/Penances" class="mw-redirect" title="Penances">penances</a>, never keeping himself warm, and only eating once a week.  He is alleged to have rejected heat from any source but the rays of the <a href="/wiki/Sun" title="Sun">sun</a>.  However, as he grew old he did occasionally make use of “hot stones” to keep himself warm.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He rejected the consumption of all <a href="/wiki/Poultry" title="Poultry">poultry</a>, eating only the <a href="/wiki/Mushrooms" class="mw-redirect" title="Mushrooms">mushrooms</a> the local peasants brought him.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The <i>Vita Pardulfi</i> records a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> performed by Pardulphus.  Some <a href="/wiki/Carpenter" class="mw-redirect" title="Carpenter">carpenters</a> were cutting wood with which to build the church of Saint-Aubin at Guéret.  After they loaded the wood onto carts and returned to the building works, it was determined that the wood was too short.  The carpenters’ superintendent wished to have the carpenters whipped in punishment for this, but Pardulphus intervened with a miracle that made the wood the right size and even surpassed the intended length.  As a result, the excess wood was sawn off and hung in the church as an object of veneration.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg/200px-Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="133" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg/300px-Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg/400px-Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3504" data-file-height="2336" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-pardoux-haute-vienne-eglise.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Church of Saint-Pardoux, <a href="/wiki/Haute-Vienne" title="Haute-Vienne">Haute-Vienne</a>, is dedicated to Pardulphus.</div></div></div>
<p>According to tradition, during the <a href="/wiki/Umayyad" class="mw-redirect" title="Umayyad">Umayyad</a> invasion of southern France, Pardulphus remained in his monastery.  Umayyad forces, retreating after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tours" title="Battle of Tours">Battle of Tours</a>, arrived at the monastery.  However, his monastery was spared from attack; this was attributed to Pardulphus’ prayers.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pardulphus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is October 6.  A <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> that contained his arm had been preserved at the church of Sardent.  However, it is now found at the Museum of Fine Art at Guéret.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  A number of places in France, such as <a href="/wiki/Saint-Pardoux-de-Dr%C3%B4ne" title="Saint-Pardoux-de-Drône">Saint-Pardoux-de-Drône</a>, take their name from him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Vita_Pardulfi">Vita Pardulfi</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pardulphus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Vita Pardulfi">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><b>Vita Pardulfi Abbatis Waractensis</b></i> (<a href="/wiki/Medieval_Latin" title="Medieval Latin">Medieval Latin</a> for "Life of (Saint) Pardulf of Guéret") is a work on the life of Pardulphus, which was written around the middle of the eighth century. The <i>Vita Pardulfi</i> is notable for the insight it provides into life in <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a> during the time of its writing. The work also indicates the high esteem in which Duke <a href="/wiki/Odo_of_Aquitaine" class="mw-redirect" title="Odo of Aquitaine">Odo of Aquitaine</a> was held, in contrast to the negative picture provided by the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Fredegar" title="Chronicle of Fredegar">Continuations of Fredegar</a></i>, which were compiled on the instructions of Duke <a href="/wiki/Childebrand" class="mw-redirect" title="Childebrand">Childebrand</a>, half-brother of Odo's rival <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>.
</p>
