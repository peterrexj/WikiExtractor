<p><b>Solange</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 10 May, c. 880<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Shepherdess" class="mw-redirect" title="Shepherdess">shepherdess</a> and a locally <a href="/wiki/Venerated" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerated">venerated</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">cephalophore</a>, whose cult is restricted to <a href="/wiki/Sainte-Solange" title="Sainte-Solange">Sainte-Solange</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cher_(d%C3%A9partement)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cher (département)">Cher</a>. Saint Solange was the patron of the traditional <a href="/wiki/Berry_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Berry (province)">Province of Berry</a>, of which Cher is a part.
</p><p>Solange was born to a poor, but devout family in the town of Villemont, near <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a>, and consecrated her virginity at the age of seven; according to some, her mere presence cured the sick and <a href="/wiki/Exorcism" title="Exorcism">exorcised</a> devils. The son of the count of <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> was highly taken with the beauty and popularity of Solange and approached her when she was tending to her sheep, but she rejected his suit. He argued with her to no avail, and so he decided to abduct her.
</p><p>At night, he came and took Solange by force, but she struggled so violently that she fell from his horse while he was crossing a stream. Her abductor grew enraged and beheaded her with his sword. According to the fully-developed legend, Solange's severed head invoked three times the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Name" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Name">Holy Name</a> of Jesus, and like <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Saint Denis</a> and other saints in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaulish</a> territories, Solange picked up her head in her own hands and walked with it as far as the church of Saint-Martin in the village of Saint-Martin-du-Crot (which now bears the name of Sainte-Solange, the only commune in France to bear this name), only dropping truly dead there.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solange&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Immediately, a cult surrounding her grew up. Many miraculous cures were attributed to her intercession. In 1281, an altar was erected in her honor at that church, and it preserved her severed head as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> and began to call itself the church of St. Solange, while a nearby field where she had prayed began to be referred to as the "Field of St. Solange". It was a habit of the locals, in times of great stress, to form a procession through Bourges with the reliquary head before them and to invoke her against drought.
</p><p>Solange's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> is May 10.
</p>
