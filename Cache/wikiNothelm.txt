<p><b>Nothhelm</b> (sometimes <b>Nothelm</b>;<sup id="cite_ref-Coming69_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming69-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> died 739) was a medieval Anglo-Saxon <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. A correspondent of both <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a>, it was Nothhelm who gathered materials from <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> for Bede's historical works. After his appointment to the archbishopric in 735, he attended to ecclesiastical matters, including holding church councils. Although later <a href="/wiki/Antiquaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiquaries">antiquaries</a> felt that Nothhelm was the author of a number of works, later research has shown them to be authored by others. After his death he was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">considered a saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nothhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nothhelm was a contemporary of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, whom he supplied with correspondence from the papal library following a trip to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley93_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley93-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He also researched the history of <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a> and the surrounding area for Bede, supplying the information through the abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a> in Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Before his appointment to the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishopric</a>, he was the <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Saxon</a>-built <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Paul%27s_Cathedral" title="Old St Paul&#39;s Cathedral">St Paul's Cathedral</a>, London.<sup id="cite_ref-Yorke31_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Yorke31-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop">Archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nothhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Named to the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a> in 735, Nothhelm was consecrated the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_III" title="Pope Gregory III">Gregory III</a> sent him a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> in 736.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He may have been appointed by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbald_of_Mercia" title="Æthelbald of Mercia">Æthelbald</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Mercia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Mercia">King of Mercia</a>, whose councilor he was.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley93_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley93-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Whether or not he owed his appointment to Æthelbald, Nothhelm was one of a number of Mercians who became Archbishop of Canterbury during the 730s and 740s, during a time of expanding Mercian influence.<sup id="cite_ref-Kingship24_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kingship24-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He held a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> in 736 or 737, which drew nine bishops;<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> the meeting adjudicated a dispute over the ownership of a monastery located at <a href="/wiki/Withington,_Gloucestershire" title="Withington, Gloucestershire">Withington</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils18_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils18-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> A significant feature of this synod was the fact that no king attended, but yet the synod still rendered judgement in the ownership even without secular oversight, which was more usual.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils56_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils56-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nothhelm oversaw the reorganisation of the <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercian</a> dioceses which took place in 737. The archbishop consecrated <a href="/wiki/Witta_(bishop_of_Lichfield)" title="Witta (bishop of Lichfield)">Witta</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lichfield" title="Bishop of Lichfield">Bishop of Lichfield</a> and <a href="/wiki/Torhthelm" title="Torhthelm">Totta</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Leicester" title="Bishop of Leicester">Bishop of Leicester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Leicester" title="Diocese of Leicester">diocese of Leicester</a> was firmly established by this action,<sup id="cite_ref-Introduction169_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Introduction169-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> although earlier attempts had been made to establish a bishopric there.<sup id="cite_ref-Intro136_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Intro136-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 738, Nothhelm was a witness on the <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charter</a> of <a href="/wiki/Eadbert_I_of_Kent" title="Eadbert I of Kent">Eadberht I</a>, the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Kent" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Kent">King of Kent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bede addressed his work <i>In regum librum XXX quaestiones</i> to Nothhelm, who had asked the thirty questions on the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">biblical</a> book of <i><a href="/wiki/Books_of_Kings" title="Books of Kings">Kings</a></i> that Bede answered.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Bede's work <i>De VIII Quaestionibus</i> may have been written for Nothhelm.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> While he was archbishop, Boniface wrote to him, requesting a copy of the <i><a href="/wiki/Libellus_responsionum" title="Libellus responsionum">Libellus responsionum</a></i> of Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Gregory I</a> for use in Boniface's missionary efforts.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks83_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks83-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Boniface also asked for information on when the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a> to England arrived in England.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This text of the <i>Libellus responsionum</i> has been the subject of some controversy, with the historian Suso Brechter arguing that the text was a forgery created by Nothhelm and a Roman archdeacon. The historian Paul Meyvaert has refuted this view, and most historians incline towards the belief that the text is genuine, although it is not considered conclusively proven.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nothhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nothhelm died on 17 October 739<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He is considered a saint, and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 17 October.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Antiquary" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiquary">antiquaries</a> and writers <a href="/wiki/John_Leland_(antiquary)" title="John Leland (antiquary)">John Leland</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Bale" title="John Bale">John Bale</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Tanner_(bishop)" title="Thomas Tanner (bishop)">Thomas Tanner</a> all felt that Nothhelm was the author of various works, but later research has shown them to be authored by other writers. A verse eulogy for Nothhelm, of uncertain date, survives in a 16th-century manuscript now at the <a href="/wiki/Lambeth_Palace" title="Lambeth Palace">Lambeth Palace</a> library.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_8-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
