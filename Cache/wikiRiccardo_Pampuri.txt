<p><b>Riccardo Pampuri</b>, <a href="/wiki/Brothers_Hospitallers_of_Saint_John_of_God" title="Brothers Hospitallers of Saint John of God">OH</a> (2 August 1897 – 1 May 1930) - born <b>Erminio Filippo Pampuri</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> medical doctor and a veteran of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> who was also a professed member from <a href="/wiki/Brothers_Hospitallers_of_St._John_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Brothers Hospitallers of St. John of God">Hospitallers of Saint John of God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Pampuri worked as a field doctor on the battlefield during the Great War and was discharged in 1920 when he was able to resume his studies and soon begin his own practice as a doctor where he tended to the poor without charge. He became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> as "Antonio" while founding the Band of Pius X which he dedicated to the medical care of poor people. But Pampuri later became a professed religious for the call was too great for him to ignore; he managed a free dental clinic in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> for his order.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SR_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pampuri's canonization cause opened in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in 1970 and he became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> while <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> titled him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 12 June 1978 after confirming that he had lived a model life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified Pampuri on 4 October 1981 and later canonized him at the end of that decade on 1 November 1989.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Pampuri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_education">Childhood and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Pampuri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Erminio Filippo Pampuri was born on 2 August 1897 in <a href="/wiki/Trivolzio" title="Trivolzio">Trivolzio</a> as the tenth of the eleven children to Innocenzo Pampuri and Angela Campari (1856-1900) and he was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 3 August.<sup id="cite_ref-SR_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother died in 1900 and he was then taken into the home of Giovanni and Maria Campari who were his maternal uncle and aunt at Torrino which was a village close to his.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later in 1907 his father died in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in a traffic accident and he remained in the care of his maternal aunt and maternal grandparents. His sister Maria became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> (with the new name of "Longina") and she died in <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a> in 1977.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his childhood he wanted nothing more than to become a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> in the missions but was dissuaded from this on account of his delicate health. But his doctor uncle Carlo inspired him to learn medicine and he soon became resolved to become a doctor. He attended two schools as a child at villages near his and then went to Milan where he attended a junior high school.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He completed his high school studies as a boarder at Augustine's College in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> where - after graduation - he enrolled in the medical department of the <a href="/wiki/Pavia_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Pavia University">Pavia college</a>. Whilst he studied there he was an active member of the college's Severino Boezio Club for <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Action" title="Catholic Action">Catholic Action</a>. He also belonged to the <a href="/wiki/St._Vincent_de_Paul_Society" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Vincent de Paul Society">Società San Vincenzo de' Paoli</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> which he joined as a secular on 20 March 1921 in the name of "Antonio" at the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of Canepanova in Pavia.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SR_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Between 1 April 1917 and 1920 he was in the fighting zone in <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> where he worked in the field hospitals. He first served as a <a href="/wiki/Sergeant" title="Sergeant">sergeant</a> and later went into training as an officer in the medical corps. He resumed his studies in 1920 after being discharged that April. On 6 July 1921 he graduated at the top of his class in surgical and medical studies.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> From 1921 to 1924 he spent time in doing practical experience with his doctor uncle and after this for a short time served for a brief period as an assistant in the medical practice at <a href="/wiki/Vernate,_Lombardy" title="Vernate, Lombardy">Vernate</a> before being appointed to the practice of Morimondo in Milan. In 1922 he passed his internship with high honours at the Milano Instituto di Ostetricia e Ginecologia.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SR_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1923 he was registered at the college in Pavia as a <a href="/wiki/General_practitioner" title="General practitioner">general practitioner</a> in medicine. He later arrived in Morimondo to practice medicine where he gave valuable assistance to the parish priest and helped him to set up a musical band and a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Action" title="Catholic Action">Catholic Action</a> Youth Club of which he was the first president. He was also the secretary of the Parish Missionary Aid Society and he founded the Band of Pius X which he dedicated to the medical care of the poor whom he treated for free.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Pampuri would soon leave his practice to become a religious after seeing that his call to the religious life was far too great to ignore.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life">Religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Pampuri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pampuri joined the <a href="/wiki/Brothers_Hospitallers_of_St._John_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Brothers Hospitallers of St. John of God">Hospitallers of Saint John of God</a> in Milan on 22 June 1927 so as to follow evangelical holiness in a closer manner and at the same time to be able to continue his medical profession so as to alleviate the suffering of his fellow neighbours. He had hoped to become a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> but rethought it and decided against it.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> and at that time was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of the order and the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of "Riccardo". Once he had completed probation period of training he made his profession of <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> on 24 October 1928. He was then appointed as the director of the free dental clinic attached to the order's hospital at Brescia.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SR_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Illness_and_death">Illness and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Pampuri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Illness and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pampuri had a fresh outbreak of <a href="/wiki/Pleurisy" title="Pleurisy">pleurisy</a> which he had first contracted during his war service and this soon degenerated into specific <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">bronco-pneumonia</a> coupled with <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> which worsened in January 1930 to the point where he had to cut back on his work; these difficulties first were of a respiratory kind since 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 18 April 1930 he was taken from Brescia to Milan where he died on 1 May; he had met his nephew Alessandro on 30 April to bid him farewell. His remains are in his old parish church in his village of Trivolzio after being exhumed and relocated on 16 May 1951.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Pampuri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The canonization process opened in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">Milan archdiocese</a> under Cardinal Blessed <a href="/wiki/Ildefonso_Schuster" class="mw-redirect" title="Ildefonso Schuster">Ildefonso Schuster</a> on 1 April 1949 in an informative process that collected documentation until the process closed in 1951. His writings were all investigated and found to be in line with official doctrine thus received theological approval on 4 May 1954 while the formal introduction to the cause came on 10 July 1970 in which he was named as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Colombo" title="Giovanni Colombo">Giovanni Colombo</a> oversaw an apostolic process in 1971 after which stage all documentation collected was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> who validated both processes on 3 December 1971.
</p><p>The members of the C.C.S. and their official consultants voiced their approval to the cause on 29 November 1977 while the C.C.S. members alone met and approved the cause on 21 February 1978 after reviewing all the documentation. On 12 June 1978 he was proclaimed to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> confirmed that Pampuri had led a model life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>One miracle that science could not explain - a healing - had to be investigated and approved for Pampuri to be beatified. The approval of one such healing allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to preside over Pampuri's beatification on 4 October 1981. The second miracle required for full sainthood was investigated in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alcadozo" title="Alcadozo">Alcadozo</a> and concerned the 5 January 1982 cure of the child Manuel Cifuentes Rodenas (b. 1971) who would normally have been blinded in his right eye, due to a certain injury his eye received from an <a href="/wiki/Almond_tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Almond tree">almond tree</a> branch, but inexplicably his eye healed back to still provide vision, after his father appealed to the late Pampuri for a healing. This was investigated on a diocesan level and the C.C.S. validated the process on 6 February 1987 with a medical panel approving it on 19 October 1988. Theologians also approved this miracle on 17 January 1989 as did the C.C.S. on 11 February 1989. John Paul II canonized Pampuri as a saint of the Roman Catholic Church on 1 November 1989.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
