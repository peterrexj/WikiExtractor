					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Italian Roman Catholic saint</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Galla</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">550<br /><a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">Chiesa di Santa Galla, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">6 April</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Galla of Rome</b> was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Roman_citizenship" title="Roman citizenship">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Widow" title="Widow">widow</a> known for her generosity. She is considered a saint in the Roman Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galla_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Galla was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Roman_citizenship" title="Roman citizenship">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(ancient_Rome)" title="Patrician (ancient Rome)">patrician</a> <a href="/wiki/Quintus_Aurelius_Memmius_Symmachus" title="Quintus Aurelius Memmius Symmachus">Symmachus the Younger</a>, who was appointed consul in 485. Galla was also the <a href="/wiki/Sister-in-law" class="mw-redirect" title="Sister-in-law">sister-in-law</a> of <a href="/wiki/Anicius_Manlius_Severinus_Boethius" class="mw-redirect" title="Anicius Manlius Severinus Boethius">Boethius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her father, Quintus Aurelius Memmius Symmachus, was condemned to death, unjustly, by <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great" title="Theodoric the Great">Theodoric</a> in 525. Galla had been married at a relatively young age, but was soon widowed, just over a year after her marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Being wealthy, she decided to retreat to the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Hill" title="Vatican Hill">Vatican Hill</a>, and founded a <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> near <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>. Galla is reputed to have once healed a deaf and mute girl, by blessing some water, and giving it to the girl to drink. 
Galla remained there for the rest of her life, tending to the sick and poor, before dying in 550.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg/200px-Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="126" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg/300px-Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg/400px-Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1445" data-file-height="910" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Chiesa_ed_Ospizio_di_S._Galla_-_Plate_177_-_Giuseppe_Vasi.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Hospice and church of St. Galla</div></div></div>
<p>According to tradition, in 524 in the portico of the house of Santa Galla, an icon of the Mother of God appeared in the pantry where the saint kept the food for the poor. An oratory, Santa Maria in Portico, was built on the site to house the icon, which was said to be carried in processions since 590 to avert the plague.<sup id="cite_ref-Schofield_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Schofield-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Hospice of St. Galla, an overnight shelter for paupers, was established next door by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_III" title="Pope Celestine III">Pope Celestine III</a>. When the icon was transferred to the newly built <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Campitelli" title="Santa Maria in Campitelli">Santa Maria in Campitelli</a>, Santa Maria in Portico began to be called Santa Galla Antiqua in honor of its foundress.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galla_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Galla's biography is in the <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I#Writings" title="Pope Gregory I">Dialogues</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gregory the Great">Saint Gregory the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Galla is also believed to be the inspiration for the letter of <a href="/wiki/Fulgentius_of_Ruspe" title="Fulgentius of Ruspe">Saint Fulgentius of Ruspe</a>, titled "De statu viduarum".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The new church dedicated to St. Galla, located in the <a href="/wiki/Ostiense" title="Ostiense">Ostiense quarter</a>, was consecrated in 1940. 
</p><p>Galla is one of the 140 saints whose images adorn <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a>'s colonnade.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
