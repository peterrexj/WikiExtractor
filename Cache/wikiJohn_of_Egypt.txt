					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Saint John of Egypt</b>, (c.305<sup id="cite_ref-faithnd_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-faithnd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> - 394), also known as <b>John the Hermit</b>, <b>John the Anchorite</b>, or <b>John of Lycopolis</b>, was one of the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Nitrian_Desert" title="Nitrian Desert">Nitrian Desert</a>.  He began as a <a href="/wiki/Carpenter" class="mw-redirect" title="Carpenter">carpenter</a> but at the age of twenty-five began to live a life of solitude.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John of Egypt was born in <a href="/wiki/Lycopolis_(Delta)" title="Lycopolis (Delta)">Lycopolis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Holtzclaw_1980_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holtzclaw_1980-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His parents were poor and he trained as a carpenter. At the age of 25, he became a monk under the guidance of an elderly hermit.<sup id="cite_ref-Holtzclaw_1980_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holtzclaw_1980-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He spent a decade with the hermit, taking direction from him and learning from him. <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">John Cassian</a> recounts a tale that the hermit directed John to water a dry stick every day for a year. After this test of obedience his superior threw the stick away.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When the hermit died, John spent the next five years travelling and visiting monasteries. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_as_a_hermit">Life as a hermit</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life as a hermit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographer</a> <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, John was noted for performing seemingly absurd acts such as rolling rocks from place to place and cultivating dead trees.  Finally, he withdrew to the top of a cliff near Lycopolis, Egypt, where he could avoid all human contact. There he carved three small cells out of rock; one for sleeping, one for work and the last for praying. Then he walled them up with himself inside, leaving only a small window.<sup id="cite_ref-faithnd_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-faithnd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He communicated through the window to people who brought him food and water twice a week. Crowds would gather on those two days to hear him preach.
</p><p>John never ate until sunset and lived on a diet of <a href="/wiki/Dried_fruit" title="Dried fruit">dried fruit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vegetable" title="Vegetable">vegetables</a> for fifty years.<sup id="cite_ref-Bangley_2005_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bangley_2005-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He refused bread and never ate anything cooked.<sup id="cite_ref-Bangley_2005_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bangley_2005-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He lived this way well into his nineties.<sup id="cite_ref-faithnd_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-faithnd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was believed to possess the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_gift" title="Spiritual gift">spiritual gift</a> of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a> and often predicting the future and knowing the details of persons he had never met. He predicted future victories to the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodosius the Great">Theodosius the Great</a>.
</p><p>He avoided seeing women, in particular, to avoid temptation, but he avoided all people for the last fifty years of his life.  <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Saint Augustine</a> wrote that John was tempted by <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devils</a> and performed miraculous cures.  He cured a woman, according to Augustine, of blindness and then appeared to her in a vision to avoid seeing her in person. 
</p><p>According to Butler, John prayed incessantly, and he spent the last three days of his life without food or drink or any interactions but prayer.  He was discovered in his cell, with his body in a position of prayer.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is March 27 in the Western churches and June 12 in Eastern Orthodoxy.
</p>
