<p><b>Prince John Konstantonovich of Russia</b>, born as <b>Grand Duke John Konstantinovich of Russia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Иоанн Константинович</span>; 5 July 1886 – 18 July 1918), sometimes also known as <b>Prince Ivan</b> or <b>Prince Ioann</b> or <b>Prince Johan,</b> was the eldest son of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Konstantin_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia">Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia</a> by his wife, Yelizaveta Mavrikievna, née <a href="/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth_of_Saxe-Altenburg_(1865%E2%80%931927)" title="Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg (1865–1927)">Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg</a>. He was described by contemporaries as a gentle, religious person, nicknamed "Ioannchik" by his relatives.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prince_John_Konstantinovich_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg/100px-Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg.png" decoding="async" width="100" height="127" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg/150px-Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg/200px-Imperial_Monogram_of_Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="251" data-file-height="320" /></a><figcaption>Imperial Monogram of Prince Ioann Konstantinovich</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ioann Konstantinovich was born in <a href="/wiki/Pavlovsk_Palace" title="Pavlovsk Palace">Pavlovsk Palace</a>, as a <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duke of Russia">Grand Duke of Russia</a> with the style <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Highness" title="Imperial Highness">Imperial Highness</a>, but at the age of 9 days, a <a href="/wiki/Ukaz" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukaz">Ukaz</a> of his cousin Emperor <a href="/wiki/Alexander_III_of_Russia" title="Alexander III of Russia">Alexander III of Russia</a> stripped him of that title, as the Ukaz amended the House Law by limiting the grand-ducal title to grandsons of a reigning emperor. As a result, he received the title <a href="/wiki/List_of_Princes_of_Russia" title="List of Princes of Russia">Prince of the Imperial Blood</a> (Prince of Russia) with the style <a href="/wiki/Highness" title="Highness">Highness</a>.
</p><p>He once entertained the possibility of becoming an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, but eventually fell in love with <a href="/wiki/Princess_Helen_of_Serbia" title="Princess Helen of Serbia">Princess Helen of Serbia</a>, the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Peter_I_of_Serbia" title="Peter I of Serbia">Peter I of Serbia</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Zorka_of_Montenegro" class="mw-redirect" title="Zorka of Montenegro">Princess Zorka of Montenegro</a>, niece of <a href="/wiki/Princess_Milica_of_Montenegro" title="Princess Milica of Montenegro">Grand Duchess Militza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Anastasia_of_Montenegro" title="Princess Anastasia of Montenegro">Grand Duchess Anastasia of Russia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Elena_of_Montenegro" title="Elena of Montenegro">Queen Elena of Italy</a>. They married on 2 September 1911, and Helen took the name Princess Yelena Petrovna of <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russia</a>. As a daughter of the reigning King, after the marriage, she retained her style of <a href="/wiki/Royal_Highness" title="Royal Highness">Royal Highness</a> and as such she had the right to receive foreign diplomats in her own right, unlike her husband. They had a son, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Vsevolod_Ivanovich_of_Russia" title="Prince Vsevolod Ivanovich of Russia">Prince Vsevolod Ivanovich</a> (20 January 1914 &#8211; 18 June 1973), and a daughter, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Catherine_Ivanovna_of_Russia" title="Princess Catherine Ivanovna of Russia">Princess Yekaterina Ivanovna</a> (12 July 1915 &#8211; 13 March 2007), who was the last member of the Imperial Family to be born before the fall of the dynasty, and was ultimately to become the last surviving uncontested dynast of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Romanov" title="House of Romanov">Imperial House of Russia</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Revolution_and_death">Revolution and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prince_John_Konstantinovich_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Revolution and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Prince Ioann fought in the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a> of 1914-1918, was decorated as a war hero, and was at the front when the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Revolution of 1917">Russian Revolution of 1917</a> started. In April 1918 the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks"> Bolshevik</a> authorities exiled him to the <a href="/wiki/Urals" class="mw-redirect" title="Urals">Urals</a>, and in July the same year had him murdered in a mineshaft near <a href="/wiki/Alapayevsk" title="Alapayevsk">Alapayevsk</a>, along with his brothers <a href="/wiki/Prince_Konstantin_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Konstantin Konstantinovich</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince_Igor_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Igor Konstantinovich</a>, his cousin <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Paley" title="Vladimir Paley"> Prince Vladimir Pavlovich Paley</a>, and other relatives and friends.
</p><p>His body was eventually buried in <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>, in the cemetery of the Russian Orthodox Mission, which was destroyed in 1986 to build a park. His sister <a href="/wiki/Princess_Vera_Konstantinovna_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Vera Konstantinovna of Russia"> Princess Vera Konstantinovna</a>, mother <a href="/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth_of_Saxe-Altenburg_(1865%E2%80%931927)" title="Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg (1865–1927)">Grand Duchess Yelizaveta Mavrikievna</a> and wife Princess Yelena Petrovna left Russia in April 1919 with help from the King of Norway. His daughter Princess Yekaterina married Ruggero, <a href="/wiki/Marquis" class="mw-redirect" title="Marquis">Marquis</a> <a href="/wiki/Princess_Catherine_Ivanovna_of_Russia#Farace_di_Villaforesta_family" title="Princess Catherine Ivanovna of Russia"> Farace di Villaforesta</a>. Yekaterina lived in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a> and later in <a href="/wiki/Montevideo" title="Montevideo">Montevideo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Uruguay" title="Uruguay">Uruguay</a> where she died in 2007. His sister <a href="/wiki/Princess_Vera_Constantinovna_of_Russia" title="Princess Vera Constantinovna of Russia"> Princess Vera Konstantinovna</a>, the youngest daughter of Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich, died in New York in 2001, aged 94.
</p>
