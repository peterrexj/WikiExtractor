<p><b>Peter, Metropolitan of Kiev and all Rus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Пётр</span>; c. 1260 – 20 December 1326) was the Russian <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a> who moved his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> from <a href="/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia" title="Vladimir, Russia">Vladimir</a> to <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a> in 1325. Later he was proclaimed a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>. In spite of the move, the office remained officially entitled "<a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Kiev_and_All_Rus%27" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus&#39;">Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus'</a>" until the <a href="/wiki/Autocephaly" title="Autocephaly">autocephalous</a> election of <a href="/wiki/Jonah_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Jonah Metropolitan of Moscow">Jonah of Moscow</a> in 1448.<sup id="cite_ref-encofuk_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encofuk-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Peter_of_Moscow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter was born in <a href="/wiki/Volhynia" title="Volhynia">Volhynia</a><sup id="cite_ref-encofuk_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encofuk-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia%E2%80%93Volhynia" title="Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia">Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia</a>). His parents were Theodore and Eupraxia. At the age of twelve, young Peter entered a monastery where he learned iconography. The igumen of the monastery had Peter ordained as a hieromonk. After years of ascetic labors at the monastery, the hieromonk Peter, with the blessing of the igumen, left the monastery in search of a solitary place.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He built a cell at the Rata River and began to pursue asceticism in silence. Afterwards, at the place of his ascetic exploits, a monastery was formed, called the Novodvorsk. The Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos “Of St Peter” was so called because it was painted by Peter while he was igumen of the Ratsk monastery near Volhynia. During a visit to the <a href="/wiki/Ratne" title="Ratne">Ratne</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> monastery by <a href="/wiki/Maximus,_Metropolitan_of_all_Rus" class="mw-redirect" title="Maximus, Metropolitan of all Rus">Maximus</a>, Metropolitan of Kiev and All Rus', Peter gave him the icon as a gift.<sup id="cite_ref-icon_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-icon-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1308 king <a href="/wiki/Boleslaw-Yuri_II_of_Galicia" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaw-Yuri II of Galicia">Boleslaw-Yuri II of Galicia</a> nominated and the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> appointed Peter to the vacant see of Kiev and all Rus'.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mikhail_Yaroslavich" class="mw-redirect" title="Mikhail Yaroslavich">Mikhail Yaroslavich</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prince" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Prince">Grand Prince</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia" title="Vladimir, Russia">Vladimir</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tver" title="Tver">Tver</a>, wanted to advance his own candidate for this position. Peter's nomination caused prolonged animosity between Mikhail and Peter to the point that the latter had to ask for protection from the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Russian_rulers#Grand_Princes_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Russian rulers">Prince of Moscow</a> in 1325.
</p><p>Peter travelled to Constantinople where Patriarch Athanasius consecrated him as Metropolitan of Rus and bestowed on him the hierarchal vestments, staff and icon. Upon his return to Rus' in 1308, Metropolitan Peter arrived at Kiev after a year, and then proceeded on to Vladimir. During this time of Tatar (Mongol) authority Rus was in turmoil, and Peter was often obliged to change the place of his residence.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter transferred his metropolitan duties from depopulated Kiev to Vladimir. In 1325 Metropolitan Peter, at the request of the Grand Prince of Moscow, <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Kalita" class="mw-redirect" title="Ivan Kalita">Ivan Kalita</a>, transferred the metropolitan cathedra-chair from Vladimir to Moscow as part of Ivan's relocation.<sup id="cite_ref-Janos_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Janos-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The move strengthened the political position of Moscow and established it as the spiritual capital of fragmented Rus. After Peter's move to Moscow, the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Dormition" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of the Dormition">Cathedral of the Dormition</a> and several other stone churches were built by Ivan Kalita in the <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Kremlin" class="mw-redirect" title="Moscow Kremlin">Moscow Kremlin</a>. The foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Vysokopetrovsky_Monastery" title="Vysokopetrovsky Monastery">Vysokopetrovsky Monastery</a> in Moscow is ascribed to Peter. He also authored a few <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> and <a href="/wiki/Epistle" title="Epistle">epistles</a>.
</p><p>Peter died on 21 December 1326.<sup id="cite_ref-Janos_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Janos-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> by <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Alexis" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan Alexis">Metropolitan Alexis</a>, his veneration was propagated all over <a href="/wiki/Moscovy" class="mw-redirect" title="Moscovy">Moscovy</a>. Accordingly, many churches were dedicated to Peter the Metropolitan in Moscow and other cities of Russia. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 24 August (the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Dormition_Cathedral,_Moscow" title="Dormition Cathedral, Moscow">Dormition Cathedral</a> in Moscow) and 5 October  (in common with Metropolitans <a href="/wiki/Jonah_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Jonah Metropolitan of Moscow">Jonah</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexius,_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexius, Metropolitan of Moscow">Alexis</a>)
</p>
