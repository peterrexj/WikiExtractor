saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">9 July</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Gregory Mary Grassi</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a>, (in <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian language</a> Gregorio Maria Grassi) (13 December 1833 – 9 July 1900) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p><p>He is one of the <a href="/wiki/Martyr_Saints_of_China" title="Martyr Saints of China">120 Martyrs of China</a> who were canonized on 1 October 2000 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_mission">Early life and mission</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Grassi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and mission">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Pier Luigi Grassi</b> in <a href="/wiki/Castellazzo_Bormida" title="Castellazzo Bormida">Castellazzo Bormida</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, on 13 December 1833.
</p><p>At the age of 15, on 2 November 1848, he took the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">Friary</a> of <a href="/wiki/Montiano" title="Montiano">Montiano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romagna" title="Romagna">Romagna</a>, with the name Gregory. His solemn <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">profession</a> was made one year later, on 14 December. He was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> to do his <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> studies, and was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> priest on 17 August 1856 in <a href="/wiki/Mirandola" title="Mirandola">Mirandola</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for further training to prepare for his mission to <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>.
</p><p>In 1860 Grassi was assigned to <a href="/wiki/Taiyuan" title="Taiyuan">Taiyuan</a>, China, where he was appointed Mission Promoter, Director of the mission orphanage, and <a href="/wiki/Choirmaster" class="mw-redirect" title="Choirmaster">choirmaster</a> at the seminary there.
</p><p>On 25 January 1876, he was chosen as the <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_Vicar_Apostolic" class="mw-redirect" title="Coadjutor Vicar Apostolic">Coadjutor Vicar Apostolic</a> for the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Apostolic_Vicariate_of_Shanxi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Apostolic Vicariate of Shanxi (page does not exist)">Apostolic Vicariate of Shansi</a>. On 17 June 1891, he assumed authority over the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Taiyuan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Taiyuan">Apostolic Vicariate of Northern Shanxi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 6 September 1891, he established a <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> to provide access to Franciscan life for the Chinese of all four vicariates in <a href="/wiki/Shanxi" title="Shanxi">Shanxi</a> and a rest home for overworked missionaries.
</p><p>He also dealt with the suffering of the local population brought on by <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a> and <a href="/wiki/Famine" title="Famine">famine</a>, which led him to enlarge the orphanage in the city and establish several others, in order to cope with the orphans left behind by these catastrophes.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Grassi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg/220px-Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="336" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg/330px-Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Franciscan_Martyrs_of_Shanxi.jpg 2x" data-file-width="369" data-file-height="563" /></a><figcaption>Franciscan Martyrs of Shanxi</figcaption></figure>
<p>When the short but bloody <a href="/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion" title="Boxer Rebellion">Boxer Rebellion</a> broke out in <a href="/wiki/Peking" class="mw-redirect" title="Peking">Peking</a> in June 1900 and the <a href="/wiki/Empress_Dowager_Cixi" title="Empress Dowager Cixi">Empress Dowager Cixi</a> issued the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Decree_of_declaration_of_war_against_foreign_powers" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Decree of declaration of war against foreign powers">Imperial Decree of declaration of war against foreign powers</a>, Grassi was urged to flee. He responded, "Ever since I was twelve, I have desired and also asked God for martyrdom. Now that this longed-for hour has come, must I run away?"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the beginning of July, the Provincial Governor of Shanxi, <a href="/wiki/Yuxian_(Qing_dynasty)" title="Yuxian (Qing dynasty)">Yuxian</a>, ordered the arrest of the European missionaries in the province. On 4 July a mob attacked the Franciscan mission in <a href="/wiki/Hengyang" title="Hengyang">Hengyang</a> (southern <a href="/wiki/Hunan" title="Hunan">Hunan</a>), murdering one of the friars, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cesidio_Giacomantonio&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cesidio Giacomantonio (page does not exist)">Cesidio Giacomantonio</a>, and burning the mission to the ground. A few days later, on 7 July, the friar who served as <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Changsha" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Changsha">Apostolic Vicar of Southern Hunan</a>, bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Antonio_Fantosati&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Antonio Fantosati (page does not exist)">Antonio Fantosati</a>, and his companion, the friar Giuseppe Maria Gambario, were attacked while returning to the mission in Hengyang. Both were killed.
</p><p>On 27 June 1900, Grassi described the situation of Christians in the Shanxi province in a letter, saying, "The European establishments... are seriously threatened by the mob, united with the Boxers and the soldiers: a catastrophe could occur at any moment. The gates of the city are open, but they are guarded by the Boxers, who prevent the travel of Christians. Now, in the outskirts, we are in the throes of a real revolution: nothing and no one can be said to be safe."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During the night of 5 July 1900, Grassi, along with fellow bishop <a href="/wiki/Francis_Fogolla" title="Francis Fogolla">Francis Fogolla</a>, three friars, seven <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Missionaries_of_Mary" title="Franciscan Missionaries of Mary">Franciscan Missionaries of Mary</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 11 Chinese members of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, of whom six were <a href="/wiki/Seminarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminarian">seminarians</a>, and three employees of the Taiyuan Mission were imprisoned by Boxer forces.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 9 July 1900, Grassi, along with the rest, was escorted from prison with his hands bound behind his back to public trial presided by Yuxian. After a false trial, the group was sentenced to death. Grassi was stripped naked before a crowd of onlookers and Yuxian himself cut Grassi to pieces with a sword. His heart was removed from his body and delivered to Chinese Buddhist monks so that they could study its alleged occult powers. By custom, Grassi's head was severed from his corpse so that it could be put on display in a small cage at the city entrance. What remained of his corpse, along with the corpses of all the others killed that day, was tossed over the city wall and left unburied so that dogs would eat the remains.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> These killings are known as the <a href="/wiki/Taiyuan_massacre" title="Taiyuan massacre">Taiyuan massacre</a>. 
</p><p>Throughout China during the Boxer Uprising, 5 bishops, 50 priests, 2 brothers, 15 sisters and 40,000 Chinese Christians were killed. 
</p><p>The 146,575 Catholics served by the Franciscans in China in 1906 had grown to 303,760 by 1924 and were served by 282 Franciscans and 174 local priests.
</p>
