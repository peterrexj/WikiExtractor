<p><b>Flavia Domitilla</b> was a Roman noblewoman of the 1st century. She was a granddaughter of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Vespasian" title="Vespasian">Vespasian</a> and a niece of Emperors <a href="/wiki/Titus" title="Titus">Titus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Domitian" title="Domitian">Domitian</a>. She married her cousin, the <a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Clemens_(consul)" title="Titus Flavius Clemens (consul)">Titus Flavius Clemens</a>, a grand-nephew of Vespasian through his father <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Sabinus_(consul_AD_69)" title="Titus Flavius Sabinus (consul AD 69)">Titus Flavius Sabinus</a>.
</p><p>There is disagreement about whether the Flavia Domitilla mentioned in certain fourth-century and later Christian writings is the same person or another.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Flavia Domitilla was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Domitilla_the_Younger" title="Domitilla the Younger">Domitilla the Younger</a> by an unknown father, perhaps <a href="/wiki/Quintus_Petillius_Cerialis" title="Quintus Petillius Cerialis">Quintus Petillius Cerialis</a> whom her mother had two sons with.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_and_children">Marriage and children</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Marriage and children">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A contemporary inscription indicates that Flavia Domitilla and her husband <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Clemens_(consul)" title="Titus Flavius Clemens (consul)">Titus Flavius Clemens</a> had seven children,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who were thus grandnephews and grandnieces of Domitian. <a href="/wiki/Suetonius" title="Suetonius">Suetonius</a>, who was born in 69 and thus a contemporary, states that Domitian designated as the emperor's successors two very young sons of Flavius Clemens, and changed their names to Domitian and Vespasian.<sup id="cite_ref-Suetonius_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Suetonius-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Quintilian" title="Quintilian">Quintilian</a> (c. 35 – c. 100) mentions that Domitian had entrusted to him the tutelage of two grandsons of Domitian's sister, presumably the two boys designated by the emperor as his successors.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mommsen also claimed that she had a daughter also named Flavia Domitilla<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who married Titus Flavius Onesimus<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;Note 1&#93;</a></sup> but later retracted this theory.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Banishment">Banishment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Banishment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Suetonius writes: "Finally he put to death his own cousin Flavius Clemens, suddenly and on a very slight suspicion, almost before the end of his consulship; and yet Flavius was a man of most contemptible laziness and Domitian had besides openly named his sons, who were then very young, as his successors, changing their former names and calling the one Vespasian and the other Domitian. And it was by this deed in particular that he hastened his own destruction."<sup id="cite_ref-Suetonius_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Suetonius-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cassius_Dio" title="Cassius Dio">Cassius Dio</a> (c. 155–235) reports in his <i>Roman History</i>: "Domitian slew, along with many others, Flavius Clemens the consul, although he was a cousin and married to Flavia Domitilla, who was also a relative of the emperor's. The charge brought against them both was that of sacrilege or godlessness (Ancient Greek <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%80%CE%B8%CE%B5%CF%8C%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%82" class="extiw" title="wikt:ἀθεότης">ἀθεότης</a>), a charge on which many others who drifted into Jewish ways were condemned. Some of these were put to death, and the rest were at least deprived of their property. Domitilla was merely banished to <a href="/wiki/Ventotene" title="Ventotene">Pandateria</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Suetonius says that one of those involved in the assassination of Domitian on 18 September 96 was Domitilla's steward Stephanus.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_traditions">Religious traditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Religious traditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jewish">Jewish</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Jewish">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Some scholars connect Domitilla with a character in Jewish tradition, a Roman matron in the <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">Talmud</a> (<a href="/wiki/Avodah_Zarah" title="Avodah Zarah">Avodah Zarah</a> 10b) and <a href="/wiki/Deuteronomy_Rabbah" title="Deuteronomy Rabbah">Deuteronomy Rabbah</a> 2.25. When the emperor had decreed that in 30 days the Senate would confirm an edict to kill all <a href="/wiki/Jews" title="Jews">Jews</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> in the Roman Empire, the Roman matron convinced her husband to stand up for the Jews. In that tradition, Flavius Clemens converted to <a href="/wiki/Judaism" title="Judaism">Judaism</a> after having contact with the great sage <a href="/wiki/Rabbi_Akiva" title="Rabbi Akiva">Rabbi Akiva</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Christian">Christian</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Christian">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Nereus,_Achilleus,_Domitilla,_and_Pancras" class="mw-redirect" title="Nereus, Achilleus, Domitilla, and Pancras">Nereus, Achilleus, Domitilla, and Pancras</a></div>
<p>Both the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> celebrate a Flavia Domitilla as a saint.
</p><p>The Greek Orthodox Church celebrates Saint Flavia Domitilla on <a href="/wiki/May_12_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="May 12 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">12 May</a> and a publication of the Apostolic Service of the Church of Greece states that she lived in Rome in the first century, that she was the wife of the Roman official Titus Flavius Clemens and a daughter of Emperor Domitian's sister, and that she died a martyr for refusing to sacrifice to idols.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> This description identifies her with the Flavia Domitilla described above.
</p><p>The identity of the saint honoured in the Catholic Church, whose feast is now on 7 May, is less clear.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Catholic">Catholic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Catholic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In his <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Church History</a></i>, written at the end of 323 or 324, <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>, speaking of "writers who were far from our religion", says that "they recorded that in the fifteenth year of Domitian Flavia Domitilla, daughter of a sister of Flavius Clement, who at that time was one of the consuls of Rome, was exiled with many others to the island of Pontia in consequence of testimony borne to Christ."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_(Eusebius)" title="Chronicon (Eusebius)">Chronicon</a></i>, which survives in a <a href="/wiki/Chronicon_(Jerome)" title="Chronicon (Jerome)">translation</a> by <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> (c. 340–420), Eusebius quotes an earlier writer who gives similar information. Under Year 16 of Domitian in the 218th Olympiad (96 AD, the year of Domitian's assassination), he states: "Brutius writes that there were very many Christian martyrs under Domitian, among whom were Flavia Domitilla, niece (on his sister's side) of Flavius Clemens the consul; she was exiled to the island of Pontia, because she bore witness that she was a Christian."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The identity and religion of this Brutius or Bruttius are uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/George_Syncellus" title="George Syncellus">George Syncellus</a>, who died after 810, repeats the statement by Eusebius in his <i>Chronicon</i> and adds that Flavius Clemens died for Christ. He is the first to speak of this first-century <a href="/wiki/Roman_consul" title="Roman consul">Roman consul</a> as a Christian.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In quoting Eusebius, Syncellus describes Domitilla as Clement's ἐξαδελφή (<i>exadelphe</i>), a term that can mean "cousin" and not only, as Jerome understood it, "niece".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Writing of the death in 404 of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paula" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Paula">Saint Paula</a>, Jerome says that on her way to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, "the vessel touched at the island of Pontia ennobled long since as the place of exile of the illustrious lady Flavia Domitilla who under the Emperor Domitian was banished because she confessed herself a Christian; and Paula, when she saw the cells in which this lady passed the period of her long martyrdom, taking to herself the wings of faith, more than ever desired to see Jerusalem and the holy places".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h5><span id="One_person_or_two.3F"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="One_person_or_two?">One person or two?</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: One person or two?">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h5>
<p>The information that Eusebius attributes to Brutius about Flavia Domitilla does not agree with what Cassius Dio says of the Flavia Domitilla exiled by Domitian. The Domitilla of Cassius Dio was the <i>wife</i> of Flavius Clemens and was exiled to the island of <i><a href="/wiki/Ventotene" title="Ventotene">Pandateria</a></i> in the context of persons who "drifted into <i>Jewish</i> ways". The Domitilla of Eusebius was the <i>niece</i> of Flavius Clemens and was banished to the island of <i><a href="/wiki/Ponza" title="Ponza">Pontia</a></i> for declaring herself a <i>Christian</i>.
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus#Legend" title="Saints Nereus and Achilleus">Acts of Saints Nereus and Achilleus</a></i>, a legend of no historical value<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> composed in the fifth or sixth century, begins by stating that Domitilla was Domitian's niece,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> but then goes on the speak of her as the consul's niece.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Furthermore it presents Domitilla, not as a wife and mother of seven children but as a formally consecrated Christian virgin, a description absent also from the writings of Eusebius and Jerome.
</p><p>Some modern scholars posit the existence of two distinct victims of Domitian, each called Flavia Domitilla, both belonging to the same senatorial family.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The first to propound this view was <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a>:<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> no ancient author speaks of two and Suetonius was able to speak of Stephanus, the assassin of Domitian, simply as "the steward of Domitilla" without having to indicate which Domitilla he served.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Others consider it more likely that Domitian banished only one Flavia Domitilla and that there are errors in the texts of either Cassius Dio or Eusebius or of both.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> They instance the possibility of confusion between the geographically close islands of Pontia and Pandataria.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h5><span class="mw-headline" id="Liturgy">Liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h5>
<p>Flavia Domitilla is for the first time included in a list of saints in the ninth-century <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrology</a> of <a href="/wiki/Florus_of_Lyon" title="Florus of Lyon">Florus of Lyon</a>, in which there is in her regard a long entry based on the <i>Acts of Nobles Nereus and Achilleus</i> and which puts her feast on 7 May, perhaps because the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> had on that date a mention of a Saint Flavius.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-JE130_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE130-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> The same martyrology mentions her also on 12 May, the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus" title="Saints Nereus and Achilleus">Saints Nereus and Achilleus</a>, saying (again on the basis of these <i>Acts</i>) that these were her <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuchs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The martyrology of <a href="/wiki/Ado_of_Vienne" title="Ado of Vienne">Ado of Vienne</a>, in the same century, did the same, but more briefly.<sup id="cite_ref-JE130_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE130-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The ninth-century <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Usuard" title="Martyrology of Usuard">Martyrology of Usuard</a></i> copied that of Ado and was in turn copied by the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a>, who gave credence to the legendary <i>Acts of Saints Nereus and Achilleus</i><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> and in 1595 succeeded in having Domitilla's name added in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> to those of Nereus and Achilleus on their feast, 12 May.
</p><p>Until the <i>Roman Martyrology</i> was completely revised in 2001, it had the following entry under 7 May: "At Terracina, in Campania, the birthday of blessed Flavia Domitilla, virgin and martyr, niece of the Consul Flavius Clemens. She received the religious veil at the hands of St Clement, and in the persecution of Domitian was exiled with many others to the island of Pontia, where she endured a long martyrdom for Christ. Taken afterwards to Terracina, she converted many to the faith of Christ by her teaching and miracles. The judge ordered the chamber in which she was, with the virgins Euphrosina and Theodora, to be set on fire, and she thus consummated her glorious martyrdom. She is also mentioned with the holy martyrs Nereus and Achilleus, on the 12th of this month."<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">Roman Breviary</a></i> presented her as the niece not of the Consul Clemens, but of the Emperors Titus and Domitian, and as a virgin, not a wife. Based closely on the <i>Acts of Saints Nereus and Achilleus</i>, it said under the date of 12 May: "The Roman virgin Flavia Domitilla, niece of Emperors Titus and Domitian, after receiving the religious veil of virginity at the hands of blessed Pope Clement, was accused by her fiancé Aurelianus, son of Consul Titus Aurelius, of being a Christian and was banished by Emperor Domitian to the island of Pontia, where she underwent a long martyrdom in prison. Finally she was taken to Terracina and again confessed to being a Christian. She was always shown to be more and more constant and, under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>, fire was set, by order of the judge, to her room and, together with her foster sisters Theodora and Euphrosyna, she consummated her glorious martyrdom on 7 May. Their bodies were found intact and were buried by the deacon <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Terracina" title="Caesarius of Terracina">Caesarius</a>. On this day [12 May] the bodies of the two brothers [Nereus and Achilleus] and Domitilla were brought back from the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-deaconry" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-deaconry">cardinal-deaconry</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Adriano_al_Foro" title="Sant&#39;Adriano al Foro">Saint Adrian</a> to the basilica of these martyrs, the <i><a href="/wiki/Santi_Nereo_e_Achilleo" title="Santi Nereo e Achilleo">Titulus Fasciolae</a></i>."<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The translation of relics in question was arranged with the greatest solemnity by Cardinal Baronius in 1597.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is thus disagreement between the Roman Breviary of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Tridentine</a> period and the Roman Martyrology of the same period about the identity of Saint Domitilla.
</p><p>The 2001 decree of promulgation of the revised Roman Martyrology declared: "In accordance with the Constitution <i><a href="/wiki/Sacrosanctum_Concilium" title="Sacrosanctum Concilium">Sacrosanctum Concilium</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Ecumenical Council</a> on the Sacred Liturgy, 'the accounts of martyrdom or the lives of the saints are to accord with the facts of history' (art. 92 c), the names of saints included in the Martyrology and their notices have to be subjected more carefully than before to the judgment of historical study."<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Roman Martyrology now states under 7 May: "At Rome, commemoration of Saint Domitilla, martyr, daughter of the sister of Consul Flavius Clemens, who, being accused during the persecution of Emperor Domitian of denying the pagan gods, was because of her witness to Christ banished with others to the island of Pontia and there underwent a long martyrdom."<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Earlier than that, the name of Domitilla was in 1969 removed from the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> (into which it was inserted only in 1595), because her liturgical cult has no basis in [Roman] tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> Accordingly, Domitilla is no longer mentioned on 12 May, feast of the soldier martyrs Nereus and Achilleus.
</p>
