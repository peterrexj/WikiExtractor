					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rusticus_of_Narbonne&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Rusticus of Narbonne">Saint Rusticus of Narbonne</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Saint Rusticus of Narbonne</b> (in French <b>Saint Rustique</b>) (d. 26 October<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> perhaps 461 AD<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) was a monk of the <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Narbonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Narbonne">bishop of Narbonne</a> and Catholic <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, born either at <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a> or at <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>.
</p><p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, when he had completed his education in Gaul, Rusticus went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he soon gained a reputation as a <a href="/wiki/Public_speaker" class="mw-redirect" title="Public speaker">public speaker</a>, but he wished to embrace the contemplative life. He wrote to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jerome">Jerome</a>, who advised him to continue his studies, commending him to imitate the virtues of St. <a href="/wiki/Exuperius" title="Exuperius">Exuperius</a> of <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> and to follow the advice of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Proculus_of_Marseille&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Proculus of Marseille (page does not exist)">Proculus</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procule_de_Marseille" class="extiw" title="fr:Procule de Marseille">fr</a>&#93;</span>, then <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Marseille" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Marseille">Bishop of Marseille</a>. Thus Rusticus entered the <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins Abbey</a>. 
</p><p>He was ordained at Marseilles, and on October 3, 430 (or 427) was consecrated Bishop of Narbonne. He was present at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Ephesus" class="mw-redirect" title="First Council of Ephesus">First Council of Ephesus</a> in 431<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> With all his zeal, he could not prevent the progress of the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> heresy which the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a> were spreading abroad; there is evidence that an Arian rival bishop was established in Narbonne.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Narbonne_(436)" title="Battle of Narbonne (436)">siege of Narbonne</a> by the Goths in 436 and dissensions among the <a href="/wiki/Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholics">Catholics</a> so disheartened him that he wrote to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_I" title="Pope Leo I">Pope Leo I</a>, renouncing the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">bishopric</a>, but St. Leo dissuaded him (<i>Epistle</i> CLXVII).
</p><p>Rusticus then endeavored to consolidate the Catholics. In 444–448, he rebuilt the church in Narbonne dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Genesius_of_Arles" title="Genesius of Arles">Saint Genès of Arles</a>, which had burned in 441;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in 451, he assisted at the convocation of forty-four bishops of Gaul and approved St. Leo's letter to <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Flavian_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Flavian of Constantinople">Flavian</a>, concerning <a href="/wiki/Nestorianism" title="Nestorianism">Nestorianism</a>; he was present also at a <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Arles_(435)" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Arles (435)">Council of Arles</a>, with thirteen bishops, to decide the debate between <a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore,_Bishop_of_Fr%C3%A9jus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Theodore, Bishop of Fréjus (page does not exist)">Theodore, Bishop of Fréjus</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_L%C3%A9rins" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Lérins">Abbey of Lérins</a>. He was one of the twelve bishops who assembled to elect Ravennius <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arles">bishop of Arles</a> in 449;<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> a letter from Ravennius to Rusticus, proves the high esteem in which he was held. Rusticus' own letters are lost, with the exception of the one to St. Jerome and two others to St. Leo, written either in 452 or 458.
</p>
