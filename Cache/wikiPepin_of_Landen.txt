<p><b>Pepin I</b> (also <b>Peppin</b>, <b>Pipin</b>, or <b>Pippin</b>) <b>of Landen</b> (c. 580 &#8211; 27 February 640), also called <b>the Elder</b> or <b>the Old</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_palace" title="Mayor of the palace">Mayor of the palace</a> of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Merovingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingians">Merovingian</a> King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> from 623 to 629. He was also the Mayor for <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_III" title="Sigebert III">Sigebert III</a> from 639 until his death.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pepin_of_Landen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pepin's father was named Carloman<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> by the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Fredegar" title="Chronicle of Fredegar">Chronicle of Fredegar</a>,</i> the chief source for his life. Carloman is occasionally said to be the son of <a href="/wiki/Merovech" title="Merovech">Merovech</a>. His byname comes from his probable birthplace: <a href="/wiki/Landen" title="Landen">Landen</a>, modern <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. However, according to <a href="/wiki/Godefroid_Kurth" title="Godefroid Kurth">Godefroid Kurth</a>, it was only in the twelfth century that the chroniclers of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Brabant" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Brabant">Brabant</a> began to associate him with that locality.<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is sometimes called <b>Pepin I</b> and his other nicknames (Elder and Old) come from his position at the head of the family called the <a href="/wiki/Pippinids" title="Pippinids">Pippinids</a> after him.
</p><p>He was lord of a great part of Brabant, and governor of Austrasia, when their king, Theodebert II, was defeated by Theodoric II, king of Burgundy.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 613, several leading magnates of Austrasia and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy" title="Kingdom of Burgundy">Burgundy</a> abandoned <a href="/wiki/Brunhilda_of_Austrasia" title="Brunhilda of Austrasia">Brunhilda</a>, the great-grandmother and regent of their king, <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_II" title="Sigebert II">Sigebert II</a>, and turned to <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a> of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> for support, promising not to rise in defense of the queen-regent and recognizing Chlothar as rightful regent and guardian of the young king. Chief among these leading men were <a href="/wiki/Warnachar_II" title="Warnachar II">Warnachar II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rado_(mayor_of_the_palace)" title="Rado (mayor of the palace)">Rado</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">Arnulf</a>, and Pepin.  The latter two were described by <a href="/wiki/Fredegar" class="mw-redirect" title="Fredegar">Fredegar</a> as the "two most powerful barons of Austrasia" and they made some agreement with Chlothar at <a href="/wiki/Andernach" title="Andernach">Andernach</a>.  However, while Rado was confirmed as mayor in Austrasia and Warnachar in Burgundy, Pepin did not receive his reward until 623, when he was appointed mayor in Austrasia after Chlothar made his young son Dagobert king there.  Arnulf, his lifelong friend, was appointed adviser to the new king alongside him.
</p><p>Pepin was praised by his contemporaries for his good government and wise counsel.  Though some enemies tried to turn the king against him, their plots were foiled and Pepin remained on good terms with the king until 629, when, for reasons unknown, he retired (or was retired) to his estates, where he remained for the next decade, until Dagobert's death.
</p><p>On Dagobert's death, Pepin came out of retirement to take on the mayoralty in Austrasia for the heir Sigebert III<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and to oversee the distribution of the treasury between Sigebert and his brother, <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a>, and his stepmother <a href="/wiki/Nanthild" title="Nanthild">Nanthild</a>, who was ruling on Clovis' behalf in Neustria and Burgundy.  Sigebert's share of the inheritance was amicably surrendered, partly because of the friendship between Pepin and the Burgundian mayor of the palace, <a href="/wiki/Aega_(mayor_of_the_palace)" title="Aega (mayor of the palace)">Aega</a>.  Pepin and Arnulf's successor as chief counselor to the king, <a href="/wiki/Cunibert,_Bishop_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Cunibert, Bishop of Cologne">Cunibert, Bishop of Cologne</a>, received the treasure at <a href="/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne" title="Compiègne">Compiègne</a> and brought it back to <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>. Pepin died in 640.<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was so popular in Austrasia that, though he was never <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>, he was listed as a saint in some <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>.  Butler lists him as "blessed".<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> was 21 February.
</p>
