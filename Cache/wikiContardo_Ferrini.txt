<p><b>Contardo Ferrini</b> (5 April 1859 – 17 October 1902) was a noted <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Jurist" title="Jurist">jurist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Legal_scholar" class="mw-redirect" title="Legal scholar">legal scholar</a>. He was also a fervent <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a>, who lived a devout life of prayer and service to the poor. He has been <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Contardo_Ferrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born on 5 April 1859 in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, Italy, to Rinaldo Ferrini and Luigia Buccellati. He was baptized at the same <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_font" title="Baptismal font">baptismal font</a> where <a href="/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Ozanam" title="Frédéric Ozanam">Frédéric Ozanam</a>, also a native of Milan, had been baptized 46 years prior. After receiving his <a href="/wiki/First_Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="First Holy Communion">First Holy Communion</a> at the age of twelve, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Confraternity" title="Confraternity">Confraternity</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>.
</p><p>Rinaldo Ferrini, a professor of mathematics and science, taught his son at an early age. Contardo learned to speak several languages. His love for the Catholic faith caused friends to nickname him "Saint Aloysius" (after <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a>). He entered the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Pavia" title="University of Pavia">University of Pavia</a> at age seventeen and, two years later, he was appointed dean of students. At age twenty-one he became a doctor of the law at the university. His doctoral thesis, which related <a href="/wiki/Penal_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Penal Law">Penal Law</a> to <a href="/wiki/Homeric" class="mw-redirect" title="Homeric">Homeric</a> <a href="/wiki/Poetry" title="Poetry">poetry</a>, was the basis of his being awarded a scholarship to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Berlin" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Berlin">University of Berlin</a>, where he specialized in Roman-Byzantine law, a field in which he became internationally recognized as an expert.
</p><p>During Ferrini's stay in <a href="/wiki/Berlin" title="Berlin">Berlin</a>, he wrote of his excitement at receiving the Sacrament of <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">Penance</a> for the first time in a foreign land. The experience brought home to him, he wrote, the universality of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Upon his return to Italy, Ferrini was a lecturer in the universities at Messina, Modena, and Pavia. He received his first professorship at the young age of twenty-six. Contardo attempted to discern his <a href="/wiki/Vocation" title="Vocation">vocation</a> whether as a <a href="/wiki/Secular_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Secular priest">secular priest</a>, a member of a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic religious order">religious order</a>, or as a married person. Ultimately, he fulfilled his vocation as an unmarried <a href="/wiki/Lay_preacher" title="Lay preacher">layperson</a>. He vowed himself to God, became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a> in 1886, and was also a member of the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_de_Paul_Society" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vincent de Paul Society">Saint Vincent de Paul Society</a>, to which he had been introduced by his father, a member of the Society.
</p><p>As a faculty member at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Pavia" title="University of Pavia">University of Pavia</a>, Ferrini was considered an expert in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Law">Roman Law</a>. Over the course of his career he published books, articles and reviews. He taught for a time at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>. He later became a <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon lawyer</a> in addition to being a civil lawyer.
</p><p>An unconfirmed anecdote about Ferrini is that he was asked to attend a dinner party and, once there, found it tedious. His resort was to invite all the guests to join him in praying the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a>.
</p><p>In 1900, Ferrini developed a heart <a href="/wiki/Lesion" title="Lesion">lesion</a>. In autumn 1902, in order to rest, he went to his country home in the village of Suna, <a href="/wiki/Novara" title="Novara">Novara</a>, (now part of the <a href="/wiki/Comune" title="Comune">commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Verbania" title="Verbania">Verbania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Verbano-Cusio-Ossola" title="Province of Verbano-Cusio-Ossola">Province of Verbano-Cusio-Ossola</a>), on the shores of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Maggiore" title="Lake Maggiore">Lake Maggiore</a>. While there, he became ill with <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a>. He died at age forty-three on 17 October 1902. Residents of Suna immediately declared him a saint. His colleagues at the University of Pavia wrote letters in which he was described as a saint.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Contardo_Ferrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1909 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> authorized <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari" title="Andrea Carlo Ferrari">Andrea Carlo Ferrari</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a>, to open a process to promote Ferrini's <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>. He was subsequently declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> and he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 13 April 1947. His body is venerated in a chapel of Milan's Catholic University.
</p><p>Ferrini is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of universities, professors and <a href="/wiki/Homeric_scholarship" title="Homeric scholarship">Homeric scholarship</a>.
</p>
