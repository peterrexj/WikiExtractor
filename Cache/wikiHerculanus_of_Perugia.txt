<p><b>Herculanus of Perugia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Ercolano</i>; died 549 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Perugia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Perugia">bishop of Perugia</a>. He was canonized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the Catholic Church and is recognised as <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>.  His main feast day is November 7; his second feast is celebrated on March 1.  According to Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Dialogues_(Pope_Gregory)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dialogues (Pope Gregory)">Dialogues</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Herculanus suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> when <a href="/wiki/Totila" title="Totila">Totila</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Ostrogoths" title="Ostrogoths">Ostrogoths</a>, captured Perugia in 549.
</p><p>Before the city was captured, Herculanus is said to have tried to save the city by feeding the last sack of <a href="/wiki/Grain" title="Grain">grain</a> to the last <a href="/wiki/Domestic_sheep" class="mw-redirect" title="Domestic sheep">lamb</a>.  This was meant to give the Ostrogoth forces the impression that the Perugians had food to spare, and were able to feed a weak lamb with their precious grain.  With food to spare, they were thus able to withstand the siege.  However, Totila was not fooled by this trick and captured the city just the same.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Totila is said to have given orders for Herculanus to be completely <a href="/wiki/Flaying" title="Flaying">flayed</a>.  However, the Ostrogoth soldier who had to perform this task took pity on the bishop and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> Herculanus before the flaying had been completed.
</p><p>Gregory writes that forty days after the head of Herculanus had been cut off, it was found to have been miraculously reunited to his body.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The inhabitants of the castle of Cisterna in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, above the River Puglia, were under Perugian rule, and were obliged to send three pounds of <a href="/wiki/Wax" title="Wax">wax</a> to Perugia for the feast of St Herculanus.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
