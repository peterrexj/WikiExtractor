					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">English Roman Catholic layman and martyr</div>
<p><b>John Speed</b> (alias Spence) (executed at <a href="/wiki/Durham,_England" title="Durham, England">Durham</a>, 4 February 1594) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> layman, condemned for aiding and abetting Catholic priests in their ministry. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-Burns_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burns-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Speed_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Speed was one of a group of four known as "The Durham Martyrs of 1594".<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
He was hanged at Durham 4 February 1594 for aiding and abetting priests, specifically for escorting <a href="/wiki/John_Boste" title="John Boste">John Boste</a> from one safe house to another,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which was a capital offense under the Supremacy of the Realm Act of 1585.<sup id="cite_ref-Burns_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burns-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Boste worked as a missionary priest in Northern England, and visited <a href="/wiki/Hylton_Castle" title="Hylton Castle">Hylton Castle</a>, but his activities were largely centered around the old Neville estate at <a href="/wiki/Brancepeth_Castle" title="Brancepeth Castle">Brancepeth Castle</a> near Durham.. He was often accompanied by John Speed. With Speed's help, Boste was able to avoid arrest for over ten years but was eventually betrayed in 1593 to the authorities near Durham by an apostate informer. Just as he had finished saying Mass at a private house on the Neville estate, he and those with him were arrested.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Richard_Challoner" title="Richard Challoner">Richard Challoner</a>, Speed turned down the inducements offered to bring him to conformity with the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. With him was condemned Grace Claxton, wife of William Claxton, of the Waterhouse, in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Brancepeth" title="Brancepeth">Brancepeth</a>, Durham, at whose house Boste was taken and probably Speed also. She was, however, reprieved on being found to be pregnant.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The others executed inn County Durham at that time were: John Boste (24 July), George Swallowell at <a href="/wiki/Darlington" title="Darlington">Darlington</a> 26 July, and John Ingram at <a href="/wiki/Gateshead" title="Gateshead">Gateshead</a> 26 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Burns_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burns-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
