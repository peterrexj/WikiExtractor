					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Isidore_of_Alexandria" title="Saint Isidore of Alexandria">Saint Isidore of Alexandria</a>.</div>
<p><b>Isidore of Alexandria</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;z&#39; in &#39;zoom&#39;">z</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/ɔːr/: &#39;ar&#39; in &#39;war&#39;">ɔːr</span></span>/</a></span></span>; also <b>Isidorus</b> <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˌ/: secondary stress follows">ˌ</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;z&#39; in &#39;zoom&#39;">z</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/ɔːr/: &#39;ar&#39; in &#39;war&#39;">ɔːr</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἰσίδωρος ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς</span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;450</span>&#160;– c.<span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;520</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a><sup id="cite_ref-Ref_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ref_-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Philosopher" title="Philosopher">philosopher</a> and one of the last of the <a href="/wiki/Neoplatonism" title="Neoplatonism">Neoplatonists</a>. He lived in <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> toward the end of the 5th century AD. He became head of the school in Athens in succession to <a href="/wiki/Marinus_of_Neapolis" title="Marinus of Neapolis">Marinus</a>, who followed <a href="/wiki/Proclus" title="Proclus">Proclus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-eb1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Isidore was born in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a>, he studied under <a href="/wiki/Proclus" title="Proclus">Proclus</a>, and learned the doctrine of <a href="/wiki/Aristotle" title="Aristotle">Aristotle</a> from <a href="/wiki/Marinus_of_Neapolis" title="Marinus of Neapolis">Marinus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mu199_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mu199-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Damascius" title="Damascius">Damascius</a>, "Isidore was awestruck at the sight of Proclus, venerable and marvelous to see; he thought he was seeing in him the very face of true philosophy."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Proclus for his part used to "marvel at Isidore's appearance, as it was possessed by the divine and full of the philosophical life within."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Damascius further tells us that "Isidore, besides simplicity, loved truthfulness especially, and undertook to be straight-talking beyond what was necessary, and had no pretence in himself whatsoever."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The claim made in the <i><a href="/wiki/Suda" title="Suda">Suda</a></i> that Isidore was the husband of <a href="/wiki/Hypatia" title="Hypatia">Hypatia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> must be in error since Isidore was born long after Hypatia died.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It is elsewhere related that Isidore had a wife called Domna, who died five days after the birth of their son whom they named Proclus.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Isidore returned to Alexandria accompanied by <a href="/wiki/Sallustius_of_Emesa" title="Sallustius of Emesa">Sallustius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In Alexandria he taught philosophy. He was in Athens when Proclus died (in 485), and later when Marinus took over as head (<i><a href="/wiki/Scholarch" title="Scholarch">scholarch</a></i>) of the Neoplatonist school.<sup id="cite_ref-mu199_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mu199-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Marinus persuaded him to be his successor as head of the school, but he left Athens not long after Marinus died,<sup id="cite_ref-isidorus5_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-isidorus5-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> resigning his position to <a href="/wiki/Hegias" title="Hegias">Hegias</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-eb1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Isidore is known principally as the teacher of Damascius, whose testimony in his <i>Life of Isidore</i> presents Isidore in a very favourable light as a man and a thinker.<sup id="cite_ref-eb1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Damascius' <i>Life</i>, which is dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Theodora_of_Emesa" title="Theodora of Emesa">Theodora</a>, a disciple of both Isidore and Damascius, is preserved in summary form by <a href="/wiki/Photios_I_of_Constantinople" title="Photios I of Constantinople">Photius</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Bibliotheca_(Photius)" title="Bibliotheca (Photius)">Bibliotheca</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and in fragments in the <i><a href="/wiki/Suda" title="Suda">Suda</a></i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Philosophy">Philosophy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Philosophy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is generally admitted that he was rather an enthusiast than a thinker; reasoning with him was subsidiary to inspiration, and he preferred the theories of <a href="/wiki/Pythagoras" title="Pythagoras">Pythagoras</a> and <a href="/wiki/Plato" title="Plato">Plato</a> to the unimaginative <a href="/wiki/Logic" title="Logic">logic</a> and the practical <a href="/wiki/Ethics" title="Ethics">ethics</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Stoics" class="mw-redirect" title="Stoics">Stoics</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peripatetic_school" title="Peripatetic school">Aristotelians</a>. He seems to have given loose rein to theosophical speculation and attached great importance to <a href="/wiki/Dream" title="Dream">dreams</a> and waking <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">visions</a>, on which he used to expatiate in his public discourses.<sup id="cite_ref-eb1911_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
