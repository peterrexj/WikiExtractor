<p>

<b>Patrick O'Loughran</b> (<i>in <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: Pádraig Ó Lochráin</i>) (died 1 February 1612) was a priest of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Armagh" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Armagh">archdiocese of Armagh</a> and an <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyr</a>.
</p><p>O'Loughran was born in <a href="/wiki/Donaghmore,_County_Tyrone" title="Donaghmore, County Tyrone">Donaghmore, County Tyrone</a> to an <i><a href="/wiki/Erenagh" title="Erenagh">Erenagh</a></i> family. He later became chaplain to <a href="/wiki/Hugh_O%27Neill,_Earl_of_Tyrone" title="Hugh O&#39;Neill, Earl of Tyrone">Hugh O'Neill, Earl of Tyrone</a>. He left Ireland for <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a> to pursue his education some time before the <a href="/wiki/Flight_of_the_Earls" title="Flight of the Earls">Flight of the Earls</a> in September 1607. He administered the sacraments to exiled Irish noblemen who had fled to <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, and served as chaplain to <a href="/wiki/Catherine_O%27Neill,_Countess_of_Tyrone" title="Catherine O&#39;Neill, Countess of Tyrone">Catherine O'Neill, Countess of Tyrone</a>. He also attended the <a href="/wiki/Irish_College,_Douai" title="Irish College, Douai">Irish College, Douai</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-clavin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clavin-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where he met O'Neill and traveled with him to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>O'Loughran returned to Ireland in June 1611 and was arrested upon landing in Cork. Under interrogation, O'Loughran admitted that he had given sacraments to the exiled noblemen, and that he had assisted the Irish bishop, Blessed <a href="/wiki/Conor_O%27Devany" class="mw-redirect" title="Conor O&#39;Devany">Conor O'Devany</a>. Upon his confession he was committed to  a dungeon and in January 1612, he and Bishop O'Devany were condemned to death. O'Loughran's closeness to Tyrone appears to have been the reason behind the government's decision to single him out.<sup id="cite_ref-clavin_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clavin-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 1 February 1612, they were brought from <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle" title="Dublin Castle">Dublin Castle</a> to George's Hill. The bishop asked to die last so that he could provide Father O'Loughran moral support in his moments before death, but the request was refused.<sup id="cite_ref-Peel_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Peel-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Father O'Loughran replied: "Aid me by your prayers with God, by whose help I am sure that neither death, nor life, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor anything else, will separate me from the love of Christ or from my companionship with you." Bishop O'Devany knelt to pray for O'Loughran before they were both drawn and quartered. Their bravery and faith
inspired the crowd to surge forward to acquire relics.
</p><p>The remains were buried on George's Hill but disinterred the following night and given honourable burial. They were buried ‘with other martyrs’ in St James's graveyard. O'Loughran and O'Devany were beatified in 1992 as part of the Irish Catholic Martyrs. The Archdiocese of Armagh marked the 400th Anniversary of the Death of Blessed Patrick O’Loughran with a Mass at Blessed Patrick O’Loughran Primary School, Castlecaulfield.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
