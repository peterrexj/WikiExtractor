<p><b>Hripsime</b> (<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D5%80%D5%BC%D5%AB%D6%83%D5%BD%D5%AB%D5%B4%D5%A7" class="extiw" title="wikt:Հռիփսիմէ">Հռիփսիմէ</a></span>, died c. 290<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>), also called <b>Rhipsime</b>, <b>Ripsime</b>, <b>Ripsima</b>, <b>Ripsimia</b>, <b>Ripsimus</b>, <b>Arbsima</b> or <b>Arsema</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez language">Ge'ez</a>: <span lang="gez">አርሴማ</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">martyr</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Roman</a> origin; she and her companions in martyrdom are <a href="/wiki/Venerate" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerate">venerated</a> as some of the first Christian martyrs of <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hripsime&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Hripsime was possibly of noble birth.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She belonged to a community of virgins in Rome, numbering 35<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> under the leadership of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Gayane_(person)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Gayane (person) (page does not exist)">Gayane</a>. She was known to be extremely beautiful, and attracted the notice of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. To avoid his advances she, along with her community, fled the city, going first to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> before settling in <a href="/wiki/Echmiadzin" class="mw-redirect" title="Echmiadzin">Vagharshapat</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The varying accounts of her martyrdom diverge at this point. One story indicates that Hripsime again was noticed for her beauty, this time by <a href="/wiki/Tiridates_III_of_Armenia" title="Tiridates III of Armenia">King Tiridates III</a>, who proceeded to pursue her. Upon being brought before him, she refused his advances and was punished by being <a href="/wiki/Roasting" title="Roasting">roasted</a> alive. Gayane was then put to death by Tiridates' soldiers, as were all the members of her community except for Nune (or Marine), who was later a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a><sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and, as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nino" title="Saint Nino">Saint Nino</a>, is praised as the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Georgian_Orthodox_Church" title="Georgian Orthodox Church">Georgian Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>Another version of the saint's Acts indicates that, upon discovering Hripsime's whereabouts, Diocletian sent a letter to Tiridates insisting that he either send her back or take her for himself. The king's servants found her among her companions, here described as <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, and urged that she follow his wishes. She responded that she could not marry as she was betrothed to <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>, as were the others. At this, a voice from heaven was heard, saying, "Be brave and fear not, for I am with you".<sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Upon this, Tiridates ordered that Hripsime be tortured; her tongue was cut out, her stomach cut open, and she was blinded before being killed. Her body was then cut into pieces. Inspired by her example, Gayane and two other nuns gave themselves over to similar treatment before being beheaded. The rest of the community was put to the sword, their bodies thrown to the beasts to be eaten. Supposedly, Tiridates and his soldiers were then punished by God for their actions; the soldiers were beset by <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devils</a>, and began to act like wild animals, running through the forests, gnawing at themselves, and tearing their clothes.<sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The legend states that the King was turned into a wild <a href="/wiki/Boar" class="mw-redirect" title="Boar">boar</a> for his actions, and had to be saved by the intervention of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator" title="Gregory the Illuminator">Gregory the Illuminator</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyc_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> These accounts are likely highly fictionalized; about the only thing certain about Hripsime's story is that she and her companions were, in fact, martyred in Armenia in about 290.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Hripsime_Church.2C_Echmiadzin"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Hripsime_Church,_Echmiadzin">St. Hripsime Church, Echmiadzin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hripsime&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: St. Hripsime Church, Echmiadzin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg/220px-Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg/330px-Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg/440px-Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2304" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tombstone_of_Saint_Hripsime.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tombstone of Saint Hripsime</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/St._Hripsime_Church,_Echmiadzin" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Hripsime Church, Echmiadzin">St. Hripsime Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Echmiadzin" class="mw-redirect" title="Echmiadzin">Echmiadzin</a> is dedicated to Hripsime; the current structure was consecrated in 618, and contains her tomb in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">catacombs</a> beneath the building.<sup id="cite_ref-armeniapedia_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armeniapedia-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> According to legend, Christ designated the spot for the shrine by descending from heaven in a shaft of light and smiting the ground with a golden hammer until the earth shook.<sup id="cite_ref-britannica_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-britannica-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Some of the saint's relics, along with items relating to Tiridates and Gregory the Illuminator, were pillaged by <a href="/wiki/Persian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Persian people">Persians</a> during an invasion in 1604, but were restored in 1638.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyc2_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyc2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast">Feast</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hripsime&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Catholic tradition, Hripsime and her companions are commemorated with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 29 September;<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> commemorates them on 30 September.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Apostolic Church</a> remembers Hripsime and her companions on 4 June; Gayane and her companions are commemorated separately, on 5 June.<sup id="cite_ref-AAC_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAC-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_in_Ethiopia">Veneration in Ethiopia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hripsime&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration in Ethiopia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Hripsime is one of the most venerated saints in Ethiopia. She is known as "Arsema" (አርሴማ) to <a href="/wiki/Ethiopian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethiopian Orthodox">Ethiopian Orthodox</a> Christians. Currently there are three well known churches and monasteries named after her in Ethiopia. Among the churches, the oldest one, is found in one of the <a href="/wiki/Lake_Tana" title="Lake Tana">Lake Tana</a> islands. There are many old icons in the church portraying how she was martyred by Tiridates III (<i>Dirtadis</i> in Ethiopian languages) and how the cruel king was changed into a beast after killing her. There is annual pilgrimage by Ethiopian Christians to this church in January. There are also Christian songs praising her faith and martyrdom. The book entitled <i>Gedle Arsema</i>, meaning "The Life of Arsema", is found almost in every spiritual bookshop throughout Ethiopia. Her story is told in the Ethiopian Synaxarium on Mäskäräm 29 (which equates to 26 or 27 September on the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, and 9 or 10 October on the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>) which coincides with the date of the saint's martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hripsime&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In honour of the saint, Hripsime remains a fairly common female name in Armenia, as do its variants; likewise, Arsema is a very popular name among Ethiopian and Eritrean Christians.
</p>
