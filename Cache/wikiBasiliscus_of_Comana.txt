<p>Saint <b>Basiliscus of Comana</b> (or <b>Basilicus of Pontus</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;310</span>) in what is now northern Turkey was a Greek martyr.
His feast day is 22 May, or 30 July in the Greek calendar.
</p>

<p>The story of Basilicus is an example of an itinerant martyrdom.
He was arrested in <a href="/wiki/Amasya" title="Amasya">Amasya</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pontus_(region)" title="Pontus (region)">Pontus</a>, but was allowed to go to the village of Choumiala to see his family before returning to Amasya for trial.
He was then taken to the village of Dakozara before being martyred outside <a href="/wiki/Comana_Pontica" title="Comana Pontica">Comana</a>.
He is associated with the martyrs Eutropius and Cleonicus during his journey, in which he was followed by a crowd and performed various miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJacobsElton201850_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacobsElton201850-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another version say Basiliscus, Bishop of Comana, was decapitated around 312 at <a href="/wiki/Nicomedia" title="Nicomedia">Nicomedia</a> by the tyrant <a href="/wiki/Maximinus_Daza" title="Maximinus Daza">Maximinus Daza</a> (r. 310–313).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDrakeDrake191617_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDrakeDrake191617-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
The biography of <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a> says that Basiliscus, Bishop of Comana, was martyred under <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> (r. 286–305).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMullen2003123_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMullen2003123-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basiliscus_of_Comana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a>, wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921),
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>BASILICUS (St.) M. (May 22)<br />(4th century) A Bishop of Comana in Pontus (Asia Minor), who was beheaded and his body thrown into a river near Nicomedia (A.D. 312), under the Emperor Maximin Daza. The Greeks honour him on July 30. This was the holy Martyr who, appearing to Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>, intimated to him that on the morrow that Saint’s work for God on earth would end..<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate192140_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate192140-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Roman_Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basiliscus_of_Comana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Roman Martyrology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> says, under <i>The Twenty-Second Day of May</i>,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>At Comana, in Pontus, under the emperor Maximian and the governor Agrippa, the holy martyr Basiliscus, who was forced to wear iron shoes pierced with heated nails, and endured many other trials. Being at last decapitated and thrown into a river, he obtained the glory of martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBenedict_XIV191616_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBenedict_XIV191616-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basiliscus_of_Comana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> wrote in his <i>Lives of the Primitive Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i> under May 22, 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Saint Basiliscus, Bishop of Comana in Pontius, Martyr
</p><p>
He received the crown of martyrdom together with Saint Lucia at Nicomedia in 312, under the tyrant Maximinus Daia. Peace being soon after restored to the church, his body was honourably brought back to Comana. Saint Chrysostom died in the Presbyterium or community of the clergy belonging to the church of Saint Basiliscus. The martyr had before admonished the priest in a dream to prepare a lodging for his brother John; and he comforted Saint Chrysostom by a vision, in which he bade him be of good courage, for the next day they should be together. See Palladius in vita Chrys. Theodoret, and Sozomen.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1821297_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1821297-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p> </blockquote>
