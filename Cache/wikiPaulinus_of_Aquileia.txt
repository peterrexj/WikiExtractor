He participated in a number of <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synods</a> which opposed <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Adoptionism" title="Spanish Adoptionism">Spanish Adoptionism</a> and promoted both reforms and the adoption of the <i><a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">Filioque</a></i> into the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a>. In addition, Paulinus arranged for the peaceful <a href="/wiki/Christianisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianisation">Christianisation</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Avars" title="Pannonian Avars">Avars</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Alpine_Slavs" class="mw-redirect" title="Alpine Slavs">alpine Slavs</a> in the territory of the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia_(episcopal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Aquileia (episcopal)">Aquileian patriarchate</a>. For this, he is also known as the <a href="/wiki/Apostle" title="Apostle">apostle</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Slovenes" title="Slovenes">Slovenes</a>.
</p><meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Paulinus was born at <a href="/wiki/Premariacco" title="Premariacco">Premariacco</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Cividale" class="mw-redirect" title="Cividale">Cividale</a> (the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <i>Forum Iulii</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Friuli" title="Friuli">Friuli</a> region of north-eastern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, during the latter days of <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombard</a> rule. He received his education in the patriarchal school at Cividale and, after <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a>, he became master of the same school.  There he acquired a thorough <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> culture, both in pagan and Christian <a href="/wiki/Classics" title="Classics">classics</a>. He also acquired a relatively deep knowledge of <a href="/wiki/Jurisprudence" title="Jurisprudence">jurisprudence</a>, and an extensive Scriptural, theological, and <a href="/wiki/Patristics" title="Patristics">patristic</a> training.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Carolingian_Renaissance">Carolingian Renaissance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Carolingian Renaissance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Paulinus' educational background prepared him to play a key part in the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Renaissance" title="Carolingian Renaissance">Carolingian Renaissance</a>, allowing him to capably assist in the promotion of <a href="/wiki/Christendom" title="Christendom">Christendom</a> and the restoration of <a href="/wiki/Western_Civilization" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Civilization">Western Civilization</a> after centuries of unstable <a href="/wiki/Barbarian" title="Barbarian">barbarian</a> rule.
</p><p>It was precisely because of his exceptional learning that Paulinus first came to the attention of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> in 774 when the <a href="/wiki/King" title="King">King</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> conquered all of Lombard northern Italy for the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a>. Moreover, because of his loyalty to Charlemagne during Duke <a href="/wiki/Hrodgaud_of_Friuli" title="Hrodgaud of Friuli">Hrodgaud's</a> rebellion in 776, Paulinus was rewarded with many favors, among them the gift of the property of Waldand, son of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mimo_of_Lavariano&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mimo of Lavariano (page does not exist)">Mimo of Lavariano</a>, by means of a diploma issued by Charlemagne from <a href="/wiki/Ivrea" title="Ivrea">Ivrea</a>. In the same year, Charlemagne also invited Paulinus to the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty" title="Carolingian dynasty">palace court</a> to be royal "master of grammar (<i>grammaticus magister</i>)." It was at the itinerant <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Aachen" title="Palace of Aachen">palace school</a> (<i>schola palatina</i>) that Paulinus would stay for about ten years and make the acquaintance of other leading scholars of the age, including <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Pisa" title="Peter of Pisa">Peter of Pisa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin of York</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fardulf&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fardulf (page does not exist)">Fardulf</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arno_of_Salzburg" title="Arno of Salzburg">Arno of Salzburg</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Albrico&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Albrico (page does not exist)">Albrico</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Riculph&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Riculph (page does not exist)">Riculph</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Raefgot&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Raefgot (page does not exist)">Raefgot</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Rado_(chancellor)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Rado (chancellor) (page does not exist)">Rado</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lullus" title="Lullus">Lullus</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bassinus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bassinus (page does not exist)">Bassinus</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fuldrad&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fuldrad (page does not exist)">Fuldrad</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eginard" class="mw-redirect" title="Eginard">Eginard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adalard_of_Corbie" title="Adalard of Corbie">Adalard</a> and <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_(mystic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalbert (mystic)">Adelbert</a>.  He formed an enduring friendships with Alcuin as attested to by numerous letters.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patriarch_of_Aquileia">Patriarch of Aquileia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patriarch of Aquileia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On the death of Patriarch <a href="/w/index.php?title=Siguald&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Siguald (page does not exist)">Siguald</a> in 787, Charles appointed Paulinus to be consecrated as the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Aquileia">Patriarch of Aquileia</a>. Paulinus returned from court to his episcopal see and took up residence at Cividale, also the seat of the Carolingian <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">count</a> in charge of the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Friuli" title="March of Friuli">March of Friuli</a>. (<a href="/wiki/Aquileia" title="Aquileia">Aquileia</a> itself had been reduced to a tiny village after its <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Aquileia" title="Sack of Aquileia">destruction</a> in 452 by <a href="/wiki/Attila_the_Hun" class="mw-redirect" title="Attila the Hun">Attila the Hun</a>, although the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Aquileia">patriarchal basilica</a> remained there.)  As patriarch, Paulinus was able to take a more active and prominent part in implementing societal reforms. In his relations with the churches of <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a>, or with the nearby <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Grado" title="Patriarch of Grado">Patriarch of Grado</a>, the representative of Byzantine interests, he exhibited prudence and pastoral zeal. Meanwhile, from Charlemagne, Paulinus obtained diplomas for the free election of the future patriarchs by the cathedral chapter of Aquileia, and other privileges for his patriarchate as well as for the monastery of St. Mary in Organo, the church of St. Lawrence in <a href="/wiki/Buja" title="Buja">Buja</a>, and the hospitals of St. John at Cividale and St. Mary at <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Synods">Synods</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Synods">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paulinus was solicitous for the integrity of Catholic doctrine.  In 792, he took part in the Council of <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Ratisbon</a>, which condemned the heresy of <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Adoptionism" title="Spanish Adoptionism">Spanish Adoptionism</a> taught by Spanish bishops, <a href="/wiki/Elipandus" title="Elipandus">Elipandus</a> of <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Felix,_Bishop_of_Urgel" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix, Bishop of Urgel">Felix of Urgel</a>. In 794, he took a leading part in the Frankish national council at <a href="/wiki/Frankfort-am-Main" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankfort-am-Main">Frankfort</a>, where Adoptionism was again condemned, and composed a book against the heresy which was sent to Spain in the name of the assembled bishops. Departing Frankfort, Paulinus returned to his episcopal residence at Cividale.
</p><p>In 796 he accompanied Charlemagne's son <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Italy" title="Pepin of Italy">Pepin</a> in his military campaign against the nearby hostile <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Avars" title="Pannonian Avars">Avars</a>. In late summer of 796, after the Avars had been defeated, Paulinus presided over a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> of bishops at Pepin's military camp on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> in which the bishops decided on a program of evangelization and catechesis for the recently subdued territories inhabited by the Avars and the <a href="/wiki/Alpine_Slavs" class="mw-redirect" title="Alpine Slavs">Slavs</a>. With the consent of Paulinus, the synod also assigned the patriarchate of Aquileia's northernmost territory to the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>, headed by <a href="/wiki/Arno_of_Salzburg" title="Arno of Salzburg">Arno</a>. The border between the dioceses was drawn on the <a href="/wiki/Drava" title="Drava">Drava</a> River. The agreement was confirmed in 811 by Charlemagne and lasted for almost a millennium, until the middle of the 18th century. At the advice of <a href="/wiki/Alcuin_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Alcuin of York">Alcuin of York</a>, it was decided that the areas should be <a href="/wiki/Christianisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianisation">Christianised</a> nonviolently.<sup id="cite_ref-SH2008_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH2008-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Nowadays, the <a href="/wiki/Slovenes" title="Slovenes">Slovenes</a> regard Paulinus as their apostle who peacefully brought them Christian religion,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> although the true missionary activity in the Aquileian sphere started only after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SH2008_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH2008-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning from the synod, Paulinus once more opposed the Adoptionism at the Council of Cividale. The patriarch expounded the Western Catholic doctrine of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity">Holy Trinity</a>, affirming the <a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">double-procession of the Holy Spirit</a> from the Father "and the Son" (<i>filioque</i>). At this synod fourteen canons of ecclesiastical discipline, and on the sacrament of marriage, were framed and a copy of the Acts was sent to Charlemagne. [Paulinus was once thought to have assisted at a Council of <a href="/wiki/Altinum" title="Altinum">Altinum</a>, but the theologian <a href="/wiki/Karl_Josef_von_Hefele" title="Karl Josef von Hefele">Karl Josef von Hefele</a> has provided evidence that such a council never occurred.]
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missus_dominicus"><i>Missus dominicus</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Missus dominicus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Always protesting the immunity of the Church from secular obligations and interference in his correspondences with Charlemagne, Paulinus, nonetheless, served as one of Charlemagne's <i><a href="/wiki/Missus_dominicus" title="Missus dominicus">missi dominici</a></i> at <a href="/wiki/Pistoia" title="Pistoia">Pistoia</a>, with Arno of Salzburg and ten other bishops, in 798. Afterwards he also traveled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> as legate to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_III" title="Pope Leo III">Pope Leo III</a>.
</p><p>Much of the activity of Paulinus as patriarch can be gathered from the <i>Sponsio Episcoporum ad S. Aquileiensem Sedem</i>.
</p><p>He died, revered as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Among his works are: <i>Libellus Sacrosyllabus contra Elipandum</i>, <i>Liber Exhortationis</i>, <i>Libri III contra Felicem</i>, and the protocol of the conference with Pepin and the bishops on the Danube, a work very important for the history of that expedition. Among his early works is a Commentary on the <a href="/wiki/Letter_to_the_Hebrews" class="mw-redirect" title="Letter to the Hebrews">Letter to the Hebrews</a> which, however, remains in <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscript</a> form.
</p><p>Paulinus was also a poet.  Among his better known poetical productions are his <i>Carmen de regula fidei</i>; a <i>Versus de Lazaro</i>; a <i>planctus</i> or <a href="/wiki/Elegy" title="Elegy">elegy</a> inspired by the death of his friend, Duke <a href="/wiki/Eric_of_Friuli" title="Eric of Friuli">Eric of Friuli</a> who was killed in <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Trsat" title="Siege of Trsat">Siege of Trsat</a>, 799; a <a href="/wiki/Rhythm" title="Rhythm">rhythm</a> on the destruction of Aquileia; and eight liturgical rhythms or hymns to be sung in his own church at <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Candlemas" title="Candlemas">Candlemas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saint_Mark" title="Feast of Saint Mark">feast of Saint Mark</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">feast of Saints Peter and Paul</a> (known by incipit <i>Felix per omnes festum mundi cardines</i>), and the feast of the dedication of his cathedral.
</p><p>Paulinus may be the author of the <i><a href="/wiki/Carmen_de_conversione_Saxonum" title="Carmen de conversione Saxonum">Carmen de conversione Saxonum</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Letters written by and to Paulinus are preserved in the <i><a href="/wiki/Monumenta_Germaniae_Historica" title="Monumenta Germaniae Historica">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologia Latina</a></i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After several <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translations</a> the relics of the patriarch were laid to rest under the altar of the crypt of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Basilica_of_Cividale_del_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Basilica of Cividale del Friuli (page does not exist)">basilica of Cividale del Friuli</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q3716343#sitelinks-wikipedia" class="extiw" title="d:Special:EntityPage/Q3716343"><span title="&quot;basilica of Cividale del Friuli&quot; in other languages">Wikidata</span></a>&#93;</span>.
</p><p>The first appearance of the name St. Paulinus in the Liturgy occurs in the "Litaniae" of <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a> of the 9th century. It appears also in the "Litaniae Carolinae," in the "Litaniae a S. Patribus constitutae," and finally in the manuscript "Litaniae of the Gertrudian" of the 10th century.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulinus_II_of_Aquileia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In manuscripts prior to the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Usuard" title="Martyrology of Usuard">Martyrology of Usuard</a></i> his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is recorded on 11 January. In the calendars of saints of the 13th, 14th and 15th centuries, used in the Church of Aquileia and <a href="/wiki/Cividale" class="mw-redirect" title="Cividale">Cividale</a>, his feast has a special rubric. Until the 16th century, the feast continued to be celebrated on 11 January, during the privileged <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">octave</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Epiphany_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Epiphany (Christian)">Epiphany</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Barbaro_(Patriarch_of_Aquileia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco Barbaro (Patriarch of Aquileia)">patriarch Francesco Barbaro</a> at the beginning of the 17th century translated the feast to 9 February. The Church of Cividale keeps his feast on 2 March.
</p><p>According to the most recent (2004) edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, Paulinus' feast day is assigned to the date of his death, 11 January.
</p>
