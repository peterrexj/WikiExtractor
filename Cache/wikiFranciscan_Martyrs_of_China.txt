<p>The <b>Martyr Saints of China</b> (<a href="/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters" title="Traditional Chinese characters">traditional Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh-Hant">中華殉道聖人</span>; <a href="/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters" title="Simplified Chinese characters">simplified Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh-Hans">中华殉道圣人</span>; <a href="/wiki/Pinyin" title="Pinyin">pinyin</a>&#58; <i><span lang="zh-Latn-pinyin">Zhōnghuá xùndào shèngrén</span></i>), or <b>Augustine Zhao Rong and his Companions</b>, are 120 <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. The 87 <a href="/wiki/Underground_church" title="Underground church">Chinese Catholics</a> and 33 Western missionaries<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> from the mid-17th century to 1930 were martyred because of their ministry and, in some cases, for their refusal to apostatize.
</p><p>Many died in the <a href="/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion" title="Boxer Rebellion">Boxer Rebellion</a>, in which anti-Western peasant rebels slaughtered 30,000 Chinese converts to Christianity along with missionaries and other foreigners.
</p><p>In the ordinary form of the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Rite">Latin Rite</a>, they are remembered with an <a href="/wiki/Optional_memorial" class="mw-redirect" title="Optional memorial">optional memorial</a> on 9 July.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_17th_and_18th_centuries">The 17th and 18th centuries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The 17th and 18th centuries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 15 January, 1648, during the <a href="/wiki/Manchu_people" title="Manchu people">Manchu</a> <a href="/wiki/Transition_from_Ming_to_Qing" title="Transition from Ming to Qing">Invasion</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ming_dynasty" title="Ming dynasty">Ming China</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manchu_people" title="Manchu people">Manchu</a> <a href="/wiki/Tatars" title="Tatars">Tatars</a>, invaded the region of <a href="/wiki/Fujian" title="Fujian">Fujian</a> and captured <a href="/wiki/Francis_Ferdinand_de_Capillas" title="Francis Ferdinand de Capillas">Francisco Fernández de Capillas</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> priest aged 40.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After having imprisoned and tortured him, they beheaded him while he recited with others the Sorrowful Mysteries of the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">Rosary</a>. Father de Capillas has since been recognised by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> of China.
</p><p>After the first wave of missionary activities in <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a> during the late <a href="/wiki/Ming_dynasty" title="Ming dynasty">Ming</a> to early <a href="/wiki/Qing_dynasty" title="Qing dynasty">Qing</a> dynasties, the Qing government officially banned Catholicism (Protestantism was considered outlawed by the same decree, as it was linked to Catholicism) in 1724 and lumped it together with other 'perverse sects and sinister doctrines' in Chinese folk religion.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While Catholicism continued to exist and increase many-fold in areas beyond the government's control (<a href="/wiki/Sichuan" title="Sichuan">Sichuan</a> notably), and many Chinese Christians fled the persecution to go to port cities in <a href="/wiki/Guangdong" title="Guangdong">Guangdong</a> or to <a href="/wiki/The_Philippines" class="mw-redirect" title="The Philippines">The Philippines</a>, where many translations of Christian works into Chinese occurred during this period, there were also many missionaries who broke the law and secretly entered the forbidden mainland territory.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They eluded Chinese patrol boats on the rivers and coasts; however, some of them were caught and put to death.
</p><p>Towards the middle of the 18th century five Spanish missionaries, who had carried out their activity between 1715 and 1747, were put to death as a result of a new wave of persecution that started in 1729 and broke out again in 1746. This was in the epoch of the <a href="/wiki/Yongzheng_Emperor" title="Yongzheng Emperor">Yongzheng Emperor</a> and of his successor, the <a href="/wiki/Qianlong_Emperor" title="Qianlong Emperor">Qianlong Emperor</a>.
</p>
<ol><li><a href="/wiki/Peter_Sanz" title="Peter Sanz">Peter Sanz</a>, O.P., bishop, was martyred on 26 May 1747, in <a href="/wiki/Fuzhou" title="Fuzhou">Fuzhou</a>.</li></ol>
<p>All four of the following were killed on 28 October 1748:
</p>
<ol><li>Francis Serrano, O.P., <a href="/wiki/Vicar_Apostolic" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar Apostolic">vicar apostolic</a> and bishop-elect</li>
<li>Joachim Royo, O.P., priest</li>
<li>John Alcober, O.P., priest</li>
<li>Francis Diaz, O.P., priest.</li></ol>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_19th-century_martyrdoms">Early 19th-century martyrdoms</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early 19th-century martyrdoms">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"/><table class="box-Unreferenced_section plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Unreferenced" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>does not <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources">cite</a> any <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">sources</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please help <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit">improve this section</a> by <a href="/wiki/Help:Referencing_for_beginners" title="Help:Referencing for beginners">adding citations to reliable sources</a>. Unsourced material may be challenged and <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Burden_of_evidence" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">removed</a>.</span>  <span class="date-container"><i>(<span class="date">September 2020</span>)</i></span><span class="hide-when-compact"><i> (<small><a href="/wiki/Help:Maintenance_template_removal" title="Help:Maintenance template removal">Learn how and when to remove this template message</a></small>)</i></span></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>A new period of persecution in regard to the Christian religion occurred in the 19th century.
</p><p>While Catholicism had been authorised by some Chinese emperors in the preceding centuries, the <a href="/wiki/Jiaqing_Emperor" title="Jiaqing Emperor">Jiaqing Emperor</a> published, instead, numerous and severe decrees against it. The first was issued in 1805. Two edicts of 1811 were directed against those among the Chinese who were studying to receive sacred orders, and against priests who were propagating the Christian religion. A decree of 1813 exonerated voluntary apostates from every chastisement – that is, Christians who spontaneously declared that they would abandon their faith – but all others were to be dealt with harshly.
</p><p>In this period the following underwent martyrdom:
</p>
<ol><li>Peter Wu, a Chinese lay <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a>. Born of a pagan family, he received <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> in 1796 and passed the rest of his life proclaiming the truth of the Christian religion. All attempts to make him apostatize were in vain. The sentence having been pronounced against him, he was strangled on 7 November 1814.</li>
<li>Joseph <a href="/wiki/Zhang_Dapeng" title="Zhang Dapeng">Zhang Dapeng</a>, a lay catechist, and a merchant. Baptized in 1800, he had become the heart of the mission in the city of Guiyang. He was imprisoned, and then strangled to death on 12 March 1815.</li></ol>
<p>Also in the same year, there came two other decrees, with which approval was given to the conduct of the <a href="/wiki/Viceroy_of_Sichuan" title="Viceroy of Sichuan">Viceroy of Sichuan</a> who had beheaded Monsignor Dufresse, of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a>, and some Chinese Christians. As a result, there was a worsening of the persecution.
</p><p>The following martyrs belong to this period:
</p>
<ol><li><a href="/wiki/Gabriel-Taurin_Dufresse" class="mw-redirect" title="Gabriel-Taurin Dufresse">Gabriel-Taurin Dufresse</a>, <a href="/wiki/M.E.P." class="mw-redirect" title="M.E.P.">M.E.P.</a>, Bishop. He was arrested on 18 May 1815, taken to <a href="/wiki/Chengdu" title="Chengdu">Chengdu</a>, condemned, and executed on 14 September 1815.</li>
<li>Augustine Zhao Rong, a Chinese <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocesan priest">diocesan priest</a>. Having first been one of the soldiers who had escorted Monsignor Dufresse from Chengdu to Beijing, he was moved by his patience and had then asked to be numbered among the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/neophyte" class="extiw" title="wikt:neophyte">neophytes</a>. Once baptized, he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> and then ordained a priest. Arrested, he was tortured and died in 1815.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>John da Triora, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a>, priest. Put in prison together with others in the summer of 1815, he was then condemned to death, and strangled on 7 February 1816.</li>
<li>Joseph Yuan, a Chinese diocesan priest. Having heard Monsignor Dufresse speak of the Christian faith, he was overcome by its beauty and then became an exemplary neophyte. Later, he was ordained a priest and, as such, was dedicated to evangelisation in various districts. He was arrested in August 1816, condemned to be strangled, and was killed in this way on 24 June 1817.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Paul_Liu_Hanzuo" title="Paul Liu Hanzuo">Paul Liu Hanzuo</a>, a Chinese diocesan priest, killed in 1819.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Francis_Regis_Clet" title="Francis Regis Clet">Francis Regis Clet</a> of the Congregation of the Mission (<a href="/wiki/Vincentian_Family" title="Vincentian Family">Vincentians</a>). After obtaining permission to go to the missions in China, he embarked for the Orient in 1791. Having reached there, for 30 years he spent a life of missionary sacrifice. Upheld by an untiring zeal, he evangelised three immense Chinese provinces: <a href="/wiki/Jiangxi" title="Jiangxi">Jiangxi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hubei" title="Hubei">Hubei</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hunan" title="Hunan">Hunan</a>. Betrayed by a Christian, he was arrested and thrown into prison where he underwent atrocious tortures. Following sentence by the <a href="/wiki/Jiaqing_Emperor" title="Jiaqing Emperor">Jiaqing Emperor</a> he was killed by strangling on 17 February 1820.</li>
<li>Thaddeus Liu, a Chinese diocesan priest. He refused to apostatize, saying that he was a priest and wanted to be faithful to the religion that he had preached. Condemned to death, he was strangled on 30 November 1823.</li>
<li>Peter Liu, a Chinese lay catechist. He was arrested in 1814 and condemned to exile in Tartary, where he remained for almost twenty years. Returning to his homeland he was again arrested, and was strangled on 17 May 1834.</li>
<li>Joachim Ho, a Chinese lay catechist. He was baptised at the age of about twenty years. In the great persecution of 1814 he had been taken with many others of the faithful and subjected to cruel torture. Sent into exile in <a href="/wiki/Tartary" title="Tartary">Tartary</a>, he remained there for almost twenty years. Returning to his homeland he was arrested again and refused to apostatize. Following that, and the death sentence having been confirmed by the Emperor, he was strangled on 9 July 1839.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/John_Gabriel_Perboyre" title="John Gabriel Perboyre">John Gabriel Perboyre</a>, C.M., entered the Vincentians as a high school student. The death of his younger brother, also a Vincentian priest, moved his superiors to allow him to take his brother's place, arriving in China in 1835. Despite poor health, he served the poverty-stricken residents of <a href="/wiki/Hubei" title="Hubei">Hubei</a>. Arrested during a revival of anti-Christian persecution, upon imperial edict, he was strangled to death in 1840.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Auguste_Chapdelaine" title="Auguste Chapdelaine">Augustus Chapdelaine</a>, <a href="/wiki/M.E.P." class="mw-redirect" title="M.E.P.">M.E.P.</a>, a priest of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Coutances" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Coutances">Diocese of Coutances</a>. He entered the Seminary of the Paris Foreign Missions Society, and embarked for China in 1852. He arrived in Guangxi at the end of 1854. Arrested in 1856, he was tortured, condemned to death in prison, and died in February 1856.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Bai_Xiaoman" title="Bai Xiaoman">Lawrence Bai Xiaoman</a>, a Chinese layman, and an unassuming worker. He joined Blessed Chapdelaine in the refuge that was given to the missionary and was arrested with him and brought before the tribunal. Nothing could make him renounce his religious beliefs. He was beheaded on 25 February 1856.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Agnes_Tsao_Kou_Ying" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Agnes Tsao Kou Ying">Agnes Cao Guiying</a>, a widow, born into an old Christian family. Being dedicated to the instruction of young girls who had recently been converted by Blessed Chapdelaine, she was arrested and condemned to death in prison. She was executed on 1 March 1856.</li></ol>

<li>Martin Wu Xuesheng, lay catechist,</li>
<li>John Zhang Tianshen, lay catechist,</li>
<li>John Chen Xianheng, lay catechist,</li>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="19th-century_social_and_political_developments">19th-century social and political developments</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: 19th-century social and political developments">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"/><table class="box-Original_research plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Original_research" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b4/Ambox_important.svg/40px-Ambox_important.svg.png" decoding="async" width="40" height="40" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b4/Ambox_important.svg/60px-Ambox_important.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/b/b4/Ambox_important.svg/80px-Ambox_important.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="40" data-file-height="40" /></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>possibly contains <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research" title="Wikipedia:No original research">original research</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit">improve it</a> by <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">verifying</a> the claims made and adding <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources#Inline_citations" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources">inline citations</a>. Statements consisting only of original research should be removed.</span>  <span class="date-container"><i>(<span class="date">September 2020</span>)</i></span><span class="hide-when-compact"><i> (<small><a href="/wiki/Help:Maintenance_template_removal" title="Help:Maintenance template removal">Learn how and when to remove this template message</a></small>)</i></span></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>In June 1840, <a href="/wiki/Qing_dynasty" title="Qing dynasty">Qing China</a> was forced to open the borders and afforded multiple concessions to European Christian missions after the <a href="/wiki/First_Opium_War" title="First Opium War">First Opium War</a>, including allowing the Chinese to follow the Catholic religion and restoring the property confiscated in 1724.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The 1844 treaty also allowed for missionaries to come to China, provided if they come to the treaty ports opened to Europeans.
</p><p>The subsequent Taiping Rebellion significantly worsened the image of Christianity in China. <a href="/wiki/Hong_Xiuquan" title="Hong Xiuquan">Hong Xiuquan</a>, the rebel leader, claimed to be a Christian and brother of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> who received a special mission from God to fight evil and usher in a period of peace. Hong and his followers achieved considerable success in taking control of a large territory, and destroyed many Buddhist and Taoist shrines, temples to local divinities and opposed Chinese folk religion.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The rebellion was one of the bloodiest armed conflicts in human history, accounting for an estimated number of 20–30&#160;million deaths. As missionary activities became increasingly associated with European imperialism, violence against missionaries arose.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1856, the death of missionary <a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_Chapedelaine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Augustus Chapedelaine (page does not exist)">Augustus Chapedelaine</a> trigged a French military expedition during the <a href="/wiki/Second_Opium_War" title="Second Opium War">Second Opium War</a>, which China lost. The resulting <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Tientsin" title="Treaty of Tientsin">Treaty of Tientsin</a>, granted Christian missionaries the freedom of movement throughout China and the right to land ownership.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As missionaries started to build churches or schools in offensive locations like old temples or near official buildings, tensions with the local Chinese population arose. The missionaries also abolished indigenous Chinese Catholic institutions that had survived the imperial ban.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In some regions, Catholic missionaries started "quarantining" new Chinese converts from the hostile social environment as they see the mission as "enclaves of Christianity in an alien world". The separation sparked conspiracy theories about the Christians and eventually accumulated in <a href="/wiki/Tianjin_massacre" class="mw-redirect" title="Tianjin massacre">the massacre</a> of 60 people in a Catholic orphanage.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In comparison, Protestant missions were less secretive and treated more favorably by the authorities.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chinese literati and gentry produced a pamphlet attacking Christian beliefs as socially subversive and irrational. Incendiary handbills and fliers distributed to crowds were also produced, and were linked to outbreaks of violence against Christians. Sometimes, no such official incitement was needed in order to provoke the populace to attack Christians. For example, among the Hakka people in southeastern China, Christian missionaries frequently flouted village customs that were linked with local religions, including refusal to take part in communal prayers for rain (and because the missionaries benefitted from the rain, it was argued that they had to do their part in the prayers) and refusing to contribute funds to operas for Chinese gods (these same gods honoured in these village operas were the same spirits that the Boxers called to invoke in themselves, during the later rebellion).<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Catholic missions offered protection to those who came to them, including criminals, fugitives from the law, and rebels against the government; this also led to hostile attitudes developing against the missions by the government.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Boxer_Rebellion">Boxer Rebellion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Boxer Rebellion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>And so passed an era of expansion in the Christian missions, with the exception of the period in which they were struck by the uprising by the "<a href="/wiki/Society_for_Justice_and_Harmony" class="mw-redirect" title="Society for Justice and Harmony">Society for Justice and Harmony</a>" (commonly known as the "Boxers"). This occurred at the beginning of the 20th century and caused the shedding of the blood of many Christians.
</p><p>It is known<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> that mingled in this rebellion were all the secret societies and the accumulated and repressed hatred against foreigners in the last decades of the 19th century, because of the political and social changes following the <a href="/wiki/Second_Opium_War" title="Second Opium War">Second Opium War</a> and the imposition of the so-called <a href="/wiki/Unequal_treaty" title="Unequal treaty">unequal treaties</a> on China by the Western Powers.
</p><p>Very different, however, was the motive for the persecution of the missionaries, even though they were of European nationalities. Their slaughter was brought about solely on religious grounds. They were killed for the same reason as the Chinese faithful who had become Christians. Reliable historical documents provide evidence of the anti-Christian hatred which spurred the Boxers to massacre the missionaries and the Christians of the area who had adhered to their teaching. In this regard, an edict<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> was issued on 1 July 1900, which, in substance, said that the time of good relations with European missionaries and their Christians was now past: that the former must be repatriated at once and the faithful forced to apostatize, on penalty of death.
</p><p>Following the failure of the Boxer Rebellion, China was further subject to Western <a href="/wiki/Spheres_of_influence" class="mw-redirect" title="Spheres of influence">spheres of influence</a>, which in turn led to a booming conversion period in the following decades. The Chinese developed respect for the moral level that Christians maintained in their hospital and schools.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindenfeld_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindenfeld-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The continuing association between Western imperialism in China and missionary efforts nevertheless continued to fuel hostilities against missions and Christianity in China. All missions were banned in China by the new communist regime after the outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/Korean_War" title="Korean War">Korean War</a> in 1950, and officially continue to be legally outlawed to the present.
</p><p>As a result, the martyrdom took place of several missionaries and many Chinese who can be grouped together as follows:
</p><p>a) Martyrs of Shanxi, killed on 9 July 1900 (known as the <a href="/wiki/Taiyuan_massacre" title="Taiyuan massacre">Taiyuan massacre</a>), who were <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Friars_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan Friars Minor">Franciscan Friars Minor</a>:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Gregorio_Grassi" title="Gregorio Grassi">Gregorio Grassi</a>, bishop</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Francis_Fogolla" title="Francis Fogolla">Francis Fogolla</a>, bishop</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elias_Facchini&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Elias Facchini (page does not exist)">Elias Facchini</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lie_Facchini" class="extiw" title="fr:Élie Facchini">fr</a>&#93;</span>, priest</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9odoric_Balat&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Théodoric Balat (page does not exist)">Théodoric Balat</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9odoric_Balat" class="extiw" title="fr:Théodoric Balat">fr</a>&#93;</span>, priest</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Bauer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Andrew Bauer (page does not exist)">Andrew Bauer</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Bauer_(saint)" class="extiw" title="fr:André Bauer (saint)">fr</a>&#93;</span>, religious brother;</li></ul>
<p>b) Martyrs of Southern Hunan, who were also Franciscan Friars Minor:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Fantosati&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Anthony Fantosati (page does not exist)">Anthony Fantosati</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Fantosati" class="extiw" title="fr:Anthony Fantosati">fr</a>&#93;</span>, bishop (martyred on 7 July 1900)</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Mary_Gambaro&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Joseph Mary Gambaro (page does not exist)">Joseph Mary Gambaro</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mary_Gambaro" class="extiw" title="fr:Joseph Mary Gambaro">fr</a>&#93;</span> priest (martyred on 7 July 1900)</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cesidio_Giacomantonio&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cesidio Giacomantonio (page does not exist)">Cesidio Giacomantonio</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9side_Giacomantonio" class="extiw" title="fr:Céside Giacomantonio">fr</a>&#93;</span>, priest (martyred on 4 July 1900)</li></ul>
<p>To the martyred Franciscans of the First Order were added seven <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Missionaries_of_Mary" title="Franciscan Missionaries of Mary">Franciscan Missionaries of Mary</a>, of whom three were French, two Italian, one Belgian, and one Dutch:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Mary_Hermina_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary Hermina of Jesus">Mary Hermina of Jesus</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/In_saeculo" class="mw-redirect" title="In saeculo">in saeculo</a></i></span>: Irma Grivot)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Marie_de_la_Paix_Giuliani" title="Marie de la Paix Giuliani">Marie de la Paix Giuliani</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Mary Ann Giuliani)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Maria_Chiara_Nanetti" title="Maria Chiara Nanetti">Maria Chiara Nanetti</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Clelia Nanetti)<sup id="cite_ref-fmm_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmm-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Marie_of_Saint_Natalie" title="Marie of Saint Natalie">Marie of Saint Natalie</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Joan Mary Kerguin)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Marie_of_Saint_Just" title="Marie of Saint Just">Marie of Saint Just</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Ann Moreau)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Marie-Adolphine" title="Marie-Adolphine">Marie-Adolphine</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Ann Dierk)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Amandina_of_Schakkebroek" title="Amandina of Schakkebroek">Mary Amandina</a> (<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">in saeculo</i></span>: Paula Jeuris)</li></ul>
<p>Of the martyrs belonging to the Franciscan family, there were also eleven Secular Franciscans, all Chinese:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Zhang_Huan&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John Zhang Huan (page does not exist)">John Zhang Huan</a>, seminarian,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Patrick_Dong_Bodi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Patrick Dong Bodi (page does not exist)">Patrick Dong Bodi</a>, seminarian,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Wang_Rui&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John Wang Rui (page does not exist)">John Wang Rui</a>, seminarian,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Zhang_Zhihe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Philip Zhang Zhihe (page does not exist)">Philip Zhang Zhihe</a>, seminarian,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Zhang_Jingguang&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John Zhang Jingguang (page does not exist)">John Zhang Jingguang</a>, seminarian,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Shen_Jihe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thomas Shen Jihe (page does not exist)">Thomas Shen Jihe</a>, layman and a manservant,</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Simon_Qin_Chunfu" class="mw-redirect" title="Simon Qin Chunfu">Simon Qin Chunfu</a>, lay catechist,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wu_Anbang&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter Wu Anbang (page does not exist)">Peter Wu Anbang</a>, layman,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Zhang_Rong&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Francis Zhang Rong (page does not exist)">Francis Zhang Rong</a>, layman and a farmer,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthew_Feng_De&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Matthew Feng De (page does not exist)">Matthew Feng De</a>, layman and neophyte,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Zhang_Banniu&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter Zhang Banniu (page does not exist)">Peter Zhang Banniu</a>, layman and labourer.</li></ul>
<p>To these are joined a number of Chinese lay faithful:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Yan_Guodong&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James Yan Guodong (page does not exist)">James Yan Guodong</a>, farmer,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Zhao_Quanxin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James Zhao Quanxin (page does not exist)">James Zhao Quanxin</a>, manservant,</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wang_Erman&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter Wang Erman (page does not exist)">Peter Wang Erman</a>, cook.</li></ul>
<p>When the uprising of the Boxers, which had begun in Shandong and then spread through Shanxi and Hunan, also reached South-Eastern <a href="/wiki/Tcheli" class="mw-redirect" title="Tcheli">Tcheli</a> (currently named <a href="/wiki/Hebei" title="Hebei">Hebei</a>), which was then the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicariate_of_Xianxian" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicariate of Xianxian">Apostolic Vicariate of Xianxian</a>, in the care of the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuits</a>, the Christians killed could be counted in thousands. Among these were four French Jesuit missionaries and at least 52 Chinese lay Christians: men, women and children&#160;– the oldest of them being 79 years old, while the youngest were aged only nine years. All suffered martyrdom in the month of July 1900. Many of them were killed in the church in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Zhujiahe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Zhujiahe (page does not exist)">Zhujiahe</a> Village, in which they were taking refuge and where they were in prayer together with the first two of the missionaries listed below:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leo_Mangin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Leo Mangin (page does not exist)">Leo Mangin</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on-Ignace_Mangin" class="extiw" title="fr:Léon-Ignace Mangin">fr</a>&#93;</span>, S.J., priest</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Paul_Denn" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul Denn">Paul Denn</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Denn" class="extiw" title="fr:Paul Denn">fr</a>&#93;</span>, S.J., priest</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9my_Isor%C3%A9" title="Rémy Isoré">Rémy Isoré</a>, S.J., priest</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Modeste_Andlauer" class="mw-redirect" title="Modeste Andlauer">Modeste Andlauer</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modeste_Andlauer" class="extiw" title="fr:Modeste Andlauer">fr</a>&#93;</span>, S.J., priest</li></ul>
<p><br />
Besides all those already mentioned who were killed by the Boxers, there were the following:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Alberic_Crescitelli" title="Alberic Crescitelli">Alberic Crescitelli</a>, a priest of the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Institute_for_Foreign_Missions" title="Pontifical Institute for Foreign Missions">Pontifical Institute for Foreign Missions</a> of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, who carried out his ministry in Southern <a href="/wiki/Shaanxi" title="Shaanxi">Shaanxi</a> and was martyred on 21 July 1900</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_martyrs">Later martyrs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyr_Saints_of_China&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Later martyrs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Some years later, members of the <a href="/wiki/Salesian_Society_of_St_John_Bosco" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesian Society of St John Bosco">Salesian Society of St John Bosco</a> were added to the considerable number of martyrs recorded above:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Luigi_Versiglia" title="Luigi Versiglia">Luigi Versiglia</a>, bishop</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Callistus_Caravario" title="Callistus Caravario">Callistus Caravario</a>, priest</li></ul>
<p>They were killed together on 25 February 1930, at Li-Thau-Tseul.
