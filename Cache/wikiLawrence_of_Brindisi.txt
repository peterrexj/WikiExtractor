<p><b>Lawrence of Brindisi</b> (22 July 1559 &#8211; 22 July 1619), born <b>Giulio Cesare Russo</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and a theologian as well as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</a>. An accomplished linguist, in addition to his native Italian, Lawrence could read and speak Latin, Hebrew, Greek, German, Czech, Spanish, and French fluently. Lawrence was ordained a priest at the age of 23. Lawrence was beatified on 1 June 1783 and canonized as a saint on 8 December 1881.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_of_Brindisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Giulio Cesare Russo was born in <a href="/wiki/Brindisi" title="Brindisi">Brindisi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>, to a family of <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venetian</a> merchants. The Conventuals of Brindisi were entrusted with his education. Showing an early gift for oratory, he was always the one chosen to address, in accordance with the Italian custom, a short sermon to his compatriots on the Infant Jesus during the Christmas festivities.<sup id="cite_ref-Candide_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Candide-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was twelve when his father died, and continued his education at Saint Mark's College in Venice under the supervision of one of his uncles. 
</p><p>Cesare joined the Capuchins in <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a> as Brother Lawrence. He received further instruction from the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Padua" title="University of Padua">University of Padua</a>. An accomplished linguist, in addition to his native Italian, Lawrence could read and speak Latin, Hebrew, Greek, German, Bohemian, Spanish, and French fluently. While  still a deacon he preached the Lenten sermons in Venice. Lawrence was ordained a priest at the age of 23.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of thirty-one, Lawrence was elected superior of the Capuchin Franciscan province of Tuscany.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1596 he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Definitor" title="Definitor">definitor</a> general in Rome for the Capuchins. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> assigned him the task of preaching to the <a href="/wiki/Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Jew">Jews</a> in the city. His knowledge of the Hebrew language was of great help in this regard. Beginning in 1599, Lawrence established Capuchin monasteries in modern Germany and Austria, furthering the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a> and bringing many <a href="/wiki/Protestants" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestants">Protestants</a> back to the Catholic faith.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He also founded friaries in  Vienna, Prague, and Graz.
</p><p>It was on the occasion of the foundation of the convent of Prague in 1601 that he was named the <a href="/wiki/Empire" title="Empire">imperial</a> <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> for the army of <a href="/wiki/Rudolph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Rudolph II, Holy Roman Emperor">Rudolph II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, and successfully recruited <a href="/wiki/Philippe_Emmanuel,_Duke_of_Merc%C5%93ur" title="Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur">Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur</a>, to help fight against the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Turks" title="Ottoman Turks">Ottoman Turks</a>. He then led the army during the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r" title="Siege of Székesfehérvár">siege of Székesfehérvár</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>, armed only with a <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Candide_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Candide-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1602, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_friars" class="mw-redirect" title="Capuchin friars">Capuchin friars</a>, at that time the highest office in the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was elected again in 1605, but refused the office. Until his death, he was the best adviser of his successors. He entered the service of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>, becoming <a href="/wiki/Papal_nuncio" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal nuncio">papal nuncio</a> to <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>. After serving as nuncio to Spain, he retired to a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in 1618. He was recalled as a special envoy to the King of Spain regarding the actions of the <a href="/wiki/Viceroy" title="Viceroy">Viceroy</a> of Naples in 1619, and after finishing his mission, died on his 60th birthday in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>.
</p><p>He was entombed at the Poor Clares' Convento de la Anunciada (Convent of the Annunciation) in <a href="/wiki/Villafranca_del_Bierzo" title="Villafranca del Bierzo">Villafranca del Bierzo</a>, Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_of_Brindisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1783 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius VI">Pius VI</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1881 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1959 <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> declared him Doctor of the Church with the title “Apostolic Doctor”.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_of_Brindisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Lawrence of Brindisi Complete Works were published in 15 volumes, in a critical edition, between 1926 and 1956. They comprise:
</p>
<ul><li><i>Mariale</i></li>
<li><i>Lutheranismi hypotyposis</i></li>
<li><i>Explanatio in Genesim</i></li>
<li><i>Quadragesimale primum</i></li>
<li><i>Quadragesimale secundum</i></li>
<li><i>Quadragesimale tertium</i></li>
<li><i>Quadragesimale quartum</i></li>
<li><i>Adventus</i></li>
<li><i>Dominicalia</i></li>
<li><i>Sanctorale</i></li>
<li><i>Sermones de tempore</i></li></ul>
<p>His original manuscripts comprise 13 volumes in parchment and are located at the Archivio dei Cappuccini di Mestre.
</p>
