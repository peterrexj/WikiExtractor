<p><b>Florian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Florianus</i>; 250 – <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 304 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> holy man and the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chimney_sweep" title="Chimney sweep">chimney sweeps</a>; soapmakers, and <a href="/wiki/Firefighting" title="Firefighting">firefighters</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 4 May. Florian is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, the city of <a href="/wiki/Linz" title="Linz">Linz</a>, Austria, and <a href="/wiki/Upper_Austria" title="Upper Austria">Upper Austria</a>, jointly with <a href="/wiki/Leopold_III,_Margrave_of_Austria" title="Leopold III, Margrave of Austria">Leopold III, Margrave of Austria</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Florian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Florian was born around 250 AD in the ancient Roman city of Aelium Cetium, present-day <a href="/wiki/Sankt_P%C3%B6lten" title="Sankt Pölten">Sankt Pölten</a>, Austria. He joined the Roman Army and advanced in the ranks,<sup id="cite_ref-parish_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> rising to commander of the imperial army in the Roman province of <a href="/wiki/Noricum" title="Noricum">Noricum</a>. In addition to his military duties, he was also responsible for organizing and leading firefighting brigades. Florian organized and trained an elite group of soldiers whose sole duty was to fight fires.<sup id="cite_ref-brookline_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brookline-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> of Christians, reports reached <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> that Florian was not enforcing the proscriptions against Christians in his territory. Aquilinus was sent to investigate these reports. When Aquilinus ordered Florian to offer sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a> in accordance with <a href="/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Rome" title="Religion in ancient Rome">Roman religion</a>, Florian refused. Florian was sentenced to be burned at the stake. Standing on the funeral pyre, Florian is reputed to have challenged the Roman soldiers to light the fire, saying "If you wish to know that I am not afraid of your torture, light the fire, and in the name of the Lord I will climb onto it."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Apprehensive of his words, the soldiers did not burn Florian, but executed him by drowning him in the <a href="/wiki/Enns_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Enns River">Enns River</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Millstone" title="Millstone">millstone</a> tied around his neck instead.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-brookline_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brookline-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His body was later retrieved by Christians and buried at an Augustinian monastery near <a href="/wiki/Lorch,_Austria" title="Lorch, Austria">Lorch</a>. Later a woman named Valeria had a vision in which she saw him; Florian, in this vision, declared his intent to be buried in a more appropriate location.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Florian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Florian is very widely venerated in <a href="/wiki/Central_Europe" title="Central Europe">Central Europe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austrian</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Florian" title="Sankt Florian">Sankt Florian</a> is named after him. According to legend, his body was interred at <a href="/wiki/St._Florian_Monastery" title="St. Florian Monastery">St. Florian Monastery</a>, around which the town grew up.
</p><p>Florian was adopted as patron saint of Poland in 1184, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Pope Lucius III</a> consented to the request of Prince Casimir II to send relics of Florian to that country.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Kraków thus claims some of his relics.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A statue of Florian by Josef Josephu was unveiled in Vienna in 1935. It stood at the main <a href="/wiki/Fire_station" title="Fire station">firehouse</a> of Vienna, in the city's main square, Am Hof. After the firehouse was bombed in 1945 during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> the statue was moved on to the Fire Brigade Museum (<i>Wiener Feuerwehrmuseum</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-publicsafety.net_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-publicsafety.net-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Seeking the <a href="/wiki/Saint%27s_name" title="Saint&#39;s name">sponsorship of a helpful saint</a> was and still is a part of the namegiving practice in Catholic areas. In the southern, Catholic, parts of the <a href="/wiki/German_Empire" title="German Empire">German Empire</a> (mainly present <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>), peasants regularly have used the name, Florian, as one of the given names for at least one of their male children: to secure the saint's patronage against fire. Hence the given name is still widespread in these areas.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In Austria and Germany, fire services use <i>Florian</i> in <a href="/wiki/Radio_communications" class="mw-redirect" title="Radio communications">radio communications</a> as universal <a href="/wiki/Call_sign" title="Call sign">call sign</a> for <a href="/wiki/Fire_station" title="Fire station">fire stations</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fire_apparatus" class="mw-redirect" title="Fire apparatus">fire trucks</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Call_sign" title="Call sign">call sign</a> <i>Florentine</i> for firefighting-related, handheld <a href="/wiki/Radio_communications" class="mw-redirect" title="Radio communications">radio equipment</a> is also derived, somewhat inaccurately, from that usage.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Florian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Florian is a patron saint of Upper Austria and Poland; also firefighters, chimneysweeps, and brewers. Florian is associated with brewers because of a legendary incident in which he miraculously stopped a fire with a single pitcher of water. That is why he is often depicted holding a pitcher. Florian is also the patron of chimney sweeps, Austria, Poland and soap boilers.
</p><p>Florian is invoked against fires, floods, lightning, and the pains of <a href="/wiki/Purgatory" title="Purgatory">Purgatory</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A famous <a href="/wiki/St._Florian%27s_Church" title="St. Florian&#39;s Church">St. Florian's Church</a> is located in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, Poland. His <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> has been particularly intense since 1528, when a fire burned the neighborhood without destroying the church.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_contemporary_culture">In contemporary culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Florian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In contemporary culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The "Florian Principle" (known in German language areas as "Sankt-Florians-Prinzip") is named after a somewhat ironic prayer to Saint Florian: "<i>O heiliger Sankt Florian, verschon' mein Haus, zünd' and're an</i>," equivalent to "O Holy St. Florian, please spare my house, set fire to another one". This saying is used in German much like the English "<a href="/wiki/Not_in_my_back_yard" class="mw-redirect" title="Not in my back yard">not in my back yard</a>", when the speaker wants to point out that some person tries to get out of an unpleasant situation by an action that will put others in that very same situation.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Germany, "Florian" is the call sign for fire engines.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The protagonist in <a href="/wiki/Felix_Salten" title="Felix Salten">Felix Salten's</a> novel <i><a href="/wiki/Florian:_The_Emperor%E2%80%99s_Stallion" class="mw-redirect" title="Florian: The Emperor’s Stallion">Florian: The Emperor’s Stallion</a></i> was named after Florian, as the animal was born on 4 May 1901 in <a href="/wiki/Lipizza" class="mw-redirect" title="Lipizza">Lipizza</a>, Austria.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Alfred_Schnittke" title="Alfred Schnittke">Alfred Schnittke</a>'s Symphony No. 2, is subtitled "St. Florian".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In multiple cities across Slovakia, streets are named after Saint Florian, often in correlation with local fire departments. Florian Street (<i>Floriánska ulica</i>) occurs in historic boroughs of major cities: currently in <a href="/wiki/Ko%C5%A1ice" title="Košice">Košice</a> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Town,_Ko%C5%A1ice" title="Old Town, Košice">Staré Mesto</a>) and also formerly in <a href="/wiki/Bratislava" title="Bratislava">Bratislava</a> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Town,_Bratislava" title="Old Town, Bratislava">Staré Mesto</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
