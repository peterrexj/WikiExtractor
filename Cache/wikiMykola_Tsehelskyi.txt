<p><b>Mykola Tsehelskyi</b> (Ukrainian: Мико́ла Цеге́льський, alternative English spelling: Nicholas), December 17, 1896- May 25, 1951, was a Ukrainian <a href="/wiki/Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Greek Catholic Church">Greek Catholic</a> priest.<sup id="cite_ref-:04_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:04-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The Church considers him a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> due to his refusal to convert under duress.
</p><p>Tsehelskyi was born on December 17, 1896, in the village of Strusiv, <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Region</a> in what has then <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a>. In 1923, he graduated from the <a href="/wiki/Lviv_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Lviv University">Lviv University</a> theology faculty. On April 5, 1925, Sheptyskyi ordained Tsehelskyi to the priesthood.  He was assigned to a parish.<sup id="cite_ref-:04_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:04-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the end of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> authorities started pressuring Tsehelskyi  to convert to the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>, threatening to beat him.  He refused to convert. On October 28, 1946, Tsehelskyi was arrested and on January 27, 1947, was sent to prison in the <a href="/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic" title="Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic">Moldova SSR</a>.   After Tsehelskyi's imprisonment, his family was exiled to <a href="/wiki/Chita,_Zabaykalsky_Krai" title="Chita, Zabaykalsky Krai">Chita</a> in Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-:04_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:04-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in prison, Tsehelskyi wrote to his wife, saying  "My dearest wife, the feast of the Dormition was our 25th wedding anniversary. I recall fondly our family life together, and every day in my dreams I am with you and the children, and this makes me happy... I  happy give a fatherly kiss to all their foreheads and I hope to live honestly, behaving blamelessly, keeping far from everything that is foul. I pray for this most of all. "<sup id="cite_ref-:04_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:04-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On May 25, 1951, Tsehelskyi died in prison in Moldova and was buried in the camp cemetery.
</p>
