					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_of_Cyprus&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Cyprus">Paul of Cyprus</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><a href="/wiki/Mar" title="Mar">Mar</a> <b>Paul</b> (<a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> <i>Pawla</i>), usually known as <b>Paul of Edessa</b> or <b>Paul of Cyprus</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Syriac Orthodox">Syriac Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Edessa" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Edessa">metropolitan of Edessa</a> who was forced to abandon his see between about 602 and 629, when it fell under the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_Empire" title="Sasanian Empire">Sasanian Empire</a>. He was an important translator of <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> theological works into Syriac.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrock2011_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock2011-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He should not be confused with the Bishop <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Edessa" title="Paul of Edessa">Paul of Edessa</a> who died in 526.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Jacob_of_Edessa" title="Jacob of Edessa">Jacob of Edessa</a>, Paul fled to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Egypt" title="Roman Egypt">Egypt</a> with many other bishops to escape the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sasanian_War_of_602%E2%80%93628" title="Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628">Sasanian invasion and occupation of Mesopotamia</a> precipitated by the defection and rebellion of <a href="/wiki/Narses_(magister_militum_per_Orientem)" title="Narses (magister militum per Orientem)">Narses</a>, governor of Mesopotamia, who seized <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a> with Sasanian support in 602–03.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He then fled Egypt for <a href="/wiki/Roman_Cyprus" title="Roman Cyprus">Cyprus</a> to escape the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Egypt" title="Sasanian conquest of Egypt">Sasanian conquest of Egypt</a> in 618–621. At the time of his translation work on Cyprus he was described as an <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">archimandrite</a> (supervisor of abbots).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Paul probably came out of the monastic complex of <a href="/wiki/Qenneshre" title="Qenneshre">Qenneshre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> A scribal notation in a manuscript dated to 675, refers to a Syriac version of the <i><a href="/wiki/Gloria_in_excelsis" class="mw-redirect" title="Gloria in excelsis">Gloria in excelsis</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> as "translated by Paul, according to the tradition of Qenneshre".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETannous201394,_n._40_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETannous201394,_n._40-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 623 or 624, during his time on Cyprus, Paul revised an earlier translation of the complete <i>Discourses</i> of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus" title="Gregory of Nazianzus">Gregory of Nazianzus</a> and the <i>Commentaries</i> of <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Nonnos" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudo-Nonnos">Pseudo-Nonnos</a>. His edition survives in its entirety.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrock2011_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock2011-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On Cyprus, he also translated a collection of 295 hymns (<i>maʿnyoto</i>, <a href="/wiki/Antiphon" title="Antiphon">antiphons</a>) by <a href="/wiki/Severus_of_Antioch" title="Severus of Antioch">Severus of Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_bar_Aphtonia" title="John bar Aphtonia">John bar Aphtonia</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Psaltes" title="John Psaltes">John Psaltes</a> and others.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJullienNicholson2018-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrock2011_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock2011-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETannous2018172_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETannous2018172-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This translation, made between 619 and 629, was revised by Jacob of Edessa in 675 to make it more literal.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks19095-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to Jacob's note of explanation, the hymns were "translated from the Greek tongue into the Edessene or Syriac speech by the saintly Mar Paul who was bishop of the city of Edessa, while he was in the island of Cyprus, in flight from the Persians"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrooks1911800_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks1911800-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paul may also be the translator of the famous pericope in the <i><a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">Gospel of John</a></i> (7:50–8:12) concerning <a href="/wiki/Jesus_and_the_woman_taken_in_adultery" title="Jesus and the woman taken in adultery">the woman taken in adultery</a>, which is not found in the earliest <a href="/wiki/List_of_Syriac_New_Testament_manuscripts" title="List of Syriac New Testament manuscripts">Syriac New Testament manuscripts</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Peshitta" title="Peshitta">Peshitta</a> and <a href="/wiki/%E1%B8%A4arqlean_Version" class="mw-redirect" title="Ḥarqlean Version">Ḥarqlean</a> versions. It was attributed to a certain "Abbas Pawla" (Abbot Paul), which is generally assumed to be Paul of Edessa, although <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tella" title="Paul of Tella">Paul of Tella</a>, a contemporary and also an exile from the Sasanian invasion, has been suggested.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrock2011_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock2011-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At least one Syriac Orthodox liturgical calendar commemorates Paul on 23 August as "Paul, bishop of Edessa, who translated the books".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrock2011_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrock2011-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
