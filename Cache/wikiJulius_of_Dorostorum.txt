<p><b>Saint Julius the Veteran</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Iulius</i>), also known as <i>Julius of <a href="/wiki/Silistra" title="Silistra">Durostorum</a></i>, is a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 27 May.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_the_Veteran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Julius of Durostorum was born to pagan parents.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The date of Julius' conversion to Christianity is unknown. Julius served as a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> soldier for 27 years first as a conscript, then returning as a [veteran], totaling seven military campaigns in total. Given the years and locations in which Julius served, Rev. Herbert Musurillo, S.J. writes that Julius likely served in the <a href="/wiki/Legio_XI_Claudia" title="Legio XI Claudia">Legio XI Claudia</a>. Julius was Christian his entire military career.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In accordance with the fourth edict of the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian Persecution">Diocletian Persecution</a>, Julius was brought to trial before the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a>, Maximus, after being arrested by Maximus' staff soldiers for refusing to make a public sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a>. Upon hearing of his military service, Maximus complimented Julius for being a wise and serious man. In gratitude for his military service, Maximus proposed Julius a bargain: if Julius offered the public sacrifice, Maximus would accept blame for the sin of the sacrifice and would give Julius freedom, a ten-year bonus payment, and immunity from future charges. Julius declined the offer and was sentenced to death. Julius was killed by the sword in Durostorum, the Roman camp in <a href="/wiki/Moesia_Inferior" class="mw-redirect" title="Moesia Inferior">Moesia Inferior</a> (modern Silistra,  <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>) sometime between January and March of 304. Julius is widely considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
