<div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Polish Roman Catholic nun and martyr</div>
<p><b>Maria Julia Rodzińska</b>, <i>Stanisława Maria Józefa Rodzińska</i> (16 March 1899 – 20 February 1945) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> and is Blessed of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julia_Rodzi%C5%84ska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Julia Rodzińska was born on 16 March 1899 in <a href="/wiki/Nawojowa" title="Nawojowa">Nawojowa</a>. She was one of five children of the organist Michał and Marianna née Sekuła.<sup id="cite_ref-Julia_Rodzińska_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Julia_Rodzińska-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Rodzińska orphaned at the age of ten and became a ward of the Dominican Sisters convent in Nawojowa.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There she finished school and then started her studies in the Teachers’ Seminar in <a href="/wiki/Nowy_S%C4%85cz" title="Nowy Sącz">Nowy Sącz</a> but didn't complete them because she began her religious formation in Wielowieś.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At the age of 17, she entered the Congregation of the Sisters of St. Dominika in Tarnobrzeg-Wielowieś and there (5 August 1924) made her religious vows and completed her interrupted education.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a qualified teacher, she carried out her ministry in Mielżyn, <a href="/wiki/Rava-Ruska" title="Rava-Ruska">Rawa Ruska</a> and Vilnius (from 13 December 1922, for 22 years).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> From 1934, she was the head of a house in Vilnius and ran an orphanage.<sup id="cite_ref-Julia_Rodzińska_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Julia_Rodzińska-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After the outbreak of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, she secretly taught Polish language, history and religion, and conducted humanitarian activities.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> On 12 July 1943, Rodzińska was jailed by the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Luki%C5%A1k%C4%97s_Prison" title="Lukiškės Prison">Łukiszki prison</a> in Vilnius.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In a year, Rodzińska was sent to German concentration camp <a href="/wiki/Stutthof_concentration_camp" title="Stutthof concentration camp">Stutthof</a>, registered as number 40992.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There she was subjected to torture, isolation and humiliation.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_beatification">Death and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julia_Rodzi%C5%84ska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rodzińska died of exhaustion and disease on 20 February 1945 in Stutthof, two months before the concentration camp was liberated by the <a href="/wiki/Red_Army" title="Red Army">Red Army</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her beatification process began on 26 January 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 1999, she was proclaimed blessed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in a group of 108 blessed martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Honors">Honors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julia_Rodzi%C5%84ska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Honors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rodzińska is the patron of the primary school Blessed Julia Rodzińska in Nawojowa<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and kindergarten of Blessed Julia Rodzińska in Poznań. In 2019, on the occasion of 120th anniversary of Rodzińska's birthday her family home in Nawojowa was open for visitors.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
