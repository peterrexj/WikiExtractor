<p><b>Jerzy Popiełuszko</b> (<small>Polish pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pl-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Polish" title="Help:IPA/Polish">[ˈjɛʐɨ popʲɛˈwuʂkɔ]</a></span> born Alfons Popiełuszko; 14 September 1947&#8211;19 October 1984<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">Roman Catholic priest</a> who became associated with the opposition <a href="/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union)" title="Solidarity (Polish trade union)">Solidarity</a> trade union in <a href="/wiki/Communist_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist Poland">communist Poland</a>. He was murdered in 1984 by three agents of <span title="Polish-language text"><i lang="pl"><a href="/wiki/S%C5%82u%C5%BCba_Bezpiecze%C5%84stwa" class="mw-redirect" title="Służba Bezpieczeństwa">Służba Bezpieczeństwa</a></i></span> (Security Service of the Ministry of Internal Affairs), who were shortly thereafter tried and convicted of the murder.
</p><p>He has been recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, and was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 6 June 2010 by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> on behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A miracle attributed to his intercession and required for his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> is now under investigation.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_priesthood">Early life and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_(07).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg/220px-Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg/330px-Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg/440px-Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_%2807%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1586" data-file-height="1060" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Popieluszko_celebration_Europeana_(07).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Popiełuszko meeting with workers at the <a href="/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_Shipyard" title="Gdańsk Shipyard">Gdańsk Shipyard</a></div></div></div>
<p>Popiełuszko was born on 14 September 1947 in <a href="/wiki/Okopy,_Podlaskie_Voivodeship" title="Okopy, Podlaskie Voivodeship">Okopy</a> near <a href="/wiki/Suchowola" title="Suchowola">Suchowola</a>. After finishing school, he attended the priests' seminary at <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a>. In 1966–1968, he served his army duties in a special force in <a href="/wiki/Bartoszyce" title="Bartoszyce">Bartoszyce</a>, aimed at keeping young men from becoming priests.<sup id="cite_ref-pr_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pr-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This treatment had no effect on Popiełuszko's beliefs, as, after finishing his army service, he continued his studies, however, the repeated punishments for his resistance affected his health for the rest of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-pr_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pr-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Popiełuszko was ordained a priest by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Wyszy%C5%84ski" title="Stefan Wyszyński">Stefan Wyszyński</a> in May 1972.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As a young priest he first served in <a href="/wiki/Z%C4%85bki" title="Ząbki">Ząbki</a> near Warsaw in 1972–1975.<sup id="cite_ref-pnz_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pnz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Afterwards, he served in parishes in Warsaw, which consisted of the common people as well as students.<sup id="cite_ref-http&#58;//ststanskostka.org_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-http://ststanskostka.org-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1981, Jerzy Popiełuszko joined the workers, taking part with <a href="/wiki/1981_warning_strike_in_Poland" title="1981 warning strike in Poland">strikers in the Warsaw Steelworks</a>. Thereafter he was associated with workers and trade unionists from the <a href="/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union)" title="Solidarity (Polish trade union)">Solidarity</a> movement who opposed the <a href="/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="People&#39;s Republic of Poland">communist regime in Poland</a>.
</p><p>He was a staunch opponent of the communist regime and, in his sermons, interwove spiritual exhortations with political messages, criticizing the government, expressing solidarity with the interned and motivating people to protest. During the period of <a href="/wiki/Martial_law_in_Poland" title="Martial law in Poland">martial law</a> the Catholic Church was the only force that could voice protest comparatively openly, with the regular celebration of <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> presenting opportunities for public gatherings in churches.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:187px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg/185px-Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg" decoding="async" width="185" height="249" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg/278px-Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg/370px-Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1225" data-file-height="1648" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Jerzy_Popieluszko_Funeral_-_37.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Funeral (1984)</div></div></div>
<p>Popiełuszko's sermons were routinely broadcast by <a href="/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe" class="mw-redirect" title="Radio Free Europe">Radio Free Europe</a>, and thus became famous throughout Poland for their uncompromising stance against the regime. The <a href="/wiki/S%C5%82u%C5%BCba_Bezpiecze%C5%84stwa" class="mw-redirect" title="Służba Bezpieczeństwa">Służba Bezpieczeństwa</a> tried to silence or intimidate him. When those techniques did not work, they fabricated evidence against him; he was arrested in 1983, but soon released on intervention of the clergy and pardoned by an amnesty.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Assassination">Assassination</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Assassination">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Invited by the Pastoral Care of the Working People (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Duszpasterstwa Ludzi Pracy</i>), Jerzy Popiełuszko arrived in <a href="/wiki/Bydgoszcz" title="Bydgoszcz">Bydgoszcz</a> on October 19, 1984. At 18.00 he celebrated his last <a href="/wiki/Holy_Mass" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Mass">Holy Mass</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Polish_Brothers_Martyrs,_Bydgoszcz" title="Church of the Holy Polish Brothers Martyrs, Bydgoszcz">Church of the Holy Polish Brothers Martyrs</a>.
A car accident was set up to kill Jerzy Popiełuszko on 13 October 1984 but he evaded it. The alternative plan was to kidnap him; it was carried out on 19 October 1984. The priest was beaten to death by three Security Police officers: Captain Grzegorz Piotrowski, Leszek Pękala, and Waldemar Chmielewski. They pretended to have problems with their car and flagged down Jerzy Popiełuszko's car for help. Popiełuszko was severely beaten, tied up and put in the trunk of the car.<sup id="cite_ref-http&#58;//ststanskostka.org_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-http://ststanskostka.org-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The officers bound a stone to his feet and dropped him into the Vistula Water Reservoir near <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82oc%C5%82awek" title="Włocławek">Włocławek</a> from where his body was recovered on 30 October 1984.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>News of the political murder caused an uproar throughout Poland, and the murderers and one of their superiors, Colonel Adam Pietruszka, were convicted of the crime. More than 250,000 people, including <a href="/wiki/Lech_Wa%C5%82%C4%99sa" title="Lech Wałęsa">Lech Wałęsa</a>, attended his funeral on 3 November 1984. The murder was widely used in political propaganda of Polish opposition in late 1980s. Popiełuszko's murderers – Captain Grzegorz Piotrowski, Leszek Pękala, Waldemar Chmielewski and Colonel Adam Pietruszka, responsible for giving the order to kill – were sentenced to jail.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Popiełuszko was posthumously awarded the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_White_Eagle_(Poland)" title="Order of the White Eagle (Poland)">Order of the White Eagle</a>, Poland's highest decoration, in 2009.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> After death, he was buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_Kostka_Church,_Warsaw" title="St. Stanislaus Kostka Church, Warsaw">St. Stanislaus Kostka Church, Warsaw</a>, where millions of visitors had paid tribute, including famous politicians like then Vice-President George Bush in September 1987.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Media">Media</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Media">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Noted Polish composer <a href="/wiki/Andrzej_Panufnik" title="Andrzej Panufnik">Andrzej Panufnik</a> composed his <a href="/wiki/Bassoon_Concerto_(Panufnik)" title="Bassoon Concerto (Panufnik)">Bassoon Concerto</a> (1985) in memory of Popiełuszko. The work is inspired by Popiełuszko's work and death.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Christopher_Lambert" title="Christopher Lambert">Christopher Lambert</a> played a character inspired by Popiełuszko in the film <i><a href="/wiki/To_Kill_a_Priest" title="To Kill a Priest">To Kill a Priest</a></i>.
</p><p>A track entitled "Homily to Popiełuszko" is featured on the B-side to the album <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flajelata&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Flajelata (page does not exist)">Flajelata</a></i> (1986) by <a href="/wiki/Muslimgauze" title="Muslimgauze">Muslimgauze</a>. The entire B-side of that album is dedicated to all dissidents from the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a>.
</p><p>A transcript of the trial of Popiełuszko's murderers was used in producing a play, <a href="/wiki/Ronald_Harwood" title="Ronald Harwood">Ronald Harwood</a>'s documentary drama <i>The Deliberate Death of a Polish Priest.</i> It was premiered at the <a href="/wiki/Almeida_Theatre" title="Almeida Theatre">Almeida Theatre</a> in October 1985.
</p><p>A movie, <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Popie%C5%82uszko_(film)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Popiełuszko (film) (page does not exist)">Popiełuszko,</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popie%C5%82uszko._Wolno%C5%9B%C4%87_jest_w_nas" class="extiw" title="pl:Popiełuszko. Wolność jest w nas">pl</a>&#93;</span></i> documenting the life and death of Popiełuszko, was released in Poland in February 2009. Another film, <i>Jerzy Popieluszko: Messenger of the Truth</i>, was produced by <a href="/wiki/Paul_G._Hensler" title="Paul G. Hensler">Paul G. Hensler</a> in 2013.
</p><p>In the television show <i><a href="/wiki/The_Americans" title="The Americans">The Americans</a></i>, the episode "<a href="/wiki/Duty_and_Honor" title="Duty and Honor">Duty and Honor</a>" in season one featured a fictional Polish resistance leader loosely based on Popiełuszko.
</p><p>In their work <i><a href="/wiki/Manufacturing_Consent" title="Manufacturing Consent">Manufacturing Consent</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_S._Herman" title="Edward S. Herman">Edward S. Herman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Noam_Chomsky" title="Noam Chomsky">Noam Chomsky</a> used Popiełuszko's murder and subsequent media coverage in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> as a case study of their "<a href="/wiki/Propaganda_model" title="Propaganda model">propaganda model</a>", in which it is compared and contrasted with the coverage of murders of <a href="/wiki/%C3%93scar_Romero" title="Óscar Romero">Óscar Romero</a> and other <a href="/wiki/Latin_America" title="Latin America">Latin American</a> clergy by US-backed forces.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monuments">Monuments</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Monuments">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:177px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG/175px-Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG" decoding="async" width="175" height="233" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG/263px-Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG/350px-Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2726" data-file-height="3635" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Podlaskie_-_Suchowola_-_Suchowola_-_Ko%C5%9Bciuszki_-_Park_20110925_03.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Monument in <a href="/wiki/Suchowola" title="Suchowola">Suchowola</a></div></div></div>
<p>There are numerous monuments to Fr. Popiełuszko in Poland, including the towns of <a href="/wiki/Suchowola" title="Suchowola">Suchowola</a>, where he attended and graduated high school, <a href="/wiki/Bartoszyce" title="Bartoszyce">Bartoszyce</a>, where he served his army duties,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Z%C4%85bki" title="Ząbki">Ząbki</a>, where he first served as a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-pnz_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pnz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The places of his kidnapping in <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B3rsk" title="Górsk">Górsk</a> and death in Włocławek are commemorated with monuments in the form of crosses. There is also a museum dedicated to him in Suchowola.
</p><p>The first monument of Jerzy Popiełuszko was created by Polish sculptor and dissident Marek Sobociński, and was unveiled in 1986 in Mariaholm near <a href="/wiki/Askim" title="Askim">Askim</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A monument to Fr. Popiełuszko in the form of a symbolic gravestone in the shape of a cross was erected by <a href="/wiki/Chicago%27s_Polish_community" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicago&#39;s Polish community">Chicago's Polish community</a> in the garden of memory next to <a href="/wiki/St._Hyacinth_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Hyacinth Basilica">St. Hyacinth Basilica</a>.
</p><p>A monument to Fr. Popiełuszko in the form of a bust bearing his likeness with a chain wrapped about his neck was erected on the property of Saint Hedwig Catholic Church in <a href="/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey" title="Trenton, New Jersey">Trenton</a>, <a href="/wiki/New_Jersey" title="New Jersey">New Jersey</a>.
</p><p>A pocket park across from McCarren Park on the <a href="/wiki/Williamsburg,_Brooklyn" title="Williamsburg, Brooklyn">Williamsburg</a>-<a href="/wiki/Greenpoint,_Brooklyn" title="Greenpoint, Brooklyn">Greenpoint</a> border in Brooklyn, New York, a historically Polish neighborhood, is named for Fr. Popiełuszko and features a stone bust bearing his likeness.
</p><p>Popieluszko Court in <a href="/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut" title="Hartford, Connecticut">Hartford, Connecticut</a>, was named in his memory. The SS. Cyril &amp; Methodius Church is located on this street, serving as an important cornerstone for the area's Roman Catholic Polish-American community. The street intersects with Charter Oak Boulevard, with the main entrance to the parking lot of the Polish National Home of Hartford across the street at the end of Popieluszko Court.
</p><p>A two-part monument has been installed in <a href="/wiki/New_Britain,_Connecticut" title="New Britain, Connecticut">New Britain's</a> <a href="/wiki/Walnut_Hill_Park" title="Walnut Hill Park">Walnut Hill Park</a> consisting of an inscribed stone plaque near an abstract sculpture of an eternal flame.
</p><p>The stone is inscribed with the Polish Title <i>Zło Dobrem Zwyciężaj</i>: "This human rights monument of common fieldstone
and steel is built in memory of Father Jerzy
Popieluszko who gave his life to God and to the
goals of <i>Solidarność</i> – human rights, justice,
peace and freedom for Poland and for all mankind.
May this eternal flame of liberty and the
memory of his courage and sacrifice burn forever
in the hearts of all freedom-loving people.
1947 Good shall vanquish evil 1984"
</p><p>The abstract sculpture of the eternal flame was created by Henry Chotkowski and dedicated on June 16, 1989. An aluminum plaque on the stone wall surrounding the sculpture explains the significance of the sculpture.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg/220px-Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="148" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg/330px-Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg/440px-Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2281" data-file-height="1535" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiber.island.church.rome.arp.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/San_Bartolomeo_all%27Isola" title="San Bartolomeo all&#39;Isola">San Bartolomeo all'Isola</a> in Rome, where Popiełuszko is commemorated in the memorial to 20th- and 21st- century martyrs.</div></div></div>
<p>The rock that was used to kill Popiełuszko was placed in the <a href="/wiki/San_Bartolomeo_all%27Isola" title="San Bartolomeo all&#39;Isola">San Bartolomeo all'Isola</a> in Rome as a relic of a 20th-century martyr, part of the memorial to 20th– and 21st–century martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-Father_Popieluszko_in_Rome_pantheon_of_modern_martyrs_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Father_Popieluszko_in_Rome_pantheon_of_modern_martyrs-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are two monuments commemorating Father Popiełuszko in <a href="/wiki/Budapest" title="Budapest">Budapest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>. In 2017, a monument containing Father Popiełuszko's motto "Overcome evil with good" written in Polish and in Hungarian was unveiled in the <a href="/wiki/Csepel" title="Csepel">Csepel</a> district of the Hungarian capital. In Csepel, there is also a street named after Father Popiełuszko and a belfry in his honor. Meanwhile, in 2016 in the <a href="/wiki/%C3%93buda-B%C3%A9k%C3%A1smegyer" title="Óbuda-Békásmegyer">Óbuda-Békásmegyer</a> district of Budapest there is a square named after Father Popiełuszko and a monument consisting of stones that make up a rosary featuring quotes by Popiełuszko.<sup id="cite_ref-W_Budapeszcie_odsłonięto_pomnik_błogosławionego_ks._Popiełuszki_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W_Budapeszcie_odsłonięto_pomnik_błogosławionego_ks._Popiełuszki-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also one monument in the French city of Béziers.<sup id="cite_ref-Béziers_honors_Fr_Popieluszko_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Béziers_honors_Fr_Popieluszko-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_and_canonization"><span class="anchor" id="Beatification_and_Canonisation"></span>Beatification and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerzy_Popie%C5%82uszko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Beatification and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> started the process of his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> with the declaration of <i><a href="/wiki/Nihil_obstat" title="Nihil obstat">nihil obstat</a></i> (nothing against) on 15 March 1996 and held a diocesan process from 8 February 1997 to 8 February 2001. This conferred upon him the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. In 2008 the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> was submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> and on 19 December 2009 it was announced that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> had approved the decree for the beatification of Father Popiełuszko.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was beatified by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> on 6 June 2010 in Warsaw's <a href="/wiki/Pi%C5%82sudski_Square" title="Piłsudski Square">Piłsudski Square</a>. His mother, Marianna Popiełuszko, was present at the event.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> More than 100,000 people attended the open-air Mass in the Polish capital Warsaw to beatify Father Jerzy Popieluszko. <a href="/wiki/Poland_Post" class="mw-redirect" title="Poland Post">Poland Post</a> issued a set of stamps on that same day to mark the beatification.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In October 2013, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Kazimierz_Nycz" title="Kazimierz Nycz">Kazimierz Nycz</a> – the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Warsaw" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Warsaw">Archbishop of Warsaw</a>, the diocese where Popiełuszko was killed – announced that a miracle attributed to the intercession of the Polish priest has been identified and confirmed in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. Thus Cardinal Nycz predicts that Popiełuszko will likely be <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> soon, based on the credibility of the case presented.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> A miracle was investigated in a diocesan process in France from 20 September 2014 until 14 September 2015 and the results of that investigation turned over to the Vatican for assessment.
</p>
