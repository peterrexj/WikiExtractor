					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">English Roman Catholic martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the English Catholic martyr. For the assassin of the Duke of Buckingham, see <a href="/wiki/John_Felton_(assassin)" title="John Felton (assassin)">John Felton (assassin)</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">John Felton</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Bermondsey_Abbey" title="Bermondsey Abbey">Bermondsey Abbey</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Southwark" title="Southwark">Southwark</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">8 August 1570<br />St. Paul's Churchyard, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">29 December 1886 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">8 August</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Martyr's palm, scroll with the Emblem of the Papacy</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p><p><b>John Felton</b> (fl. before 1566 - died 8 August 1570) was an English Catholic martyr, executed during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>. Felton was arrested for fixing a copy of Pope Pius V's bull <i><a href="/wiki/Regnans_in_Excelsis" title="Regnans in Excelsis">Regnans in Excelsis</a></i> excommunicating Queen Elizabeth, to the gates of the Bishop of London's palace near St. Paul's.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Felton_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Almost all of what is known about Felton's background comes from the narrative of his daughter, Frances Salisbury. The manuscript that holds her story has a blank where his age should be, but it does say that he was a wealthy man of <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a> ancestry, who lived at <a href="/wiki/Bermondsey_Abbey" title="Bermondsey Abbey">Bermondsey Abbey</a> near <a href="/wiki/Southwark" title="Southwark">Southwark</a>. Bermondsey was a mansion built the generation before on the site of and out of the materials of a great Cluniac monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-vch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He "was a man of stature little and of complexion black". His wife had been a playmate of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>, a maid-of-honour to <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Queen Mary</a> and the widow of one of Mary's auditors (a legal official of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">papal court</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> John Felton was a well-known Catholic, and the father of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Felton_(martyr)" title="Thomas Felton (martyr)">Thomas Felton</a> (born 1566).
</p><p>Felton was arrested for fixing a copy of Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius V">Pius V</a>'s bull <i><a href="/wiki/Regnans_in_Excelsis" title="Regnans in Excelsis">Regnans in Excelsis</a></i> ("reigning on high"), excommunicating Queen Elizabeth, to the gates of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a>'s palace near <a href="/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral" title="St Paul&#39;s Cathedral">St. Paul's</a>. Elizabeth and her ministers were by no means indifferent to the potential political effects of the papal bull. In the Europe of the sixteenth century there were still Catholic powers who might be ready to execute the sentence of deposition that was in those days the corollary of excommunication.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Posting the copy of the bull was a significant act of <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treason</a> as the document, which released Elizabeth's subjects from their allegiance, needed to be promulgated in England before it could take legal effect. The deed brought about the end of the previous policy of tolerance towards those Catholics who were content occasionally to attend their parish church while keeping their true beliefs to themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The reaction seemed soon to be justified: it was the publication in England of Pius's exhortation that gave the impetus to the <a href="/wiki/Ridolfi_plot" title="Ridolfi plot">Ridolfi plot</a>, in which the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_4th_Duke_of_Norfolk" title="Thomas Howard, 4th Duke of Norfolk">Duke of Norfolk</a> was to kidnap or murder Queen Elizabeth, install <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a>, on the throne and then become <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">de facto</i></span> king by marrying her.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The law records say that the act was committed around eleven at night on 24 May 1570, but Salisbury claims it happened between two and three in the morning of the following day, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Corpus_Christi" title="Feast of Corpus Christi">Feast of Corpus Christi</a>. Felton had received the bulls in Calais and given one to a friend, William Mellowes of <a href="/wiki/Lincoln%27s_Inn" title="Lincoln&#39;s Inn">Lincoln's Inn</a>. A general search of known Catholic houses in and near London was undertaken and the copy was soon discovered.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After being <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">racked</a>, Mellowes implicated Felton,<sup id="cite_ref-Jardine_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jardine-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> who was arrested on 26 May. Felton immediately confessed and glorified in his deed, "treasonably declar[ing] that the queen … ought not to be the queen of England",<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but he was still racked as the authorities were seeking, through his testimony, to implicate <a href="/wiki/Guerau_de_Spes" class="mw-redirect" title="Guerau de Spes">Guerau de Spes</a>, the Ambassador of Spain, in the action.<sup id="cite_ref-Jardine_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jardine-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Felton was condemned on 4 August and executed by <a href="/wiki/Hanging" title="Hanging">hanging</a> four days later in St. Paul's Churchyard, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>. He was cut down alive for <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">quartering</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and his daughter says that he uttered the holy name of Jesus once or twice when the hangman had his heart in his hand.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was beatified in 1886 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>.
</p><p>Records of the trial from Elizabeth's state papers, and the deliberations of the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a> regarding the effect on relations with Spain, are retained in the <a href="/wiki/Public_Record_Office" title="Public Record Office">Public Record Office</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
