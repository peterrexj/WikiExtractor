<p><b>Willehad</b> or <b>Willihad</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Willehadus/Willihadus</i>); <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 745 AD – 8 November 789) was a Christian missionary and possibly the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Bremen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Bremen">Bishop of Bremen</a> from 787 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Willehad was born in <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> and probably received his education at <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_School,_York" title="St Peter&#39;s School, York">York</a> under <a href="/wiki/Ecgbert_(bishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecgbert (bishop)">Ecgbert</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained after his education, and about the year 766, he went to <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> preaching at <a href="/wiki/Dokkum" title="Dokkum">Dokkum</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Overijssel" title="Overijssel">Overijssel</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> to continue the missionary work of <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> who had been martyred by the Frisians in 754. At an assembly in <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a> in 777, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxony</a> was divided into missionary zones. The zone between the <a href="/wiki/Weser_river" class="mw-redirect" title="Weser river">Weser</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Elbe_river" class="mw-redirect" title="Elbe river">Elbe</a>, called <i>Wigmodia</i>, was given to Willehad.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick198361_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick198361-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 780 Willehad preached in the region of the lower <a href="/wiki/Weser_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Weser River">Weser River</a> on commission from <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.  He barely escaped with his life when the Frisians wanted to kill him as well and he returned to the area around <a href="/wiki/Utrecht_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Utrecht (city)">Utrecht</a>.  Once again he and his fellow missionaries barely escaped with their lives when the local pagans wanted to kill them for destroying some temples.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Finally, in 780, <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> sent him to evangelize the Saxons.  He preached to them for two years but, in 782, the Saxons under <a href="/wiki/Widukind" title="Widukind">Widukind</a>, rebelled against Charlemagne and Willehad was forced to flee to Frisia.  He took the opportunity to travel to Rome where he reported to Pope <a href="/wiki/Adrian_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Adrian I">Adrian I</a> on his work.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon his return from Rome, Willehad retired for a time to the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Echternach" title="Echternach">Echternach</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Luxembourg" title="Luxembourg">Luxembourg</a>.  He spent two years there reassembling his missionary team.
</p><p>After Charlemagne's conquest of the Saxons, Willehad preached in the region around the lower Elbe and the lower Weser. In 787 Willehad was consecrated bishop, and that part of Saxony and Friesland near the mouth of the Weser was assigned to him for his diocese. He chose as his see the city of Bremen, which is mentioned for the first time in documents of 782, and built a cathedral there. Praised for its beauty by Anschar, it was dedicated in 789.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Willehad died in Blexen upon Weser, today a part of <a href="/wiki/Nordenham" title="Nordenham">Nordenham</a>. He is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Bremen_Cathedral" title="Bremen Cathedral">city's cathedral</a>, which he consecrated shortly before his death on 8 November 789.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Anschar" class="mw-redirect" title="Anschar">Anschar</a> compiled a life of Willehad, and the preface which he wrote was considered a masterpiece for that age.<sup id="cite_ref-campbell_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 860, a sick girl from Wege (<a href="/wiki/Weyhe" title="Weyhe">Weyhe</a>) travelled to his grave. There, she was reportedly cured by a miracle. This was the first time the small village was mentioned in any historical documents.
</p>
