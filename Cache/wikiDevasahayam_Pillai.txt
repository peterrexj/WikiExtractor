<p><b>Devasahayam Pillai</b> (born <b>Neelakanta Pillai</b> and baptized as <b>Lazarus</b>; 23 April 1712 – 14 January 1752) was an <a href="/wiki/Indian_people" title="Indian people">Indian</a> layman and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-smcim-814_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smcim-814-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the church by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> on 15 May 2022.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Neelakandan Pillai was born into an affluent <a href="/wiki/Hindu" class="mw-redirect" title="Hindu">Hindu</a> <a href="/wiki/Nair" title="Nair">Nair</a> family in Nattalam in the present-day <a href="/wiki/Kanyakumari_District" class="mw-redirect" title="Kanyakumari District">Kanyakumari District</a> on 23 April 1712.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His father, Vasudevan Namboodiri, who was a <a href="/wiki/Namboodiri" class="mw-redirect" title="Namboodiri">Namboodiri</a> <a href="/wiki/Brahmin" title="Brahmin">Brahmin</a> from <a href="/wiki/Kayamkulam" title="Kayamkulam">Kayamkulam</a> in present-day <a href="/wiki/Kerala" title="Kerala">Kerala</a> state, was a priest at the Adikesava Perumal Temple in <a href="/wiki/Thiruvattar" title="Thiruvattar">Thiruvattar</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Kanyakumari_district" title="Kanyakumari district">Kanyakumari district</a> of neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a>. Neelakandan's mother, Devaki Amma, hailed from Thiruvattar in <a href="/wiki/Kanyakumari_District" class="mw-redirect" title="Kanyakumari District">Kanyakumari District</a>. In accordance with the matrilineal tradition of the <a href="/wiki/Nair" title="Nair">Nairs</a> of the day, he was raised by his maternal uncle rather than his father.
</p><p>Neelakandan's family had much influence in the royal palace of <a href="/wiki/Maharaja_Marthanda_Varma" class="mw-redirect" title="Maharaja Marthanda Varma">Maharaja Marthanda Varma</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Travancore" title="Travancore">Travancore</a> and went into the service of the royal palace as a young man. His capabilities and enthusiasm did not go unnoticed in the palace, as he was soon put in charge of state affairs as an official under <a href="/wiki/Ramayyan_Dalawa" title="Ramayyan Dalawa">Ramayyan Dalawa</a>, the Dewan of Travancore.<sup id="cite_ref-Rosario_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rosario-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 55–56">&#58;&#8202;55–56&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion_to_Christianity">Conversion to Christianity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Conversion to Christianity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1741, Captain <a href="/wiki/Eustachius_De_Lannoy" title="Eustachius De Lannoy">Eustachius De Lannoy</a>, a Dutch naval commander, was sent on command of a naval expedition by the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company" title="Dutch East India Company">Dutch East India Company</a> with the objective of capturing and establishing a trading port at <a href="/wiki/Colachel" title="Colachel">Colachel</a>, a port under the control of Travancore.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the ensuing <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Colachel" title="Battle of Colachel">Battle of Colachel</a>, fought between the Travancore forces and the Dutch, the Dutch were defeated. De Lannoy's men were either killed or captured. <a href="/wiki/Eustachius_De_Lannoy" title="Eustachius De Lannoy">Eustachius De Lannoy</a> and his assistant Donadi surrendered and were imprisoned.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>De Lannoy and the Dutchmen were later pardoned by the king, on condition that they serve in the Travancore army. In the course of time, De Lannoy earned the trust of the king and went on to become the commander of the Travancore army, the very forces that had earlier defeated him. He trained the Travancore forces on European lines and introduced new weaponry and gunpowder. As military commander, he won many battles for <a href="/wiki/Travancore" title="Travancore">Travancore</a>, resulting in the annexation of various neighbouring territories to <a href="/wiki/Travancore" title="Travancore">Travancore</a>.
</p><p>It was during their influential roles under the King of Travancore that Devasahayam Pillai and De Lannoy became well acquainted. De Lannoy's Christian faith interested Devasahayam&#160;; thus, De Lannoy enlightened him on the faith, leading to Devasahayam Pillai's conversion in 1745. At this point, he took the name "Devasahayam" ("help of God"), which is the translation into <a href="/wiki/Malayalam" title="Malayalam">Malayalam</a> of the name 'Lazarus.'<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Baptism">Baptism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Baptism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On Devasahayam's acceptance of the Christian faith, he was baptized at the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> sub-parish church at the <a href="/wiki/Vadakkankulam" title="Vadakkankulam">Vadakkankulam</a> village (in the present-day <a href="/wiki/Tirunelveli_District" class="mw-redirect" title="Tirunelveli District">Tirunelveli District</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a>), where the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuits</a> had a mission under the Rev. Fr. R. Bouttari Italus S.J.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 281">&#58;&#8202;281&#8202;</span></sup> Neelakanda Pillai, his name at birth, was then changed to "<a href="/wiki/Lazar_(name)" title="Lazar (name)">Lazar</a>", although he is more widely known by the Tamil and Malayalam translation Devasahayam (meaning <i>God's help</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-kottar-diocese_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kottar-diocese-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Pillai was married by this time to Bhargavi Ammal from Kunchu Veedu, Elanthavilai, Mayicode in <a href="/wiki/Travancore_State" class="mw-redirect" title="Travancore State">Travancore State</a>. She was baptised alongside her husband as a matter of course. She was given the baptismal name "Gnanapoo Ammaal" ("Flower of Knowledge", equivalent to <a href="/wiki/Theresa" class="mw-redirect" title="Theresa">Theresa</a> in Tamil and Malayalam). Fearing reprisal in her native Travancore against her <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">religious conversion</a>, she chose to become a migrant-resident of <a href="/wiki/Vadakkankulam" title="Vadakkankulam">Vadakkankulam</a> village. Some other members of Devasahayam Pillai's immediate family also later received baptism.<sup id="cite_ref-Rosario_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rosario-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 68–69">&#58;&#8202;68–69&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Orders_based_on_accusations_and_charges">Orders based on accusations and charges</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Orders based on accusations and charges">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Church chroniclers say that the Brahmin chief priest of the kingdom, the feudal lords, members of the royal household and the Nair community brought false charges on Devasahayam to the Dewan, <a href="/wiki/Ramayyan_Dalawa" title="Ramayyan Dalawa">Ramayyan Dalawa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 282">&#58;&#8202;282&#8202;</span></sup> The allegations were leaving Hinduism and stop following Hindu beliefs. Leaving Hinduism was a great offence because they wer Pillai was divested of his portfolio in the Travancore administration and was arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-bs87222_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bs87222-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He remained in prison for three years, during which time the king of Travancore came under tremendous pressure from the Europeans seeking his release. Orders were finally passed for him to go into exile. He was initially ordered to be seated backward on a buffalo (a public humiliation) and paraded to the Kuzhumaikkad border, where he would be released and could enter into Dutch-controlled territory (or citadel/fortress used for trade and shipping).<sup id="cite_ref-Pushparaj_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pushparaj-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 41–42">&#58;&#8202;41–42&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The original Royal order was later altered, and Devasahayam was taken on the back of a buffalo to <a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a> border (much closer to the capital, <a href="/wiki/Padmanabhapuram" title="Padmanabhapuram">Padmanabhapuram</a>), where he was tortured by ten different karyakkars (Royal officers), on the advice of the ministers, before being released into a mountainous forest area, on the other side of which was the kingdom of the Pandya kings, traditional rivals of Travancore.<sup id="cite_ref-Pushparaj_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pushparaj-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 42–65">&#58;&#8202;42–65&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_traditions_and_beliefs">Other traditions and beliefs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Other traditions and beliefs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Devasahayam Pillai was marched from <a href="/wiki/Padmanabhapuram_Palace" title="Padmanabhapuram Palace">Padmanabhapuram Palace</a> to <a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a> by soldiers, over the period of a few days. Pillai was treated like a criminal and as was customary in those days for criminals, his body was painted with red and black spots, and was intentionally marched through populated areas, sitting backward on top of a <a href="/wiki/Water_buffalo" title="Water buffalo">water buffalo</a><sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 283">&#58;&#8202;283&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Mateer_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mateer-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> (the mythical vehicle or <a href="/wiki/Vahana" title="Vahana">vahana</a> of <a href="/wiki/Yama" title="Yama">Yama</a>, the lord of death in <a href="/wiki/Hinduism" title="Hinduism">Hinduism</a>) through the streets of <a href="/wiki/South_Travancore" class="mw-redirect" title="South Travancore">South Travancore</a>. As a method of torture, he was beaten every day with eighty stripes, pepper rubbed in his wounds and nostrils, exposed to the sun, and given only stagnant water to drink.<sup id="cite_ref-Mateer_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mateer-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While halting at Puliyoorkurichi, not far away from the <a href="/wiki/Padmanabhapuram_Palace" title="Padmanabhapuram Palace">Padmanabhapuram Palace</a> of the Travancore king, it is believed by Christians that God quenched his thirst by letting water gush through a small hole on a rock, on the very place where he knelt to pray. The water hole is still found in the compound of a church at Puliyoorkurichi, about 15&#160;km from <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pushparaj_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pushparaj-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 54">&#58;&#8202;54&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 285">&#58;&#8202;285&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>It is also believed that the leaves of a <a href="/wiki/Neem" class="mw-redirect" title="Neem">neem</a> (Margosa) tree in the village of <a href="/wiki/Peruvilai" title="Peruvilai">Peruvilai</a>, to which he had been tied while being marched to <a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a>, cured illnesses of sick people in the village and around. Many more miracles are attributed to Devasahayam Pillai.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 286">&#58;&#8202;286&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1752, the original order of the King and his Dewan was to deport him from Travancore, into the Pandya country, at <a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a>. He was let off in the forested hills near <a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a>. There, he is believed to have begun deep meditations, and the people from the adjacent villages began visiting the holy man. Christian sources allege that at this time, high caste Hindus plotted to do away with Devasahayam.<sup id="cite_ref-Rosario_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rosario-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 134">&#58;&#8202;134&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Some people believe that soldiers went up the forested hills and tried to shoot Devasahayam, but were unable to fire; after which he took the gun in his hands, blessed it and gave it back to the soldiers to shoot him to death, if they wished to. The soldiers took the gun back and fired at him five times. His body was then carelessly thrown out near the foothills at Kattadimalai.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 285">&#58;&#8202;285&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Thangasami_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thangasami-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 83">&#58;&#8202;83&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>It was at Kattadimalai in <a href="/wiki/Kanyakumari_district" title="Kanyakumari district">Kanyakumari district</a> that Devasahayam Pillai died on 14 January 1752. Pillai's body was later recovered by some people of the region and carried to the church at <a href="/wiki/Kottar" title="Kottar">Kottar</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> His mortal remains were interred near the altar inside <a href="/wiki/St._Xavier%27s_Church,_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Xavier&#39;s Church, Kottar">St. Xavier's Church, Kottar</a>, Nagercoil, which is now the diocesan <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 285">&#58;&#8202;285&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Since the days of the interment of the mortal remains of Devasahayam Pillai, many Christian pilgrims have visited his tomb and offered prayers.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization_process">Canonization process</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Canonization process">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 2004, at the request of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Kottar">Diocese of Kottar</a>, the Tamil Nadu Bishops' Council (TNBC) and the <a href="/wiki/Conference_of_Catholic_Bishops_of_India" title="Conference of Catholic Bishops of India">Conference of Catholic Bishops of India</a> (CCBI) recommended Pillai for the process of <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newindpress_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindpress-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Some Hindu organizations objected to this initiative, claiming that there is no evidence of religious persecution in Travancore during the given period, and that Pillai was executed for <a href="/wiki/Sedition" title="Sedition">sedition</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hvk_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hvk-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-challenged_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-challenged-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> However, documents dating back to the period encompassing Pillai's lifetime show that religious conversion of court officials to Christianity was not tolerated.<sup id="cite_ref-Paulinus_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paulinus-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 28 June 2012, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> authorized the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> to promulgate a decree regarding the martyrdom of Pillai and he was granted the title "<a href="/wiki/Venerable#Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> On 2 December 2012, a ceremony of beatification and declaration of martyrdom was held in <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Kottar" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Kottar">Diocese of Kottar</a> in Southern <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, presided over by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints, acting as <a href="/wiki/Papal" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal">papal</a> delegate. Pillai is the first Indian <a href="/wiki/Catholic_laity" title="Catholic laity">lay man</a> who is not part of any religious institute to be elevated to the rank of "<a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>" (the step preceding the recognition of a person as a saint, as per the <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> of the Catholic Church).<sup id="cite_ref-bs87222_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bs87222-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 21 February 2020, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> recognized a miracle attributed to the intercession of Devasahayam, clearing his way to <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_News_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican_News-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He is the first Catholic in India who is neither an ordained minister nor a religious to be officially recognized as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The Vatican announced on 9 November 2021 that Pope Francis would formally canonize him on 15 May 2022. On the fifth Sunday of the resurrection, 15 May 2022, the Pope raised Devasahayam Pillai to sainthood along with nine other candidates.<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_News_2_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican_News_2-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the report submitted by the then Bishop of Cochin (under whom the Kanyakumari church was then functioning) in 1756AD the Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> of Devasahayam Pillai was promptly intimated to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a>. Prominent witnesses to his saintliness and martyrdom include <a href="/wiki/Paremmakkal_Thoma_Kathanar" title="Paremmakkal Thoma Kathanar">Paremmakkal Thoma Kathanar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1780, Kariattil Ouseph Malpan submitted a petition to the Vatican for canonization of Devasahayam Pillai.<sup id="cite_ref-Thangasami_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thangasami-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 94–96">&#58;&#8202;94–96&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The church historian C. M. Agur concluded in 1903 that although <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostasy</a> was never considered illegal in Travancore, it was not viewed indifferently, particularly in the case of the King's palace servants, and this led to the martyrdom of Devasahayam Pillai.<sup id="cite_ref-Agur_9-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agur-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 285">&#58;&#8202;285&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>In 1984, a group of lay persons from the diocese of Kottar, especially members of the Nagercoil Catholic Club, once again took the initiative to seek the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> of Devasahayam.<sup id="cite_ref-process_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-process-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> This is unusual for a layman, but he is regarded as one who was totally devoted to Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-newindpress_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindpress-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> At the beginning of the 21st century, many Christian devotees were offering prayers at his tomb in <a href="/wiki/St._Xavier%27s_Church,_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Xavier&#39;s Church, Kottar">St. Xavier's Cathedral</a> at <a href="/wiki/Kottar" title="Kottar">Kottar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newindpress_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindpress-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After a series of initiatives by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_diocese_of_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic diocese of Kottar">Roman Catholic diocese of Kottar</a> and after much deliberation, the <a href="/wiki/Conference_of_Catholic_Bishops_of_India" title="Conference of Catholic Bishops of India">Conference of Catholic Bishops of India</a> (CCBI) and the Tamil Nadu Bishops' Council (TNBC) duly recommended his beatification in the year 2004. This was done following scrutiny of available historical evidence.<sup id="cite_ref-newindpress_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindpress-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Yoohanon_Mar_Chrysostom" class="mw-redirect" title="Yoohanon Mar Chrysostom">Bishop Chrysostom</a> said that the CCBI did not intend any controversy whatsoever in moving this forward.<sup id="cite_ref-newindpress_18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newindpress-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Professor <a href="/wiki/A._Sreedhara_Menon" title="A. Sreedhara Menon">A. Sreedhara Menon</a> (1925–2010), a noted historian and writer on <a href="/wiki/Travancore" title="Travancore">Travancore</a>, said that no cases of persecution in the name of religious conversion were recorded in the history of the kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-hvk_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hvk-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-challenged_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-challenged-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
<a href="/wiki/P._Parameswaran" title="P. Parameswaran">P. Parameswaran</a>, president of the Hindu spiritual organisation <a href="/wiki/Vivekananda_Kendra" title="Vivekananda Kendra">Vivekananda Kendra</a>, accused the CCBI of an attempt to hurt Hindu sentiments. Referring to the Travancore state manual, he insisted that Devasahayam was a palace employee who was executed after confirmation of sedition, because he had tampered with palace records and passed them to <a href="/wiki/De_Lannoy" class="mw-redirect" title="De Lannoy">De Lannoy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hvk_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hvk-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-challenged_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-challenged-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, Catholic records of the time state that the kingdom of Travancore did not tolerate palace officials converting to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-Paulinus_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paulinus-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In June 2012, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> officially recognized a decree from the Congregation for the Causes of Saints stating that he lived a life of "heroic virtues" – a major step towards <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> – and Pillai was then referred to as "<a href="/wiki/Venerable#Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-ucanindia_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ucanindia-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_and_declaration_as_a_martyr">Beatification and declaration as a martyr</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Beatification and declaration as a martyr">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Devasahayam Pillai was declared a martyr and Blessed on 2 December 2012, at a solemn ceremony held in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Kottar">Diocese of Kottar</a> at <a href="/wiki/Carmel_Higher_Secondary_School" title="Carmel Higher Secondary School">Carmel Higher Secondary School Grounds</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>, near the place of his burial. The Prefect of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>, <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Cardinal_Amato" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelo Cardinal Amato">Angelo Cardinal Amato</a> presided at the function as Delegate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>.
</p><p>Several cardinals, archbishops and bishops from India and elsewhere, as well as numerous priests, religious men and women and over 100,000 Catholics<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> from all over India participated in the grand ceremony which included a <a href="/wiki/Solemn_Pontifical_Mass" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemn Pontifical Mass">Solemn Pontifical Mass</a>.
</p><p>Among the dignitaries officiating at the altar were Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Oswald_Gracias" title="Oswald Gracias">Oswald Gracias</a> (Archbishop of Mumbai), Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Telesphore_Toppo" title="Telesphore Toppo">Telesphore P. Toppo</a> (Archbishop of Ranchi), Cardinal <a href="/wiki/George_Alencherry" title="George Alencherry">George Alencherry</a> (Major Archbishop of Syro-Malabar Catholic Church), <a href="/wiki/Baselios_Cleemis" title="Baselios Cleemis">Baselios Cleemis</a> (Major Archbishop of Syro-Malankara Catholic Church), <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nunciature_to_India" title="Apostolic Nunciature to India">Archbishop Salvatore Pennacchio</a> (Apostolic Nuncio to India), and Bishop Peter Remigius (then <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Kottar">Bishop of Kottar</a>).
</p><p>Devasahayam Pillai is the first <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay person</a> from India to be beatified by the Catholic Church.
</p><p>On the same day as Devasahayam Pillai was declared a Blessed in the Diocese of Kottar, India, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> addressed the pilgrims gathered in Rome. During his <a href="/wiki/Angelus" title="Angelus">Angelus</a> Message the Pope mentioned the event in Italian and English.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> He said in Italian:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Today in Kottar, India, Devasahayam Pillai, a faithful layman, who lived in the 18th century and died a martyr, was proclaimed Blessed. Let us join in the joy of the Church in India and pray that this newly Beatified sustain the faith of the Christians of that great and noble country.</p></blockquote>
<p>Then he addressed the crowds in English:
</p>
<blockquote><p>I welcome all gathered here today to pray with me. I especially greet the people of Kottar who celebrate today the beatification of Devasahayam Pillai. His witness to Christ is an example of that attentiveness to the coming of Christ recalled by this first Sunday of Advent. May this holy season help us to centre our lives once more on Christ, our hope. God bless all of you!</p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 21 February 2020, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> recognized a miracle attributed to the intercession of Devasahayam, clearing his way to canonization (sainthood).<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_News_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican_News-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The Vatican announced on 9 November 2021 that the canonization ceremony would be held on 15 May 2022 and his canonization and that of eight others took place in <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="St Peter&#39;s Square">St Peter's Square</a> on that date.<sup id="cite_ref-Vatican_News_2_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vatican_News_2-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Places_of_interest">Places of interest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devasahayam_Pillai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Places of interest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Devasahayam Pillai is buried in the <a href="/wiki/St._Xavier%27s_Church,_Kottar" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Xavier&#39;s Church, Kottar">Cathedral of St. Francis Xavier</a> at <a href="/wiki/Kottar" title="Kottar">Kottar</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kottar-diocese_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kottar-diocese-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Devasahayam's tomb was restored and beautified after the declaration of <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Devasahyam Pillai's clothes and other belongings are kept in a church in the small town of <a href="/wiki/Vadakkankulam" title="Vadakkankulam">Vadakkankulam</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tirunelveli_District" class="mw-redirect" title="Tirunelveli District">Tirunelveli District</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a> State, India. They are exposed at the church on 15 August every year, the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption of Mary</a>. His wife was buried in the cemetery there.
</p><p>Puliyoorkurichi, location of the water fountain believed to have quenched Devasahayam's thirst, is on the <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>–<a href="/wiki/Trivandrum" class="mw-redirect" title="Trivandrum">Trivandrum</a> highway.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Aralvaimozhy" class="mw-redirect" title="Aralvaimozhy">Aralvaimozhy</a>, where Devasahayam was killed, is on the <a href="/wiki/Nagercoil" title="Nagercoil">Nagercoil</a>–<a href="/wiki/Tirunelveli" title="Tirunelveli">Tirunelveli</a> highway. At the spot on the hillock (called Kaattadimalai) where Devasahayam was killed, a rock makes bell-like sounds when knocked with a stone.
</p>
