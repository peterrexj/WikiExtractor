					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Peter</b> was a senior <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> military commander at the turn of the 9th century, who later became a monk and was canonized by the Church. He is venerated on <a href="/wiki/July_1_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 1 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">July 1</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Patrician_(9th_century)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter is known only from <a href="/wiki/Menologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Menologies">menologies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGuilland1970333,_338_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuilland1970333,_338-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of the <i><a href="/wiki/Patrikios" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrikios">patrikios</a></i> Constantine, who according to the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Synaxarium_Constantinopolitanum&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Synaxarium Constantinopolitanum (page does not exist)">Synaxarium Constantinopolitanum</a></i> held the position of <i><a href="/wiki/Strategos" title="Strategos">strategos</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15028_Konstantinos_(#3877)&#93;;_&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)&#93;_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15028_Konstantinos_(#3877)];_[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)]-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Peter was born during the joint reign of <a href="/wiki/Irene_of_Athens" title="Irene of Athens">Irene of Athens</a> and her son <a href="/wiki/Constantine_VI" title="Constantine VI">Constantine VI</a> (780–796).<sup id="cite_ref-DOHD_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOHD-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter also held the rank of <i>patrikios</i>, and served as <a href="/wiki/Domestikos_ton_scholon" class="mw-redirect" title="Domestikos ton scholon">commander</a> of the elite <i><a href="/wiki/Scholae_Palatinae" title="Scholae Palatinae">Scholai</a></i> regiment during the sole reign of Irene of Athens (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#160;797–802</span>) and as commander of the <i><a href="/wiki/Hikanatoi" title="Hikanatoi">Hikanatoi</a></i> regiment under her successor, <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_I" title="Nikephoros I">Nikephoros I</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#160;802–811</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-DOHD_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOHD-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)&#93;_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)]-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He fought in the disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Pliska" title="Battle of Pliska">Battle of Pliska</a> in 811, and was taken prisoner along with 50 other officers by the <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgars</a>. Peter managed to escape "miraculously" through the aid of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="John the Theologian">John the Theologian</a>, became a monk on the <a href="/wiki/Bithynian_Olympus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bithynian Olympus">Bithynian Olympus</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Joannicius_the_Great" title="Joannicius the Great">Joannicius the Great</a>, with whom he lived together as his disciple for 34 years.<sup id="cite_ref-DOHD_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOHD-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)&#93;_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)]-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After Joannicius' death in 846, Peter returned to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, where he built a church in the <i>ta Evandrou</i> quarter. He lived in a hut nearby and died eight years later, on 1 July 854.<sup id="cite_ref-DOHD_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOHD-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)&#93;_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)]-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The veracity of his life after 811 has been questioned by John Wortley, who considers his monastic life a legend.<sup id="cite_ref-DOHD_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOHD-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Rodolphe_Guilland" title="Rodolphe Guilland">Rodolphe Guilland</a> suggested an identity with a <i>patrikios</i> of the same name, mentioned in the history of <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Confessor" title="Theophanes the Confessor">Theophanes the Confessor</a>, who participated in the overthrow of Irene in 802, and who in 809 calmed an army mutiny against Nikephoros. However, this person is stated by Theophanes to have been killed at Pliska.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGuilland1970333,_338_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuilland1970333,_338-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)&#93;;_&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17263_Petros_(#6065)&#93;_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17244_Petros_(#6046)];_[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ17263_Petros_(#6065)]-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
