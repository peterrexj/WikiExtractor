<p><b>Maurilius</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1000</span>–1067) was a Norman <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">Archbishop of Rouen</a> from 1055 to 1067.
</p><p>Maurilius was originally from <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, and was born about 1000. He trained as a priest at Liege and became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> of <a href="/wiki/Halberstadt" title="Halberstadt">Halberstadt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas121_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas121-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Fecamp_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Fecamp Abbey">Fecamp Abbey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Spear196_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spear196-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but then became a hermit at Vallombrosa. From there he was elected <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the monastery of St Marry of Florence,<sup id="cite_ref-Spear196_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spear196-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but his austerity caused a revolt amongst the monks and he was forced to return to Fecamp.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas121_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas121-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maurilius held the office of Archbishop of Rouen from 1054, when he first occurs in documents as archbishop. The <i>Annals of Jumieges</i>, however, place his elevation as archbishop in 1055.<sup id="cite_ref-Spear196_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spear196-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Maurilius succeeded <a href="/wiki/Mauger_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mauger (archbishop)">Mauger</a>, who was deposed by a council held in 1054 or 1055 at <a href="/wiki/Lisieux" title="Lisieux">Lisieux</a> under the guidance of a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a>. After Mauger's deposition, Maurilius was appointed because of his support for <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_reform" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian reform">church reform</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas69_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas69-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Contemporaries praised his saintly character which gained him respect from his fellow clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas121_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas121-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was also known for his scholarly interests and knowledge.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas128_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas128-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was a benefactor of the abbeys of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Ouen,_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Ouen, Rouen">Saint-Ouen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jumi%C3%A8ges_Abbey" title="Jumièges Abbey">Jumièges</a>, <a href="/wiki/Le_Tr%C3%A9port" title="Le Tréport">Le Tréport</a>, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint-Ymer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint-Ymer (page does not exist)">Saint-Ymer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas124_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas124-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Along with <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a>, he convinced the future <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury" title="Anselm of Canterbury">St&#160;Anselm</a> to take monastic vows in 1060.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEODCC200573_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEODCC200573-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As archbishop, he built a large church to replace <a href="/wiki/Rouen_Cathedral" title="Rouen Cathedral">Rouen Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas125_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas125-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Douglas134_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas134-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He also held at least one provincial <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> - the 1063 <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rouen" title="Synod of Rouen">Synod of Rouen</a>, and perhaps held another ecclesiastical council sometime between 1055 and 1063.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas131_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas131-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The archbishop's last appearance in documents happens in 1067, and he died in the summer of 1067.<sup id="cite_ref-Spear196_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spear196-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His last public act was to consecrate the new abbey church at Jumièges on 1 July 1067 in the presence of Duke <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William</a>, newly returned from his conquest of England in 1066.<sup id="cite_ref-Douglas209_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Douglas209-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Two different dates are given for Maurilius' death, with a notice of his death appearing for 11 July at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Mont_Saint-Michel" class="mw-redirect" title="Mont Saint-Michel">Mont Saint-Michel</a> but Jumièges Abbey recorded his death on 9 August. He was buried in Rouen Cathedral. After his death, the archbishopric was offered to <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a>, who refused the office.<sup id="cite_ref-Spear196_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spear196-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
