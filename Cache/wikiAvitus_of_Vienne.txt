<p><b>Alcimus Ecdicius</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <b>Avitus</b> (c. 450 – February 5, 517/518 or 519) was a Latin poet and <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Vienne" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Diocese of Vienne">bishop of Vienne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. His fame rests in part on his poetry, but also on the role he played as secretary for the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Burgundians" title="Kingdom of the Burgundians">Burgundian</a> kings.
</p><p>Avitus was born of a prominent <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> senatorial family related to Emperor <a href="/wiki/Avitus" title="Avitus">Avitus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Avitus_of_Vienne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His father was <a href="/wiki/Hesychius_I_of_Vienne" class="mw-redirect" title="Hesychius I of Vienne">Hesychius, bishop of Vienne</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where episcopal honors were informally hereditary.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His paternal grandfather was a western Roman emperor whose precise identity is not known. <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_of_Valence" title="Apollinaris of Valence">Apollinaris of Valence</a> was his younger brother;<sup id="cite_ref-monks_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> their sister Fuscina became a nun.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Avitus was probably born at Vienne, for he was baptized by bishop <a href="/wiki/Mamertus" title="Mamertus">Mamertus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> About 490 he was ordained bishop of Vienne. In 499 Vienne was captured by <a href="/wiki/Gundobad" title="Gundobad">Gundobad</a>, king of the Burgundians, who was at war with Clovis, king of the Franks, where he came to the attention of that king. Avitus, as metropolitan of southern and eastern Gaul, took the lead in a conference between the Catholic and Arian bishops held in presence of Gundobad at Sardiniacum near <a href="/wiki/Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Lyons">Lyons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He won the confidence of King Gundobad, and converted his son, King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_of_Burgundy" title="Sigismund of Burgundy">Sigismund</a> to Catholicism.
</p><p>A letter of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hormisdas" title="Pope Hormisdas">Pope Hormisdas</a> to Avitus<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> records that he was made vicar apostolic in Gaul by that pontiff; and in 517 he presided in this capacity at the Council of Epaon for restoring ecclesiastical discipline in <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Narbonensis" title="Gallia Narbonensis">Gallia Narbonensis</a>. Avitus appears also to have exerted himself to terminate the dispute between the churches of Rome and Constantinople which arose out of the excommunication of <a href="/wiki/Acacius_of_Constantinople" title="Acacius of Constantinople">Acacius</a>; we gather from his later letters that this was accomplished before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon his death, Avitus was buried in the monastery of St. Peter and St. Paul at Vienne.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Avitus_of_Vienne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The literary fame of Avitus rests on his many surviving letters (his recent editors make them ninety-six in all)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and on a long poem, <i>Poematum de Mosaicae historiae gestis</i> (also known as <i>De spiritualis historiae gestis</i>), in classical <a href="/wiki/Hexameter" title="Hexameter">hexameters</a>, in five books, dealing with the Biblical themes of <a href="/wiki/Original_sin" title="Original sin">original sin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adam_and_Eve#Expulsion_from_Eden" title="Adam and Eve">expulsion from Paradise</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Deluge_(mythology)" class="mw-redirect" title="Deluge (mythology)">Deluge</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Crossing_of_the_Red_Sea" class="mw-redirect" title="Crossing of the Red Sea">Crossing of the Red Sea</a>. The first three books offer a certain dramatic unity; in them are told the preliminaries of the great disaster, the catastrophe itself, and the consequences. The fourth and fifth books deal with the Deluge and the Crossing of the Red Sea as symbols of <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>. Avitus deals freely and familiarly with the Scriptural events, and exhibits well their beauty, sequence, and significance. He is one of the last masters of the art of <a href="/wiki/Rhetoric" title="Rhetoric">rhetoric</a> as taught in the schools of Gaul in the 4th and 5th centuries. His poetic diction, though abounding in archaisms and rhythmic redundancy, is pure and select, and the laws of metre are well observed. Writing in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Joseph_Shahan" title="Thomas Joseph Shahan">Thomas Joseph Shahan</a> says "that <a href="/wiki/John_Milton" title="John Milton">Milton</a> made use of his paraphrase of Scripture in writing <i><a href="/wiki/Paradise_Lost" title="Paradise Lost">Paradise Lost</a></i>."<sup id="cite_ref-CE_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Avitus also wrote a poem for his sister Fuscina, a nun, "De consolatoriâ castitatis laude".  
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon,_1887,_figure_page_0220.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png/220px-Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="87" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png/330px-Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png/440px-Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon%2C_1887%2C_figure_page_0220.png 2x" data-file-width="836" data-file-height="330" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Catalogue_Sommaire_des_Mus%C3%A9es_de_la_Ville_de_Lyon,_1887,_figure_page_0220.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A seal of Bishop Avit</div></div></div>
<p>The letters of Avitus are of considerable importance for the ecclesiastical and political history of the years between 499 and 518. Like his contemporary, <a href="/wiki/Magnus_Felix_Ennodius" title="Magnus Felix Ennodius">Ennodius of Pavia</a>, he was strenuous in his assertion of the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic See">Apostolic See</a> as the chief bulwark of religious unity and the incipient <a href="/wiki/Christian_civilization" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian civilization">Christian civilization</a>. "If the pope," he says, "is rejected, it follows that not one bishop, the whole episcopate threatens to fall" (Si papa urbis vocatur in dubium, episcopatus videbitur, non episcopus, vaccilare. — Ep. xxxiv; ed. Peiper). His letters are also among the important primary sources of early <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> political, ecclesiastical, and social history. Among them is a famous letter to <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis</a> on the occasion of his baptism. Avitus addresses Clovis not as if he was a pagan convert, but as if he was a recent <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> sympathiser, possibly even a <a href="/wiki/Catechumen" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechumen">catechumen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The letters document the close relations between the Catholic Bishop of Vienne and the Arian king of the Burgundians, the great Gundobad, and his son, the Catholic convert Sigismund. 
</p><p>There was once extant a collection of his <a href="/wiki/Homilies" class="mw-redirect" title="Homilies">homilies</a> and sermons, but they have all perished except for two, and some fragments and excerpts from others.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The so-called <i>Dialogues with King Gundobad</i>, written to defend the Catholic faith against the Arians and which purports to represent the famous Colloquy of Lyon in 449, was once believed to be his work. <a href="/wiki/Julien_Havet" title="Julien Havet">Julien Havet</a> demonstrated in 1885, however, that it is a forgery of the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus" title="Oratory of Jesus">Oratorian</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=J%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_Vignier&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jérôme Vignier (page does not exist)">Jérôme Vignier</a>, who also forged a letter purporting to be from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Symmachus" title="Pope Symmachus">Pope Symmachus</a> to Avitus.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
