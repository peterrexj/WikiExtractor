<p><b>Oran</b> or <b>Odran</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Irish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Irish language">Old Irish</a>: <i lang="sga">Odrán</i>, <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Odhrán</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Otteranus</i>, hence sometimes <i>Otteran</i>; died AD 548), by tradition a descendant of <a href="/wiki/Conall_Gulban" title="Conall Gulban">Conall Gulban</a>, was a companion of Saint <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a> in <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, and the first Christian to be buried on that island. Saint Odhrán's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is on 27 October.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oran_of_Iona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Odran lived for over forty years in the area now known as <a href="/wiki/Silvermines" title="Silvermines">Silvermines</a>, in <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>, Ireland, building a church there in 520.<sup id="cite_ref-killaloe_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-killaloe-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to Irish tradition, Odran also served as abbot of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Meath" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Meath">Meath</a>, and founded Lattreagh. In 563, he was among the twelve who accompanied Columba to the Scottish island of Iona, where he died and was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-killaloe_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-killaloe-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Columba is said to have seen devils and angels fight over Odran's soul before it ascended into heaven.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One popular legend surrounding Odran's death is that he consented to being <a href="/wiki/Buried_alive" class="mw-redirect" title="Buried alive">buried alive</a> beneath a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> that Columba was attempting to build at Iona. A voice had told Columba that the walls of the chapel would not stand until a living man was buried below the <a href="/wiki/Foundation_(engineering)" title="Foundation (engineering)">foundations</a>, and indeed, each morning the builders would arrive at the site to find all their work of the previous day undone. So Odran was consigned to the earth, and the chapel was erected above him. One day, however, Odran lifted his head out of the ground and said: "There is no Hell as you suppose, nor Heaven that people talk about". Alarmed by this, Columba quickly had the body removed and reburied in consecrated ground – or, in other versions of the story, simply called for more earth to cover the body.<sup id="cite_ref-Folklore_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Folklore-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In a <a href="/wiki/Hebrides" title="Hebrides">Hebridean</a> version of this tale, Odran was promised that his soul would be safe in heaven. Sometime after the burial, Columba wanted to see Odran once more and opened the pit under the chapel. When Odran saw the world, he tried to climb out of his grave, but Columba had the pit covered with earth quickly to save Odran's soul from the world and its sin.<sup id="cite_ref-Folklore_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Folklore-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>These legends are one of the few instances of <a href="/wiki/Human_sacrifice" title="Human sacrifice">foundation sacrifice</a> in Great Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-Folklore_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Folklore-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> While the story of Odran's self-sacrifice does not appear in <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n" title="Adomnán">Adomnán</a>'s <i>Life of Columcille</i>, George Henderson says that the legend points to an ancient folk-belief. He believes this is similar to the Arthurian legend of the building of Dinas Emris, where <a href="/wiki/Vortigern" title="Vortigern">Vortigern</a> was counselled to find and sacrifice "a child without a father" to ensure that the fortress walls did not collapse.<sup id="cite_ref-henderson_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-henderson-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Due to the similarity of the name, some people have identified Odran with <a href="/wiki/Odran_(disciple_of_Saint_Patrick)" title="Odran (disciple of Saint Patrick)">Saint Odran</a>, the first Irish Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. There is a parallel in that each man voluntarily sacrificed himself to further the work of a better-known saint.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oran_of_Iona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The oldest remaining church on Iona is dedicated to Saint Odran. The surrounding cemetery is called <i><a href="/wiki/Iona#Iona_Abbey" title="Iona">Reilig Odhráin</a></i> in his memory.
</p><p>Odran is the patron saint of the parish of Silvermines, County Tipperary.<sup id="cite_ref-killaloe_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-killaloe-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was chosen by the <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> as patron of the city of <a href="/wiki/Waterford" title="Waterford">Waterford</a> in 1096 and later chosen as patron of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_culture">In culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oran_of_Iona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Neil_Gaiman" title="Neil Gaiman">Neil Gaiman</a>'s poem "In Relig Odhrain", published in <i>Trigger Warning: Short Fictions and Disturbances (2015)</i>, retells the story of Oran's death.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Steve_McDonald_(Celtic_music)" title="Steve McDonald (Celtic music)">Stephen McDonald</a>'s song "Oran" on CD Stone of Destiny songs of the tale of Oran and his demise.
</p>
