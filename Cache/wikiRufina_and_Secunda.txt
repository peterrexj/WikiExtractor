<p><b>Rufina and Secunda</b> (died 257) were <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> virgin-<a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a>. Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 10 July.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rufina_and_Secunda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the legendary <i>Acts</i>, they suffered in 287 during the persecution of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Valerian_(emperor)" title="Valerian (emperor)">Valerian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Their legend states that they were daughters of a Roman senator named Asterius. Their fiancés, Armentarius and Verinus, were Christians, but renounced their faith when Valerian began his persecutions.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Escaping to <a href="/wiki/Etruria" title="Etruria">Etruria</a>, Rufina and Secunda were captured and brought before a prefect, who tortured and then <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> them.
</p><p>Their bodies were buried on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Aurelia" title="Via Aurelia">Via Aurelia</a> and the church of Sante Rufina e Secunda was built in their honor in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rufina_and_Secunda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the notes attached to the publication of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, it is stated that of these two saints, whose feast was inserted into the Roman Calendar in the 12th century on the occasion of the transfer of their relics to the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Lateran Basilica">Lateran Basilica</a>, nothing is really known except their names and the fact that they were buried at the ninth milestone of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Cornelia" title="Via Cornelia">Via Cornelia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They are mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Bern" title="Bern">Bern</a> manuscript of the <i>"<a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a>,"</i> and are recorded also in seventh century <i>"Itineraries"</i> as on the Via Cornelia, where <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a> erected a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">Basilica</a> over their grave. The town on this spot named after St Rufina (<i>Santa Rufina</i>) became the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">See</a> of one of the <a href="/wiki/Suburbicarian_dioceses" class="mw-redirect" title="Suburbicarian dioceses">suburbicarian dioceses</a> that was later united with <a href="/wiki/Porto_(Italy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Porto (Italy)">Porto</a> as <i>Porto-Santa Rufina</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rufina_and_Secunda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Sts Rufina and Secunda was included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> as a "semi-double". The <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_Pope_Pius_XII" title="General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII">General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII</a> reduced it to a "simple", and in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a> it became a third-class feast. According to the rules in the present <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a>, they may now be celebrated everywhere with their own Mass on their feast day, unless in some locality an obligatory celebration is assigned to that day.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rufina_and_Secunda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sts Rufina and Secunda are sometimes depicted as two maidens floating in the <a href="/wiki/Tiber_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiber River">Tiber River</a> with weights attached to their necks.
</p><p>In the 1620s, the <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italian</a> painters <a href="/wiki/Il_Morazzone" class="mw-redirect" title="Il Morazzone">Il Morazzone</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giulio_Cesare_Procaccini" title="Giulio Cesare Procaccini">Giulio Cesare Procaccini</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Crespi" title="Giovanni Battista Crespi">Giovanni Battista Crespi</a> collaborated on the <i>"Martyrdom of Saints Rufina and Secunda,"</i> which was praised as <i>"the painting by three hands"</i> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">"il quadro delle tre mani"</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
