<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG/220px-Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="260" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG/330px-Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG/440px-Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1948" data-file-height="2304" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bonifatius_und_Wigbert_Plum.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Wigbert and Saint Boniface. Stained glass window by <a href="/wiki/Alois_Plum" title="Alois Plum">Alois Plum</a>.</div></div></div>
<p><b>Saint Wigbert</b>, (<b>Wihtberht</b>) (May 7, 675 - August 13, 747) born in <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> around 675, was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk and a missionary and <a href="/wiki/Apprenticeship" title="Apprenticeship">disciple</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a> who travelled with the latter in <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a> and northern and central <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> to convert the local tribes to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. His feast day is August 13.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wihtberht&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wihtberht was an Englishman of noble birth,<sup id="cite_ref-loffler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who embraced the monastic life.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It has been supposed that Wihtberht was a monk of Glastonbury, but Löffler finds this improbable.<sup id="cite_ref-loffler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Character">Character</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wihtberht&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Character">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At times an <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Bede.HE_v.9_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede.HE_v.9-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> he was known for his <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> work, <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-eubor_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eubor-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  
He is known to history mainly through <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> and is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Secgan" class="mw-redirect" title="Secgan">Secgan</a> <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Hagiography</a>. <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> described him as venerable,<sup id="cite_ref-vit_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vit-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and outstanding in his religious practice<sup id="cite_ref-eubor_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eubor-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> while <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> admired his <i>contempt of this world</i> and his learning.<sup id="cite_ref-Bede.HE_v.9_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede.HE_v.9-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He worked mainly in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-vit_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vit-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_mission">First mission</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wihtberht&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: First mission">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Around 664 Wihtberht was studying at <a href="/wiki/Rath_Melsigi" title="Rath Melsigi">Rath Melsigi</a> in Ireland. Also there was <a href="/wiki/Ecgberht_of_Ripon" title="Ecgberht of Ripon">Ecgberht of Ripon</a>, who had gone to Ireland to avoid an outbreak of the plague in Northumbria. However, Ecgberht and many of the students contracted the disease. Ecgberht vowed that if he recovered, he would become a "peregrinus" on perpetual pilgrimage from his homeland of Britain and would lead a life of penitential prayer and fasting.<sup id="cite_ref-mayr_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayr-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He began to organize monks in Ireland to proselytize in <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a>, but was dissuaded from going himself by a vision related to him by a monk who had been a disciple of Saint <a href="/wiki/Boisil" title="Boisil">Boisil</a> (the Prior of <a href="/wiki/Melrose_Abbey" title="Melrose Abbey">Melrose</a> under Abbot <a href="/wiki/Eata_of_Hexham" title="Eata of Hexham">Eata</a>), who advised him that this task was not for him. Ecgberht instead sent Wihtberht.<sup id="cite_ref-costambeys_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-costambeys-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Around 680, Wihtberht, went to Frisia, where he spent two years; but owing to the opposition of the ruler <a href="/wiki/Redbad,_King_of_the_Frisians" title="Redbad, King of the Frisians">Redbad, King of the Frisians</a>, Wihtberht was unsuccessful and returned.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Wihtberht’s reputation among the Irish was such that he was celebrated in the ninth-century Irish martyrology, <a href="/wiki/%C3%93engus_of_Tallaght#Félire_Óengusso" title="Óengus of Tallaght">Félire Óengusso</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_mission">Second mission</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wihtberht&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Second mission">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When Boniface felled <a href="/wiki/Thor%27s_Oak" class="mw-redirect" title="Thor&#39;s Oak">Thor's Oak</a> near <a href="/wiki/Fritzlar" title="Fritzlar">Fritzlar</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a> in 723, he built a wooden <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> from the oak's wood and in 724 established a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monastery in Fritzlar. Boniface called Wihtberht from England to become the abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Wihtberht was certainly older than Boniface. He went to Germany about 734, and Boniface made him abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">monastery of Hersfeld</a> in Hesse.  Under the new abbot the school soon became famous. Among his pupils there was <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sturm" title="Saint Sturm">St. Sturmi</a>, the first Abbot of Fulda.<sup id="cite_ref-loffler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>About 737 Boniface transferred him to Thuringia as Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Ohrdruf,_Thuringia" class="mw-redirect" title="Ohrdruf, Thuringia">Ohrdruf</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-loffler_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loffler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where he established a school for missionaries operating in <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>. Wihtberht died in 747, and was initially buried in Fritzlar in the stone basilica he had built to replace the original wooden chapel.  His former student, <a href="/wiki/Lullus" title="Lullus">Lullus</a> later had most of his body (except for a few sacred relics which remained in Fritzlar) interred in a gold and silver shrine in Hersfeld Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, the 
</p><p><br />
Wihtberht is <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the town of <a href="/wiki/Bad_Hersfeld" title="Bad Hersfeld">Bad Hersfeld</a>. His feast day is August 13.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wihtberht&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Priory of St Wigbert (Priorat Sankt Wigberti) is an ecumenical <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> for men, belonging to the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran Church">Lutheran Church</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>, located in <a href="/wiki/Werningshausen" title="Werningshausen">Werningshausen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
