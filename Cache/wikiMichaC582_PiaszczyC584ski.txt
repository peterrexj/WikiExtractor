<p><b>Michael Piaszczynski</b> (1 November 1885 – 18 December 1940) was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">Catholic priest</a> who was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Nazi" class="mw-redirect" title="Nazi">Nazis</a> and killed at <a href="/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp" title="Sachsenhausen concentration camp">Sachsenhausen concentration camp</a>. As a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> he was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 13 June 1999.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Piaszczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Michael Piaszczynski was born in <a href="/wiki/%C5%81om%C5%BCa" title="Łomża">Łomża</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, the son of Ferdynand and Anna Zientara.<sup id="cite_ref-biography_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained to the priesthood on 13 June 1911.<sup id="cite_ref-biography_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was parish priest in <a href="/wiki/Mikasz%C3%B3wka" title="Mikaszówka">Mikaszówka</a>, Poland for a year before moving to <a href="/wiki/Fribourg" title="Fribourg">Fribourg</a> to earn a doctorate in philosophy.<sup id="cite_ref-poland_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poland-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Father Piaszczynski served as a chaplain to a colony of Polish miners in France.<sup id="cite_ref-biography_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> From 1919 to 1935, he served at the Theological Seminary in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_%C5%81om%C5%BCa" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Łomża">Diocese of Łomża</a> and as a faculty member of the <a href="/wiki/Piotr_Skarga" title="Piotr Skarga">Piotr Skarga</a> Secondary School in Łomża.<sup id="cite_ref-poland_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poland-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_Death">Arrest and Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Piaszczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Arrest and Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In September 1939, Father Piaszczynski became director of St. Casimir Secondary School in Sejnach.<sup id="cite_ref-poland_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poland-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was arrested on 7 April 1940 during the <a href="/wiki/Nazi_persecution_of_the_Catholic_Church_in_Poland" title="Nazi persecution of the Catholic Church in Poland">Nazi persecution of the Catholic Church in Poland</a> and was moved to Sachsenhausen on 3 May 1940.<sup id="cite_ref-poland_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poland-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During his detention, Father Piaszczynski was in the habit of giving up his own scarce ration of bread to elderly Jewish inmates. One of them said to him "You, Catholics, believe that the living Christ is to be found in the bread within your churches; I believe that Christ lives in this very bread, which He made you share with me”.<sup id="cite_ref-biography_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Piaszczynski died of physical exhaustion and related illness on 18 December 1940.<sup id="cite_ref-poland_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poland-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Piaszczy%C5%84ski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 13 June 1999, Pope John Paul II beatified Michael Piaszczynski along with the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
