<p>The <b>Archdiocese of Besançon</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Archidiœcesis Bisuntina</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i>Archidiocèse de Besançon</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> ecclesiastical territory or <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese">archdiocese</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.  It comprises the <i><a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9partement" class="mw-redirect" title="Département">département</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Doubs" title="Doubs">Doubs</a> (except for <a href="/wiki/Montb%C3%A9liard" title="Montbéliard">Montbéliard</a>) and the <i>département</i> of <a href="/wiki/Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Haute-Saône">Haute-Saône</a> (except for the canton of <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A9ricourt,_Haute-Saone" class="mw-redirect" title="Héricourt, Haute-Saone">Héricourt</a>).
</p><p>The see is currently <a href="/wiki/Sede_vacante" title="Sede vacante">sede vacante</a>. From 1034 to 1184, the archbishop had civil authority within the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Prince-bishop" title="Prince-bishop">prince-archbishop</a> of Besançon. He gradually lost his civil power to the town council; the city became the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_city_of_Besan%C3%A7on" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial city of Besançon">Imperial city of Besançon</a> in 1184.  The city was annexed by France in stages, eventually being fully subsumed by France in 1792 during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. The Archdiocese of Besançon is a <a href="/wiki/Metropolis_(religious_jurisdiction)" title="Metropolis (religious jurisdiction)">metropolitan see</a> with five <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_diocese" title="Suffragan diocese">suffragan dioceses</a> in its <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">ecclesiastical province</a>: the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Belfort-Montb%C3%A9liard" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Belfort-Montbéliard">Dioceses of Belfort-Montbéliard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Nancy" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Nancy">Nancy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Saint-Claude" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Saint-Claude">Saint–Claude</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Saint-Di%C3%A9" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Saint-Dié">Saint-Dié</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Verdun" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Verdun">Verdun</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_history_of_the_diocese">Early history of the diocese</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Besan%C3%A7on&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early history of the diocese">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg/150px-Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg/225px-Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg/300px-Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg 2x" data-file-width="768" data-file-height="1024" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Ferr%C3%A9ol.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A statue of Ferreolus.</div></div></div>
<p>Local tradition states that the diocese was evangelized by Saints <a href="/wiki/Ferreolus_and_Ferrutio" title="Ferreolus and Ferrutio">Ferreolus and Ferrutio</a> (Ferréol and Ferjeux),<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who were sent here by <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus" title="Irenaeus">St. Irenaeus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lyon">Bishop of Lyon</a>. According to the Catholic encyclopedia, "<a href="/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" title="Louis Duchesne">Louis Duchesne</a> proved that these legends belong to a chain of narratives forged in the first half of the 6th century and of which the "passion" of <a href="/wiki/St._Benignus_of_Dijon" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Benignus of Dijon">St. Benignus of Dijon</a> was the initial link."<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> several popes visited Besançon, among them <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">pope Leo IX</a> who consecrated the altar of the old <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cathedral_of_St._Etienne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cathedral of St. Etienne (page does not exist)">Cathedral of St. Etienne</a> in 1050, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Eugenius III</a> who in 1148 consecrated the church of St. Jean, <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on_Cathedral" title="Besançon Cathedral">the new cathedral</a>. A council was held at Besançon in 1162, presided over by Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick Barbarossa</a>, in the interest of the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159%E2%80%931164)" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159–1164)">Antipope Victor IV</a> against <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>. <a href="/wiki/Guido_of_Burgundy" class="mw-redirect" title="Guido of Burgundy">Guido of Burgundy</a>, who was pope from 1119 to 1123 under the name of <a href="/wiki/Calixtus_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Calixtus II">Calixtus II</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Claude-Adrien_Nonnotte" title="Claude-Adrien Nonnotte">Claude-Adrien Nonnotte</a> (1711–1793), an adversary of <a href="/wiki/Voltaire" title="Voltaire">Voltaire</a>, were natives of Besançon.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbeys_founded_from_the_diocese">Abbeys founded from the diocese</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Besan%C3%A7on&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Abbeys founded from the diocese">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">monastery of Luxeuil</a>, founded by <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">St. Columbanus</a> (d. 615), gave to the diocese of Besançon a series of saints. First came the direct successors of St. Columbanus: the Abbot <a href="/wiki/St._Eustasius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Eustasius">St. Eustasius</a> who founded a celebrated school in this monastery; the Abbot <a href="/wiki/St._Valbert" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Valbert">St. Valbert</a> who sent monks to found the Abbeys of St. Valéry, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_St._Omer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of St. Omer (page does not exist)">St. Omer</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Bertin" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Bertin">St. Bertin</a>, and died in 665; the Abbot <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Ingofroid&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Ingofroid (page does not exist)">St. Ingofroid</a>; <a href="/wiki/Donatus_of_Besan%C3%A7on" title="Donatus of Besançon">St. Donatus</a>, who became Bishop of Besançon; and <a href="/wiki/St._Ansegisus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Ansegisus">St. Ansegisus</a>, author of a celebrated collection of capitularies.
</p><p>The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Lure&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Lure (page does not exist)">Abbey of Lure</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Haute-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Haute-Saône">Haute-Saône</a>) was founded at the beginning of the 7th century by <a href="/wiki/St._D%C3%A9icole" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Déicole">St. Déicole</a> (Deicolus), or Desle, disciple of St. Columbanus; later its abbots were <a href="/wiki/Princes_of_the_Holy_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Princes of the Holy Empire">princes of the Holy Empire</a>. The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Beaume_les_Dames&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Beaume les Dames (page does not exist)">Abbey of Beaume les Dames</a>, founded in the 5th century and in which <a href="/wiki/Gontram" class="mw-redirect" title="Gontram">Gontram</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Burgundy" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Burgundy">King of Burgundy</a>, was buried, was the school where <a href="/wiki/St._Odo" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Odo">St. Odo</a>, afterwards <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Cluny" title="Abbot of Cluny">Abbot of Cluny</a>, studied in the tenth century; at the end of the eighth century there was built near it an abbey for Benedictine nuns, members of the nobility. During the French Revolution the superb church of this abbey was laid waste.  Other saints of the Diocese of Besançon include the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Aldegrin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Aldegrin (page does not exist)">St. Aldegrin</a> (10th century).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_history">Later history</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Besan%C3%A7on&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later history">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Peter_Fourier" title="Peter Fourier">St. Peter Fourier</a> (1565–1640), who inaugurated systematic education for girls, was born in the diocese.  The miracle attributed to the "Sacred Host of Faverney", during a fire in the year 1608, was annually commemorated by elaborate ceremonies. The places of pilgrimage were Notre Dame du Chêne at <a href="/wiki/Scey" title="Scey">Scey</a>; Notre Dame d'Aigremont; the pilgrimage of <a href="/wiki/Peter_II_of_Tarentaise" title="Peter II of Tarentaise">Saint Peter of Tarentaise</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cirey-les-Bellevaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Cirey-les-Bellevaux">Cirey-les-Bellevaux</a>, where St. Pierre de Tarentaise died in 1174; Notre Dame des Jacobins at Besançon; and Notre Dame de la Motte at <a href="/wiki/Vesoul" title="Vesoul">Vesoul</a>.
</p><p>Few 19th-century dioceses have undergone similar territorial changes. The Concordat of 1802 gave the Diocese of Besançon all those districts which, in 1822, constituted the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_St.-Claude" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of St.-Claude">Diocese of St.-Claude</a>. In 1806, Besançon was given jurisdiction over the three parishes of the <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Neufch%C3%A2tel" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Neufchâtel">Principality of Neufchâtel</a> (<a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>) which fell under the control of the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Lausanne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Lausanne">bishopric of Lausanne</a> in 1814. In 1870, after the annexation of <a href="/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Alsace-Lorraine">Alsace-Lorraine</a> by Germany, the district of Belfort was withdrawn from the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Strasburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Strasburg">bishopric of Strasburg</a> and attached to the diocese of Besançon.
</p><p>The metropolitan jurisdiction of Besançon also underwent changes. In 1802 its suffragans were the Bishoprics of <a href="/wiki/Dijon" title="Dijon">Dijon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a> (in Burgundy), <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Metz">Metz</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nancy,_France" title="Nancy, France">Nancy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a> (in Alsace-Lorraine). Under the <a href="/wiki/Bourbon_Restoration_in_France" title="Bourbon Restoration in France">Bourbon Restoration</a>, Dijon and Autun were withdrawn from Besançon, which became the metropolitan of the sees of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Di%C3%A9" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Dié">Saint-Dié</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Verdun" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Verdun">Verdun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belley" title="Belley">Belley</a>. In 1874, after the <a href="/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War" title="Franco-Prussian War">Franco-Prussian War</a>, the churches of Metz and Strasburg were <a href="/wiki/Exemption_(church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Exemption (church)">exempt</a>, under the direct control of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>.
</p><p>On 3 November 1979, <a href="/wiki/Belfort" title="Belfort">Belfort</a>, Montbéliard, and the canton of Héricourt (Haute-Saône) were detached from the diocese of Besançon and constituted into a new autonomous diocese, that of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Belfort-Montb%C3%A9liard" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Belfort-Montbéliard">Belfort-Montbéliard</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
