<p><a href="/wiki/Ab_(Semitic)" title="Ab (Semitic)"><b>Abba</b></a> <b>Or of Nitria</b> (also <i><b>Hor</b> or  <b>Horus</b></i>) was an <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodoxy">Egyptian Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> who lived around the 4th century AD in Egypt in <a href="/wiki/Dalga" title="Dalga">Dalga</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a> (<a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a>), the <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Thebaid</a>, and in the deserts around Shaina. He is one of the lesser-known <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Fathers</a>, but is nevertheless regarded as one of the "chief among monks," being, "a man who stood out among many of the fathers."<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is associated with <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Enaton" title="Theodore of Enaton">Theodore</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sisoes_the_Great" title="Sisoes the Great">Sisoes the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Emory_University_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emory_University-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, at one point during his life, Or was the father of "one thousand [<a href="/wiki/Cenobitic_monasticism" title="Cenobitic monasticism">cenobitic</a><sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>] <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christian_monasticism" title="Eastern Christian monasticism">monks</a>" in the Egyptian Desert.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Or died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;390</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Desert_reclusion">Desert reclusion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Desert reclusion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ab_(Semitic)" title="Ab (Semitic)">Abba</a> (<a href="/wiki/Spiritual_father" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual father">Elder</a>) Or's early life remains unknown. He was likely born in the late 3rd or early 4th century. The name "Or" is of Hebrew origin (<a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: <span lang="he" dir="rtl">אור</span>), meaning "light, brilliance"<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="nonreliable refs such as thinkbabynames.com removed (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Most of his recorded life comes from <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome's</a> account of him in the <i><a href="/wiki/History_of_the_Monks_of_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="History of the Monks of Egypt">History of the Monks of Egypt</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Or was known for his extreme love for mankind, his hospitality, and his humility.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He lived for the majority of his life in austere <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> as a solitary <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in the deserts of <a href="/wiki/Dalga" title="Dalga">Dalga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a> says, "He had lived at first in a remote part of the desert, practicing many works of abstinence, before founding a monastery not far from the city."<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Jerome says, "When formerly the blessed man lived in the desert of <a href="/wiki/Dalga" title="Dalga">Dalga</a> he used to eat roots and herbs, and to drink water when he was able, and he passed the whole time of his life in prayer and praise... he ate only once a week."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">St. Nikolaj Velimirović</a> writes, "He especially endeavored never to speak an untruth. He had great temptations from the demons, but overpowered them all, soberly and courageously."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-reclusion">Post-reclusion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Post-reclusion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
It was said that Or received a vision from an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> in a dream who told him to leave his desert seclusion in old age and to become a teacher of other monks in <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The angel said to him,</p><blockquote><p>"You will be a great nation, and a numerous people will be entrusted to you. Those who will be saved through you will be ten myriads. And you shall be a governor in the new world, and above every one else in the world you shalt have life. For however many people you win in this world, that is the number you will lead in the age to come. Do not hesitate at all. You will never lack anything you need to the end of your life."<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>Or did as the angel commanded and became the spiritual father of many <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorites</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Egyptian_monasticism" class="mw-redirect" title="Egyptian monasticism">Egyptian Desert</a>. <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> said, "And this blessed man had for a very long time lived a life of the utmost austerity at a remote place in the desert, but subsequently he took certain monasteries in the desert which was near Shaina, and gathered together a brotherhood."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a> states that Or was, "the father of many monasteries."<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Or was unlettered, and yet by a divine miracle, he was able to read and memorized <a href="/wiki/Scripture" class="mw-redirect" title="Scripture">Holy Scripture</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">spiritual children</a> attest to his virtue, saying that Or never lied, swore, hurt anyone, or spoke without necessity.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> "And in his prayer to God he took the same pains and strove that all the needs of the brethren might be supplied&#160;..."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A certain Pistos said, "... great was Abba Or's humility."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, the brotherhood's numbers were so great that the resident monks devoted much of their time to making huts to accommodate all of them.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The brotherhood allegedly tamed the arid region of Nitria, "... he planted so many wild trees at that spot that they formed a dense wood in the wilderness."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Anecdotes">Anecdotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Anecdotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Or taught his disciples to always be ready for the Lord to take their souls. It was said of Or and <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Enaton" title="Theodore of Enaton">Theodore</a>, that as they were building a cell out of clay, they said to one another, "If God should visit us now, what should we do?" Then, weeping, they left the clay there and each of them went back into his cell.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<p><a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> also notes that Or practiced exorcism, saying, "And there was added unto him also the gracious gift of being able to cast out devils, and many of them came to him by the constraint [of this gift], against their wills, and would shriek out through his acts."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Jerome ends his writing on Or in the <i>History of the Monks of Egypt</i> by saying,</p><blockquote><p>"Now the beauty of his life and deeds made him so glorious that he was able to gather together very many monks to him, and one saw the congregation of the monks with him in the church like a band of righteous men who were splendid in their garb, and they glorified God with praises continually."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>Or is said to have received <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">the Eucharist</a> daily during his life.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He reposed circa 390,<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> being at least 90 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Or's <a href="/wiki/Troparion" title="Troparion">Troparion</a> is: "<i>'From the earth Or departed to me,' said grace, 'On behalf of Or begotten by you O law.'</i> "<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He is recognized as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. Or's feast day is <a href="/wiki/August_7_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="August 7 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">August 7</a> in the Orthodox Church.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Concerning_the_Nobleman">Concerning the Nobleman</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Concerning the Nobleman">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>One day, a charitable nobleman by the name Longinos desired to visit Or. Longinos sought out one of Or's <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">spiritual children</a> and asked to be brought to the elder. The monk immediately went to Or, saying that Longinos indeed was a very charitable man. The monk then asked Or to permit the nobleman to come and see him. However, Or discerned the true reason why the monk praised Longinos. If Longinos came to their dwelling, he would surely give money to them, and in doing so incite them to <a href="/wiki/Avarice" class="mw-redirect" title="Avarice">avarice</a>. With this discernment, Or refused the nobleman; would not compromise his God-given <a href="/wiki/Divine_grace" title="Divine grace">grace</a> for financial gain.<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He said to the monk, "Truly, there is no need for him to cross this valley in order to see me."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Concerning_the_Palm_Branches">Concerning the Palm Branches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Concerning the Palm Branches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jerome relates another story about Or. One time, Paul, Or's disciple, departed to buy some reeds in the market. He knew that others had been before him and had paid for their goods in deposits. However, Or never paid deposits for anything; instead, he paid the full price at one time. Because of this, the disciple of Or went in search of palm-branches elsewhere. Then a farmer said to the disciple, "Someone has given me a deposit, but he has not come. So why don't you take these palm-branches." He took them and he went back to Or and told him what had happened. The old man clapped his hands together and said (in the <a href="/wiki/Grammatical_person" title="Grammatical person">third-person</a>), "Or is not going to work this year." Or did not allow the palm-branches to come inside his cell, but waited for them to be returned.<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_stories">Other stories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Other stories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
Or once told this story of himself. The <i><a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a></i> confirms that the person in the story was in fact Or himself.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></p><blockquote><p>“I know a certain man in this desert who for three years past has not eaten anything which is of this earth, but an angel comes to him once in three days, and brings him heavenly food and places it in his mouth, and this suffices him instead of meat and drink. And I know that there came to this man in a lying vision devils who were in the forms of horses and chariots of fire and numerous horsemen, as if they had come from a king, and they said unto him, ‘You have become perfect in everything, O man, but now, bow down and worship me, and I will take you up like Elijah.’ Then the monk said in his mind, ‘I worship the King and Redeemer every day, and if this creature were a king he would not ask me to worship him now.’ And when he had said unto him that which was in his mind, ‘I have a King, and I worship Him always, and you are not a king,’ immediately the devil removed himself and was no more found.” <sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>Because of his many ascetic struggles which ailed his body, Or was constantly sickly. As <a href="/wiki/Sisoes_the_Great" title="Sisoes the Great">St. Sisoes</a> said, "Abba Or was ill for eighteen years."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Once, Or's disciples reminded him that it was the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus" title="Resurrection of Jesus">Resurrection</a>. He is said to have raised his hands to heaven and prayed for three days without ceasing.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sayings_of_Or">Sayings of Or</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Sayings of Or">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Or is known for the spiritual guidance that he gave to his <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciples</a>. His teachings are quoted in the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="On_humility">On humility</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: On humility">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>"The crown of the monk is humility."<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>He gave this counsel, "Whenever you want to subdue your high and proud thoughts, examine your conscience carefully: Have you kept all the <a href="/wiki/Ten_Commandments" title="Ten Commandments">commandments</a>? Have you loved your enemies and been kind to them in their misfortunes? Have you counted yourself to be an unprofitable servant and the worst of all sinners? If you find you have done all this, do not therefore think well of yourself as if you had done everything well but realize that even the thought of such things is totally destructive."<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"He who is honoured and praised beyond his merits, will suffer much condemnation, but he who is held as of no account among men will receive glory in heaven."  <sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>When <a href="/wiki/Sisoes_the_Great" title="Sisoes the Great">Sisoes</a> asked Abba Or for some guidance on the spiritual life, the old man said, "In my own opinion, I put myself below all men."<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>He would often say, "Do not speak in your heart against your brother like this: 'I am a man of more sober and austere life than he is,' but put yourself in subjection to the grace of Christ, in the spirit of poverty and genuine charity, or you will be overcome by the spirit of vainglory and lose all you have gained. For it is written in the Scriptures: 'Let him who stands take heed lest he fall.' (<a href="/wiki/1_Corinthians_10" title="1 Corinthians 10">1 Corinthians 10</a>:12) Let your salvation be founded in the Lord."<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Abba Or taught that if you slander your brother, you must say to him, "I have spoken badly of you; let this be my surety that I will not spread this slander any further."<sup id="cite_ref-Wind_Ministries_2017_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wind_Ministries_2017-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_quotes">Other quotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Other quotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>"In all temptation, do not complain about anyone else, but say about yourself, ‘These things happen to me because of my sins.’"<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"If you see that I am thinking adversely about someone, know that he is thinking in the same way about me."<sup id="cite_ref-Ward_1984_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ward_1984-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"What shall I say to you? Go, and do what you see is right; God comes to him who reproaches himself and does violence to himself in everything."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Or said to his disciple Paul, "Be careful never to let an irrelevant word come into this cell."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>He often recited this proverb, "If you are fleeing, flee from men; or the world and the men in it will make you do many foolish things."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"For the monk, this is the celebration of the Resurrection of Christ: to elevate his mind and unite it with God."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"Speak the truth, never tell a lie. I know a man who never swore, never lied, never wished evil to another."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Sisoes_the_Great" title="Sisoes the Great">St. Sisoes</a> asked Or, "Give me, Father, come instruction" to which the abba replied, "Live as you see me live!" Then Abba Sisoes said again, "Tell me more clearly, how should I see you? Does every man hide a secret in himself?" and the abba said, "Behold, I speak to you: Of all God's creations, I consider myself the worst."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Or taught his disciple Paul thus, "From every sin you will easily flee, if only you flee from evil conversation. For every other sin sprouts from this evil. Evil conversation is death to the soul of man; it smothers every good deed in his heart. I will say one more thing, and let it be enough: Repel all vain thoughts; repel iniquitous desires; distance yourself from material cares, and you will attain the immaterial, my son."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_sources">Other sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Other sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lausaic_History"><i>Lausaic History</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Lausaic History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
According to <a href="/wiki/Palladius_(bishop_of_Helenopolis)" class="mw-redirect" title="Palladius (bishop of Helenopolis)">Palladius</a> (Bishop of Helenopolis, born c. 360) in the <i><a href="/wiki/Lausaic_History" class="mw-redirect" title="Lausaic History">Lausaic History</a></i>:</p><blockquote><p>"Now in Mount <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a> [c. 390] there was a certain man whose name was Or, concerning whom men, especially all the brotherhood, testify to many of his triumphs, and also that marvelous and excellent woman Melania, the handmaid of Christ, who went into this mountain before I did. As for me, I never became acquainted with this man. And in his history they say this one thing: "He never told a lie in his life, and he never used oaths; he never uttered a curse, and beyond what was absolutely necessary he never spoke at all."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="History_of_the_Monks_of_Egypt"><i>History of the Monks of Egypt</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: History of the Monks of Egypt">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
Chapter 3 of the <i>History of the Monks of Egypt</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> speaks of a visit to Or. Selected excerpts are as follows:</p><blockquote><p>"And this blessed man had for a very long time lived a life of the utmost austerity at a remote place in the desert, but subsequently he took certain monasteries in the desert which was near Shaina, and gathered together a brotherhood, and he planted so many wild trees at that spot that they formed a dense wood in the wilderness."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"And when the man of God saw us, he rejoiced in us, and saluted us, and straightway he offered up a prayer; and he washed our feet with his own hands, and began to teach us, for he was exceedingly well acquainted with the Scriptures, even as a man who had received the gift from God, and he expounded to us many chapters of the Scriptures, and delivered to us the orthodox faith; moreover, he urged us to prayer, and to partake of the Mysteries."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
"Now this man was more glorious in his life than very many of the fathers."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Prologue_of_Ohrid"><i>The Prologue of Ohrid</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: The Prologue of Ohrid">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><i>The Prologue of Ohrid</i> (an Eastern Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">synaxarium</a>) by <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">St. Nikolaj Velimirović</a> commemorates Or on <a href="/wiki/August_7_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="August 7 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">August 7</a> and includes a 26-line "Hymn of Praise".<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Vitae_Patrum">The <i>Vitae Patrum</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: The Vitae Patrum">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Chapters II and IX of the <i><a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a></i> relate Jerome's account of Or.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Physical_appearance">Physical appearance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Or_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Physical appearance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Rufinus who visited Or described him in this way: "...he had an angelic form and was about ninety years of age; his beard flowed down over his breast, and it was white and beautiful, and his countenance was so glorious that those that saw him were reproved by the sight thereof only."<sup id="cite_ref-Orthodox_2004_7-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orthodox_2004-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a> says, "He was ninety years old [when we saw him], with a very full and splendid silvery beard, a lively face and appearance, reflecting something greater than mere human nature." In Chapter IX it furthermore says, "At the age of ninety he had lost nothing of his physical strength."<sup id="cite_ref-:2_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
