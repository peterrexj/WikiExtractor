					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Blessed André Grasset de Saint-Sauveur</b> (3 April 1758 – 2 September 1792), usually known simply as <b>André Grasset</b>, was a Canadian-born French Catholic priest who was martyred for his faith in Paris during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. In 1926 he became the first Canadian-born person to be beatified.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Escalier_des_martyrs,_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg/300px-Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg/450px-Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg/600px-Escalier_des_martyrs%2C_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Escalier_des_martyrs,_%C3%89glise_Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Escalier des martyrs, Église Saint-Joseph-des-Carmes</div></div></div>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Grasset&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a> to an immigrant from <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, André Grasset de Saint-Sauveur senior (1720-1794),<sup id="cite_ref-silva_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silva-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who had come to Canada (then known as <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a>) to be the secretary to the <a href="/wiki/Governor_General_of_New_France" title="Governor General of New France">Governor General of New France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-silva_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silva-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  After his first wife died, he remarried to Marie-Josephte Quesnel-Fonblanche<sup id="cite_ref-silva_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silva-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and had five children, of whom André was the second-born.<sup id="cite_ref-CRC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CRC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
In 1764, at the age of six, he accompanied his father, who had decided to return to France following the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)" title="Treaty of Paris (1763)">Treaty of Paris</a> in 1763.  They lived in <a href="/wiki/Calais" title="Calais">Calais</a>.  He studied at the <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Sainte-Barbe" title="Collège Sainte-Barbe">Collège Sainte-Barbe</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, then entered the priesthood in <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> being ordained there in 1783.<sup id="cite_ref-CRC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CRC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1791 the <a href="/wiki/National_Constituent_Assembly_(France)" title="National Constituent Assembly (France)">National Constituent Assembly</a> obliged all members of the clergy, under pain of death, to sign the <a href="/wiki/Civil_Constitution_of_the_Clergy" title="Civil Constitution of the Clergy">Civil Constitution of the Clergy</a>, which would nullify their allegiance to the Pope and essentially render them servants of the state. Although a small number of bishops and priests complied, most refused.<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Grasset sought refuge with the <a href="/wiki/Eudist_Fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Eudist Fathers">Eudist Fathers</a> in the Maison des Tourettes, but was captured in 1792 and imprisoned in the former Carmelite convent now known as the <a href="/wiki/Carmes_Prison" title="Carmes Prison">Carmes Prison</a>.
</p><p>On 2 September 1792, along with 3 bishops and 92 other priests also held at the prison,<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> they were once again asked to sign the Civil Constitution but all replied that their conscience forbade them to do so. All 96 clergy were then killed by the guards, using bayonets, swords and spikes, and their bodies disposed of in ditches and drains around Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In addition, 72 priests at the Seminary of Saint-Firmin, 21 priests at the Abbey of Saint-Germain and 3 priests at the Prison de la Force were similarly murdered.<sup id="cite_ref-silva_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-silva-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In the ensuing few days as many as 1,400 people were murdered.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Grasset&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>André Grasset was declared Venerable by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> under a Decree of Martyrdom on 1 October 1926,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and he was among the 188 bishops, priests, monks, nuns and laypeople <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Holy_September_Martyrs" title="Holy September Martyrs">Holy September Martyrs</a> on 17 October 1926, being the first Canadian-born person to be beatified.<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Memorials">Memorials</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Grasset&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Memorials">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Andr%C3%A9-Grasset" title="Collège André-Grasset">Collège André-Grasset</a>, a post-secondary institution in Montreal was founded by the Sulpician order in 1927.
</p><p>The altar of the Chapel of the Blessed Sacrament in <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame_Basilica_(Montreal)" title="Notre-Dame Basilica (Montreal)">Notre Dame Basilica</a>, Montreal, is dedicated to the martyrs of the revolution. A stained-glass window bearing Grasset's image is also in the Basilica.<sup id="cite_ref-kalb_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kalb-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
