<p><b>Rafqa Pietra Chobok</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a>: رفقا بطرسيّة شبق , June 29, 1832 – March 23, 1914), also known as <b>Saint Rafka</b> and <b>Saint Rebecca</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanese</a> <a href="/wiki/Maronite_Church" title="Maronite Church">Maronite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on June 10, 2001.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_youth">Birth and youth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Birth and youth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rafka was born in Himlaya, in <a href="/wiki/Matn_District" title="Matn District">Matn District</a>, on June 29, 1832, the Feast of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter">St. Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Paul">St. Paul</a>, the only child of Saber Mourad El Rayess and Rafqa Gemayel, and was <a href="/wiki/Baptise" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptise">baptised</a> <b>Boutrossieh</b> (pronounced in Arabic as the feminine of Peter). Her mother died when she was seven years of age. In 1843, her father experienced financial difficulties and sent her to work as a servant for four years in Damascus at the home of Assaad Al-Badawi. She returned home in 1847 to find that her father had remarried.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Boutrossieh was 14 years old, her stepmother wanted her to marry the stepmother's brother, and her maternal aunt wanted her to marry her son. Boutrossieh did not want to marry either of the men and this caused a great deal of discord in her family. One day, while she was coming back from the fountain, holding her jar, she overheard them arguing. She asked God to help her deal with the problem. She then decided to become a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and went straight to the Convent of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Liberation" title="Our Lady of Liberation">Our Lady of Liberation</a> at <a href="/wiki/Bikfaya" title="Bikfaya">Bikfaya</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Boutrossieh's father and stepmother tried to take her back home but she did not want to go. They returned home, saddened, and from then on they never saw her again.
</p><p>Boutrossieh's kinsman, Joseph Gemayel and his family founded a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> for women that provided them with full-time education as well as religious instruction. Boutrossieh's name, Pierine (in French), was listed last among the first four candidates of the Daughters of Mary of the Immaculate Conception ("Mariamettes", in French) in Gemayel's notebook dated January 1, 1853.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was 21.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mariamette_Sisters">Mariamette Sisters</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mariamette Sisters">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1860, while still stationed in Ghazir, Rafqa's superiors sent her on a temporary posting to Deir-el-Qamar, in Mount Lebanon - Shouf, where she helped the Jesuit mission. In less than two months the <a href="/wiki/Druze" title="Druze">Druze</a> killed 7,771 people and destroyed 360 villages, 560 churches, 28 schools, and 42 convents. Sister Rafqa saved one child's life by hiding him in the skirts of her habit as he was being chased by some soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa was deeply affected by the massacres.<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following a year of postulancy, Rafqa received the habit of her congregation on the feast of St. Joseph, March 19, 1861. She took her first temporary <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> on March 19, 1862 at the age of thirty.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Sister Rafqa's first assignment in the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> was charge of the kitchen service in the Jesuit school in <a href="/wiki/Ghazir" title="Ghazir">Ghazir</a>, where she spent seven years. She was placed in charge of the workers and had the task of giving them religious instruction in a spinning mill in Scerdanieh, where she remained for two months.
</p><p>Two years later, Sister Rafqa was sent to teach at <a href="/wiki/Byblos" title="Byblos">Byblos</a>, where she remained for one year before going to Ma'ad to establish a school there at the request of Antoun (Anthony) Issa, a prominent citizen.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1871, the "Mariamettes" religious institute merged with another to form the Order of the Sacred Hearts of Jesus and Mary. The <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">Religious Sisters</a> were given the option to join the new congregation, or a different one, or to resume lay status. Rafqa decided to become a cloistered nun rather than a teaching Sister, and, after praying in the Church of St. George, made the decision to join the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Baladita Order</a>, the monastic order now named the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Lebanese Maronite Order of St. Anthony</a>, founded in 1695, and told Antoun Issa of her decision. He offered to pay the requisite dowry.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That same night, Rafqa dreamed of three men. One with a white beard, one dressed like a soldier and the third was an old man. She recounted "One of the men said to me, 'Become a nun in the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Baladita Order</a>'. I woke up very happy ... and went to Antoun Issa, bursting with joy ... and I told him about my dream." Antoun identified the men as Anthony of <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Qozhaya" title="Monastery of Qozhaya">Qozhaia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Abbot" class="mw-redirect" title="Anthony the Abbot">Anthony the Abbot</a>) from whom the order was inspired, the soldier was <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a>, to whom the church in Ma'ad was dedicated, and the third could only be a Baladita monk. Rafqa decided to leave immediately for the Monastery of St. Simon in Al-Qarn. Antoun gave her the money as promised as well as a letter of recommendation to the archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_nun_of_the_Lebanese_Maronite_Order">A nun of the Lebanese Maronite Order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: A nun of the Lebanese Maronite Order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastery_of_St._Simon">Monastery of St. Simon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monastery of St. Simon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On July 12, 1871, at the age of thirty-nine, Rafqa began her novitiate into the new monastery and then on August 25, 1873, she "professed her perpetual vows of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity and obedience">poverty, chastity and obedience</a> in the spirit of the strict Rule of the Baladita Order". Her new name was that of her mother's, Rafqa, (Rebecca),<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the name of Abraham's great granddaughter and wife of his son Isaac. St. Simon Monastery was situated on a high altitude, where the winters were very harsh. The nuns followed a very rigid daily schedule throughout the year. Prayer and manual labour became the rule of their daily lives. The nuns planted and harvested vegetables and grain in the surrounding fields. They also cultivated silkworms and sewed vestments for churches.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa remained in this monastery until 1897.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Illness">Illness</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Illness">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4><p>
In 1885 Rafqa decided not to join the nuns for a walk around the monastery. In her autobiographical account she wrote, </p><blockquote><p>It was the first Sunday of the Rosary. I did not accompany them. Before leaving each of the nuns came and said to me, 'Pray for me sister.' There were some who asked me to say seven decades of the Rosary ... I went to the Church and started to pray. Seeing that I was in good health and that I had never been sick in my life, I prayed to God in this way, 'Why, O my God, have you distance yourself from me and have abandoned me. You have never visited me with sickness! Have you perhaps abandoned me?'"<sup id="cite_ref-australia_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Rafqa continued in her account to her superior, the next night after the prayer "At the moment of sleeping I felt a most violent pain spreading above my eyes to the point that I reached the state you see me in, blind and paralyzed, and as I myself had asked for sickness I could not allow myself to complain or murmur."<sup id="cite_ref-australia_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The mother superior sent Rafqa to Tripoli, where she submitted to a painful medical examination.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> For two years, she suffered. She went to several doctors who all agreed that there was nothing they could do. Upon the persuasion of the priest Estefan, Rafqa consulted a visiting American doctor who strongly suggested that the eye be removed. Estefan later recounted, "Before the operation I asked the doctor to anesthetize the eye so that Rafqa would not feel any pain but she refused. The doctor made her sit down and pushed a long scalpel ... into her eye ... the eye popped out and fell on the ground, palpitating slightly.... Rafqa didn't complain ... but only said, 'in communion with Christ's Passion'." The pain was then all concentrated in her left eye and nothing could be done.<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gradually Rafqa became blind. She continued to experience intense pain in her head, but considered this an opportunity to share in Jesus' passion.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa did not let this pain isolate her from the community. She continued to spin wool and cotton and knitted stockings for the other sisters; she participated in choral prayer.
</p><p>Due to the harsh winters at the Monastery of St. Simon, Rafqa was allowed to spend the coldest months on the Lebanese coast as a guest of the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity</a> and then of the residence of the Maronite Order. Unable to observe the Rule at these locales, Rafqa asked to be taken to the Monastery of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Elias_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint Elias (disambiguation)">St. Elias</a> at El Rass, which belonged to her order.
</p>

<p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">In 1897, Rafqa was one of six nuns sent to found the order's new Monastery of St. Joseph of Gerbata in Ma'ad, along with Mother Ursula Doumit, the superior, where Rafqa remained for the last 17 years of her life. It was here that her suffering increased.</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">In 1907 Rafqa confided to Mother Ursula that she felt a pain in her legs, "as if someone were sticking lances in them and pain in my toes as if they were being pulled off". This began the long list of sufferings and pains Rafqa endured for the last seven years of her life.</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Based on direct evidence<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag may use weasel words or too-vague attribution. (August 2021)">by whom?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and on the autopsy of Rafqa's remains in 1927, <span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">she had become paralyzed due to complete <a href="/wiki/Disarticulation" title="Disarticulation">disarticulation</a> in her wrist and finger joints, while the pain continued in her head, her devastated eye sockets and her nosebleeds ... completely immobile, her lower jaw touched her benumbed knee.</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">Even in this state, Rafqa was able to crawl to the chapel on the feast of <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christi_(feast)" class="mw-redirect" title="Corpus Christi (feast)">Corpus Christi</a> to the amazement of all the sisters. When asked about this, Rafqa replied, "I don't know. I asked God to help me and suddenly I felt myself slipping from the bed with my legs hanging down; I fell on the floor and crawled to the chapel."</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">On a separate occasion, when asked by her superior if she would like to see, Rafqa responded, "I would like to see for at least an hour, to be able to look at you." In an instant the superior could see Rafqa smile and suddenly said, "Look, I can see now." Not believing her, Sister Ursula put her to the test asking her to identify several objects. Shortly thereafter, Rafqa fell into a deep sleep for about two hours. Sister Ursula became worried and tried repeatedly to awaken her. Upon waking, Rafqa explained that she had entered a large, beautifully decorated building with baths full of water and people crowding to enter them; she went with them. Sister Ursula asked her why she came back; why she didn't continue to walk. Rafqa explained, "You called me, and I came."</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Rafqa's obedience and love for her superior is quite evident in this account. <span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">For a nun, the superior, "as the Rule puts it, represents Christ and is owed respect, obedience and love. Despite her condition, Rafqa did nothing without the Superior's permission."</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">Before dying, Rafqa told of her life to Mother Ursula Doumit, superior of the monastery in which she died, "There is nothing important in my life that is worthy of being recorded ... my mother died when I was seven years old. After her death my father married for a second time."</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><span class="citation-needed-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">Three days before her death, Rafqa said, "I am not afraid of death which I have waited for a long time. God will let me live through my death."</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Then on March 23, 1914, four minutes after receiving final absolution and the plenary indulgence, she died.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_and_canonization">Beatification and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Beatification and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_(St._Louis,_Missouri)_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/220px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/330px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/440px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2664" data-file-height="1499" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_(St._Louis,_Missouri)_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of St. Rafqa at <a href="/wiki/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_(St._Louis,_Missouri)" title="St. Raymond Maronite Cathedral (St. Louis, Missouri)">St. Raymond Maronite Cathedral (St. Louis, Missouri)</a></div></div></div>
<p>On June 9, 1984, the eve of <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>, in the presence of the Pope John Paul II, the decree approving the miracle of Elizabeth Ennakl, who was said to have been completely cured of uterine cancer in 1938 at the tomb of Rafka, was promulgated.
</p><p>On November 16, 1985, Pope John Paul II declared Rafqa Al Rayess a <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>, and on June 10, 2001, he <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">proclaimed her to be a saint</a> at a solemn ceremony in the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
