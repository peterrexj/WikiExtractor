<p><b>Rafqa Pietra Chobok</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a>: رفقا بطرسيّة شبق , June 29, 1832 - March 23, 1914), also known as <b>Saint Rafka</b> and <b>Saint Rebecca</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanese</a> <a href="/wiki/Maronite_Church" title="Maronite Church">Maronite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on June 10, 2001.
</p><p>She is a patron of lost parents and the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_youth">Birth and youth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Birth and youth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rafka was born in Himlaya, in <a href="/wiki/Matn_District" title="Matn District">Matn District</a>, on June 29, 1832, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Feast of Saints Peter and Paul</a>, the only child of Saber Mourad El Rayess and Rafqa Gemayel, and was <a href="/wiki/Baptise" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptise">baptised</a> <b>Boutrossieh</b> (the Arabic feminine of <i>Peter</i>). Her mother died when she was seven years old. In 1843, her father experienced financial difficulties and sent her to work as a servant for four years in <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a> at the home of Assaad Al-Badawi. She returned home in 1847 to find that her father had remarried.<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Boutrossieh was 14 years old, her stepmother wanted her to marry her brother, while her maternal aunt wanted her to marry her son. Boutrossieh did not want to marry either man, and this caused much discord in her family. One day, while she was coming back from the fountain holding her jar, she overheard them arguing. She asked God to help her deal with the problem. She then decided to become a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and went straight to the Convent of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Liberation" title="Our Lady of Liberation">Our Lady of Liberation</a> at <a href="/wiki/Bikfaya" title="Bikfaya">Bikfaya</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Boutrossieh's father and stepmother tried to take her back home but she refused. They returned home dismayed, and from then on never saw her again.
</p><p>Boutrossieh's kinsman, Joseph Gemayel, and his family founded a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> for women that provided them with full-time education as well as religious instruction. Boutrossieh's name, <i>Pierine</i> (in French), was listed last among the first four candidates of the Daughters of Mary of the Immaculate Conception ("<i>Mariamettes</i>", in French) in Gemayel's notebook dated January 1, 1853.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was 21.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mariamette_Sisters">Mariamette Sisters</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mariamette Sisters">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1860, while still stationed in Ghazir, Rafqa's superiors sent her on a temporary posting to Deir-el-Qamar, in Mount Lebanon - Shouf, where she helped the Jesuit mission. In less than two months the <a href="/wiki/Druze" title="Druze">Druze</a> killed 7,771 people and destroyed 360 villages, 560 churches, 28 schools, and 42 convents. Sister Rafqa saved one child's life by hiding him in the skirts of her habit as he was being chased by some soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-va_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa was deeply affected by the massacres.<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following a year of postulancy, Rafqa received the habit of her congregation on the feast of St. Joseph, March 19, 1861. She took her first temporary <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> on March 19, 1862 at the age of thirty.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Sister Rafqa's first assignment in the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> was in charge of the kitchen service in the Jesuit school in <a href="/wiki/Ghazir" title="Ghazir">Ghazir</a>, where she spent seven years. She was placed in charge of the workers and had the task of giving them religious instruction in a spinning mill in Scerdanieh, where she remained for two months. In her free time she studied Arabic, calligraphy and mathematics.<sup id="cite_ref-CHS_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CHS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two years later, Sister Rafqa was sent to teach at <a href="/wiki/Byblos" title="Byblos">Byblos</a>, where she remained for one year before going to Ma'ad to establish a school there at the request of Antoun (Anthony) Issa, a prominent citizen.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1871, the "Mariamettes" religious institute merged with another to form the Order of the Sacred Hearts of Jesus and Mary. The <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">Religious Sisters</a> were given the option to join the new congregation, or a different one, or to resume lay status. Rafqa decided to become a cloistered nun rather than a teaching Sister, and, after praying in the Church of St. George, made the decision to join the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Baladita Order</a>, the monastic order now named the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Lebanese Maronite Order of St. Anthony</a>, founded in 1695, and told Antoun Issa of her decision. He offered to pay the requisite dowry.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That same night, Rafqa dreamed of three men. One with a white beard, one dressed like a soldier and the third was an old man. She recounted "One of the men said to me, 'Become a nun in the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Baladita Order</a>'. I woke up very happy ... and went to Antoun Issa, bursting with joy ... and I told him about my dream." Antoun identified the men as Anthony of <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Qozhaya" title="Monastery of Qozhaya">Qozhaia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Abbot" class="mw-redirect" title="Anthony the Abbot">Anthony the Abbot</a>) from whom the order was inspired, the soldier was <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a>, to whom the church in Ma'ad was dedicated, and the third could only be a Baladita monk. Rafqa decided to leave immediately for the Monastery of St. Simon in Al-Qarn. Antoun gave her the money as promised as well as a letter of recommendation to the archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-brooklyn_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brooklyn-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_nun_of_the_Lebanese_Maronite_Order">A nun of the Lebanese Maronite Order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: A nun of the Lebanese Maronite Order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastery_of_St._Simon">Monastery of St. Simon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monastery of St. Simon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On July 12, 1871, at the age of thirty-nine, Rafqa began her novitiate into the new monastery and then on August 25, 1873, she "professed her <a href="/wiki/Perpetual_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Perpetual vows">perpetual vows</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity and obedience">poverty, chastity and obedience</a> in the spirit of the strict Rule of the Baladita Order". She took on the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">new name</a> that was her mother’s: <i>Rafqa</i>, (<a href="/wiki/Rebecca" title="Rebecca">Rebecca</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> the name of <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a>'s great-niece and wife of his son <a href="/wiki/Isaac" title="Isaac">Isaac</a>. 
</p><p>St. Simon Monastery was situated on a high altitude, where the winters were very harsh. The nuns followed a very rigid daily schedule throughout the year. Prayer and manual labour became the rule of their daily lives. The nuns planted and harvested vegetables and grain in the surrounding fields. They also cultivated <a href="/wiki/Silkworms" class="mw-redirect" title="Silkworms">silkworms</a> and sewed vestments for churches.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa remained in this monastery until 1897.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Illness">Illness</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Illness">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4><p>
In 1885, Rafqa decided not to join the nuns for a walk around the monastery. In her autobiographical account she wrote, </p><blockquote><p>It was the first Sunday of the Rosary. I did not accompany them. Before leaving each of the nuns came and said to me, 'Pray for me sister.' There were some who asked me to say seven decades of the Rosary ... I went to the Church and started to pray. Seeing that I was in good health and that I had never been sick in my life, I prayed to God in this way, 'Why, O my God, have you distance yourself from me and have abandoned me. You have never visited me with sickness! Have you perhaps abandoned me?'"<sup id="cite_ref-australia_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Rafqa continued in her account to her superior, the next night after the prayer "At the moment of sleeping I felt a most violent pain spreading above my eyes to the point that I reached the state you see me in, blind and paralyzed, and as I myself had asked for sickness I could not allow myself to complain or murmur."<sup id="cite_ref-australia_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The mother superior sent Rafqa to <a href="/wiki/Tripoli,_Lebanon" title="Tripoli, Lebanon">Tripoli</a>, where she had a painful medical examination.<sup id="cite_ref-australia_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-australia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She suffered two years, with several doctors concluding there was nothing they could do. Upon the persuasion of the priest Estefan, Rafqa consulted a visiting American doctor who strongly suggested that the affected eye be removed. Estefan later recounted, "Before the operation I asked the doctor to anesthetize the eye so that Rafqa would not feel any pain but she refused. The doctor made her sit down and pushed a long scalpel ... into her eye ... the eye popped out and fell on the ground, palpitating slightly.... Rafqa didn't complain ... but only said, 'in communion with Christ's <a href="/wiki/Passion_of_Jesus" title="Passion of Jesus">Passion</a>'." The pain was then all concentrated in her left eye and nothing could be done.<sup id="cite_ref-maronite_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maronite-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gradually, Rafqa became blind. She continued to experience intense pain in her head, but considered this an opportunity to share in Jesus' Passion.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Rafqa did not let this pain isolate her from the community. She continued to spin wool and cotton, and knitted stockings for the other sisters; she also participated in choral prayer.
</p><p>Due to the harsh winters at the Monastery of St. Simon, Rafqa was permitted to spend the coldest months on the Lebanese coast as a guest of the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity</a> and then of the residence of the Maronite Order. Unable to observe the Rule at these locales, Rafqa asked to be taken to the Monastery of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Elias_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint Elias (disambiguation)">St. Elias</a> at El Rass, which belonged to her order.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastery_of_St._Joseph">Monastery of St. Joseph</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Monastery of St. Joseph">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1897, the Lebanese Maronite Order decided to build a monastery of St. Joseph al Dahr in Jrabta, <a href="/wiki/Batroun" title="Batroun">Batroun</a>. Rafqa was one of six nuns, led by Mother Ursula Doumit, who were sent to the new monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>By 1899 she was completely blind and paralysed. She was confined to bed and used her time to knit socks.<sup id="cite_ref-CHS_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CHS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Near the end of her life, she was asked by Sister Ursula if she would like to see; Rafqa prayed to be able to see for an hour, and this prayer was answered.<sup id="cite_ref-MP_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MP-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On March 23, 1914, four minutes after receiving the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">Last Rites</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Plenary_indulgence" class="mw-redirect" title="Plenary indulgence">plenary indulgence</a>, she died. Her body was buried in the monastery cemetery and was later transferred to the monastery chapel.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_and_canonization">Beatification and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Beatification and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_(St._Louis,_Missouri)_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/220px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/330px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg/440px-St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_%28St._Louis%2C_Missouri%29_-_relic_of_Rafqa_Pietra_Choboq_Ar-Ray%C3%A8s.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2664" data-file-height="1499" /></a><figcaption>A <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of Saint Rafqa (here still “Blessed”) at <a href="/wiki/St._Raymond_Maronite_Cathedral_(St._Louis,_Missouri)" title="St. Raymond Maronite Cathedral (St. Louis, Missouri)">St. Raymond Maronite Cathedral (St. Louis, Missouri)</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>On June 9, 1984, the <a href="/wiki/Vigil_(liturgy)" title="Vigil (liturgy)">vigil</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>, in the presence of the Pope John Paul II, the decree approving the miracle of Elizabeth Ennakl, who was said to have been completely cured of <a href="/wiki/Uterine_cancer" title="Uterine cancer">uterine cancer</a> in 1938 at the tomb of Rafqa, was promulgated.
</p><p>On November 16, 1985, Pope John Paul II declared Rafqa Al Rayess a <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>, and on June 10, 2001, he <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">proclaimed her to be a saint</a> at a solemn ceremony in the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
