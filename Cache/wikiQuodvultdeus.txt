<p><b>Quodvultdeus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> for "what God wills", died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 450 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>) was a fifth-century <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Father</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Carthage" title="Archdiocese of Carthage">Bishop of Carthage</a> who was exiled to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>. He was known to have been living in <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> around 407 and became a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> in 421 AD. He corresponded with <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, who served as Quodvultdeus' spiritual teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Augustine also dedicated some of his writings to Quodvultdeus.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Quodvultdeus was exiled when <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> was captured by the <a href="/wiki/Vandals" title="Vandals">Vandals</a> led by King <a href="/wiki/Gaiseric" title="Gaiseric">Gaiseric</a>, who followed <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>. Tradition states that he and other churchmen (such as <a href="/wiki/Gaudiosus_of_Naples" title="Gaudiosus of Naples">Gaudiosus of Naples</a>) were loaded onto leaky ships that landed at Naples around 439 AD and Quodvultdeus established himself in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He would go on to convert dozens of Arian <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">orthodox Christian</a> faith during his lifetime.
</p><p>One of the mosaic burial portraits in the Galleria dei Vescovi in the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Gennaro" title="Catacombs of San Gennaro">Catacombs of San Gennaro</a> depicts Quodvultdeus.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Quodvultdeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BSB_Clm_14098,_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg/170px-BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="241" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg/255px-BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg/340px-BSB_Clm_14098%2C_f._61v_-_Quodvultdeus_opening_page.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1081" data-file-height="1530" /></a><figcaption>Opening page of a 9th-century manuscript of the <i>Sermon against Jews, Pagans and Arians</i> by Quodvultdeus, erroneously attributed to <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Bavarian_State_Library" title="Bavarian State Library">Bavarian State Library</a> Clm 14098, f. 61v)</figcaption></figure>
<p>None of the surviving writings by Quodvultdeus were transmitted under his name. While <i>Liber promissionum et praedicatorum Dei</i> was attributed to <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a> already in the 6th century, Quodvultdeus's sermons came down to us in <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustinian</a> collections. In 1914 <a href="/wiki/Germain_Morin" title="Germain Morin">Germain Morin</a> was the first to suppose that 12 sermons by Pseudo-Augustine were actually written by Quodvultdeus.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1920 <a href="/w/index.php?title=Desiderius_Franses&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Desiderius Franses (page does not exist)">Desiderius Franses</a> showed this hypothese to be highly plausible.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="List_of_writings">List of writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Quodvultdeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: List of writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><i>Liber promissionum et praedicatorum Dei</i> ("Book of promises and predictions of God")</li>
<li>Thirteen sermons:</li></ul>
<ol><li><i>Contra iudaeos, paganos et arrianos</i> ("Against Jews, Pagans and Arians")</li>
<li><i>Adversus quinque haereses</i> ("On five heresies")</li>
<li><i>De symbolo I</i> ("On the creed I")</li>
<li><i>De symbolo II</i> ("On the creed II")</li>
<li><i>De symbolo III</i> ("On the creed III")</li>
<li><i>De quattuor virtutibus caritatis</i> ("On the four virtues of love")</li>
<li><i>De cantico novo</i> ("On the new song")</li>
<li><i>De ultima quarta feria</i> ("On the last Wednesday")</li>
<li><i>De cataclismo</i> ("On the cataclysm")</li>
<li><i>De tempore barbarico I</i> ("On barbaric times I")</li>
<li><i>De accedentibus ad gratiam I</i> ("On the approach to grace I")</li>
<li><i>De accedentibus ad gratiam II</i> ("On the approach to grace II")</li>
<li><i>De tempore barbarico II</i> ("On barbaric times II")</li></ol>
<ul><li>Two letters to Augustine (Ep. CCXXI and CCXXIII)</li></ul>
