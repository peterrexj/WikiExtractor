<p><b>Cungagnaq</b> (Russian&#58; <span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Чукагнак</span></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">tr.</a></small> <span title="Russian-language text"><i lang="ru-Latn">Chukagnak</i></span>; date of birth unknown - d. 1815) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> (as <b>Peter the <a href="/wiki/Aleut" title="Aleut">Aleut</a></b>; Russian&#58; <span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Пётр Алеу́т</span></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">tr.</a></small> <span title="Russian-language text"><i lang="ru-Latn">Pyotr Aleút</i></span>) by some jurisdictions of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.  He was allegedly a native of <a href="/wiki/Kodiak_Island" title="Kodiak Island">Kodiak Island</a> (<a href="/wiki/Alutiiq_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Alutiiq people">Alutiiq or Sugpiaq</a>), and is said to have received the <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> name of <b>Peter</b> when he was baptized into the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> faith by the monks of <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Alaska" title="Herman of Alaska">St Herman</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionaries</a> operating in the north.<sup id="cite_ref-allsaints_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allsaints-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He is purported to have been captured by <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> soldiers near "San Pedro" and tortured and killed at the instigation of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priests either there or at a nearby location. At the time identified for his death, <a href="/wiki/Alta_California" title="Alta California">California</a> was Spanish territory, and Spain was worried about <a href="/wiki/Russian_colonization_of_the_Americas" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian colonization of the Americas">Russian advances</a> southwards from <a href="/wiki/Russian_Alaska" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Alaska">Alaska</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-umich_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-umich-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Hubert_Howe_Bancroft" title="Hubert Howe Bancroft">Hubert Howe Bancroft</a>, in his multi-volume <i>History of California</i>, only notes that, in connection with an incident wherein a <a href="/wiki/Russian_fur-hunting" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian fur-hunting">Russian fur-hunting</a> expedition was taken into custody after declining to leave San Pedro; one Russian source accused "the Spaniards of cruelty to the captives, stating that according to Kuskof’s<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> report one Aleut who refused to become a Catholic died from ill-treatment received from the padre at San Francisco."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Aleut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the most fully developed version of the story, in 1815 a group of Russian employees of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_American_Company" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian American Company">Russian American Company</a> and their <a href="/wiki/Aleut_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Aleut people">Aleut</a> <a href="/wiki/Seal_(mammal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Seal (mammal)">seal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Otter" title="Otter">otter</a> <a href="/wiki/Hunter" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunter">hunters</a>, including Peter, was captured by <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> soldiers, while hunting illicitly for seals near San Pedro, (which has variably been interpreted as either <a href="/wiki/San_Pedro,_Los_Angeles" title="San Pedro, Los Angeles">San Pedro, Los Angeles</a><sup id="cite_ref-primary-sources_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-primary-sources-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> or as <a href="/wiki/San_Pedro_y_San_Pablo_Asistencia" title="San Pedro y San Pablo Asistencia">San Pedro y San Pablo Asistencia</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Pacifica,_California" title="Pacifica, California">Pacifica, California</a>).  According to the original account, the soldiers took them to "the mission in Saint-Pedro" for interrogation.<sup id="cite_ref-original-account_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-original-account-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> One Russian source states that after being taken prisoner near modern Los Angeles, the captives were taken to Mission Dolores—that is, modern <a href="/wiki/San_Francisco" title="San Francisco">San Francisco</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-umich_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-umich-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> With threats of torture, the Roman Catholic priests attempted to force the Aleuts to deny their Orthodox faith and to convert to Roman Catholicism.
</p><p>When the Aleuts refused, the priest had a toe severed from each of Peter's feet. Peter still refused to renounce his faith and the Spanish priest ordered a group of <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">Native Americans</a>, indigenous to California, to cut off each finger of Peter's hands, one joint at a time, finally removing both his hands. They eventually disemboweled him, making him a martyr to the Eastern Orthodox faith.  The Spanish captors were about to torture the next Aleut when orders were received to release the other Russian and Native Alaskan prisoners.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Aleut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>An account of the martyrdom of Peter the Aleut is contained in a lengthy letter written on November 22, 1865, by <a href="/wiki/Semyon_Ivanovich_Yanovsky" class="mw-redirect" title="Semyon Ivanovich Yanovsky">Semyon Yanovsky</a> to Damascene, abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Valaam_Monastery" title="Valaam Monastery">Valaam Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Yanovsky (1789–1876), who is also one of the chief sources of information about <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Alaska" title="Herman of Alaska">St. Herman of Alaska</a>, was chief manager of the Russian colonies from 1818 to 1820. In the letter he was reporting on an incident that he had heard from a supposed eyewitness, and that had taken place fifty years earlier in 1815.  The letter contains the description of Peter being tortured by "Jesuits" but this would have been virtually impossible, as the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit order">Jesuit order</a> had been expelled from all Spanish territories in 1767,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> suppressed generally in 1773,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and had only been reconstituted in 1814 (one year before Peter's alleged death). In 1815 there were no Jesuits within several thousand miles of California, as the reconstitution of the Jesuits in New Spain (that is, Mexico) would not take place until 1816.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> There were only <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a> in California at the time, and it would be highly unlikely that anyone could confuse members of the two well-known and very dissimilar orders. Yanovsky adds, "At the time I reported all this to the Head Office in <a href="/wiki/St._Petersburg" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Petersburg">St. Petersburg</a>." And indeed, this earlier communication, his official dispatch to the company's main office—dated Feb. 15, 1820, five years after the event—also relates the story of St. Peter's martyrdom, albeit with different details.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The most significant difference is that Yanovsky's original brief letter of 1820 accompanied a Russian translation of an account given in 1819 by a Kodiak Islander with the Russian name "Ivan Kiglay".  This is the only account that purports to be from a witness, and any differences found in other accounts (including in those of Yanovsky himself) are additions or embroideries that lack foundation or support.  Kiglay's account describes the capture of Russian-led fur poachers by Spanish soldiers in the vicinity of San Pedro Bay (the modern Port of Los Angeles) and taken to "the mission in Saint-Pedro".  (As there was no mission or settlement at San Pedro, it is unclear where the party was supposed to have been taken; the nearest mission would have been San Gabriel, although the non-mission village of Los Angeles would have been closer.)  While the rest of the prisoners are removed to <a href="/wiki/Mission_Santa_Barbara" title="Mission Santa Barbara">Mission Santa Barbara</a>, Kiglay and another Kodiak Islander named Chukagnak—who had been wounded in a battle with the soldiers—are imprisoned separately at "the mission at Saint-Pedro", and the next day Indians acting at the behest of a Spaniard torture and kill Chukagnak.  Kiglay is apparently going to receive the same treatment, until the Spaniard receives a letter that apparently gives other directions.  Kiglay is reimprisoned, and eventually escapes to <a href="/wiki/Fort_Ross,_California" title="Fort Ross, California">Fort Ross</a>, where he gives his testimony. There is nothing in the account that links the execution of Chucagnak to a refusal on his part to abandon Orthodoxy.  Instead, the eyewitness account states that the Kodiak islanders were all previously offered the opportunity to become Catholics, that they had all declined because they were already Christians, and then with the exceptions of Kiglay and Chukagnak were all transferred to Santa Barbara with no further mention of, or demand for, conversion.<sup id="cite_ref-original-account_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-original-account-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Location_of_martyrdom_and_.22San_Pedro.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Location_of_martyrdom_and_&quot;San_Pedro&quot;">Location of martyrdom and "San Pedro"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Aleut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Location of martyrdom and &quot;San Pedro&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter the Aleut has been referred to as a "martyr of San Francisco".<sup id="cite_ref-umich_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-umich-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-deathtotheworld_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deathtotheworld-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Additionally, many modern descriptions of the martyrdom of Peter the Aleut often describe the event as occurring "in San Francisco",<sup id="cite_ref-goarch_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goarch-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and others describe the Native Alaskan traders as being brought "to San Francisco".<sup id="cite_ref-umich_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-umich-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Other sources can be found describing the event as occurring near Los Angeles or in Southern California.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> These varying descriptions of the location may be based on varying oral traditions, varying understandings of the relationship of the location of the martyrdom and Fort Ross, and also on varying interpretations of references to "San Pedro" in the original historical documents.
</p><p>The earliest historical sources about the death of Peter the Aleut describe the event as taking place in or near "the mission of San Pedro".<sup id="cite_ref-primary-sources_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-primary-sources-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-original-account_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-original-account-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Bucko_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bucko-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Some have taken this to refer to <a href="/wiki/San_Pedro_y_San_Pablo_Asistencia" title="San Pedro y San Pablo Asistencia">San Pedro y San Pablo Asistencia</a>, a "sub-mission" of <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Francisco_de_As%C3%ADs" title="Mission San Francisco de Asís">Mission San Francisco de Asís</a> (also known as Mission Dolores). San Pedro y San Pablo Asistencia was located on the site of the modern-day <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A1nchez_Adobe_Park" title="Sánchez Adobe Park">Sánchez Adobe Park</a> in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Pacifica,_California" title="Pacifica, California">Pacifica, California</a>.
</p><p>Others have interpreted the historical description to refer to the dock in <a href="/wiki/San_Pedro,_Los_Angeles" title="San Pedro, Los Angeles">San Pedro, Los Angeles</a> (now located in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Los_Angeles" title="Los Angeles">Los Angeles</a>), which was used at the time as a trading post by Spanish missionary friars from <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Gabriel_Arc%C3%A1ngel" title="Mission San Gabriel Arcángel">Mission San Gabriel Arcángel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Such an interpretation of "San Pedro" fits well with other references to geographical locations in the historical documents, including an island named Santa Rosa (interpreted to refer to <a href="/wiki/Santa_Rosa_Island,_California" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Rosa Island, California">Santa Rosa Island</a>) an island named "Climant" (interpreted to refer to <a href="/wiki/San_Clemente_Island" title="San Clemente Island">San Clemente Island</a>) and an island named Ekaterina, (interpreted by some to refer to <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catalina_Island,_California" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Catalina Island, California">Catalina Island</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-primary-sources_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-primary-sources-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Bucko_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bucko-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> These documents also describe the captured Native Alaskan traders as transferred to Fort Ross, by way of sequential stops in Santa Barbara and <a href="/wiki/Monterey,_California" title="Monterey, California">Monterey</a>. This interpretation of a Southern Californian location for the martyrdom is further supported by a letter contemporaneous to the alleged martyrdom event from Franciscan Fr. <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Francisco_de_Paula_Se%C3%B1an" title="José Francisco de Paula Señan">José Francisco de Paula Señan</a> dated June 19, 1816 (but which runs counter to allegations of <a href="/wiki/Forced_conversion" title="Forced conversion">forced conversion</a> and violence against the Native hunters from <a href="/wiki/Russian_America" title="Russian America">Russian America</a>), which describes the capture and transfer of "Russian Indians" to the Santa Barbara Presidio from Mission San Buenaventura (in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Ventura,_California" title="Ventura, California">Ventura, California</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Bucko_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bucko-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Aleut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg/220px-SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg/330px-SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg/440px-SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3024" data-file-height="4032" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SaintPeter%26SaintHerman.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">Icon</a> of Saint Peter with <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Alaska" title="Herman of Alaska">St. Herman</a> in an Orthodox parish in the United States</div></div></div><p> According to Yanovsky's 1865 letter, upon receiving the report of Peter's death, <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Alaska" title="Herman of Alaska">St. Herman</a> on Kodiak Island was moved to cry out, "Holy new-martyr Peter, pray to God for us!"<sup id="cite_ref-umich_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-umich-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Peter the Aleut was glorified as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> and locally glorified by the <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20120810222355/http://dioceseofalaska.org/">Diocese of Alaska</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> as the "Martyr of San Francisco" in 1980.  His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on <a href="/wiki/September_24_(Orthodox_Liturgics)" class="mw-redirect" title="September 24 (Orthodox Liturgics)">September 24</a> or December 12. A number of churches have been dedicated to him in North America, including churches at <a href="/wiki/Lake_Havasu_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Lake Havasu City">Lake Havasu City</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arizona" title="Arizona">Arizona</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Minot" class="mw-redirect" title="Minot">Minot</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Dakota" title="North Dakota">North Dakota</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Calgary" title="Calgary">Calgary</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Abita_Springs" class="mw-redirect" title="Abita Springs">Abita Springs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louisiana" title="Louisiana">Louisiana</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notes">Notes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Aleut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Notes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
