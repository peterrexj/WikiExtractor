<p><b>Louis IV the Saint</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Ludwig IV. der Heilige</i>; 28 October 1200 &#8211; 11 September 1227), a member of the <a href="/wiki/Ludovingians" title="Ludovingians">Ludovingian</a> dynasty, was <a href="/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Thuringia" title="List of rulers of Thuringia">Landgrave of Thuringia</a> and Saxon <a href="/wiki/Count_palatine" title="Count palatine">Count palatine</a> from 1217 until his death. He was the husband of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_IV,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Louis was born at <a href="/wiki/Creuzburg" title="Creuzburg">Creuzburg</a> Castle, the second son of Landgrave <a href="/wiki/Hermann_I,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia" title="Hermann I, Landgrave of Thuringia">Hermann I of Thuringia</a>, from his marriage with <a href="/wiki/Sophia_of_Wittelsbach" title="Sophia of Wittelsbach">Sophia</a>, a daughter of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach" title="House of Wittelsbach">Wittelsbach</a> duke <a href="/wiki/Otto_I_Wittelsbach,_Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I Wittelsbach, Duke of Bavaria">Otto I of Bavaria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Remy_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Remy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Germany" title="Kingdom of Germany">German</a> throne quarrel between the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Hohenstaufen" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Hohenstaufen">Hohenstaufen</a> ruler <a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Swabia" title="Philip of Swabia">Philip of Swabia</a> and his <a href="/wiki/House_of_Welf" title="House of Welf">Welf</a> rival <a href="/wiki/Otto_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto IV</a>, his father switched sides several times and tried to expand his own influence by betrothing his eldest son Hermann to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_(1000%E2%80%931301)" title="Kingdom of Hungary (1000–1301)">Hungarian</a> princess <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth</a>, daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_II_of_Hungary" title="Andrew II of Hungary">Andrew II</a>. The young girl arrived in <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Thuringia" title="Duchy of Thuringia">Thuringia</a> in 1211 to be raised at the Ludovingian court, then a venue for poets and <a href="/wiki/Minnesang" title="Minnesang">minnesingers</a> like <a href="/wiki/Walther_von_der_Vogelweide" title="Walther von der Vogelweide">Walther von der Vogelweide</a> or <a href="/wiki/Wolfram_von_Eschenbach" title="Wolfram von Eschenbach">Wolfram von Eschenbach</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg/220px-Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="213" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg/330px-Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg/440px-Louis_IV_of_Thuringia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="480" data-file-height="464" /></a><figcaption>Seal of Louis IV</figcaption></figure>
<p>Louis elder brother died in 1216, therefore he himself, upon his father's death on 25 April 1217, ascended the Thuringian throne at the age of sixteen.<sup id="cite_ref-libri_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-libri-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1218, on the Feast of St. <a href="/wiki/Kilian" class="mw-redirect" title="Kilian">Kilian</a>, at age eighteen, he was armed as a knight in the Church of St. George in <a href="/wiki/Eisenach" title="Eisenach">Eisenach</a>. At <a href="/wiki/Wartburg_Castle" class="mw-redirect" title="Wartburg Castle">Wartburg Castle</a> in 1220 at age twenty, Louis married 14-year-old Elizabeth of Hungary,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> with whom he had three children: <a href="/wiki/Hermann_II,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia" title="Hermann II, Landgrave of Thuringia">Hermann II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sophie_of_Thuringia,_Duchess_of_Brabant" title="Sophie of Thuringia, Duchess of Brabant">Sophie</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Aldenberg" title="Gertrude of Aldenberg">Gertrude</a>, later abbess at Altenberg. He set up his court at <a href="/wiki/Wartburg" title="Wartburg">Wartburg</a> Castle near <a href="/wiki/Eisenach" title="Eisenach">Eisenach</a>.
</p><p>When in 1221 Louis' <a href="/wiki/House_of_Wettin" title="House of Wettin">Wettin</a> brother-in-law, Margrave <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_I,_Margrave_of_Meissen" title="Theodoric I, Margrave of Meissen">Theodoric I of Meissen</a> died, he acted as a guardian for Theodoric's minor son <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Margrave_of_Meissen" title="Henry III, Margrave of Meissen">Henry III</a>. However, his attempts to occupy the <a href="/wiki/Margraviate_of_Meissen" class="mw-redirect" title="Margraviate of Meissen">Meissen</a> and <a href="/wiki/March_of_Lusatia" title="March of Lusatia">Lusatian</a> lands were rejected by his sister <a href="/wiki/Jutta_of_Thuringia" title="Jutta of Thuringia">Jutta</a>. Like his father, Louis was in close contact with the Hohenstaufen emperor <a href="/wiki/Fredrick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Fredrick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>, who appointed him a <a href="/wiki/Marshal" title="Marshal">Marshal</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> and confirmed his rights in the Margraviate of Meissen.
</p><p>In 1226, Louis was called to the <a href="/wiki/Reichstag_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reichstag (Holy Roman Empire)">Diet</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cremona" title="Cremona">Cremona</a>, where he promised Emperor Frederick II to take up the cross and accompany him to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. He embarked for the <a href="/wiki/Sixth_Crusade" title="Sixth Crusade">Sixth Crusade</a> in 1227, partly inspired also by the tales of his uncle, who had been to the <a href="/wiki/Levant" title="Levant">Levant</a> with the Holy Roman Emperor. Fellow-travellers were five counts, Louis von Wartburg, Gunther von Kefernberg, Meinrad von Mühlberg, Heinrich von <a href="/wiki/House_of_Stolberg" title="House of Stolberg">Stolberg</a>, and Burkhard von Brandenberg<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup>; Louis left his pregnant wife behind,<sup id="cite_ref-libri_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-libri-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who had a premonition that they would never meet again.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Rosenwunder.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Rosenwunder.jpg/220px-Rosenwunder.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="357" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/Rosenwunder.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="308" data-file-height="500" /></a><figcaption>Louis and Elizabeth: <a href="/wiki/Miracle_of_the_roses" title="Miracle of the roses">Miracle of the roses</a>, altarpiece, <a href="/wiki/Mariahof" title="Mariahof">Mariahof</a> parish church, 16th century</figcaption></figure>
<p>In August 1227 Louis traversed the mountains between Thuringia and Upper <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Franconia" title="Duchy of Franconia">Franconia</a>, through the duchies of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia" title="Duchy of Swabia">Swabia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria" title="Duchy of Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, crossing the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">Tyrolian</a> <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alps</a>. He fell ill of plague after reaching <a href="/wiki/Brindisi" title="Brindisi">Brindisi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Otranto" title="Otranto">Otranto</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">Kingdom of Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cleve446_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cleve446-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He received <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Anointing of the Sick (Catholic Church)">extreme unction</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem">Patriarch of Jerusalem</a> and the Bishop of Santa Croce. He died before reaching Otranto in 1227.<sup id="cite_ref-Cleve446_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cleve446-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A few days after his death, his daughter Gertrude was born. Louis's remains were buried in <a href="/wiki/Reinhardsbrunn" title="Reinhardsbrunn">Reinhardsbrunn</a> Abbey in 1228. He was succeeded by his five-year-old son Hermann II, under the tutelage of his uncle <a href="/wiki/Henry_Raspe,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Raspe, Landgrave of Thuringia">Henry Raspe</a>.
</p><p>After his death, Elizabeth left the court, made arrangements for the care of her children, and in 1228, renounced the world, becoming a <a href="/wiki/Third_order" title="Third order">tertiary</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Francis of Assisi">St. Francis of Assisi</a>. She built the Franciscan hospital at Marburg and devoted herself to the care of the sick until her death at the age of 24 in 1231. She was officially proclaimed a saint only four years after her death. While Louis was never formally canonized, he became known among the German people as Louis the saint (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Ludwig der Heilige</i>). He is known elsewhere as Blessed Louis of Thuringia.<sup id="cite_ref-libri_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-libri-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_and_children">Family and children</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_IV,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family and children">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He and Elizabeth of Hungary had the following children:
</p>
<ol><li><a href="/wiki/Hermann_II,_Landgrave_of_Thuringia" title="Hermann II, Landgrave of Thuringia">Hermann II, Landgrave of Thuringia</a> (1222–1241), married Helen, daughter of Duke <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Duke_of_Brunswick-L%C3%BCneburg" title="Otto I, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg">Otto I of Brunswick-Lüneburg</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Sophie_of_Thuringia,_Duchess_of_Brabant" title="Sophie of Thuringia, Duchess of Brabant">Sophie of Thuringia</a> (1224–1275), married Duke <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Duke_of_Brabant" title="Henry II, Duke of Brabant">Henry II of Brabant</a>; their son <a href="/wiki/Henry_I,_Landgrave_of_Hesse" title="Henry I, Landgrave of Hesse">Henry</a> became the progenitor of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Hesse" title="House of Hesse">House of Hesse</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Aldenberg" title="Gertrude of Aldenberg">Gertrude</a> (1227–1297), abbess of the <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensians" title="Premonstratensians">Premonstratensian</a> monastery of Altenberg near <a href="/wiki/Wetzlar" title="Wetzlar">Wetzlar</a>; she was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VI" title="Pope Clement VI">Pope Clement VI</a> in 1348.</li></ol>
