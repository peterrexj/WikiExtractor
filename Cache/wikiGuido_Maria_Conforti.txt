<p><b>Guido Maria Conforti</b> (3 March 1865 – 5 November 1931) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> Italian archbishop who founded the <b>Xaverian Missionary Fathers</b> on 3 December 1895. He was known to make frequent visits to his parishes and worked to support the religious education and religious involvement among the youth.<sup id="cite_ref-sqpn_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sqpn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified him in 1996 and he was canonized in 2011 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guido_Maria_Conforti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Guido Maria Conforti was born in Casalora di Ravadese in the diocese and province of Parma, Italy, in 1865, the eighth of ten children of Rinaldo and Antonia Adorni Conforti.<sup id="cite_ref-sqpn_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sqpn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He attended an elementary school run by the <a href="/wiki/De_La_Salle_Brothers" title="De La Salle Brothers">De La Salle Brothers</a> from 1872 and each day on his way to the school he would stop by the church of Santa Maria della Pace, his parish church, where he used to have conversations with the crucified <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>. This was when his vocation became apparent. He later recalled: "I looked at Him and He looked at me and seemed to say so many things".<sup id="cite_ref-xmusa_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xmusa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although his father would have preferred that he stay and manage the farm, Conforti enrolled in the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in Parma in November 1876. He began reading the works of <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a> which inspired a desire to be a missionary, but his requests to join the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> or the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Saint_John_Bosco" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesians of Saint John Bosco">Salesians of Saint John Bosco</a> were denied. At the time, the rector of the seminary was <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari" title="Andrea Carlo Ferrari">Andrea Carlo Ferrari</a>, future cardinal and Blessed. Ferrari became his mentor. Conforti was appointed vice-rector.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Conforti was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on 22 September 1888 at <a href="/wiki/Fontanellato" title="Fontanellato">Fontanellato</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Conforti then served as a professor at the local seminary. He became the Vicar-General of the Diocese of Parma on 7 March 1896.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guido_Maria_Conforti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Conforti established the Xaverian Missionaries on 3 December 1895 and the organization received the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on December 3, 1898. In 1899, he sent the order's first missionaries to <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>. Leo XIII appointed him Archbishop of Ravenna in May 1902 following the death of Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Agostino_Gaetano_Riboldi" title="Agostino Gaetano Riboldi">Agostino Gaetano Riboldi</a>. He was consecrated bishop on June 11, 1902, at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Church of St. Paul outside the Walls</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-xmusa_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xmusa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Conforti submitted his resignation due to his ill health in October 1904. On 14 November, he was made both the Coadjutor Bishop of Parma and <a href="/wiki/Titular_Archbishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Titular Archbishop">Titular Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Stauropolis_(diocese)" title="Stauropolis (diocese)">Stauropolis</a>.
</p><p>In 1907 he became Bishop of Parma,<sup id="cite_ref-xmusa_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-xmusa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> retaining his personal title of Archbishop. He traveled to all parishes via horseback or other means to inspect his diocese. He had a particular focus on religious education.<sup id="cite_ref-pms_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pms-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Conforti is said to have provided the initiative behind <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a>'s encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Maximum_illud" title="Maximum illud">Maximum illud</a></i>, issued on 30 November 1919. That document is called the Magna Carta of modern Catholic missionary work.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He travelled to China in 1928 to visit the Xaverian Missionaries working there.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He arrived in <a href="/wiki/Shanghai" title="Shanghai">Shanghai</a> on 26 October 1928 and met with his contacts to inspect their work.
</p><p>Conforti returned to Parma and fell ill in October 1931. He died a month later and he was interred in Parma. His tomb was later relocated in 1942 and in 1996.<sup id="cite_ref-sqpn_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sqpn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guido_Maria_Conforti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cause of sainthood was introduced in Parma on 29 May 1959 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> and the work done on a diocesan level culminated on 11 February 1982 with Conforti being declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on account of his life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The requisite miracle being approved, Conforti was beatified by John Paul II on 17 March 1996.
</p><p>A second tribunal for a miracle needed for canonization opened on 4 October 2005 and closed on 16 November 2005 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> signed the decree for the miracle<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag needs further explanation. (October 2018)">further explanation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> on 10 December 2010, leading to his canonization on 23 October 2011.
</p>
