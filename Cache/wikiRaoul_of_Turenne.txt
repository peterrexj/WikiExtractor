<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG/220px-Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="136" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG/330px-Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG/440px-Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2009" data-file-height="1238" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Solignac_-_Eglise_abbatiale_-_Choeur_et_bras_Sud_du_transept.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The monastery of Solignac, where Rodulf began his ecclesiastical career.</div></div></div>
<p><b>Rodulf</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Raoul</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> died 21 June 866) was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Bourges" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Bourges">archbishop of Bourges</a> from 840 until his death. He is remembered as a skillful diplomat and a proponent of ecclesiastical reform. As a saint, his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast</a> has been celebrated on 21 June.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aquitainian_nobleman_and_monk">Aquitainian nobleman and monk</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodulf_(archbishop_of_Bourges)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Aquitainian nobleman and monk">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rodulf's family was prominent in the region of <a href="/wiki/Angoumois" title="Angoumois">Angoumois</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson199257_n._34_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson199257_n._34-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and he himself possessed lands in the <a href="/wiki/Limousin" title="Limousin">Limousin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992103_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992103-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was named after his father, the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Count_of_Turenne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Count of Turenne (page does not exist)">count of Turenne</a> (died 844), and he had four brothers and two sisters as well as an unnamed sibling.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197662_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197662-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Solignac_Abbey" title="Solignac Abbey">Solignac</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novice</a> in 823.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197660_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197660-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the conflict between King <a href="/wiki/Pippin_II_of_Aquitaine" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin II of Aquitaine">Pippin II of Aquitaine</a> and King <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles of West Francia</a> over the inheritance of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aquitaine" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Aquitaine">Aquitanian kingdom</a>, Rodulf maintained good relations with both claimants,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> although it is probable that his father fought in the war and is possible that Rodulf himself did as well.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197661_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscherHunt197661-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Contemporary documents describe him as a "faithful follower" (<i>fidelis</i>) of King Pippin.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992103_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992103-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In late 840 Rodulf was elected as archbishop of Bourges,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Since Pippin led an expedition north against Charles' forces in <a href="/wiki/Poitou" title="Poitou">Poitou</a> in September, it generally thought that he was the driving force behind the election of Rodulf and that he successfully extended his authority into the Berri (the region around Bourges), which was as far north as it would ever go.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992111_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992111-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> One of Pippin's two surviving royal charters is a confirmation to the new archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992103_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992103-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Yet if the appointment of Rodulf was political on the one hand, the <a href="/wiki/Electoral_capitulation" title="Electoral capitulation">capitulation</a> (<i>capitula</i>) which he signed upon his election "shows that [he] was in the vanguard of the Carolingian reform movement."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Shortly after becoming archbishop, Rodulf bought a large piece of land from a certain Boso for 1,500 <i><a href="/wiki/Solidus_(coin)" title="Solidus (coin)">solidi</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965157–58_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965157–58-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="West_Frankish_diplomat">West Frankish diplomat</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodulf_(archbishop_of_Bourges)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: West Frankish diplomat">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By early May 844, Rodulf had recognised Charles as king in Aquitaine. In that month he visited Charles while the latter was besieging <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> and received a charter from him at Charles's headquarters in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint-Sernin" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Saint-Sernin">monastery of Saint-Sernin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992140_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992140-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  Rodulf attended the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Ver&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Ver (page does not exist)">council of Ver</a> in December that year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992143_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992143-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Translatio_sancti_Germani&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Translatio sancti Germani (page does not exist)">Translatio sancti Germani</a></i>, Rodulf and Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ebroin_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Ebroin of Poitiers">Ebroin of Poitiers</a> played the leading rôles in the negotiations to reconcile Charles and Pippin in the winter of 844–45.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992143_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992143-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Rodulf hosted a conference at the <a href="/wiki/Fleury_Abbey" title="Fleury Abbey">monastery of Fleury</a> in June 845, where Pippin swore fealty to Charles and Charles gave Pippin lordship over most of Aquitaine (the regions of Poitou, <a href="/wiki/County_of_Saintonge" title="County of Saintonge">Saintonge</a> and <a href="/wiki/Aunis" title="Aunis">Aunis</a> excepted).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992143_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992143-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Later that same month, Rodulf attended the great <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Meaux%E2%80%93Paris" title="Council of Meaux–Paris">synod at Meaux</a> with archbishops <a href="/wiki/Wenilo_of_Sens" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenilo of Sens">Wenilo of Sens</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hincmar_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Hincmar of Reims">Hincmar of Reims</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992146–47_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992146–47-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> As a reward for his work, Charles granted Rodulf control over Fleury's resources in October 846.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992143_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992143-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoupland1989200–01-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In August or September 849, after Pippin had rebelled against Charles, Rodulf, "with the greatest enthusiasm",<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> hosted a royal assembly before the king moved south to besiege Toulouse a second time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992156_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992156-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Annales_Fontanellenses" class="mw-redirect" title="Annales Fontanellenses">Annales Fontanellenses</a></i>, Charles then spent Christmas in Bourges and stayed into January 850.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992156_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992156-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Rodulf may have served as guardian (<i><a href="/wiki/Bajulus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bajulus">bajulus</a></i>) to Charles's son, <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Child" title="Charles the Child">Charles the Child</a>, when the latter was made king of Aquitaine in 855.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992174_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992174-17">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Late in 860, Hincmar of Reims wrote a letter to Rodulf and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Frothar_(archbishop_of_Bordeaux)" title="Frothar (archbishop of Bordeaux)">Frothar of Bordeaux</a>—who may have been a kinsman of Rodulf's<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992193_n._25_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992193_n._25-18">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>—outlining the difficulties of Count Stephen, son of Count <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Tours" title="Hugh of Tours">Hugh of Tours</a>, who was trying to repudiate his wife, the daughter of Count <a href="/wiki/Raymond_I_of_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Raymond I of Toulouse">Raymond I of Toulouse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992196–97_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992196–97-19">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Rodulf and Frothar were successful in negotiating a settlement.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992196–97_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992196–97-19">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The scale of the disorders may be gauged by two charters of Rodulf's from 859 and 860, in which he laments "the presence of evil men" (<i>infestorum malorum hominum</i>) in his diocese, an indication of violence and civil strife.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965127_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965127-20">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In 860 Rodulf drew up a will and had it confirmed by Raymond of Toulouse.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965108_n._82_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965108_n._82-21">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Church_reformer">Church reformer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodulf_(archbishop_of_Bourges)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Church reformer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With Bishop <a href="/wiki/Stodilo_of_Limoges" class="mw-redirect" title="Stodilo of Limoges">Stodilo of Limoges</a>, Rodulf helped found the <a href="/wiki/Beaulieu-en-Rouergue_Abbey" title="Beaulieu-en-Rouergue Abbey">monastery of Beaulieu</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965151_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965151-22">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> His family provided the land for the foundation, and he himself consecrated the new community under the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine rule">Benedictine rule</a> in 860.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He granted the monks the right of free election of their abbot, and pronounced excommunication on any governing authority who molested them in the future.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He even procured royal protection (<i>mundeburdium</i>) for them.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Rodulf's brother Gottfried, the count of Turenne, along with Raymond of Toulouse and Aldo, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Martial%27s,_Limoges" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Martial&#39;s, Limoges">abbot of Saint Martial's</a>, were witnesses to this act of consecration.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 859, Stodilo granted a church to Rodulf and Abbot Garnulf of Beaulieu as a <i><a href="/wiki/Precarium" title="Precarium">precarium</a></i> in return for an annual rent of seven <i>solidi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965167_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965167-23">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Rodulf also helped found the convent at <a href="/wiki/Cahors" title="Cahors">Cahors</a> where his sister <a href="/w/index.php?title=Immena&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Immena (page does not exist)">Immena</a> was installed as the first abbess.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELewis1965152_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELewis1965152-24">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The earliest reference to the archbishop of Bourges as <a href="/wiki/Primate_(church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Primate (church)">primate</a> of Aquitaine dates from the episcopate of Rodulf.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELacger193731_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELacger193731-25">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> In 864, when Archbishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sigebod_of_Narbonne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sigebod of Narbonne (page does not exist)">Sigebod of Narbonne</a> complained to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> that Rodulf had called some clergy of Narbonne before him "as if by <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_(church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch (church)">patriarchal</a> right" (<i>quasi jure patriarchatus</i>), the pope confirmed the right of clergy to appeal to Bourges if all avenues in Narbonne had been exhausted and of the suffragans of Narbonne to appeal to Bourges "as if to their patriarch" (<i>quasi ad patriarchum suum</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELacger193732_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELacger193732-27">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> In his acts Rodulf sometimes titled himself "primate" (<i>primas</i>) and "bishop of the primatial see" (<i>primae sedis episcopus</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELacger193731_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELacger193731-25">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rofuld died at Bourges on 21 June 866 and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Bourges_Cathedral" title="Bourges Cathedral">basilica of Saint Ursinus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher19686_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher19686-29">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by a cleric from the royal palace of Charles the Bald named <a href="/wiki/Wulfad" title="Wulfad">Wulfad</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992212_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992212-30">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Into the twelfth century, the community of Beaulieu commemorated Rodulf as "our master of holy memory".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher19686_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher19686-29">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> A rather standard hagiography of Rodulf, the <i>Vita sancti Rodulfi</i>, survives.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01_10-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeitscher1974200–01-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
