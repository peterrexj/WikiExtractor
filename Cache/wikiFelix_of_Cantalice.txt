<p><b>Felix of Cantalice</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">OFMCap</a> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Felice da Cantalice</i>; 18 May 1515 – 18 May 1587) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> Capuchin <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> of the 16th century. Canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a> in 1712, he was the first Capuchin friar to be named a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. He worked as a shepherd and farmhand until he was twenty-eight. His task as a Capuchin was to beg alms for the friars. So successful was he that Brother Felix was able to extend his collections to assist the poor.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Cantalice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Felix was the third of four sons born to Santi and Santa Porri. They were poor farmers. At about the age of ten, Felix was hired out first as a shepherd to a family at Cittàducale, where he later worked as a farm hand. Until the age of twenty-eight he worked as a farm laborer and shepherd. He developed the habit of praying while he worked. One day, while plowing a field, something spooked the oxen and he fell. He was trampled, the plow passing over his body. However, he arose uninjured, and in gratitude immediately entered a Capuchin monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Toward the end of autumn 1543, Felix entered the newly founded <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> friars as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> at the Citta Ducale <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> in the municipality of <a href="/wiki/Anticoli_Corrado" title="Anticoli Corrado">Anticoli Corrado</a>. It is said that he was well noted for his piety. In 1547 he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> as quaestor of the Capuchin Friary of St. Bonaventure, where he spent his remaining 40 years begging alms to help in the friars' work of aiding the sick and the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Hess_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hess-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Rome, Brother Felix became a familiar sight, wandering barefoot through the streets, with a sack slung over his shoulders, knocking on doors to seek donations. He received permission from his superiors to help the needy, especially widows with many children. It is said that his begging sack was as bottomless as his heart.<sup id="cite_ref-fsi_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fsi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Brother Felix blessed all benefactors and all those he met with a humble “Deo Gratias!” (thanks be to God!), causing many to refer to him as “Brother Deo Gratias".<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Felix was so successful in his work that during the famine of 1580, the political leader of Rome asked the Capuchins if they would 'lend' Felix to them so he could collect food and provisions for the entire city. The Capuchins agreed and Felix embraced his new task.<sup id="cite_ref-capuchins_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capuchins-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg/220px-Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="239" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg/330px-Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg/440px-Guercino_Blessed_Felix_of_Cantalice.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1444" data-file-height="1568" /></a><figcaption>Felix of Cantalice reviving a dead child, Guercino</figcaption></figure>
<p>He preached in the street, rebuked corrupt politicians and officials, and exhorted young men to stop leading dissolute lives. He also composed simple teaching <a href="/wiki/Canticles" class="mw-redirect" title="Canticles">canticles</a>, and arranged for children to gather in groups to sing them as a way to teach them the catechism.<sup id="cite_ref-Hess_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hess-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The plain-spoken Brother Felix was a good friend of <a href="/wiki/St._Philip_Neri" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Philip Neri">St. Philip Neri</a> and an acquaintance of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-portiuncula_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-portiuncula-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Felix developed a reputation as a healer.<sup id="cite_ref-fsi_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fsi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As he got older, his superior ordered him to wear sandals to protect his health.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Cardinal Santori had offered to use his influence to have the elderly Felix relieved of the difficult task of questing, but Felix refused.<sup id="cite_ref-portiuncula_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-portiuncula-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Felix died in Rome in 1587 on his 72nd birthday and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Crypt" title="Capuchin Crypt">crypt</a> of the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Concezione_dei_Cappuccini" title="Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini">Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-portiuncula_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-portiuncula-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Cantalice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 1 October 1625 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 22 May 1712 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-capuchins_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capuchins-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">Calendar of Saints</a> of the Franciscan Order on May 18.
</p><p>A titular church in Rome was erected in his honor, <a href="/wiki/San_Felice_da_Cantalice_a_Centocelle" title="San Felice da Cantalice a Centocelle">San Felice da Cantalice a Centocelle</a>
</p><p>In 19th-century <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Felician_Sisters" title="Felician Sisters">Felician Sisters</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> of Franciscan Sisters of the Third Order Regular, was founded to care for the poor and adopted him as their <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Cantalice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Felix is usually represented in art as holding in his arms the <a href="/wiki/Infant_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Infant Jesus">Infant Jesus</a>, because of a vision he is said to have had, when the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> appeared to him and placed the Divine Child in his arms. <sup id="cite_ref-Hess_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hess-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> observed that Felix is "shown bearing the Infant Jesus in his arms because in bearing the burdens of the needy he had carried in his arms the poor Christ himself."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
