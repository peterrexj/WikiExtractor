<p><b>John and Paul</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Ioannes, Paulus</i>) are <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> who lived during the fourth century in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>. They were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> at Rome on 26 June. The year of their martyrdom is uncertain according to their <i>Acts</i>; it occurred under <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a> (361–3).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_and_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the second half of the fourth century, Byzantius, the Roman senator, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Pammachius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Pammachius">Saint Pammachius</a>, his son, fashioned their house on the <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Cælian Hill</a> into a Christian <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a>. In the fifth century the <i>presbyteri tituli Byzantii</i> (priests of the church of Byzantius) are mentioned in an <a href="/wiki/Inscription" class="mw-redirect" title="Inscription">inscription</a> and among the signatures of the Roman Council of 499. The church was also called the <i>titulus Pammachii</i> after Byzantius's son, the pious friend of <a href="/wiki/St._Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Jerome">St. Jerome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the ancient apartments on the ground-floor of the house of Byzantius, which were still retained under the basilica, the tomb of two Roman martyrs, John and Paul, was the object of veneration as early as the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Verona_Sacramentary" title="Verona Sacramentary">Sacramentarium Leonianum</a></i> already indicates in the preface to the feast of the saints, that they rested within the city walls, while, in one of the early itineraries to the tombs of the Roman martyrs, their grave is assigned to the church on the Cælian (<a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_de_Rossi" title="Giovanni Battista de Rossi">de Rossi</a>, <i>Roma sotterranea</i>, I, 138, 175).<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>titulus Byzantii</i> or <i>Pammachii</i> was consequently known at a very early date by the names of the two martyrs (<i>titulus SS. Joannis et Pauli</i>). That the two saints are martyrs of the Roman Church is historically certain; as to how and when their bodies found a resting-place in the house of Pammachius under the basilica, we only know that it certainly occurred in the fourth century. The year and circumstances of their martyrdom are likewise unknown. It may have occurred during the reign of Diocletian (304 AD), as the martyrdoms of Julian took place not in Rome, but mainly in the East.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Acts">Acts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_and_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Acts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to their Acts, which are legendary and may not be historically reliable, after successful military careers, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a> entrusted John and Paul with the protection of his daughter, <a href="/wiki/Constantina" title="Constantina">Constantia</a>. Through her, they became acquainted with a certain Gallicanus, who, according to a Medieval legend, was her fiancé.<sup id="cite_ref-Chris_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chris-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gallicanus is said to have built a church in <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica_(archaeological_site)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ostia Antica (archaeological site)">Ostia</a>. Constantine bequeathed significant sums to the faithful of a John and Paul, who built a house on the Caelian Hill and retired to private life, doing works of charity.<sup id="cite_ref-passio_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-passio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Other accounts say it was Constantia who left her wealth to her servants for them to spend on Christian works.<sup id="cite_ref-Chris_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chris-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They declined to return to service under <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>, whereupon the emperor gave them ten days to reconsider. They spent the ten days dispersing their wealth, distributing alms day and night. Emperor Julian had them <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> secretly by Terentianus in their house on the <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Caelian Hill</a>. Terentianus had them buried where they died and spread the rumor that they had gone into exile. Three Christians who were ministering to them were also executed and buried nearby: <a href="/wiki/Crispus,_Crispinianus,_and_Benedicta" title="Crispus, Crispinianus, and Benedicta">Crispus, Crispinianus, and Benedicta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The rooms on the ground-floor of the above-mentioned house of Pammachius were rediscovered in the 19th century under the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Rome">Santi Giovanni e Paolo</a> in Rome. They are decorated with important and interesting frescoes, while the original tomb (<i>confessio</i>) of Sts. John and Paul is covered with paintings of which the martyrs are the subject. The rooms and the tomb form one of the most important early Christian memorials in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Acts</i> of Saints John and Paul are also connected with the legend of <a href="/wiki/St_Bibiana" class="mw-redirect" title="St Bibiana">St Bibiana</a>, which has no historical claim to belief.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_and_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_(Venice)_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg/220px-Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="162" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg/330px-Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg/440px-Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_%28Venice%29_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5939" data-file-height="4384" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Exterior_of_Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_(Venice)_from_Campo_San_Zanipolo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The church of San Zanipolo</div></div></div>
<p>The basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Rome">Santi Giovanni e Paolo</a> in Rome is dedicated to them, as is the <a href="/wiki/Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo,_Venice" title="Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice">Basilica di San Zanipolo</a> in Venice ("Zanipolo" being <a href="/wiki/Venetian_language" title="Venetian language">Venetian</a> for "John and Paul"). Since the erection of the Roman basilica, the two saints have been greatly venerated, and since the 5th century, their names have been included in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Roman Canon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-passio_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-passio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their feast is kept on 26 June.<sup id="cite_ref-passio_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-passio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Saints_John_and_Paul.27s_Day"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Saints_John_and_Paul's_Day">Saints John and Paul's Day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_and_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Saints John and Paul&#039;s Day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG/220px-Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG/330px-Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG/440px-Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Celio_-_Casa_romana_al_Celio_01-0410.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The original Roman house below the present-day Basilica of Santi Giovanni e Paolo</div></div></div>
<p>The Lüneberg manuscript (c. 1440–1450) mentions the day of John and Paul in an early German account of the <a href="/wiki/Pied_Piper_of_Hamelin" title="Pied Piper of Hamelin">Pied Piper of Hamelin</a>:
</p>
<div class="poem">
<p>In 1284 on the day of John and Paul on 26th&#160;June<br />
130 children born in Hamelin were led away <br />
by a piper [clothed] in many colours<br />
to [their] Calvary near the Koppen, [and] lost'.
</p>
</div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Campania_Town">Campania Town</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_and_Paul&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Campania Town">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>A small village next to <a href="/wiki/Caiazzo" title="Caiazzo">Caiazzo</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a> region of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> is named Ss. Giovanni e Paolo in honor of these martyrs.   Many residents of this village bear the family name "San Giovanni," as do the descendants of immigrants to the United States from this village (in particular, in Michigan, New York, and Florida).
</p>
