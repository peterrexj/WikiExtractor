<p><b>Adalbert of Prague</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Sanctus Adalbertus</i>, <a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">svatý Vojtěch</i>, <a href="/wiki/Slovak_language" title="Slovak language">Slovak</a>: <i lang="sk">svätý Vojtech</i>, <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">święty Wojciech</i>, <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Szent Adalbert (Béla)</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 956&#160;&#8211;&#32;23 April 997), known in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a> by his birth name <b>Vojtěch</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Voitecus</i>), was a <a href="/wiki/Czechs" title="Czechs">Czech</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Prague">Bishop of Prague</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> to the Hungarians, Poles, and Prussians, who was martyred in his efforts to convert the <a href="/wiki/Baltic_peoples" class="mw-redirect" title="Baltic peoples">Baltic</a> <a href="/wiki/Old_Prussians" title="Old Prussians">Prussians</a> to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. He is said to be the composer of the oldest Czech hymn <i><a href="/wiki/Hospodine,_pomiluj_ny" title="Hospodine, pomiluj ny">Hospodine, pomiluj ny</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Bogurodzica" title="Bogurodzica">Bogurodzica</a></i>, the oldest known Polish hymn, but his authorship of them has not been confirmed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVlasto1970123_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVlasto1970123-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert was later declared the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the Czech Republic, <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints_of_Poland" title="Patron saints of Poland">Poland</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Prussia" title="Duchy of Prussia">Duchy of Prussia</a>. He is also the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Esztergom-Budapest" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Esztergom-Budapest">Archdiocese of Esztergom</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Katolikus.hu_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Katolikus.hu-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Born as <i><a href="/wiki/Vojt%C4%9Bch" title="Vojtěch">Vojtěch</a></i> in 952<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMolnar197812_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMolnar197812-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> or ca. 956<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVlasto197097_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVlasto197097-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Gord_(Slavic_settlement)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gord (Slavic settlement)">gord</a> <a href="/wiki/Libice_nad_Cidlinou" title="Libice nad Cidlinou">Libice</a>, he belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Slavn%C3%ADk_dynasty" title="Slavník dynasty">Slavnik clan</a>, one of the two most powerful families in Bohemia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMolnar197812_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMolnar197812-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Events from his life were later recorded by a Bohemian priest <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_of_Prague" title="Cosmas of Prague">Cosmas of Prague</a> (1045–1125). Vojtěch's father was <a href="/wiki/Slavn%C3%ADk" title="Slavník">Slavník</a> (d. 978–981), a duke ruling a province centred at Libice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200977–78_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200977–78-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His mother was <a href="/wiki/St%C5%99ezislava" class="mw-redirect" title="Střezislava">Střezislava</a> (d. 985–987),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200977–78_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200977–78-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and according to David Kalhous belonged to the <a href="/wiki/P%C5%99emyslid_dynasty" title="Přemyslid dynasty">Přemyslid dynasty</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKalhous201510_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKalhous201510-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He had five brothers: <a href="/wiki/Sob%C4%9Bslav_(d._1004)" class="mw-redirect" title="Soběslav (d. 1004)">Soběslav</a>, Spytimír, Dobroslav, Pořej, and Čáslav.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200981_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200981-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Cosmas also refers to Radim (later <a href="/wiki/Radim_Gaudentius" title="Radim Gaudentius">Gaudentius</a>) as a brother;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200987,_156_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECosmas_of_Prague200987,_156-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> who is believed to have been a half-brother by his father's liaison with another woman. After he survived a grave illness in childhood, his parents decided to dedicate him to the service of God. Adalbert was well educated, having studied for approximately ten years (970-80) in <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg" title="Magdeburg">Magdeburg</a> under <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Magdeburg" title="Adalbert of Magdeburg">Adalbert of Magdeburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The young Vojtěch took his tutor's name "Adalbert" at his <a href="/wiki/Confirmation" title="Confirmation">Confirmation</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopacy">Episcopacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg/210px-Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg" decoding="async" width="210" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg/315px-Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg/420px-Socha_Svateho_Vojtecha_a_Radima.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1178" /></a><figcaption>Monument to Adalbert and his brother Gaudentius, <a href="/wiki/Libice_nad_Cidlinou" title="Libice nad Cidlinou">Libice nad Cidlinou</a>, Czech Republic</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png/210px-Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png" decoding="async" width="210" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png/315px-Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png/420px-Adalbert_Siegel_Domkapitel_Gnesen.png 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="2000" /></a><figcaption>Adalbert on a seal of the chapter of <a href="/wiki/Gniezno_Cathedral" title="Gniezno Cathedral">Gniezno Cathedral</a> (Gnesen)</figcaption></figure> 
<p>In 981 <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Magdeburg" title="Adalbert of Magdeburg">Adalbert of Magdeburg</a> died, and his young protege Adalbert returned to <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>. Later Bishop <a href="/wiki/D%C4%9Btmar" title="Dětmar">Dietmar of Prague</a> ordained him a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. In 982, Bishop Dietmar died, and Adalbert, despite being under canonical age, was chosen to succeed him as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Prague">Bishop of Prague</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Amiable and somewhat worldly, he was not expected to trouble the secular powers by making excessive claims for the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler2003181_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler2003181-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Although Adalbert was from a wealthy family, he avoided comfort and luxury, and was noted for his charity and austerity. After six years of preaching and prayer, he had made little headway in evangelizing the Bohemians, who maintained deeply embedded pagan beliefs.
</p><p>Adalbert opposed the participation of Christians in the <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">slave</a> trade and complained of <a href="/wiki/Polygamy" title="Polygamy">polygamy</a> and idolatry, which were common among the people. Once he started to propose reforms he was met with opposition from both the secular powers and the clergy. His family refused to support Duke <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_II,_Duke_of_Bohemia" title="Boleslaus II, Duke of Bohemia">Boleslaus</a> in an unsuccessful war against Poland. Adalbert was no longer welcome and eventually forced into exile.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 988 he went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He lived as a hermit at the Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Santi_Bonifacio_e_Alessio" class="mw-redirect" title="Santi Bonifacio e Alessio">monastery of Saint Alexis</a>. Five years later, Boleslaus requested that the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> send Adalbert back to Prague, in hopes of securing his family's support. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XV" title="Pope John XV">Pope John XV</a> agreed, with the understanding that Adalbert was free to leave Prague if he continued to encounter entrenched resistance. Adalbert returned as bishop of Prague, where he was initially received with demonstrations of apparent joy. Together with a group of <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> Benedictine monks which brought with him, he founded in 14 January 993 a <a href="/wiki/Brevnov_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Brevnov Monastery">monastery in Břevnov</a> (then situated westward from Prague, now part of the city), the second oldest monastery on Czech territory.
</p><p>In 995, the <a href="/wiki/Slavniks" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavniks">Slavniks</a>' former rivalry with the Přemyslids, who were allied with the powerful Bohemian clan of the <a href="/wiki/Vr%C5%A1ovci" title="Vršovci">Vršovids</a>, resulted in the storming of the Slavnik town of <a href="/wiki/Libice_nad_Cidlinou" title="Libice nad Cidlinou">Libice nad Cidlinou</a>, which was led by the Přemyslid <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_II_the_Pious" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus II the Pious">Boleslaus II the Pious</a>. During the struggle four or five of Adalbert's brothers <a href="/wiki/Massacre_of_the_Slavn%C3%ADks" title="Massacre of the Slavníks">were killed</a>. The Zlič Principality became part of the Přemyslids' estate. Adalbert unsuccessfully attempted to protect a noblewoman caught in adultery. She had fled to a convent, where she was killed. In upholding the right of sanctuary, Bishop Adalbert responded by excommunicating the murderers. Butler suggests that the incident was orchestrated by enemies of his family.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler2003181_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler2003181-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After this, Adalbert could not safely stay in Bohemia and escaped from Prague. Strachkvas was eventually appointed to be his successor. However, Strachkvas suddenly died during the liturgy at which he was to accede to his episcopal office in Prague. The cause of his death is still ambiguous. The Pope directed Adalbert to resume his see, but believing that he would not be allowed back, Adalbert requested a brief as an itinerant missionary.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert then traveled to <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> and probably baptized <a href="/wiki/G%C3%A9za,_Grand_Prince_of_the_Hungarians" title="Géza, Grand Prince of the Hungarians">Géza of Hungary</a> and his son <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen of Hungary">Stephen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Esztergom" title="Esztergom">Esztergom</a>. Then he went to <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> where he was cordially welcomed by <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">then-Duke Boleslaus I</a> and installed as Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Gniezno" title="Gniezno">Gniezno</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_and_martyrdom_in_Prussia">Mission and martyrdom in Prussia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Mission and martyrdom in Prussia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Polska_992_-_1025.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/220px-Polska_992_-_1025.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="197" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/330px-Polska_992_-_1025.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/440px-Polska_992_-_1025.png 2x" data-file-width="4215" data-file-height="3780" /></a><figcaption>Poland, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prussia_(Baltic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Prussia (Baltic)">Prussia</a> during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Boleslaus I</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg/220px-Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="138" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg/330px-Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg/440px-Gniezno_Cathedral_detail_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="1503" /></a><figcaption>The execution of Saint Adalbert by the pagan <a href="/wiki/Old_Prussians" title="Old Prussians">Prussians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gniezno_Doors" title="Gniezno Doors">Gniezno Doors</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Adalbert again relinquished his diocese, namely that of Gniezno, and set out as a missionary to preach to the inhabitants near <a href="/wiki/Prussia_(Baltic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Prussia (Baltic)">Prussia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Bolesław I</a>, Duke (and, later, King) of Poland, sent <a href="/wiki/Soldiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldiers">soldiers</a> with Adalbert on his mission to the Prussians. The Bishop and his companions, entered Prussian territory and traveled along the coast of the <a href="/wiki/Baltic_Sea" title="Baltic Sea">Baltic Sea</a> to <a href="/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk" title="Gdańsk">Gdańsk</a>. At the borders of the Polish realm, at the mouth of the <a href="/wiki/Vistula" title="Vistula">Vistula River</a>, his half-brother Radim (Gaudentius), Benedict-Bogusza (who was probably a Pole), and at least one interpreter, ventured out into Prussia alone, as Bolesław had only sent his soldiers to escort them to the border.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert achieved some success upon his arrival,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> however his arrival mostly caused strain upon the local Prussian populations.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Partially this was because of the imperious manner with which he preached,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> but potentially because he preached utilizing a book.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The Prussians had an oral society where communication was face to face. To the locals Adalbert reading from a book may have come off as a manifestation of an evil action. He was forced to leave this first village after being struck in the back of the head by an oar by a local chieftain, causing the pages of his book to scatter upon the ground. He and his companions then fled across a river.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the next place that Adalbert tried to preach, his message was met with the locals banging their sticks upon the ground, calling for the death of Adalbert and his companions. Retreating once again Adalbert and his companions went to a market place of <a href="/wiki/Truso" title="Truso">Truso</a> (near modern-day <a href="/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g" title="Elbląg">Elbląg</a>). Here they were met with a similar response as at the previous place.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> On the 23 April 997, after mass, while Adalbert and his companions lay in the grass while eating a snack, they were set upon by a pagan mob. The mob was led by a man named Sicco,<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> possibly a pagan priest,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> who delivered the first blow against Adalbert, before the others joined in. They removed Adalbert's head from his body after he was dead, and mounted on a pole while they returned home.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_16-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> This encounter may also have taken place in Tenkitten and <a href="/wiki/Primorsk,_Kaliningrad_Oblast" title="Primorsk, Kaliningrad Oblast">Fischhausen</a> (now Primorsk, <a href="/wiki/Kaliningrad_Oblast" title="Kaliningrad Oblast">Kaliningrad Oblast</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> It is recorded that his body was bought back for its weight in <a href="/wiki/Gold" title="Gold">gold</a> by King <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Boleslaus I of Poland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_relics">Veneration and relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration and relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Catedral_de_Gniezno,_Gniezno,_Polonia,_2014-09-17,_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg/170px-Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="300" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg/255px-Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg/340px-Catedral_de_Gniezno%2C_Gniezno%2C_Polonia%2C_2014-09-17%2C_DD_25-27_HDR.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3061" data-file-height="5403" /></a><figcaption>Silver coffin of Adalbert, Cathedral in Gniezno</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG/170px-Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG/255px-Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG/340px-Dystynktorium_kanonicki_Gennadij_Jerszow.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1728" data-file-height="2304" /></a><figcaption>Canonical cross of Saint Adalbert by <a href="/wiki/Giennadij_Jerszow" title="Giennadij Jerszow">Giennadij Jerszow</a>. Collegiate Capitol in <a href="/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk" title="Gdańsk">Gdańsk</a>. Silver-Gold 2011</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg/170px-Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="255" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg/255px-Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg/340px-Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg 2x" data-file-width="503" data-file-height="754" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Adalbert in Prague</figcaption></figure>
<p>A few years after his martyrdom, Adalbert was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as <b>Saint Adalbert of Prague.</b> His life was written in <i>Vita Sancti Adalberti Pragensis</i> by various authors, the earliest being traced to imperial <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a> and the Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>, <a href="/wiki/Notger_von_L%C3%BCttich" class="mw-redirect" title="Notger von Lüttich">Notger von Lüttich</a>, although it was previously assumed that the <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Roman</a> monk <a href="/wiki/John_Canaparius" title="John Canaparius">John Canaparius</a> wrote the first <i>Vita</i> in 999. Another famous biographer of Adalbert was <a href="/wiki/Bruno_of_Querfurt" title="Bruno of Querfurt">Bruno of Querfurt</a> who wrote a hagiography of him in 1001–4.
</p><p>Notably, the Přemyslid rulers of Bohemia initially refused to ransom Adalbert's body from the Prussians who murdered him, and therefore it was purchased by Poles. This fact may be explained by Adalbert's belonging to the Slavniks family which was rival to the Přemyslids. Thus Adalbert's bones were preserved in <a href="/wiki/Gniezno" title="Gniezno">Gniezno</a>, which assisted <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Boleslaus I of Poland</a> in increasing Polish political and diplomatic power in Europe.
</p><p>According to Bohemian accounts, in 1039 the Bohemian Duke <a href="/wiki/Bretislav_I" title="Bretislav I">Bretislav I</a> looted the bones of Adalbert from Gniezno in a raid and translated them to <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>. According to Polish accounts, however, he stole the wrong relics, namely those of Gaudentius, while the Poles concealed Adalbert's relics which remain in <a href="/wiki/Gniezno" title="Gniezno">Gniezno</a>. In 1127 his severed head, which was not in the original purchase according to <i>Roczniki Polskie</i>, was discovered and translated to Gniezno. In 1928, one of the arms of Adalbert, which Bolesław I had given to <a href="/wiki/Otto_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor Otto III</a> in 1000, was added to the bones preserved in Gniezno. Therefore, today Adalbert has two elaborate shrines in the <a href="/wiki/Prague_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Prague Cathedral">Prague Cathedral</a><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Gniezno_Cathedral" title="Gniezno Cathedral">Royal Cathedral of Gniezno</a>, each of which claims to possess his relics, but which of these bones are his authentic relics is unknown. For example, pursuant to both claims two skulls are attributed to Adalbert. The one in Gniezno was stolen in 1923.
</p><p>The massive bronze <a href="/wiki/Gniezno_Doors" title="Gniezno Doors">doors</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gniezno_Cathedral" title="Gniezno Cathedral">Gniezno Cathedral</a>, dating from around 1175, are decorated with eighteen <a href="/wiki/Relief" title="Relief">reliefs</a> of scenes from Adalbert's life. They are the only <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_art" title="Romanesque art">Romanesque</a> ecclesiastical doors in Europe depicting a cycle illustrating the life of a saint, and therefore are a precious relic documenting Adalbert's martyrdom. We can read that door literally and theologically.
</p><p>The one thousandth anniversary of Adalbert's martyrdom was on 23 April 1997. It was commemorated in Poland, the Czech Republic, Germany, Russia, and other nations. Representatives of <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Evangelicalism" title="Evangelicalism">Evangelical</a> churches traveled on a pilgrimage to Adalbert's tomb located in Gniezno. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> visited the cathedral and celebrated a liturgy there in which heads of seven European nations and approximately one million faithful participated.
</p><p>A ten-meter cross was erected near the village of Beregovoe (formerly Tenkitten), <a href="/wiki/Kaliningrad_Oblast" title="Kaliningrad Oblast">Kaliningrad Oblast</a>, where Adalbert is thought to have been martyred by the Prussians.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture_and_society">In popular culture and society</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Prague&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In popular culture and society">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Dagmar and Václav Havel <a href="/wiki/VIZE_97" class="mw-redirect" title="VIZE 97">VIZE 97</a> Foundation Prize, given annually to a distinguished thinker "whose work exceeds the traditional framework of scientific knowledge, contributes to the understanding of science as an integral part of general culture and is concerned with unconventional ways of asking fundamental questions about cognition, being and human existence"
includes a massive replica of Adalbert's crozier by Czech artist Jiří Plieštík.
</p><p>St. Vojtech Fellowship was established in 1870 by Slovak Catholic priest Andrej Radlinský. It had facilitated <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovak</a> Catholic thinkers and authors, continuing to publish religious original works and translations to this day. It is the official publishing body of <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Conference_of_Slovakia" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal Conference of Slovakia">Episcopal Conference of Slovakia</a>.
</p>
