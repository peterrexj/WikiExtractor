16 September 1917 – 13 July 1943) was a <a href="/wiki/Russians" title="Russians">Russian</a>-<a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> student at <a href="/wiki/Munich_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Munich University">Munich University</a> who, with five others, formed a resistance group (part of the <i><a href="/wiki/German_resistance_to_Nazism" title="German resistance to Nazism">Widerstand</a></i>) known as <a href="/wiki/White_Rose" title="White Rose">White Rose</a> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Weiße Rose</i>) which was active against the <a href="/wiki/Nazi_Germany" title="Nazi Germany">Nazi German</a> regime from June 1942 to February 1943.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 2012, he was glorified as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Passion_bearer" title="Passion bearer">passion bearer</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a>, and is venerated by <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Christians</a> throughout the world.<sup id="cite_ref-sobor_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sobor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alexander Schmorell was born in Orenburg, Russia on September 3/16, 1917 (Russia still used the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a> when he was born)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Schmorell's father was Hugo Schmorell, a German-born physician who was raised in the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a>. Schmorell's mother was Natalia Vedenskaya, a Russian and the daughter of a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox">Russian Orthodox</a> priest. Schmorell was baptised in the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>. His mother died of <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Civil_War" title="Russian Civil War">Russian Civil War</a> when he was two years old. In 1920, his widowed father married a German woman, Elisabeth Hoffman, who, like him, was raised in Russia. In 1921 the family fled from Russia and moved to <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>, <a href="/wiki/Weimar_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Weimar Germany">Weimar Germany</a>, Schmorell was four years old at the time. In Germany, he grew up with his step-siblings Erich Schmorell (born 1921) and Natalie Schmorell (born 1925), as well as his Russian <a href="/wiki/Nanny" title="Nanny">nanny</a>, Feodosiya Lapschina.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She took his late mother's place in his upbringing. 
</p><p>His nanny never learned how to speak German. Because of this, Alexander Schmorell grew up <a href="/wiki/Bilingual" class="mw-redirect" title="Bilingual">bilingual</a>, speaking both <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a> and <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a> natively.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His friends gave him the nickname 'Schurik', a nickname he would be called by his closer friends for the rest of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Christian">Eastern Orthodox Christian</a> who considered himself both German and Russian. As declared in the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a>'s interrogations, he was a convinced <a href="/wiki/Tsar" title="Tsar">Tsarist</a> and then an archenemy of the <a href="/wiki/Bolshevism" title="Bolshevism">Bolsheviks</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_service">Military service</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Military service">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his <i><a href="/wiki/Abitur" title="Abitur">Abitur</a></i> (equivalent to high level High School diploma), he was called into the Reich Labour Service (<i><a href="/wiki/Reichsarbeitsdienst" class="mw-redirect" title="Reichsarbeitsdienst">Reichsarbeitsdienst</a></i>) and then into the <a href="/wiki/Wehrmacht" title="Wehrmacht">Wehrmacht</a> (German Army during the Nazi era).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1937, he volunteered to join the <a href="/wiki/Wehrmacht" title="Wehrmacht">Wehrmacht</a>. At the last moment, however, he had second thoughts and refused to swear the <a href="/wiki/Hitler_Oath" title="Hitler Oath">Hitler Oath</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Surprisingly, he was still allowed to join the Wehrmacht. In 1938, he took part in the <i><a href="/wiki/Anschluss" title="Anschluss">Anschluss</a></i> (the Nazi Annexation of Austria) and eventually in the <a href="/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia" class="mw-redirect" title="German occupation of Czechoslovakia">invasion of Czechoslovakia</a>.
</p>
<h2><span id="Work_in_the_White_Rose.2C_Summer_1942"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Work_in_the_White_Rose,_Summer_1942">Work in the White Rose, Summer 1942</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Work in the White Rose, Summer 1942">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his <a href="/wiki/Conscription" title="Conscription">military service</a>, the artistically gifted Alexander Schmorell began studies in <a href="/wiki/Medicine" title="Medicine">medicine</a> in 1939 in <a href="/wiki/Hamburg" title="Hamburg">Hamburg</a>. In the autumn of 1940, he returned with his student corps to Munich where he came to know <a href="/wiki/Hans_Scholl" title="Hans Scholl">Hans Scholl</a>, whom he met through <a href="/wiki/Christoph_Probst" title="Christoph Probst">Christoph Probst</a>, his life-long friend.<sup id="cite_ref-sobor_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sobor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In June 1942, Schmorell, together with Hans Scholl, began the Nazi Resistance Movement "The White Rose". Their form of resistance was simple but dangerous: writing leaflets. Quoting extensively from the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aristotle" title="Aristotle">Aristotle</a> and <a href="/wiki/Novalis" title="Novalis">Novalis</a>, as well as <a href="/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_von_Goethe" title="Johann Wolfgang von Goethe">Goethe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Friedrich_Schiller" title="Friedrich Schiller">Schiller</a>, the iconic poets of German bourgeoisie, the leaflets appealed to what Schmorell and Scholl considered the German <a href="/wiki/Intelligentsia" title="Intelligentsia">intelligentsia</a>, believing that these people would be easily convinced by the same arguments that also motivated the authors themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> These leaflets were left in telephone books in public phone booths, mailed to professors and students, and taken by courier to other universities for distribution.
</p>
<h2><span id="Deployment_to_Russia_July.E2.80.93November_1942"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Deployment_to_Russia_July–November_1942">Deployment to Russia July–November 1942</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Deployment to Russia July–November 1942">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In June 1942, male students at the <a href="/wiki/Ludwig_Maximilian_University_of_Munich" title="Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich">Ludwig Maximilian University</a> were required to deploy to the Eastern Front over Summer break. Schmorell, along with <a href="/wiki/Hans_Scholl" title="Hans Scholl">Hans Scholl</a>, <a href="/wiki/Willi_Graf" title="Willi Graf">Willi Graf</a>, and Jurgen Wittenstein, served as medics on the Russian Front from June to November 1942.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> During this time, White Rose activities ceased, and were not continued until the medics came home from the deployment.
</p><p>While in Russia, Schmorell felt like he was at home. Although he had been born in Russia, he had no memories of his homeland, as he had emigrated when he was only four years old. In Russia, Schmorell, Scholl, Graf, and Wittenstein would sneak out of camp at night and would gather at the home of Russian peasants, where Schmorell and his friends would take part in Russian festivities.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In August 1942, Schmorell came down with <a href="/wiki/Diphtheria" title="Diphtheria">diphtheria</a>. At first, he didn't tell his father and stepmother, as he didn't want to burden them. He only told them of his sickness after he had recovered.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Schmorell and his friends left Russia on October 31, 1942. Schmorell, who had become infatuated with Russia, considered deserting the <a href="/wiki/Wehrmacht" title="Wehrmacht">Wehrmacht</a>, but decided against it.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They returned to Munich on November 5, 1942.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span id="Work_in_the_White_Rose.2C_November_1942-January_1943"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Work_in_the_White_Rose,_November_1942-January_1943">Work in the White Rose, November 1942-January 1943</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Work in the White Rose, November 1942-January 1943">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In December 1942, Schmorell, along with Hans Scholl, sought contact with Professor <a href="/wiki/Kurt_Huber" title="Kurt Huber">Kurt Huber</a>. Together in 1943 they wrote the fifth leaflet, <i>"Aufruf an alle Deutschen!"</i> ('Appeal to all Germans!'), which Schmorell then distributed in <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austrian</a> cities.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Graffiti_Campaigns">Graffiti Campaigns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Graffiti Campaigns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On February 3, 1943, the news of the defeat of <a href="/wiki/Stalingrad" class="mw-redirect" title="Stalingrad">Stalingrad</a> was broadcast to the German public. Later that day, Graf, Schmorell, and Scholl snuck out at night and graffitied public buildings with slogans such as "down with Hitler" and "Hitler the Mass murderer!" During the campaign, Schmorell would hold up the stencils while Graf painted the slogans on with tar paint. Scholl stood guard, armed with a pistol in case anyone walked in on their graffitiing. On February 8, 1943, Graf and Scholl graffitied again. This time, they used green oil-based paint.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On February 15, 1943, Scholl, Schmorell, and Graf snuck out and graffitied the <i><a href="/wiki/Feldherrnhalle" title="Feldherrnhalle">Feldherrnhalle</a>,</i> then a Nazi monument to the Nazis who were killed during the failed <a href="/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch" title="Beer Hall Putsch">Beer Hall Putsch</a>. The graffiti campaigns put the Gestapo on high alert.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Capture">Capture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Capture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 18 February 1943, Sophie and Hans Scholl went to the Ludwig Maximilian University to leave flyers out for the students to read. They were seen by <a href="/wiki/Jakob_Schmid" title="Jakob Schmid">Jakob Schmid</a>, a custodian at the University who was also a Gestapo informer. Schmid alerted the Gestapo, who took Hans and Sophie in custody.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Alexander soon learned of their capture. He then went to Willi Graf's house with the intention of warning him that Hans and Sophie had been captured. Graf was not at home, so Schmorell left a coded message and went to one of his friend's houses.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His friend helped him to get fake papers and gave him food and extra clothing. Schmorell's original plan was to enter a prisoner of war camp for Russia POWs, but that plan fell through when his contact did not show up.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Schmorell then attempted to escape to Switzerland. Fierce weather forced him back, and he returned to Munich on February 24, 1943.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At around 10 PM, the air raid alarm sounded. When Schmorell attempted to enter the air raid shelter, he was recognized by a former girlfriend. The Gestapo were called, and Schmorell was arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He was captured by the Gestapo on February 24, the same day as Sophie, Hans and Christoph's funeral.<sup id="cite_ref-sobor_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sobor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During the time between his capture and his trial, Schmorell was interrogated multiple times.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trial_and_execution">Trial and execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Trial and execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/File:Gestapo_photos_of_Alexander_Schmorell,_taken_after_his_capture_on_February_24,_1943.jpg" class="image" title="Gestapo photos of Alexander Schmorell, taken after his capture on February 24, 1943"><img alt="Gestapo photos of Alexander Schmorell, taken after his capture on February 24, 1943" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/Gestapo_photos_of_Alexander_Schmorell%2C_taken_after_his_capture_on_February_24%2C_1943.jpg" decoding="async" width="775" height="308" data-file-width="775" data-file-height="308" /></a>
</p><p><i>Gestapo Photo of Alexander Schmorell, taken after his capture on April 24, 1943</i>
</p><p>On April 19. 1943 Alexander Schmorell was put on trial, along with 13 other members of the White Rose group. Schmorell, <a href="/wiki/Willi_Graf" title="Willi Graf">Willi Graf</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Kurt_Huber" title="Kurt Huber">Kurt Huber</a> were sentenced to death. The others received prison sentences.
</p><p>Unlike the first trial, where the death sentences had been carried out the same day as the verdict, Alexander's execution was delayed as his family petitioned for clemency. After about two months of deliberation, a letter came to the prison that said: “I reject all petitions for mercy.” It was signed “Adolf Hitler”.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On July 13, 1943, Alexander and Kurt Huber were alerted that their execution would happen later that day. After receiving communion from an Orthodox priest, he was allowed to write a last letter to their family. Alexander wrote to his parents:
</p><p><i>"I am going with the awareness that I followed my deepest convictions and the truth. This allows me to meet my hour of death with a conscience at peace. Think of the millions of young men who have lost their lives out on the field-their fate is the same as mine...In a few hours I will be in a better life, with my mother, and I will not forget you; I will ask God to grant you solace and peace. Yours, Shurik."</i><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The executions were supposed to be carried out at 5 pm, but as Schmorell prepared himself, he learned that the execution would be delayed. Several SS officers had appeared at Stadelheim under orders to observe the execution to see how long it took for each man to die; the SS officers were then supposed to report back to their superiors with suggestions on how to shorten or prolong the suffering of the man being hanged. The officers were put off when they learned that the execution was to be by guillotine and not hanging. They then demanded a detailed explanation of how the guillotine worked, so their time was not wasted. The execution was delayed until the SS officers left.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The guards came for Alexander a little after 5 pm. He was led out of his cell and into the courtyard, walking to his death with his head held high, he said to his lawyer, <i>"I'm convinced that my life has to end now, early as it may seem, for I have fulfilled my life's mission. I wouldn't know what else I have to do on this earth."</i><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In the execution chamber, the state attorney asked if his name was Alexander Schmorell, to which he replied, "yes".<sup id="cite_ref-:4_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Then the blade fell, and Alexander Schmorell was no more. Kurt Huber was executed a few minutes later.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Schmorell&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Completing the act of canonization, Schmorell was glorified as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Passion_bearer" title="Passion bearer">passion bearer</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> on February 5, 2012.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
