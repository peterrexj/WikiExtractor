<p><b>Julian and Basilissa</b> (died <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;304</span>) were <a href="/wiki/Husband" title="Husband">husband</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wife" title="Wife">wife</a>, and are venerated as saints in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.  They were <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> who died at either <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> or, more probably, at <a href="/wiki/Antinopolis" class="mw-redirect" title="Antinopolis">Antinoe</a>, in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, early in the fourth century, on 6 January, according to the <i>Roman Martyrology</i>, or 8 January, according to the Greek <i><a href="/wiki/Menaea_Graeca" title="Menaea Graeca">Menaea</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There exists no historically certain data relating to these two personages, and more than once this Julian of Antinoe has been confounded with <a href="/wiki/Julian_of_Cilicia" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian of Cilicia">Julian of Cilicia</a>. The confusion is easily explained by the fact that thirty-nine saints of this name are mentioned in the <i>Roman Martyrology</i>, eight of whom are commemorated in the one month of January. But little is known of this saint, aside from the exaggerations of his <i>Acts</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_and_Basilissa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Forced by his family to marry, he agreed with his spouse, Basilissa, that they should both preserve their <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virginity</a>, and further encouraged her to found a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> for women, of which she became the superior, while he himself gathered a large number of <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> and undertook their direction.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The two converted their home into a <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a> which could house up to 1,000 people (thus, Julian is often confused with <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Hospitaller" title="Julian the Hospitaller">Julian the Hospitaller</a>).
</p><p>Basilissa, after having stood severe persecutions, died in peace; Julian survived her many years, but was martyred, (together with Celsus a youth, Antony a priest, Anastatius, and Marcianilla the mother of Celsus) under the <a href="/wiki/Persecutions_of_Diocletian" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecutions of Diocletian">Persecutions of Diocletian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Julian.27s_martyrdom"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Julian's_martyrdom">Julian's martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_and_Basilissa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Julian&#039;s martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the persecution of Diocletian he was arrested, tortured, and put to death at Antioch, in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, by the order of the governor, Martian, according to the Latins, at Antinoe, in <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, according to the Greeks, which seems more probable. Unfortunately, as with most saints lives the exact historical details are hard to parse from the religious tropes and maxims.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Celsus_and_Marcionilla" title="Celsus and Marcionilla">Celsus</a>, the young son of <a href="/wiki/Celsus_and_Marcionilla" title="Celsus and Marcionilla">Marcionilla</a>, was martyred along with Julian.  The priest <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Antioch" title="Anthony of Antioch">Anthony (Antony)</a> was martyred at the same time, as well as a convert and <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/neophyte" class="extiw" title="wikt:neophyte">neophyte</a> named <a href="/wiki/Anastasius_of_Antioch_(martyr)" title="Anastasius of Antioch (martyr)">Anastasius</a>.  Marcionilla's seven brothers are also said to have been killed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_and_Basilissa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In any case, these two must have enjoyed a great reputation in <a href="/wiki/Ancient_history" title="Ancient history">antiquity</a>, and their veneration was well established before the eighth century. In the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> they are mentioned under 6 January; <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a>, <a href="/wiki/Notker_of_St_Gall" class="mw-redirect" title="Notker of St Gall">Notker of St Gall</a>, and others place them under the ninth, and <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a> under the thirteenth of the same month, while Vandelbert puts them under 13 February, and the Menology of Canisius under 21 June, the day to which the Greek Menaea assign St. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Julian of Caesarea (page does not exist)">Julian of Caesarea</a>. There used to exist at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> a church under the invocation of these saints, the dedication of which is inscribed in the Greek Calendar under 5 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Only a fragment of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfric_of_Eynsham" title="Ælfric of Eynsham">Ælfric's</a> <i>Passion of St. Julian and His Wife Basilissa</i> from his <i>Lives of the Saints</i> has survived, but the traditional legend is there: the two vow not to <a href="/wiki/Consummate" class="mw-redirect" title="Consummate">consummate</a> their marriage on their wedding night, and devote themselves to <i>clænnysse</i> ("chastity").  Julian suffers martyrdom by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheading</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
