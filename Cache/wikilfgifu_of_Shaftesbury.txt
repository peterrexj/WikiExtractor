<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above fn" style="background-color: #cbe; width:100%;"></div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 944</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Burial</th><td class="infobox-data"><div style="display:inline" class="label"><a href="/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey" title="Shaftesbury Abbey">Shaftesbury Abbey</a></div></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Spouse</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Edmund_I,_King_of_the_English" class="mw-redirect" title="Edmund I, King of the English">Edmund I, King of the English</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Issue</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/King_Eadwig" class="mw-redirect" title="King Eadwig">King Eadwig</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Edgar,_King_of_England" title="Edgar, King of England">King Edgar</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Mother</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Wynflaed" title="Wynflaed">Wynflaed</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Ælfgifu of Shaftesbury</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">18 May</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Ælfgifu of Shaftesbury</b> (died 944) was the first wife of King <a href="/wiki/Edmund_I" title="Edmund I">Edmund I</a> (r. 939–946). She was <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_the_English" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen of the English">Queen of the English</a> from her marriage in around 939 until her death in 944. Ælfgifu and Edmund were the parents of two future English kings, <a href="/wiki/Eadwig" title="Eadwig">Eadwig</a> (r. 955–959) and <a href="/wiki/Edgar,_King_of_England" title="Edgar, King of England">Edgar</a> (r. 959–975).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilliams2004_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams2004-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Like her mother <a href="/wiki/Wynflaed" title="Wynflaed">Wynflaed</a>, Ælfgifu had a close and special if unknown connection with the royal <a href="/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey" title="Shaftesbury Abbey">nunnery of Shaftesbury</a> (Dorset), founded by <a href="/wiki/King_Alfred" class="mw-redirect" title="King Alfred">King Alfred</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ftn18_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn18-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where she was buried and soon revered as a saint. According to a pre-Conquest tradition from <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a>, her feast day is 18 May.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn24_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn24-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_background">Family background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfgifu_of_Shaftesbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d,_circa_AD_950_(11th-century_copy,_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg/350px-S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="151" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg/525px-S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg/700px-S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d%2C_circa_AD_950_%2811th-century_copy%2C_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1447" data-file-height="624" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:S_1539_Will_of_Wynfl%C3%A6d,_circa_AD_950_(11th-century_copy,_BL_Cotton_Charters_viii._38).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Will of <a href="/wiki/Wynflaed" title="Wynflaed">Wynflæd</a> (<a href="/wiki/British_Library" title="British Library">British Library</a> Cotton Charters viii. 38)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></div></div></div>
<p>Her mother appears to have been an associate of Shaftesbury Abbey called <a href="/wiki/Wynflaed" title="Wynflaed">Wynflaed</a> (also Wynnflæd). The vital clue comes from a charter of <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceable" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceable">King Edgar</a>, in which he confirmed the grant of an estate at <i>Uppidelen</i> (<a href="/wiki/Piddletrenthide" title="Piddletrenthide">Piddletrenthide</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dorset,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Dorset, England">Dorset</a>) made by his grandmother (<i>ava</i>) Wynflæd to Shaftesbury<i>.</i><sup id="cite_ref-ftn1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She may well be the nun or vowess (<i>religiosa femina</i>) of this name in a charter dated 942 and preserved in the abbey's <a href="/wiki/Chartulary" class="mw-redirect" title="Chartulary">chartulary</a>. It records that she received and retrieved from King Edmund a handful of estates in Dorset, namely <a href="/wiki/Cheselbourne" title="Cheselbourne">Cheselbourne</a> and Winterbourne Tomson, which somehow ended up in the possession of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn2_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn2-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Since no father or siblings are known, further speculation on Ælfgifu's background has largely depended on the identity of her mother, whose relatively uncommon name has invited further guesswork. <a href="/wiki/H._P._R._Finberg" title="H. P. R. Finberg">H. P. R. Finberg</a> suggests that she was the Wynflæd who drew up a will, supposedly sometime in the mid-10th century, after Ælfgifu's death. This lady held many estates scattered across <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Somerset,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Somerset, England">Somerset</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Wiltshire, England">Wiltshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Berkshire,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Berkshire, England">Berkshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxfordshire, England">Oxfordshire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Hampshire,_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Hampshire, England">Hampshire</a>) and was well connected with the nunneries at Wilton and Shaftesbury, both of which were royal foundations. On that basis, a number of relatives have been proposed for Ælfgifu, including a sister called Æthelflæd, a brother called Eadmær, and a grandmother called Brihtwyn.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn3_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn3-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is, however, no consensus among scholars about Finberg's suggestion. Simon Keynes and Gale R. Owen object that there is no sign of royal relatives or connections in Wynflæd's will and Finberg's assumptions about Ælfgifu's family therefore stand on shaky ground.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn4_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn4-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Wareham" title="Andrew Wareham">Andrew Wareham</a> is less troubled about this and suggests that different kinship strategies may account for it.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn5_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn5-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Much of the issue of identification also seems to hang on the number of years by which Wynflæd can plausibly have outlived her daughter. In this light, it is significant that on palaeographical grounds, David Dumville has rejected the conventional date of <i>c</i>. 950 for the will, which he considers “speculative and too early” (and that one Wynflæd was still alive in 967).<sup id="cite_ref-ftn6_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn6-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Married_life">Married life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfgifu_of_Shaftesbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Married life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The sources do not record the date of Ælfgifu's marriage to <a href="/wiki/Edmund_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Edmund I of England">Edmund</a>. The eldest son Eadwig, who had barely reached majority on his accession in 955, may have been born around 940, which gives us only a very rough <i>terminus ante quem</i> for the betrothal. Although as the mother of two future kings, Ælfgifu proved to be an important royal bed companion, there is no strictly contemporary evidence that she was ever consecrated as queen. In a charter of doubtful authenticity dated 942–946, she attests as the king's concubine (<i>concubina regis</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> but later in the century <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelweard_the_Chronicler" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelweard the Chronicler">Æthelweard the Chronicler</a> styles her queen (<i>regina</i>).
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:502px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Shaftesbury_Abbey_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Shaftesbury_Abbey_1.jpg/500px-Shaftesbury_Abbey_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="500" height="342" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Shaftesbury_Abbey_1.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="438" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Shaftesbury_Abbey_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The remains of the Norman buildings which replaced the earlier ones at Shaftesbury Abbey.</div></div></div>
<p>Much of Ælfgifu's claim to fame derives from her association with Shaftesbury. Her patronage of the community is suggested by a charter of <a href="/wiki/Ethelred_the_Unready" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethelred the Unready">King Æthelred</a>, dated 984, according to which the abbey exchanged with King Edmund the large estate at <a href="/wiki/Tisbury,_Wiltshire" title="Tisbury, Wiltshire">Tisbury</a> (Wiltshire) for <i>Butticanlea</i> (unidentified). Ælfgifu received it from her husband and intended to bequeath it back to the nunnery, but such had not yet come to pass (her son Eadwig demanded that <i>Butticanlea</i> was returned to the royal family first).<sup id="cite_ref-ftn13_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn13-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ælfgifu predeceased her husband, probably in 944, and may have died in childbirth of Edgar.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEYorke2014143Keynes199965,_n._78_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEYorke2014143Keynes199965,_n._78-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In the early 12th century, <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a> wrote that she suffered from an illness during the last few years of her life, but there may have been some confusion with details of Æthelgifu's life as recorded in a forged foundation charter of the late 11th or 12th century (see below).<sup id="cite_ref-ftn8_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn8-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Her body was buried and enshrined at the nunnery.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn22_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn22-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86lfgifu_of_Shaftesbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ælfgifu was venerated as a saint soon after her burial at Shaftesbury. Æthelweard reports that many miracles had taken place at her tomb up to his day,<sup id="cite_ref-ftn9_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn9-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and these were apparently attracting some local attention. <a href="/wiki/Lantfred" title="Lantfred">Lantfred</a> of Winchester, who wrote in the 970's and so can be called the earliest known witness of her cult, tells of a young man from Collingbourne (possibly Collingbourne Kingston, Wiltshire), who in the hope of being cured of blindness travelled to Shaftesbury and kept vigil. What led him there was the reputation of “the venerable St Ælfgifu [...] at whose tomb many bodies of sick person receive medication through the omnipotence of God”.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn10_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn10-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Despite the new prominence of Edward the Martyr as a saint interred at Shaftesbury, her cult continued to flourish in later Anglo-Saxon England, as evidenced by her inclusion in a list of saints' resting places, at least 8 pre-Conquest calendars and 3 or 4 litanies from Winchester.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn15_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn15-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ælfgifu is styled a saint (<i>Sancte Ælfgife</i>) in the D-text of the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i> (mid-11th century) at the point where it specifies Eadwig's and Edgar's royal parentage.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn11_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn11-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Her cult may have been fostered and used to enhance the status of the royal lineage, more narrowly that of her descendants.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn14_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn14-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Lantfred attributes her healing power both to her own merits and those of her son Edgar. It may have been due to her association that in 979 the supposed body of her murdered grandson Edward the Martyr was exhumed and in a spectacular ceremony, received at the nunnery of Shaftesbury, under the supervision of ealdorman Ælfhere.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn12_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn12-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to William of Malmesbury, Ælfgifu would secretly redeem those who were publicly condemned to severe judgment, she gave expensive clothes to the poor, and she also had prophetic powers as well as powers of healing.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ælfgifu's fame at Shaftesbury seems to have eclipsed that of its first abbess, King Alfred's daughter <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelgifu" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelgifu">Æthelgifu</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ftn23_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn23-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> so much so perhaps that William of Malmesbury wrote contradictory reports on the abbey's early history. In the <i>Gesta regum</i>, he correctly identifies the first abbess as Alfred's daughter, following <a href="/wiki/Asser" title="Asser">Asser</a>, although he gives her the name of Ælfgifu (<i>Elfgiva</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-ftn20_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn20-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> while in his <i>Gesta pontificum</i>, he credits Edmund's wife Ælfgifu with the foundation.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn21_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn21-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Either William encountered conflicting information, or he meant to say that Ælfgifu refounded the nunnery.<sup id="cite_ref-ftn19_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn19-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> In any event, William would have had access to local traditions at Shaftesbury, since he probably wrote a now lost metrical <i>Life</i> for the community, a fragment of which he included in his <i>Gesta pontificum</i>:<sup id="cite_ref-ftn17_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ftn17-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
