<p><b>Saint Fiacre</b>  (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Fiachra</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Fiacrius</i>) is the name of three different <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a>, the most famous of which is <b>Saint Fiacre of Breuil</b> (c. AD 600 – 18 August 670<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gardener" title="Gardener">gardener</a> of the seventh century who was famous for his sanctity and skill in curing infirmities. He emigrated from his native <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, where he constructed for himself a hermitage together with a <a href="/wiki/Vegetable" title="Vegetable">vegetable</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herb_garden" class="mw-redirect" title="Herb garden">herb garden</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Hostel" title="Hostel">hospice</a> for travellers. He is the patron saint of gardeners.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Fiacre_of_Breuil">Saint Fiacre of Breuil</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Saint Fiacre of Breuil">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Fiacre_Window,_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg/200px-St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="388" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg/300px-St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg/400px-St._Fiacre_Window%2C_Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1011" data-file-height="1963" /></a><figcaption>St. Fiacre window, <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Good_Shepherd_(Rosemont,_Pennsylvania)" title="Church of the Good Shepherd (Rosemont, Pennsylvania)">Church of the Good Shepherd (Rosemont, Pennsylvania)</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span title="Irish-language text"><i lang="ga">Fiachra</i></span> is an ancient pre-<a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> name. It has been interpreted to denote "battle king"<sup id="cite_ref-Ó_Corráin_1981_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ó_Corráin_1981-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> or to derive from <span title="Irish-language text"><i lang="ga">fiach</i></span> ("raven").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The name is found in ancient <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> folklore and stories such as the <i><a href="/wiki/Children_of_Lir" title="Children of Lir">Children of Lir</a></i>.
</p><p>The appellation "of Breuil" can in present times be misleading: the site of the hermitage, garden, oratory, and hospice of Saint Fiacre was in the place denominated "Brogillum" in ancient times and later renamed "Breuil", forming his epithet. However, Breuil was then again renamed "<a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a>" in his honor, which is the name of the present commune on the same site, in the <a href="/wiki/Seine-et-Marne" title="Seine-et-Marne">Department of Seine-et-Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The commune of <a href="/wiki/Breuil,_Marne" class="mw-redirect" title="Breuil, Marne">Breuil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marne_(department)" title="Marne (department)">Department of Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> is located far from and is not the same as the commune of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a> (formerly named "Breuil"), although the two communes probably were both in the ancient French <a href="/wiki/Brie_(region)" title="Brie (region)">Province of Brie</a>, which adds to the confusion.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_fiacre.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint_fiacre.jpg/170px-Saint_fiacre.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint_fiacre.jpg/255px-Saint_fiacre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Saint_fiacre.jpg/340px-Saint_fiacre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="2560" /></a><figcaption>Saint Fiacre, 15th-century statue in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Taurinus" title="Saint Taurinus">Saint Taurin d'Évreux</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>"Though not mentioned in the earlier Irish calendars, Fiacre was born in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> at the end of the sixth century AD. He was raised in a monastery where he became a monk and imbibed knowledge of herbal medicine."<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Fiacre was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> at some point, and elevated to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> "In time he had his own <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> and perhaps a monastery, possibly near St. Fiachra’s Well at Cill Fiachra (Kilferagh), Sheastown, in the barony of Shillelogher near Bennetsbridge, County Kilkenny, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.  As crowds flocked to him because of his reputation for his holiness and cures, he sailed to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> in search of greater solitude."<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He arrived in <a href="/wiki/Meaux" title="Meaux">Meaux</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> in AD 628.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Faro" title="Saint Faro">Saint Faro</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Meaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Meaux">Bishop of Meaux</a>, was "well-disposed to him due to kindnesses he and his father’s house had received from the Irish missionary <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>," and so "granted him a site at Brogillum (Breuil), in the province of <a href="/wiki/Brie_(region)" title="Brie (region)">Brie</a>"<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (presently <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seine-et-Marne" title="Seine-et-Marne">Department of Seine-et-Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>) when Saint Fiacre approached him and manifested his desire to live a life of solitude in the forest.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> There Saint Fiacre built a <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> for his dwelling, a <a href="/wiki/Vegetable" title="Vegetable">vegetable</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herb_garden" class="mw-redirect" title="Herb garden">herb garden</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> in honor of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Hostel" title="Hostel">hospice</a> in which he cared for travellers. He lived a life of great <a href="/wiki/Mortification_in_Roman_Catholic_teaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortification in Roman Catholic teaching">mortification</a> devoted to prayer, <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a>, keeping <a href="/wiki/Vigil" title="Vigil">vigils</a>, and manual cultivation of his garden. "His fame for miracles was widespread. He cured all manner of diseases by laying on his hands".<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died on 18 August AD 670, and his body was interred in the local church of the site of his hermitage complex, which church became his original shrine.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The site of his hermitage complex developed into a village, which was later named <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a> and is presently in the <a href="/wiki/Seine-et-Marne" title="Seine-et-Marne">Department of Seine-et-Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Faro" title="Saint Faro">Saint Faro</a> allowed Saint Fiacre as much land as he might entrench in one day with a <a href="/wiki/Furrow" class="mw-redirect" title="Furrow">furrow</a>; Fiacre turned up the earth with the end of his staff, toppling trees and uprooting briers and weeds. A suspicious woman hastened to tell <a href="/wiki/Saint_Faro" title="Saint Faro">Saint Faro</a> that he was being beguiled and that this was <a href="/wiki/Witchcraft" title="Witchcraft">witchcraft</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Faro" title="Saint Faro">Saint Faro</a>, however, recognized that this was the work of God. It is said that thereafter Saint Fiacre prohibited women, on pain of severe bodily infirmity, from the precincts of his hermitage.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fiacre's sister <a href="/wiki/Syra_of_Troyes" title="Syra of Troyes">Syra</a> came to join him in France and became a nun. She was blind, but as <a href="/wiki/John_O%27Hanlon_(writer)" title="John O&#39;Hanlon (writer)">John O'Hanlon</a> relates, "Through his merits, St. Fiacre had an inspiration, that his sister should recover her sight, while to her in like manner was revealed the spot where the body of <a href="/wiki/Sabinian_of_Troyes" title="Sabinian of Troyes">St. Savinien</a> lay. There, prostrating herself, she poured forth her soul in prayer, and her face bedewed with tears, she would not rise from the ground until her petition was heard. She was restored miraculously to the use of vision."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg/220px-St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg/330px-St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg/440px-St_Fiachra%27s_Garden.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a><figcaption>St. Fiachra's garden, <a href="/wiki/Irish_National_Stud" title="Irish National Stud">Irish National Stud and Gardens</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Fiacre's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were preserved in his original shrine in the local church of the site of his hermitage, garden, oratory, and hospice, in present <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seine-et-Marne" title="Seine-et-Marne">Seine-et-Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, but later transferred in 1568 to their present shrine in <a href="/wiki/Meaux_Cathedral" title="Meaux Cathedral">Meaux Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Meaux" title="Meaux">Meaux</a>, which is near <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a> and in the same French department, because of fear that fanatical Calvinists endangered them. The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> commemorates him on 30 August,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> although some Catholic sources use an alternative date of 1 September.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  <a href="/wiki/Meaux" title="Meaux">Meaux</a> continued to be a great centre of devotion to him, especially in the 17th and 18th centuries. Visitors to his shrine included <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Austria" title="Anne of Austria">Anne of Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jacques-B%C3%A9nigne_Bossuet" title="Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet">Bishop Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_of_Matha" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John of Matha">Saint John of Matha</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIII_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XIII of France">King Louis XIII of France</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vincent de Paul">Saint Vincent de Paul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CathEncyclopedia_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncyclopedia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Saint Fiacre had a reputation for healing <a href="/wiki/Haemorrhoids" class="mw-redirect" title="Haemorrhoids">haemorrhoids</a>, which were denominated "Saint Fiacre's figs" in the Middle Ages. <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu" title="Cardinal Richelieu">Cardinal Richelieu</a> venerated his relics hoping to be relieved of the infirmity.<sup id="cite_ref-Marius_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marius-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Pubmed_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pubmed-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To celebrate the Second Millennium, "Saint Fiachra's Garden" opened in 1999 at the <a href="/wiki/Irish_National_Stud" title="Irish National Stud">Irish National Stud and Gardens</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Tully,_County_Kildare&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Tully, County Kildare (page does not exist)">Tully</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Kildare" title="County Kildare">County Kildare</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, his nation of birth.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Fiacre is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the commune of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fiacre,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Saint-Fiacre, Seine-et-Marne">Saint-Fiacre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seine-et-Marne" title="Seine-et-Marne">Seine-et-Marne</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. He is the patron of growers of vegetables and medicinal plants, and gardeners in general, including ploughboys.<sup id="cite_ref-Marius_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marius-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His reputed aversion to women is believed to be the reason that he is also considered the patron of victims of <a href="/wiki/Sexually_transmitted_infection" title="Sexually transmitted infection">venereal disease</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He is further the patron of victims of <a href="/wiki/Hemorrhoids" class="mw-redirect" title="Hemorrhoids">hemorrhoids</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fistula" title="Fistula">fistulas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Taxi_cab" class="mw-redirect" title="Taxi cab">taxi cab</a> drivers, box makers, <a href="/wiki/Florist" class="mw-redirect" title="Florist">florists</a>, hosiers, pewterers, tilemakers, and those suffering from infertility.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Finally, he is commonly invoked to heal persons suffering from various infirmities, premised on his reputed skill with medicinal plants.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fiacre_cabs">Fiacre cabs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Fiacre cabs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Fiacre_(carriage)" title="Fiacre (carriage)">Fiacre (carriage)</a></div>
<p>From about 1650, the <a href="/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Saint_Fiacre" title="Hôtel de Saint Fiacre">Hôtel de Saint Fiacre</a>, in the rue St-Martin in Paris, hired out <a href="/wiki/Carriage" title="Carriage">carriages</a>. These carriages came to be known as <a href="/wiki/Fiacre_(carriage)" title="Fiacre (carriage)">fiacres</a>, which became a generic term for hired horse-drawn transport. Although sometimes claimed by taxi-drivers as a patron saint, St. Fiacre is not recognized as such by the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-Marius_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marius-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_St._Fiacres">Other St. Fiacres</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fiacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Other St. Fiacres">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Two other <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbots</a> are named Saint Fiacre or Fiachra: Saint Fiachra, Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Ullard_Church" title="Ullard Church">Urard</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Carlow" title="County Carlow">County Carlow</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> and Saint Fiachra, Abbot of Clonard.<sup id="cite_ref-Ó_Corráin_1981_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ó_Corráin_1981-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
