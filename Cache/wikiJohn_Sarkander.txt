<p><b>Jan Sarkander</b> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a> and <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i>Jan Sarkander</i>) (20 December 1576 – 17 March 1620<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) was a <a href="/wiki/Poles" title="Poles">Polish</a>-<a href="/wiki/Czechs" title="Czechs">Czech</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Sarkander was married for a short period of time before he became widowed and pursued a path to the priesthood where he became active in defence of the faith during a period of anti-Catholic sentiment and conflict. He himself was arrested on false accusations as a means of silencing him and he refused to give in to his tormenters who tortured him for around a month before he died.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> beatified Sarkander at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> in 1860 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> canonized him as a saint in 1995 on his visit to the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Sarkander&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jan Sarkander was born on 20 December 1576 in <a href="/wiki/Skocz%C3%B3w" title="Skoczów">Skoczów</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> (now in Poland) into a Silesian household as the son of Georg Mathias Sarkander and Helene Górecka. He had one sister and three other brothers: Nicholas (a priest himself), Paul, and Wenceslas.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His father died in 1589 and so his family moved to <a href="/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADbor" title="Příbor">Příbor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His mother remarried and gave birth to his half-brother Matthew. Sarkander intended to become a priest but dropped the idea and instead married. He and his wife then settled in <a href="/wiki/Brno" title="Brno">Brno</a>. The marriage was short-lived for his wife died one year later. There were no children. He then took up studies for the priesthood, convinced that God was calling him.<sup id="cite_ref-SK_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SK-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sarkander studied at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Olomouc" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Olomouc">Olomouc college</a> from 1597 until 1600 due to the plague which forced him to transfer to the <a href="/wiki/Charles_University" title="Charles University">Charles University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>. He obtained his degree of master of philosophy at Prague in 1603.<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He continued theological studies in <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> from 1604. He later underwent theological studies at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Graz" title="University of Graz">University of Graz</a> and passed his examinations on 21 December 1607. He was made a sub-deacon on 20 December 1608 and elevated into the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> on 16 March 1609.
<sup id="cite_ref-CE_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarkander_torture.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sarkander_torture.png/170px-Sarkander_torture.png" decoding="async" width="170" height="300" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sarkander_torture.png/255px-Sarkander_torture.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sarkander_torture.png/340px-Sarkander_torture.png 2x" data-file-width="442" data-file-height="780" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarkander_torture.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A 1715 etching depicting Sarkander's martyrdom encripted with "A sacred treasure of memory".</div></div></div>
<p>On 22 December 1607, he received the minor orders from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Franz_von_Dietrichstein" title="Franz von Dietrichstein">Franz von Dietrichstein</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Olomouc" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Olomouc">Bishop of Olomouc</a> Jan Křtitel Civalli <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> him to the priesthood on 22 March 1609 in <a href="/wiki/Graz" title="Graz">Graz</a>. Sarkander was assigned to work as a parish priest in <a href="/wiki/Boskowitz" class="mw-redirect" title="Boskowitz">Boskowitz</a> and then sent to <a href="/wiki/Hole%C5%A1ov" title="Holešov">Holešov</a> in 1616.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Baron von Lobkowitz from Moravia supported Sarkander's efforts in the region but the rich anti-Catholic landowner Bitowsky von Bistritz opposed him to the extent that he wanted Sarkander killed. The <a href="/wiki/Thirty_Years_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Thirty Years War">Thirty Years War</a> began in 1618 and it saw a bitter conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Protestants" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestants">Protestants</a> and Catholics and this forced him to flee to Poland on 17 May 1619 for a brief period of time when the Protestants occupied <a href="/wiki/Hole%C5%A1ov" title="Holešov">Holleschau</a>. He made a pilgrimage to the Shrine of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Czestochowa" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of Czestochowa">Our Lady of Czestochowa</a>, and passed a few weeks of retreat with the <a href="/wiki/Minims_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minims (religious order)">Minims</a>, who had a house there. He also spent a few months in Cracow. Then he returned home.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In February 1620 Polish auxiliary troops sent to the emperor by King Sigismund, passed through Moravia and committed many depredations on the lands of the Protestants, but spared Holleschau when John met them with the Blessed sacrament in his hands. The Jesuits helped him to reconcile 200 non-Catholics to the faith but other non-Catholics were severely angered by this.<sup id="cite_ref-SK_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SK-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the ongoing <a href="/wiki/Bohemian_Revolt" title="Bohemian Revolt">Bohemian Revolt</a>—Protestant Moravian Estates (under von Bistritz) accused Sarkander of collaborating with Baron von Lobkowitz to bring the enemy into the territory. He was taken prisoner and brought to Olmütz where he was tortured. Sarkander was interrogated regarding who had called the troops into the country; what dealings had he in Poland, and with whom; and what had Lobkowitz confided to him in confession.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He refused to divulge what was said under the seal of <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a> under which priests are bound. The rack was used on him on 13 February and again on the 17th and 18th for up to three hours. Lit candles were applied to him and feathers soaked in oil, pitch, and sulphur strewn over his body and ignited.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He lingered from the effects for a month and died in prison on March 17, 1620.<sup id="cite_ref-SK_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SK-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Sarkander&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg/170px-Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="152" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg/255px-Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg/340px-Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1908" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarkandrova_kaple_-_epitaf_detail.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relief of the torturing of Jan Sarkander on the <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">torturing rack</a> at Sarkander's gravestone.</div></div></div>
<p>The beatification process opened under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a>, but the process was interrupted and thus remained inactive following this. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> approved the fact that Sarkander was killed <i>in odium fidei</i> ("in hatred of the faith") on 11 September 1859 and beatified him as a result on 6 May 1860.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He is acknowledged as a martyr. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved a miracle due to the intercession of Sarkander on 2 April 1993 and canonized him on the occasion of his visit to the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a> on 21 May 1995.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG/180px-OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG" decoding="async" width="180" height="135" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG/270px-OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG/360px-OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2304" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlomoucCathedralSarkander.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relics of Sarkander in Olomouc</div></div></div>
<p>Jan Sarkander's relics lie in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Wenceslas_Cathedral" title="Saint Wenceslas Cathedral">Saint Wenceslas Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Olomouc" title="Olomouc">Olomouc</a> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A chapel dedicated to him stands at the top of Michael's Hill on the former site of the prison. The original torturing rack and Sarkander's gravestone are preserved here.
</p>
