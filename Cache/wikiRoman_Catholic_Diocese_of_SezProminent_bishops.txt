<p>The <b>Roman Catholic Diocese of Séez</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Dioecesis Sagiensis</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i>Diocèse de Séez</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. Originally established in the 3rd century, the diocese encompasses the <a href="/wiki/Departments_of_France" title="Departments of France">department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Orne" title="Orne">Orne</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Regions_of_France" title="Regions of France">region</a> of <a href="/wiki/Normandy_(administrative_region)" title="Normandy (administrative region)">Normandy</a>. The episcopal see is the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9es_Cathedral" title="Sées Cathedral">cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9es" title="Sées">Sées</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and the diocese is a <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_diocese" title="Suffragan diocese">suffragan</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rouen" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rouen">Archdiocese of Rouen</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_S%C3%A9ez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint <a href="/wiki/Ebrulf" title="Ebrulf">Ebrulf</a>, a native of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Bayeux">Diocese of Bayeux</a>, founded, after 560, several monasteries in the Diocese of Séez; one of them became the important Abbey of Saint-Martin-de-Séez, which, owing to the influence of Richelieu, its administrator-general, was reformed in 1636 by the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines_of_Saint-Maur" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictines of Saint-Maur">Benedictines of Saint-Maur</a>. Rotrou II, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Perche" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Perche">Count of Perche</a>, in fulfillment of a vow, established in 1122, at <a href="/wiki/Soligny-la-Trappe" title="Soligny-la-Trappe">Soligny</a>, the <a href="/wiki/La_Trappe_Abbey" title="La Trappe Abbey">Abbey of La Trappe</a>, in favour of which <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bulls</a> were issued by popes <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Eugene III</a> (1147), <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Alexander III</a> (1173) and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Innocent III</a> (1203), and which was reformed in 1662 by <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> <a href="/wiki/Armand_Jean_le_Bouthillier_de_Ranc%C3%A9" title="Armand Jean le Bouthillier de Rancé">Armand Jean le Bouthillier de Rancé</a>.
</p><p>During the French Revolution the <a href="/wiki/Trappists" title="Trappists">Trappists</a> went with Dom Augustin de Lestranges, 26 April 1791, into Switzerland, where they founded the convent of La Val Saint, but returned to Soligny soon after the accession of Louis XVII. Among the abbots of the Trappist monastery at Soligny were: Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Jean_du_Bellay" title="Jean du Bellay">Jean du Bellay</a>, who held a number of bishoprics and resigned his abbatial dignity in 1538; the historian <a href="/wiki/Dom_Gervaise" class="mw-redirect" title="Dom Gervaise">Dom Gervaise</a>, superior of the abbey from 1696–8.
</p><p>On the occasion of the <a href="/wiki/St_Bartholomew%27s_Day_massacre" class="mw-redirect" title="St Bartholomew&#39;s Day massacre">St Bartholomew's Day massacre</a> in 1572 Matignon, leader of the Catholics, succeeded in saving the lives of the Protestants at Alençon. The cathedral of Séez dates from the twelfth century; that of Alençon was begun in the fourteenth.
</p><p>The diocese was re-established by the <a href="/wiki/Concordat_of_1802" class="mw-redirect" title="Concordat of 1802">Concordat of 1802</a>, which, by adding to it some parishes of the Dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Bayeux">Bayeux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lisieux" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Lisieux">Lisieux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Le_Mans" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Le Mans">Le Mans</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Chartres" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Chartres">Chartres</a>, and by cutting off some districts formerly included in it, made it exactly coextensive with the department. It is <a href="/wiki/Suffragan" class="mw-redirect" title="Suffragan">suffragan</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rouen" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rouen">Archdiocese of Rouen</a> in Normandy.
</p><p>In 1884 Monseigneur Buguet, curé of Montligeon chapel, founded an expiatory society for the abandoned souls in Purgatory, since erected by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> into a Prima Primaria archconfraternity, which publishes six bulletins in different languages and has members in every part of the world. Notre Dame de la Chapelle Montligeon is also a place of pilgrimage. The Grande Trappe of Soligny still exists in the Diocese of Séez, which before the application of the law of 1901 against religious congregations had different teaching congregations of brothers, in addition to the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Redemptorists</a>. Among the congregations of nuns originating in the diocese may be mentioned: the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sisters_of_Providence_(S%C3%A9ez,_France)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sisters of Providence (Séez, France) (page does not exist)">Sisters of Providence</a>, a teaching and nursing institute founded in 1683 with mother-house at Séez; the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sisters_of_Christian_Education&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sisters of Christian Education (page does not exist)">Sisters of Christian Education</a>, established in 1817 by Abbé Lafosse, mother-house at Argentan, and a branch of the order at Farnborough in England; the Sisters of Mercy, founded in 1818 by Abbé Bazin to nurse the sick in their own homes.
</p>

<p>Some saints were especially venerated in this diocese. These included <a href="/wiki/Ravennus_and_Rasyphus" class="mw-redirect" title="Ravennus and Rasyphus">Ravennus and Rasyphus</a>, martyred in the diocese about the beginning of the third century. Saint Céronne (d. about 490) founded two monasteries of nuns near Mortagne; and Saint Cenerius, or Céneri (d. about 669), born at <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>, was the founder of the monastery of Saint Cenerius. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Opportuna" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Opportuna">Saint Opportuna</a>, sister of Saint Chrodegang,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and her aunt, Saint Lanthilda, were abbesses of the two monasteries of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Almen%C3%A8ches&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Almenèches (page does not exist)">Almenèches</a> (end of the seventh or beginning of the eighth century). Saint Evremond (d. about 720) was the founder of the monasteries of Fontenay les Louvets and Montmevrey. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Osmund" title="Saint Osmund">Saint Osmund</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salisbury" title="Bishop of Salisbury">Bishop of Salisbury</a> (d. 1099), as Comte de Séez, followed <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a> into <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.
</p><p>The chief <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimages" class="mw-redirect" title="Pilgrimages">pilgrimages</a> in the diocese were <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_de_Champs&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame de Champs (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame de Champs</a> at Séez, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_du_Vallet&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame du Vallet (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame du Vallet</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_du_Repos&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame du Repos (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame du Repos</a>, near Almenèches, three very ancient shrines; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_de_Lignerolles&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame de Lignerolles (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame de Lignerolles</a>, a pilgrimage of the seventh century; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_de_Recouvrance&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame de Recouvrance (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame de Recouvrance</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Les_Tourailles" title="Les Tourailles">Les Tourailles</a>, dating beyond 900; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_de_Longny&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame de Longny (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame de Longny</a>, established in the sixteenth century; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_du_Lignon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame du Lignon (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame du Lignon</a>, a pilgrimage of the seventeenth century.
</p>
