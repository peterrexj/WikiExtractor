<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the English Catholic martyr. <p><b>John Houghton</b> (c. 1486 – 4 May 1535) was <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">Carthusian order</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> to die as a result of the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Supremacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Supremacy">Act of Supremacy</a> by King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a>. He was also the first of the Carthusians to die as a martyr. As one of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian_Martyrs_of_London" title="Carthusian Martyrs of London">Carthusian Martyrs of London</a> he is among the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Houghton_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born around 1487, Houghton was (according to one of his fellow <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusians</a>) educated at Cambridge, but cannot be identified among surviving records.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Similarly, no certain records can be found of his ordination.
</p><p>It is said that he escaped an arranged marriage as soon as he completed his education and took refuge with a devout priest.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_Life">Monastic Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Houghton_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monastic Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He joined the <a href="/wiki/London_Charterhouse" title="London Charterhouse">London Charterhouse</a> in 1516, progressed to be <a href="/wiki/Sacristan" title="Sacristan">sacristan</a> in 1523, and <a href="/wiki/Treasurer" title="Treasurer">procurator</a> in 1528.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawrence_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawrence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1531, he became <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Beauvale_Priory" title="Beauvale Priory">Beavale</a> in Nottinghamshire. However, in November of that year, he was elected prior of the London house, to which he returned.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In addition, the following spring he was named Provincial Visitor, at the head of the English Carthusians.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawrence_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawrence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 1534, two royal agents visited the Charterhouse. Houghton advised them that "it pertained not to his vocation and calling nor to that of his subjects to meddle in or discuss the king's business, neither could they or ought they to do so, and that it did not concern him who the king wished to divorce or marry, so long as he was not asked for any opinion."<sup id="cite_ref-Gasquet_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gasquet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He asked that he and his community be exempted from the <a href="/wiki/Act_Respecting_the_Oath_to_the_Succession" title="Act Respecting the Oath to the Succession">oath</a> required under the new <a href="/wiki/First_Succession_Act" title="First Succession Act">Act of Succession</a>, which resulted in both him and his procurator, <a href="/wiki/Humphrey_Middlemore" title="Humphrey Middlemore">Humphrey Middlemore</a>, being arrested and taken to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>. However, by the end of May, they had been persuaded that the oath was consistent with their Catholicism, with the clause "as far as the law of Christ allows" and they returned to the Charterhouse, where (in the presence of a large armed force) the whole community made the required professions.<sup id="cite_ref-Gasquet_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gasquet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_Execution">Arrest and Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Houghton_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Arrest and Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>However, in 1535, the community was called upon to make the new <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">oath</a> as prescribed by the 1534 <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Supremacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Supremacy">Act of Supremacy</a>, which recognised Henry as the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Head_of_the_Church_of_England" title="Supreme Head of the Church of England">Supreme Head of the Church of England</a>. Again, Houghton, this time accompanied by the heads of the other two English Carthusian houses (<a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a>, prior of Beauvale, and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a>, prior of <a href="/wiki/Axholme_Charterhouse" title="Axholme Charterhouse">Axholme</a>), pleaded for an exemption, but this time they were summarily arrested. They were called before a special commission in April 1535, and sentenced to death, along with <a href="/wiki/Richard_Reynolds_(martyr)" title="Richard Reynolds (martyr)">Richard Reynolds</a>, a monk from <a href="/wiki/Syon_Abbey" title="Syon Abbey">Syon Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Houghton, along with the other two Carthusians, Reynolds and <a href="/wiki/John_Haile" title="John Haile">John Haile</a> of Isleworth, was <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a> on 4 May 1535.<sup id="cite_ref-Cranmer_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cranmer-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The three priors were taken to Tyburn in their <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habits</a> and were not previously <a href="/wiki/Defrocking" title="Defrocking">laicised</a> from the priesthood and religious state as was the custom of the day. From his prison cell in the Tower, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a> saw the three Carthusian priors being dragged to Tyburn on <a href="/wiki/Hurdle" title="Hurdle">hurdles</a> and exclaimed to his daughter: "Look, Meg! These blessed Fathers be now as cheerfully going to their deaths as bridegrooms to their marriage!" John Houghton was the first to be executed. After he was hanged, he was taken down alive, and the process of quartering him began.
</p><p>Catholic tradition relates that when Houghton was about to be quartered, as the executioner tore open his chest to remove his heart, he prayed, "O Jesus, what wouldst thou do with my heart?". His companions were butchered next. After his death, his body was chopped to pieces and hung in different parts of London. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Houghton_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was beatified on 29 December 1886 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> and canonized on 25 October 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>.
</p><p>There is a mosaic of Houghton in the Holy Name of Jesus Roman Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Oxford_Road,_Manchester" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford Road, Manchester">Oxford Road</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chorlton-on-Medlock" title="Chorlton-on-Medlock">Chorlton-on-Medlock</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manchester" title="Manchester">Manchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A painting of the Carthusian <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> by the noted painter <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Zurbar%C3%A1n" class="mw-redirect" title="Francisco Zurbarán">Francisco Zurbarán</a>, depicts him with his heart in his hand and a noose around his neck. 
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Chapter_house" title="Chapter house">Chapter house</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Hugh%27s_Charterhouse,_Parkminster" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Hugh&#39;s Charterhouse, Parkminster">St. Hugh's Charterhouse</a> in England, there is a painting depicting the martyrdom of the three priors. 
</p><p>There are stained glass windows of Saint John Houghton in the following churches:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St_Dominic%27s_Priory_Church" title="St Dominic&#39;s Priory Church">St. Dominic's Priory Church</a> in London<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_and_the_English_Martyrs_Church" title="Our Lady and the English Martyrs Church">Our Lady and the English Martyrs</a> in Cambridge<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Belmont_Abbey,_Herefordshire" title="Belmont Abbey, Herefordshire">Belmont Abbey</a> outside Hereford<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Mark%27s_Church,_Mansfield" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mark&#39;s Church, Mansfield">St. Mark's Church</a> in Mansfield<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Shrewsbury_Cathedral" title="Shrewsbury Cathedral">Shrewsbury Cathedral</a>, he is next to Thomas More in the fifth window.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p><a href="/wiki/St_John_Houghton_Catholic_Voluntary_Academy" title="St John Houghton Catholic Voluntary Academy">St John Houghton Catholic Voluntary Academy</a> is named in his honor.
</p>
