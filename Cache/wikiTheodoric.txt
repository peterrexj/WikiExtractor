<p><b>Theodoric</b>  is a <a href="/wiki/Germanic_given_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Germanic given name">Germanic given name</a>. First attested as a <a href="/wiki/Gothic_name" title="Gothic name">Gothic name</a> in the 5th century, it became widespread in the Germanic-speaking world, not least due to its most famous bearer, <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great" title="Theodoric the Great">Theodoric the Great</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Ostrogoths" title="Ostrogoths">Ostrogoths</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Overview">Overview</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Overview">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name was <a href="/wiki/Latinisation_of_names" title="Latinisation of names">Latinized</a> as <i>Theodoricus</i> or <i>Theodericus</i>, originally from a <a href="/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language" title="Proto-Germanic language">Common Germanic</a> form <i>*<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/%C3%9Eeudar%C4%ABks" class="extiw" title="wikt:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/Þeudarīks">Þeudarīks</a></i> ("people-ruler") from *<i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/%C3%BEeud%C5%8D" class="extiw" title="wikt:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/þeudō">þeudō</a></i> ("people") and *<i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/r%C4%ABks" class="extiw" title="wikt:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/rīks">rīks</a></i>, which would have resulted in a <a href="/wiki/Gothic_language" title="Gothic language">Gothic</a> *𐌸𐌹𐌿𐌳𐌰𐍂𐌴𐌹𐌺𐍃 (*þiudareiks).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Anglicized spellings of the name during Late Antiquity and the Early Middle Ages include <i>Theodoric</i>, <i>Theoderic</i>, <i>Theudoric</i>, <i>Theuderic</i>.
<a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> Latinized the name as   <i><a href="/wiki/Theodore_(given_name)" title="Theodore (given name)">Theodorus</a></i>, in origin the unrelated Greek name <a href="/wiki/Theodore_(given_name)" title="Theodore (given name)">Theodore</a> (Θεόδωρος, meaning "God's gift").
</p><p>As the name survived throughout the Middle Ages, it transformed into a multitude of forms in the languages of Western Europe. 
These include the  <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">High German</a>  form  <i><a href="/wiki/Dietrich_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Dietrich (disambiguation)">Dietrich</a></i>, abbreviated <i><a href="/wiki/Dieter_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Dieter (disambiguation)">Dieter</a></i>, the  Low German and Dutch form <i><a href="/wiki/Diederik" title="Diederik">Diederik</a></i>, or <i>Dierik</i>, abbreviated <i><a href="/wiki/Dirck" title="Dirck">Dirck</a></i>,  <i><a href="/wiki/Dirk_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Dirk (disambiguation)">Dirk</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Dik_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Dik (disambiguation)">Dik</a></i> or <i>Diede</i>,  the <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_language" title="Norwegian language">Norwegian</a>  <i>Tjodrik</i>, <i>Diderik</i> and <i>Didrik</i>. Of the Romance languages, French has <i><a href="/wiki/Thierry_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thierry (disambiguation)">Thierry</a></i> and <a href="/wiki/Italian_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian (language)">Italian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Portuguese (language)">Portuguese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a> has <i>Teodorico</i>.
</p><p>The English forms <i><a href="/wiki/Derek" title="Derek">Derek</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Derrick_(name)" title="Derrick (name)">Derrick</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Terry" title="Terry">Terry</a></i> have been re-introduced from the continent, from Low German, Dutch and French sources. The derived Welsh form is <i><a href="/wiki/Tewdrig" title="Tewdrig">Tewdrig</a></i>, however there also exists the related Welsh name <i><a href="/wiki/Tudur_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tudur (disambiguation)">Tudur</a></i> (from <a href="/wiki/Proto-Celtic" class="mw-redirect" title="Proto-Celtic">Proto-Celtic</a> <i>*Toutorīxs</i>, exactly cognate with Proto-Germanic <i>*Þeudarīks</i>) which is the origin of the name of the English <a href="/wiki/Tudor_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Tudor dynasty">Tudor dynasty</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Late_antiquity_to_early_Middle_Ages">Late antiquity to early Middle Ages</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Late antiquity to early Middle Ages">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><span class="anchor" id="Apollo_Teutorix"></span>
The earliest record of the name is in a Roman-era (3rd century) inscription, discovered in 1784 in <a href="/wiki/Wiesbaden" title="Wiesbaden">Wiesbaden</a> (at the time known as <i>Aquae Mattiacorum</i> in <a href="/wiki/Germania_Superior" title="Germania Superior">Germania Superior</a>), edited in <i>Codex inscriptionum romanarum Danubii et Rheni</i> as no. 684: <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">IN. H. D. D. APOLLINI. TOVTIORIGI</i></span>, interpreted as <i>In honorem domus divinae, Apolloni toutiorigi</i>.
This has given rise to a supposed "Apollo Teutorix" in 19th-century literature.
Rhys (1892) opined that "the interest attached to the word <i>Toutiorix</i> is out of all proportion to its single occurrence".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
The existence of a genuinely <a href="/wiki/Celtic_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic name">Celtic name</a> <i>Teutorix</i> or <i>Tout(i)orix</i> is uncertain, though Welsh <i><a href="/wiki/Tudur_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tudur (disambiguation)">Tudur</a></i>, Old Welsh <i>Tutir</i> presupposes a precise cognate of <i>Toutorix</i> at least in <a href="/wiki/Common_Brittonic" title="Common Brittonic">ancient British Celtic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Rhys surmises that the "historical Teuton" (viz. <a href="/wiki/Theoderic_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoderic the Great">Theoderic the Great</a>) bore a name of the <a href="/wiki/Gaulish_Apollo" class="mw-redirect" title="Gaulish Apollo">Gaulish Apollo</a> as adopted into early <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Germanic_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Germanic religion">Germanic religion</a>.
</p><p>The first known bearer of the name  was <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_I" title="Theodoric I">Theodoric I</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Alaric_I" title="Alaric I">Alaric I</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Visigoths</a> (d. 451). The <a href="/wiki/Gothic_language" title="Gothic language">Gothic</a> form of the name would have been <i>Þiudareiks</i>, which was Latinized as <i>Theodericus</i>. The notability of the name is due to <a href="/wiki/Theoderic_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoderic the Great">Theoderic the Great</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Theodemir_(Ostrogothic_king)" title="Theodemir (Ostrogothic king)">Theodemir</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Ostrogoths" title="Ostrogoths">Ostrogoths</a> (454&#8211;526), who became a legendary figure of the <a href="/wiki/Germanic_Heroic_Age" title="Germanic Heroic Age">Germanic Heroic Age</a> as <a href="/wiki/Dietrich_von_Bern" title="Dietrich von Bern">Dietrich von Bern</a>.
</p><p>After the end of <a href="/wiki/Late_Antiquity" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Antiquity">Late Antiquity</a>, during the 6th to 8th century there were also several kings of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> called Theodoric (or Theuderic).
Finally, there was an early <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> king of <a href="/wiki/Bernicia" title="Bernicia">Bernicia</a>  called <a href="/wiki/Theodric_of_Bernicia" title="Theodric of Bernicia">Theodric</a> (also spelled <i>Deoric</i>, Old English <i><span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Þēodrīc</i></span></i>).
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Theodoric_I" title="Theodoric I">Theodoric I</a> (died 451), king of the Visigoths</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theodoric_II" title="Theodoric II">Theodoric II</a> (died 466), king of the Visigoths</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theoderic_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoderic the Great">Theoderic the Great</a> (454–526), ruler of the Ostrogoths, Italy, and the Visigoths</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theodoric_Strabo" title="Theodoric Strabo">Theodoric Strabo</a> (died 481)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theuderic_I" title="Theuderic I">Theuderic I</a> (died ca. 534), Frankish king</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theuderic_II" title="Theuderic II">Theuderic II</a> (587–613), Frankish king</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theuderic III</a> (died 691), king of the Franks</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theuderic_IV" title="Theuderic IV">Theuderic IV</a> (died 737), king of the Franks</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theodric_of_Bernicia" title="Theodric of Bernicia">Theodric of Bernicia</a>, 6th century Anglo-Saxon king</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Theodoric_of_Mont_d%27Hor" title="Theodoric of Mont d&#39;Hor">Theodoric of Mont d'Hor</a> died 533), disciple of Saint Remigius who became abbot of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Thierry_Abbey" title="Saint-Thierry Abbey">Saint-Thierry Abbey</a> near Reims, France</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Tewdrig" title="Tewdrig">Saint Tewdrig</a> (alternatively Tewdric or Theodoric) (c. 580 – c. 630), Welsh king of Gwent and Glywysing, who was martyred fighting the Saxons</li>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="High_and_late_Middle_Ages">High and late Middle Ages</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: High and late Middle Ages">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While the Anglo-Saxon name <i>Theodric</i> (<i>Deoric</i>, old form: th = d) became extinct in the Middle English period, it was adopted in Welsh as <i><a href="/wiki/Tewdrig" title="Tewdrig">Tewdrig</a></i>.
The name remains popular in medieval German as <i>Dietrich</i>, and is adopted into French as <i>Thierry</i>. It is rendered in <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Latin" title="Medieval Latin">Medieval Latin</a> as <i>Theodoricus</i> or as <i>Theodericus</i>. 
The <a href="/wiki/Middle_High_German" title="Middle High German">Middle High German</a> legend of <i><a href="/wiki/Dietrich_von_Bern" title="Dietrich von Bern">Dietrich von Bern</a></i> is based on the historical Theodoric, king of the Ostrogoths. The German <i>Dietrich von Bern</i> is reflected as <i>Þiðrekr af Bern</i> in the Old Norse <a href="/wiki/Thidrekssaga" class="mw-redirect" title="Thidrekssaga">Thidrekssaga</a>. The medieval German legend gives rise to the <i>Dietrich</i> of the <a href="/wiki/German_Renaissance" title="German Renaissance">Renaissance era</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Heldenb%C3%BCcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Heldenbücher">Heldenbücher</a></i>.
The Old Norse form of the name was  Þjóðríkr (spelled þiaurikʀ on the 9th-century <a href="/wiki/R%C3%B6k_runestone" title="Rök runestone">Rök runestone</a>). This became <i>Tjodrik</i> in <a href="/wiki/Middle_Norwegian" title="Middle Norwegian">Middle Norwegian</a>.
</p><p>The Dutch form <i><a href="/wiki/Derek" title="Derek">Derek</a></i> was used in England from the 15th century. Similarly, the Scandinavian <i>Tjodrik</i> is attested for the 12th century, but it is replaced by the Low German forms <i>Ditrik, Dirk</i> in the late medieval period. The spread of the Low German form to <a href="/wiki/Middle_Norwegian" title="Middle Norwegian">Middle Norwegian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Middle_Danish" class="mw-redirect" title="Middle Danish">Middle Danish</a> and <a href="/wiki/Late_Middle_English" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Middle English">late Middle English</a>  or <a href="/wiki/Early_Modern_English" title="Early Modern English">Early Modern English</a> are part of a larger linguistic trend due to the influence of the <a href="/wiki/Hanseatic_League" title="Hanseatic League">Hanseatic League</a> during this period.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_era">Modern era</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoric&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Modern era">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The German form <i><a href="/wiki/Dietrich_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Dietrich (disambiguation)">Dietrich</a></i> was abbreviated to <i><a href="/wiki/Dieter_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Dieter (disambiguation)">Dieter</a></i>. The Low German and Dutch  languages abbreviated <i><a href="/wiki/Diederik" title="Diederik">Diederik</a></i> as <i><a href="/wiki/Dirk" title="Dirk">Dirk</a></i> or  <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Diede&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Diede (page does not exist)">Diede</a></i>. 
French retains <i><a href="/wiki/Thierry_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thierry (disambiguation)">Thierry</a></i>.
The  Scandinavian languages have  borrowed <i>Dirk</i> and <a href="/wiki/Diderik" title="Diderik">Diderik</a>, replacing the native <i>Tjodrik</i>, while English borrowed <i><a href="/wiki/Derek" title="Derek">Derek</a></i> from Dutch and <i><a href="/wiki/Terry" title="Terry">Terry</a></i> from French.
</p>
