<p><b>Oda</b> (or <b>Odo</b>;<sup id="cite_ref-ODS393_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS393-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> died 958), called <b>the Good</b> or <b>the Severe</b>, was a 10th-century <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> in England. The son of a Danish invader, Oda became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ramsbury_(ancient)" title="Bishop of Ramsbury (ancient)">Bishop of Ramsbury</a> before 928. A number of stories were told about his actions both prior to becoming and while a bishop, but few of these incidents are recorded in contemporary accounts. After being named to Canterbury in 941, Oda was instrumental in crafting royal legislation as well as involved in providing rules for his clergy. Oda was also involved in the efforts to reform religious life in England. He died in 958 and legendary tales afterwards were ascribed to him. Later he came to be regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> was written in the late 11th or early 12th century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career">Early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oda_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oda's parents were <a href="/wiki/Danish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Danish people">Danish</a>, and he may have been born in <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks222_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks222-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His father was said to have been a Dane who came to England in 865, together with the <a href="/wiki/Great_Heathen_Army" title="Great Heathen Army">Viking army</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ubba" title="Ubba">Ubba</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ivar_the_Boneless" title="Ivar the Boneless">Ivar</a>, and presumably settled in East Anglia. Oda's nephew <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Worcester" title="Oswald of Worcester">Oswald of Worcester</a> later became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a>. It is possible that Oswald's relatives <a href="/wiki/Oscytel" title="Oscytel">Oscytel</a>, afterwards Archbishop of York, and Thurcytel, an abbot, were also relatives of Oda, but this is not known for sure.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Byrhtferth" title="Byrhtferth">Byrhtferth of Ramsey</a>'s <i>Life of Saint Oswald</i>, Oda is said to have joined the household of a pious nobleman called Æthelhelm, whom he accompanied to Rome on pilgrimage. While on pilgrimage, Oda healed the nobleman's illness.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Other stories, such as those by the 12th-century writer <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, describe Oda as fighting under <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Elder" title="Edward the Elder">Edward the Elder</a> and then becoming a priest, but these statements are unlikely. Other statements in the <i>Life</i> have Oda being named "Bishop of Wilton" by the king, who is stated to have been Æthelhelm's brother.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The chronicler may be referring, slightly inaccurately, to <a href="/wiki/Aethelhelm" class="mw-redirect" title="Aethelhelm">Aethelhelm</a> cousin of the king. This benefactor has also been associated with bishop <a href="/wiki/Athelm" title="Athelm">Athelm</a>, who reportedly sponsored Oda in his ecclesiastical career.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Some sources state that Oda became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Fleury-sur-Loire" title="Fleury-sur-Loire">Fleury-sur-Loire</a> in France.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ASE448_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE448-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Ramsbury">Bishop of Ramsbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oda_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop of Ramsbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oda was consecrated Bishop of Ramsbury sometime between 909 and 927,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook220_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook220-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> not to Wilton as stated by both William of Malmesbury and the <i>Life</i>. The appointment was most likely made by King <a href="/wiki/Athelstan_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Athelstan of England">Æthelstan</a>, and the first securely attested mention in documents of the new bishop occurs in 928, when he is a witness to royal charters as bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to the late tenth-century chronicler, <a href="/wiki/Richerus" title="Richerus">Richer of Rheims</a>, in 936 Æthelstan sent Oda to France to arrange the return to the throne of France of King <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV_of_France" title="Louis IV of France">Louis IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE347_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE347-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Foot169_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foot169-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> Louis was Æthelstan's nephew<sup id="cite_ref-BASE159_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE159-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and had been in exile in England for a number of years.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE347_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE347-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> However, this story is not related in any contemporary records.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Oda was said to have accompanied King Æthelstan at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Brunanburh" title="Battle of Brunanburh">Battle of Brunanburh</a> in 937.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DictSaint_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictSaint-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It was at this battle that Oda is said to have miraculously provided a sword to the king when the king's own sword slipped out of its scabbard. A Ramsey chronicle records that in the 1170s, the sword was still preserved in the royal treasury, although the chronicler carefully states the story "as is said" rather than as fact.<sup id="cite_ref-Clanchy40_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clanchy40-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> There are no contemporary records of Oda's appearance at the battle.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 940, Oda arranged a truce between <a href="/wiki/Olaf_III_Guthfrithson" class="mw-redirect" title="Olaf III Guthfrithson">Olaf III Guthfrithson</a>, king of Dublin and York, and <a href="/wiki/Edmund_I" title="Edmund I">Edmund I</a>, king of England.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oda_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Canterbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oda was appointed Archbishop of Canterbury following <a href="/wiki/Wulfhelm" title="Wulfhelm">Wulfhelm</a>'s death on 12 February 941.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> It is not known whether he went to Rome to receive his <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> or when he received it, but it was before he issued his <i>Constitutions</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> During his time as archbishop, he helped King Edmund with the new royal law-code,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which had a number of laws concerned with ecclesiastical affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The archbishop was present, along with Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Wulfstan_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfstan I">Wulfstan</a> of York, at council that proclaimed the first of these law codes and which was held by Edmund<sup id="cite_ref-Wormald310_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wormald310-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> at London, over Easter around 945 or 946.<sup id="cite_ref-Wormald440_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wormald440-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Oda also settled a dispute over the <a href="/wiki/Five_Burghs" class="mw-redirect" title="Five Burghs">Five Boroughs</a> with Wulfstan.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oda also issued <i>Constitutions</i>, or rules, for his clergy. His <i>Constitutions of Oda</i> are the first surviving constitutions of a 10th-century English ecclesiastical reformer.<sup id="cite_ref-Stafford9_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stafford9-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Oda reworked some statutes from 786 to form his updated code, and one item that was dropped were any clauses dealing with paganism.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Other items covered were relations between laymen and the clergy, the duties of bishops, the need for the laity to make canonical marriages, how to observe fasts, and the need for tithes to be given by the laity.<sup id="cite_ref-Darlington386_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Darlington386-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The work is extant in just one surviving manuscript, <a href="/wiki/British_Library" title="British Library">British Library</a> <a href="/wiki/Cotton_library" title="Cotton library">Cotton MS</a> Vespasian A XIV, folios 175v to 177v. This is an 11th-century copy done for <a href="/wiki/Wulfstan_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfstan II">Wulfstan II</a>, Archbishop of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Schoebe_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Schoebe-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the death of King <a href="/wiki/Eadred_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Eadred of England">Eadred of England</a> in 955, Oda was one of the recipients of a bequest from the king, in his case a large amount of gold.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher24_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher24-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He was probably behind the reestablishment of a bishopric at <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Elmham" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Elmham">Elmham</a>, as the line of bishops in that see starts with <a href="/wiki/Eadwulf_of_Elmham" title="Eadwulf of Elmham">Eadwulf of Elmham</a> in 956.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE437_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE437-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Oda crowned King <a href="/wiki/Eadwig_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Eadwig of England">Eadwig</a> in 956, but in late 957 the archbishop joined Eadwig's rival and brother <a href="/wiki/Edgar_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar of England">Edgar</a> who had been proclaimed king of the <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercians</a> in 957, while Eadwig continued to rule <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Stafford48_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stafford48-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The exact cause of the rupture between the two brothers that led to the division of the previously united kingdom is unknown, but may have resulted from Eadwig's efforts to promote close kinsmen and his wife. The division was peaceful, and Eadwig continued to call himself "King of the English" in contrast to Edgar's title of "King of the Mercians".<sup id="cite_ref-BASE151_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE151-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> In early 958 Oda annulled the marriage of Eadwig and his wife <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfgifu,_wife_of_Eadwig" class="mw-redirect" title="Ælfgifu, wife of Eadwig">Ælfgifu</a>, who were too closely related.<sup id="cite_ref-Stafford48_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stafford48-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> This act was likely a political move connected to the division between Eadwig and Edgar, as it is unlikely that the close kinship between Eadwig and Ælfgifu had not been known before their marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE151_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE151-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oda was a supporter of <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>'s monastic reforms,<sup id="cite_ref-Darlington387_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Darlington387-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> and was a reforming agent in the church along with <a href="/wiki/Koenwald" title="Koenwald">Cenwald</a> the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alphege_the_Bald" class="mw-redirect" title="Alphege the Bald">Ælfheah</a> the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>. He also built extensively, and re-roofed <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral#Later_Saxon_and_Viking_periods" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury Cathedral</a> after raising the walls higher.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 948, Oda took Saint <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>'s relics from Ripon.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair314_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair314-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Frithegod" title="Frithegod">Frithegod</a>'s verse <i>Life of Wilfrid</i> has a preface that was written by Oda, in which the archbishop claimed that he rescued the relics from Ripon, which he described as "decayed" and "thorn-covered".<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks53_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks53-31">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> He also acquired the relics of <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">St Ouen</a>, and Frithegod also wrote, at Oda's behest, a verse life of that saint, which has been lost.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was also an active in reorganizing the diocesan structure of his province, as the sees of Elmham and Lindsey were reformed during his archbishopric.<sup id="cite_ref-Darlington386_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Darlington386-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The archbishop died on 2 June 958<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and is regarded as a saint, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 4 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Other dates were also commemorated, including 2 June or 29 May. After his death, legendary tales ascribed miracles to him, including one where the Eucharist dripped with blood. Another was the miraculous repair of a sword.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS393_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS393-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There is no contemporary evidence for veneration being made to Oda, with the first indication of cult coming in the hagiography written by Byrhtferth about Oswald, but no hagiography specifically about Oda was written until <a href="/wiki/Eadmer" title="Eadmer">Eadmer</a> wrote the <i>Vita sancti Odonis</i> sometime between 1093 and 1125.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Oda was known by contemporaries as "The Good"<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and also became known as <i>Severus</i> "The Severe".<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
