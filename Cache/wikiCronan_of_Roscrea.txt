<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Crónán</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Ely O'Carroll, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="dday deathdate">640-04-28</span>)</span>28 April 640<br /><a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">Orthodoxy</a><br />

<p>Saint <b>Crónán</b> (died 640) was the abbot-bishop and patron of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Roscrea" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Roscrea">diocese of Roscrea</a><sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (a <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> later incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Killaloe" title="Diocese of Killaloe">diocese of Killaloe</a>), Ireland. He is not to be confused with his contemporary <a href="/wiki/Mo_Chua_of_Balla" title="Mo Chua of Balla">Saint Crónán Mochua</a> (died 637).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cr%C3%B3n%C3%A1n_of_Roscrea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Crónán was born in the territory of <a href="/wiki/Ely_O%27Carroll" class="mw-redirect" title="Ely O&#39;Carroll">Ely O'Carroll</a>, Ireland.  His father’s name was Odhran, while his mother came from west Clare.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After spending his youth in <a href="/wiki/Connacht" title="Connacht">Connacht</a>, he returned to his native district about the year 610 and founded Roscrea Abbey, where he established a school.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Previously he settled at a place known as Sean Ros or Loch Cré, which was a wooded morass far from the haunts of men; in fact, it was utterly wild, so much so, that pilgrims would get lost, so St. Crónán abandoned it and moved to the wood of Cré, that is <a href="/wiki/Roscrea" title="Roscrea">Ros Cré</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/William_Reeves_(bishop)" title="William Reeves (bishop)">William Reeves</a> notes that the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Tigernach" title="Annals of Tigernach">Annals of Tigernach</a></i> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a></i> both describe Crónán as "bishop of <a href="/wiki/Nendrum" class="mw-redirect" title="Nendrum">Nendrum</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cr%C3%B3n%C3%A1n_of_Roscrea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg/220px-BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="290" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg/330px-BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg/440px-BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="1979" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:BookDimmaJohnSymbol.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Eagle symbol of St John the Evangelist from the Book of Dimma folio 104v. (Dublin, Trinity College, MS.A.IV.23).</div></div></div>
<p>Like those of so many other Irish saints the Acts of St. Crónán abound in miracles. The most surprising, perhaps, is the legend as to the transcribing of the Four Gospels by one of his monks, named Dimma, which is associated with the manuscript now known as the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Dimma" title="Book of Dimma">Book of Dimma</a>. It appears that Dimma could only undertake one day's task, from sunrise to sunset. Crónán, however, bade him write, and then Dimma set to work, never ceasing until he had finished the Four Gospels, the sun continuing to shine for the space of forty days and forty nights - the scribe himself being unconscious that the work occupied more than one day. The scribe, Dimma MacNathi, signs his name at the conclusion of each of the Gospels, and he has been identified with Dimma, subsequently Bishop of Connor, who is mentioned with Crónán in the letter of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_IV" title="Pope John IV">Pope John IV</a> in 640, in regard to <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a> in Ireland, but this identification cannot be sustained.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The case containing the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Dimma" title="Book of Dimma">Book of Dimma</a> was richly gilt by order of O'Carroll, Lord of Ely, in the twelfth century. Notwithstanding the conflicting statements arising from the number of contemporary Irish saints bearing the name Crónán, it is more than probable that St. Crónán of Roscrea, as <i><a href="/wiki/Les_Petits_Bollandistes" title="Les Petits Bollandistes">les Petits Bollandistes</a></i> say, lived as late as the year 640, and his death occurred on 28 April of that year.
</p><p>Crónán is the patron of Roscrea, Tipperary; his feast day is 28 April. <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
