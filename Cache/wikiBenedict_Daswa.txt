<p><b>Benedict Daswa</b> (born <b>Tshimangadzo Samuel Daswa</b>; 16 June 1946 – 2 February 1990), was a <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South African</a> school teacher and principal. He was given the name of "Samuel" by his parents when he started to attend school and assumed the name "Benedict" upon his conversion to <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>. A local mob murdered him after he refused to pay support for anti–Christian superstition.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>His martyrdom was confirmed by the Catholic Church in 2015, paving the way for his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>. He was beatified in Limpopo on 13 September 2015. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> – on behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> – presided over the beatification Mass.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Daswa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benedict Daswa was born as Tshimangadzo Samuel Daswa on 16 June 1946 in <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South Africa</a> and was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Lemba_people" title="Lemba people">Lemba people</a>. He was the first child born to Tshililo Petrus Daswa (Bakali) and Thidziambi Ida Daswa (Gundula).<sup id="cite_ref-auto_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This tribe followed <a href="/wiki/Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish">Jewish</a> rituals and laws. Daswa had three younger brothers and one sister: Thanyani Mackson, Muvhulawa Calson, Thinavhuyo Mavis and Humbulani Innocent. Daswa worked as a herd boy before he attended school which started at Vondwe Primary School in 1957 which was followed by secondary school at Mphaphuli High School. After his father's accidental death, it fell to him to provide for his siblings and did this by paying for their education while in the workforce. In the workforce, he helped pay for his siblings' education and constantly encouraged them to take pride in their studies.<sup id="cite_ref-auto1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daswa was exposed to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a> through a friend he met while in <a href="/wiki/Johannesburg" title="Johannesburg">Johannesburg</a> while living with an uncle there. He learnt about the Catholic faith and learnt catechism every Sunday from Benedict Risimati. After two years of instruction, Daswa was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 21 April 1963 by the priest Augustine O'Brien.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He took the name of "Benedict" due to the fact that he was inspired by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Benedict of Nursia</a>, also selecting as his life motto "Ora et labora" (pray and work).<sup id="cite_ref-auto1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was confirmed by Abbot Bishop F. Clemens van Hoek three months later on 21 July 1963.
</p><p>He became an active member of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_South_Africa" title="Catholic Church in South Africa">Catholic Church in South Africa</a>. Daswa went to Venda Teacher Training College to do a primary teacher's certificate and later obtained his <a href="/wiki/Matriculation" title="Matriculation">matriculation</a> through correspondence in 1973. He served as a teacher and catechist as he worked with adolescents and he assisted families that endured economic hardship. Daswa was a highly respected individual in his local community and became known for his honesty, truthfulness and integrity, even known to fetch students who decided to skip schools. Daswa later helped to build the first church in his area and later became the principal of the school that he taught at on 1 January 1979 at Nweli Primary School.<sup id="cite_ref-auto1_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He once convinced a father who wanted to marry off his daughter to an older man to allow the girl to complete her education.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daswa married Shadi Eveline Monyai (d. 2008) in 1974 and the pair had a total of eight children, including Lufuno (b. 1977) who was the eldest, Benedicta (b. 1990), Helen and Faith. He would help his wife with household chores, unheard of at that time in his area, and he valued his family to the point of hosting Daswa Family Days each 16 December where gifts would be exchanged and a meal held.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> For his family, he personally built his brick house. Daswa also established a <a href="/wiki/Soccer" class="mw-redirect" title="Soccer">soccer</a> team called the Mbahe Eleven Computers and left this team when members wanted to use "<a href="/wiki/Muti" title="Muti">muti</a>" (medicine) in order to win games. He started a new team, Mbahe Freedom Rebels. He was an active member of his community, serving as the secretary of the local traditional council; the local chief valued his counsel.
</p><p>In November 1989, heavy rains and lightning strikes plagued the area. When his village suffered strong storms again in January 1990, the elders decided that the lightning occurred due to <a href="/wiki/Magic_(supernatural)" title="Magic (supernatural)">magic</a> and thus demanded a tax from all their residents to pay for a sangoma to "sniff out" the witch who caused the storms, who they would then burn. Daswa said they were just a natural phenomenon and declined to pay the tax; he stated that his Catholic faith prevented him "from taking part in anything connected with witchcraft."<sup id="cite_ref-Samasumo202_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Samasumo202-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 2 February 1990, Daswa drove his sister-in-law and her sick child to a doctor in <a href="/wiki/Thohoyandou" title="Thohoyandou">Thohoyandou</a> and en route, picked up a man who asked for his help to take a bag of <a href="/wiki/Mealie_meal" class="mw-redirect" title="Mealie meal">mealie meal</a> to his home in a town next to Mbahe. At around 7:30pm, he returned to Mbahe where he left his sister-in-law and child near their home. He told his daughter that he would soon return after taking the passenger to the next village.
</p><p>Returning home, found his path blocked by fallen trees and as he attempted to clear the road he was ambushed by a mob of young men. Bleeding as a result of <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoning</a>, he left his damaged car and ran for assistance at a woman's hut. However, the woman revealed where he was when the mob threatened to kill her if she did not comply. As a result of this, he was beaten and clubbed over the head. Boiling water was poured over him in his ears and nostrils after he was attacked to ensure that he was dead. His final words were, "God, into Your hands receive my spirit".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The woman of the hut informed Daswa's brother what had happened and the latter remained with his dead brother for the night.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-mortem">Post-mortem</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Daswa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Post-mortem">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The funeral took place on Saturday, 10 February 1990. The funeral procession commenced at Daswa's home and then proceeded to Nweli Church. Celebrants wore red vestments to indicate their belief that Daswa died at the hands of his attackers in hatred of his faith.
</p><p>Daswa's mother later converted to Roman Catholicism and saved her pension to purchase a tombstone for her murdered son. This was unveiled after a special Mass on 26 November 2000. Daswa's remains were later transferred to Nweli Church on 24 August 2015.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Daswa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cause of beatification commenced on 10 June 2008 on a diocesan level in <a href="/wiki/Tzaneen" title="Tzaneen">Tzaneen</a> which concluded in 2009 with the commencement of the "Roman Phase". <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved a decree that recognized his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> on 22 January 2015. The beatification was celebrated by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> in Limpopo on 13 September 2015; the beatification date was confirmed in March 2015.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Present at the celebration was Daswa's mother and children as well as Augustine O'Brien who baptized him. Approximately 30,000 people attended the beatification.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Daswa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On the 1st of February 2020 thousands of the people gathered at the Benedict Daswa Shrine Site in Tshitanini to witness the 30th anniversary celebration of the martyrdom of Blessed Benedict Daswa. Cardinal Wilfrid Napier was the main celebrant, assisted by Bishop Joao Rodrigues and Bishop Emeritus Hugh Slattery of <a href="/wiki/Tzaneen" title="Tzaneen">Tzaneen</a> Diocese. Napier based his homily on the gospel of the day: "unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies it remains itself alone, but it if dies it produces many more seeds like itself".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Daswa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Daswa's remains were exhumed from his grave on 24 August 2015 in the presence of the police, a nurse and a doctor. The exhumation took more than four hours due to the secure burial with several layers of thick cement between layers of soil; the steel coffin was reported to be in good condition. At the behest of the family, his remains were taken to the grave of his wife and the coffin opened to reveal Daswa's skeletal remains intact. The family desired the remains to be left undisturbed in the original coffin for reinterment, which was cleaned and covered in white material and colored traditional Venda cloth. A document to testify to the exhumation was placed in the coffin. His remains were relocated to a grave in a church in Mbahe and a toe and a piece of the garment he was wearing was removed and was sent away to be used as relics.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b>Medal</b>
</p><p>Daswa's Medal was created in South Africa in 2015 by <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://web.facebook.com/immaculateheartjhb">Immaculate Heart Catholic Supply</a>, which has his face on the front and the Knob-Kierrie and stones at the back with "Pray for us" these items symbolize his Martydom. The devotional medal was the very first created in South Africa by a South African Catholic company. The Medal can be found and bought by the Cause of Canonization, Radio Veritas, Immaculate Heart Catholic Supply or your nearest Catholic Church who has them available.
</p>
