<p><b>Alcmund of Hexham</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 7 September 780 or 781) became the 7th bishop of the see of <a href="/wiki/Hexham_Abbey" title="Hexham Abbey">Hexham</a> in <a href="/wiki/Northumberland" title="Northumberland">Northumberland</a> when he was consecrated on 24 April 767;<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook217_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook217-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the see was centred on the church there founded by <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ba-n060-0801_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ba-n060-0801-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alcmund died on 7 September 780 or 781<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook217_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook217-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was buried beside <a href="/wiki/Acca_(bishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Acca (bishop)">Acca</a> outside the church. Virtually nothing is now known of his life, but he was apparently deeply venerated as one of the Hexham saints.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alchmund_of_Hexham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By the early 11th century, after the <a href="/wiki/Danes_(Germanic_tribe)" class="mw-redirect" title="Danes (Germanic tribe)">Danes</a> had ravaged this part of the country, it seems that his tomb had been entirely forgotten. <a href="/wiki/Symeon_of_Durham" title="Symeon of Durham">Symeon of Durham</a> writes that Alcmund appeared in a vision to Dregmo, a man of Hexham, urging him to tell <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfred_son_of_Westou&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Alfred son of Westou (page does not exist)">Alfred son of Westou</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sacrist" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrist">sacrist</a> of <a href="/wiki/Durham,_England" title="Durham, England">Durham</a>, to have his body <a href="/wiki/Translation_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (religion)">translated</a> (removed and re-buried as a relic). Alfred did so, but stole one of the bones to take back with him to Durham; the shrine however could not be moved by any strength of man until the bone was replaced.<sup id="cite_ref-thurston_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thurston-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1154, the church, having been ruined again, was again restored, and the bones of the Hexham saints, including Alcmund, were gathered into a single <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Scot" class="mw-redirect" title="Scot">Scots</a> however pillaged and finally destroyed both church and shrine in a border raid in 1296.<sup id="cite_ref-thurston_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thurston-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
