<p><b>Pope Clement XI</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Clemens XI</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Clemente XI</i>; 23 July 1649&#160;– 19 March 1721), born <b>Giovanni Francesco Albani</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 23 November 1700 to his death in March 1721.
</p><p>Clement XI was a patron of the arts and of science. He was also a great benefactor of the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Library" title="Vatican Library">Vatican Library</a>; his interest in archaeology is credited with saving much of Rome's antiquity. He authorized expeditions which succeeded in rediscovering various ancient Christian writings and authorized excavations of the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome" title="Catacombs of Rome">Roman catacombs</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Giovanni Francesco Albani was born in 1649 in <a href="/wiki/Urbino" title="Urbino">Urbino</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Albani_family" title="Albani family">Albani family</a>, a distinguished family of <a href="/wiki/Albanians" title="Albanians">Albanian</a> origin in central Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Frazee167168_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Frazee167168-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Martucci154_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martucci154-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His mother Elena Mosca (1630–1698) was a high-standing Italian of <a href="/wiki/Bergamo" title="Bergamo">bergamasque</a> origin, descended from the noble Mosca family of <a href="/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro">Pesaro</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ancestry_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ancestry-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His father Carlo Albani (1623–1684) was a patrician. His mother descended in part from the Staccoli family,<sup id="cite_ref-ancestry_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ancestry-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who were patricians of <a href="/wiki/Urbino" title="Urbino">Urbino</a>, in part from the Giordani,<sup id="cite_ref-ancestry_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ancestry-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who were nobles of Pesaro.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Williams116_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams116-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The original name of the Albani was Lazzi (Laçi) which they changed to <i>Albani</i> in memory of their origin. Francesco Albani funded an expedition in Albania to locate the exact settlement of his family's origins. In the final report, the two most probable locations which were presented to him were <a href="/wiki/La%C3%A7" title="Laç">Laç</a> near Lezhë and Laç near Kukës, both in northern Albania.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrunga2021134_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunga2021134-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Albani was educated at the <a href="/wiki/Collegio_Romano" class="mw-redirect" title="Collegio Romano">Collegio Romano</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> from 1660 onwards. He became a very proficient Latinist and gained a doctorate in both canon and civil law. He was one of those who frequented the academy of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Christina_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Christina of Sweden">Queen Christina of Sweden</a>.  He would serve as a papal prelate under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a> and was appointed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> as the Referendary of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Signatura" title="Apostolic Signatura">Apostolic Signatura</a>. Throughout this time, he also served as the governor of <a href="/wiki/Rieti" title="Rieti">Rieti</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sabina_(region)" title="Sabina (region)">Sabina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Orvieto" title="Orvieto">Orvieto</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinalate">Cardinalate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cardinalate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:ALBANI_GIOVANFRANCESCO.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/ALBANI_GIOVANFRANCESCO.jpg/170px-ALBANI_GIOVANFRANCESCO.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="268" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d6/ALBANI_GIOVANFRANCESCO.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="200" data-file-height="315" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Cardinal Albani, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1690</span></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a> elevated him to the cardinalate in 1690 despite his protests and made him the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Aquiro" title="Santa Maria in Aquiro">Cardinal-Deacon of Santa Maria in Aquiro</a> but he later opted for the <i>Diaconia</i> of San Adriano al Forno and later, as the Cardinal-Priest, for the <i>titulus</i> of San Silvestro in Capite. He was then ordained to the priesthood in September 1700 and celebrated his first Mass in Rome on 6 October 1700.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>After the death of Pope Innocent XII in 1700, a conclave was convoked to elect a successor. Albani was regarded as a fine diplomat known for his skills as a peacemaker and so was unanimously elected pope on 23 November 1700. He agreed to the election after three days of consultation.
</p><p>Unusually, from the viewpoint of current practice, his election came within three months after his ordination as a priest and within two months after he celebrated his first Mass, though he had been a cardinal for ten years previously. Having accepted election after some hesitation, he was ordained a bishop on 30 November 1700 and assumed the pontifical name of "Clement XI". Cardinal protodeacon <a href="/wiki/Benedetto_Pamphili" title="Benedetto Pamphili">Benedetto Pamphili</a> crowned him on 8 December 1700 and he took possession of the <a href="/wiki/Archbasilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran" title="Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran">Basilica of Saint John Lateran</a> on 10 April 1701.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Actions">Actions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Actions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg/160px-Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="373" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg/240px-Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg/320px-Stained_glass_depiction_of_Pope_Clement_XI_at_the_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa_in_Prishtina.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1112" data-file-height="2592" /></a><figcaption>Stained glass depiction of Clement XI at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Mother_Teresa" title="Cathedral of Saint Mother Teresa">Catholic Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pristina" title="Pristina">Pristina</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Soon after his accession to the pontificate, the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession" title="War of the Spanish Succession">War of the Spanish Succession</a> broke out.
</p><p>In 1703 Pope Clement XI <a href="/wiki/Arb%C3%ABni_Council" title="Arbëni Council">ordered a synod</a> of Catholic bishops in northern Albania that discussed promotion of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a> decrees within Albanian dioceses, <a href="/wiki/Islamization_of_Albania#Northern_Albania" title="Islamization of Albania">stemming conversions among locals to Islam</a> and securing agreement to deny communion to crypto-Catholics who outwardly professed the Muslim faith.<sup id="cite_ref-Frazee167168_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Frazee167168-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Skendi235242_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Skendi235242-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Martucci154_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martucci154-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite initially holding an ambiguous neutrality in world affairs, Clement XI was later forced to name <a href="/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles, Archduke of Austria</a>, as the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Spain">King of Spain</a>, since the imperial army had conquered much of northern Italy and was threatening Rome itself in January 1709.
</p><p>By the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht" class="mw-redirect" title="Treaty of Utrecht">Treaty of Utrecht</a> that put an end to the war, the Papal States lost their suzerainty over the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Farnese" title="House of Farnese">Farnese</a> <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Parma_and_Piacenza" title="Duchy of Parma and Piacenza">Duchy of Parma and Piacenza</a> in favour of <a href="/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy" title="Habsburg monarchy">Austria</a>, and lost <a href="/wiki/Comacchio" title="Comacchio">Comacchio</a> as well, a blow to the prestige of the Papal States.
</p><p>In 1713 Clement XI issued the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Unigenitus" title="Unigenitus">Unigenitus</a></i> in response to the spread of the <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenist</a> heresy. There followed great upheaval in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">France</a>, where apart from theological issues, a strong <a href="/wiki/Gallicanism" title="Gallicanism">Gallican</a> tendency persisted. The bull, which was produced with the contribution of Gregorio Selleri, a lector at the College of Saint Thomas, the future <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas" title="Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas">Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas, <i>Angelicum</i></a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> condemned <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenism</a> by extracting and anathematizing as <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical</a> 101 propositions from the works of <a href="/wiki/Pasquier_Quesnel" title="Pasquier Quesnel">Quesnel</a>, declaring them to be identical in substance with propositions already condemned in the writings of <a href="/wiki/Cornelius_Jansen" title="Cornelius Jansen">Jansenius</a>.
</p><p>The resistance of many French ecclesiastics and the refusal of the French <i><a href="/wiki/Parlement" title="Parlement">parlements</a></i> to register the bull led to controversies extending through the greater part of the 18th century. Because the local governments did not officially receive the bull, it was not, technically, in force in those areas&#160;– an example of the interference of states in religious affairs common before the 20th century.
</p><p>Clement XI supported <a href="/wiki/James_Francis_Edward_Stuart" title="James Francis Edward Stuart">James Francis Edward Stuart</a>, the exiled Stuart Prince by paying for their residence in Rome, the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Muti" title="Palazzo Muti">Palazzo Muti</a>, as well as donating a summerhouse near the shores of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Albano" title="Lake Albano">Lake Albano</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kybett_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kybett-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He also performed the baptism of James' son <a href="/wiki/Charles_Edward_Stuart" title="Charles Edward Stuart">Charles Edward Stuart</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kybett_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kybett-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his reign as a pope the famous <i><a href="/wiki/Illyricum_sacrum" title="Illyricum sacrum">Illyricum Sacrum</a></i> was commissioned, and today it is one of the main sources of the field of <a href="/wiki/Balkans#Early_modern_period" title="Balkans">Balkan region during Middle Ages</a>, with over 5,000 pages divided in several volumes written by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Daniele_Farlati" title="Daniele Farlati">Daniele Farlati</a> and Dom <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacopo_Coleti&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jacopo Coleti (page does not exist)">Jacopo Coleti</a>.
</p><p>Clement XI made a concerted effort to acquire Christian manuscripts in <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> from Egypt and other places in the Middle East, greatly expanding the <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Apostolica_Vaticana" class="mw-redirect" title="Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana">Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana</a>'s collection of Syriac works.<sup id="cite_ref-syriacmanu_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-syriacmanu-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_activities">Other activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Other activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Clement XI extended the feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary" title="Our Lady of the Rosary">Our Lady of the Rosary</a> to the Universal Church of the Roman Rite in 1716.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatifications_and_canonizations">Beatifications and canonizations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Beatifications and canonizations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Nelli-Clement.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Nelli-Clement.jpg/185px-Nelli-Clement.jpg" decoding="async" width="185" height="226" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Nelli-Clement.jpg/278px-Nelli-Clement.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Nelli-Clement.jpg/370px-Nelli-Clement.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="610" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Clement XI by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Nelli" title="Pietro Nelli">Pietro Nelli</a> (c.1714), at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Palazzo_Albani&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Palazzo Albani (page does not exist)">Palazzo Albani</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Albani" class="extiw" title="it:Palazzo Albani">it</a>&#93;</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>Clement XI confirmed the cultus of <a href="/wiki/Ceslas_Odrowaz" class="mw-redirect" title="Ceslas Odrowaz">Ceslas Odrowaz</a> (27 August 1712), <a href="/wiki/Jakov_Varingez" class="mw-redirect" title="Jakov Varingez">Jakov Varingez</a> (29 December 1700), John of Perugia (11 September 1704), <a href="/wiki/Peregrine_Laziosi" title="Peregrine Laziosi">Peregrine Laziosi</a> (11 September 1702), Peter of Sassoferrato (11 September 1704), Buonfiglio Monaldi (1 December 1717), <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a> (8 July 1713) and <a href="/wiki/Humbeline_of_Jully" title="Humbeline of Jully">Humbeline of Jully</a> (1703). He formally beatified a number of individuals: <a href="/wiki/Alexis_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</a>, Bartholomew degli Amidei and Benedict Dellantella, (1 December 1717) and <a href="/wiki/John_Francis_Regis" title="John Francis Regis">John Francis Régis</a> (24 May 1716). He also beatified the sisters <a href="/wiki/Theresa_of_Portugal,_Queen_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Theresa of Portugal, Queen of León">Theresa</a> (20 May 1705) and <a href="/wiki/Sancha_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Sancha of Portugal">Sancha</a> (10 May 1705).
</p><p>He canonized <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Avellino" title="Andrew Avellino">Andrew Avellino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Bologna" title="Catherine of Bologna">Catherine of Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Cantalice" title="Felix of Cantalice">Felix of Cantalice</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> on 22 May 1712, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Humility" title="Saint Humility">Humility</a> on 27 January 1720, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_Obazine" title="Stephen of Obazine">Stephen of Obazine</a> in 1701 and <a href="/wiki/Boniface_of_Lausanne" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface of Lausanne">Boniface of Lausanne</a> in 1702.
</p><p>Clement XI, on 8 February 1720, named <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury" title="Anselm of Canterbury">Saint Anselm of Canterbury</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a>, providing him the supplementary titles of "<i>Doctor magnificus</i>" ("Magnificent Doctor") and "<i>Doctor Marianus</i>" ("Marian Doctor").
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Consistories">Consistories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Consistories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cardinals_created_by_Clement_XI" title="Cardinals created by Clement XI">Cardinals created by Clement XI</a></div>
<p>Clement XI created a total of 70 cardinals in 15 consistories. Notably, two cardinals of his own creation were Michelangelo dei Conti, who became his immediate successor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XIII" title="Pope Innocent XIII">Pope Innocent XIII</a>, and Lorenzo Corsini, who later became <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a>. The pope also nominated eight cardinals "<i><a href="/wiki/In_pectore" title="In pectore">in pectore</a></i>", later publishing their names which validated their appointments as cardinals.
</p><p>During his pontificate, Gabriele Filippucci resigned his cardinalate which the pope accepted on 7 June 1706. Clement XI also accepted the resignation of <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Maria_de%27_Medici,_Duke_of_Rovere_and_Montefeltro" title="Francesco Maria de&#39; Medici, Duke of Rovere and Montefeltro">Francesco Maria de' Medici</a> from the cardinalate on 19 June 1709.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Chinese_Rites_controversies">Chinese Rites controversies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Chinese Rites controversies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png/220px-Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="156" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png/330px-Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png/440px-Open_letter_from_Kangxi_to_Pope_Clement_XI.png 2x" data-file-width="1215" data-file-height="861" /></a><figcaption>Open letter from <a href="/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor" title="Kangxi Emperor">Kangxi Emperor</a> to Pope Clement XI</figcaption></figure>
<p>Another important decision of Clement XI was in regard to the <a href="/wiki/Chinese_Rites_controversy" title="Chinese Rites controversy">Chinese Rites controversy</a>: the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuit</a> missionaries were forbidden to take part in honors paid to <a href="/wiki/Confucius" title="Confucius">Confucius</a> or the ancestors of the Emperors of <a href="/wiki/Manchu_China" class="mw-redirect" title="Manchu China">China</a>, which Clement XI identified as "idolatrous and barbaric", and to accommodate Christian language to pagan ideas under plea of conciliating the heathen.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg/220px-Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="167" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg/330px-Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg/440px-Funeral_of_Pope_Clement_XI.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3900" data-file-height="2968" /></a><figcaption>Clement's funeral</figcaption></figure>
<p>Clement XI died in Rome on 19 March 1721 at 12:45pm and was buried in the pavement of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> rather than in an ornate tomb like those of his predecessors.
</p><p>On March 10, Clement XI had a meeting at about 11:00am with the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Sisteron" title="Ancient Diocese of Sisteron">Bishop of Sisteron</a> Pierre François Lafitau. When the pope met with the bishop, he said that his time was drawing to a close and that he would soon die, despite protests to the contrary by Lafitau. On 14 March, Clement XI took ill while Lafitau was trying to get the pope's nephew to persuade the pope to name the French First Minister <a href="/wiki/Guillaume_Dubois" title="Guillaume Dubois">Guillaume Dubois</a> to the cardinalate. However, Clement XI was in a state of delirium and was not responsive to his pleas. On 16 March, <a href="/wiki/Quadragesima_Sunday" title="Quadragesima Sunday">Quadragesima Sunday</a>, the pope did not participate in the services, however, celebrated Mass in his private chapel at the <a href="/wiki/Quirinale_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Quirinale Palace">Quirinale Palace</a>. He took medication that day but experienced pains in his thorax and had trouble breathing from the cold air in his rooms.<sup id="cite_ref-CSUN_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CSUN-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The following day, Clement XI celebrated Mass in his private chapel before meeting various prelates which included the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Ravenna-Cervia" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Ravenna-Cervia">Archbishop of Ravenna</a> Geronimo Crispi. However, at around noon, he was suddenly struck with an extraordinary chill which was accompanied by a very strong <a href="/wiki/Fever" title="Fever">fever</a> that immediately forced him to his bed, with the pope declining a meal that evening. His pulse was exceptionally slow and he even coughed up a thick liquid that was streaked with blood. Unable to sleep that night, his fever abated somewhat. But the following day saw his fever return much more violently, and he had an irregular pulse. The <a href="/wiki/Sputum" title="Sputum">sputum</a> was foamy, once more with blood, indicating that there was something wrong with his lungs, causing his doctors to realize that his condition would more than likely prove fatal. Clement XI made his confession and the profession of faith before receiving <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Holy Communion</a> at 8:00pm. <a href="/wiki/James_Francis_Edward_Stuart" title="James Francis Edward Stuart">James Francis Edward Stuart</a>, the "Pretender", tried to see the dying pope, however he was denied on the grounds of the dangerous state of the pope's condition. That night, the papal sacristan and the Bishop of Porfirio, Niccolo Agostino degli Abbati Olivieri, Bishop of Porfirio, administered the <a href="/wiki/Extreme_Unction" class="mw-redirect" title="Extreme Unction">Extreme Unction</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CSUN_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CSUN-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 19 March, the fever returned violently, and Clement XI slowly lost his ability to speak as his eyes clouded over and his respiration slowly diminished as the pope died just after midday.<sup id="cite_ref-CSUN_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CSUN-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Contemporary_influence">Contemporary influence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Contemporary influence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his book "<i>Journal of a Soul</i>", while he was preparing for the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> resolved to pray the <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/St._Vincent%27s_Manual/Universal_Prayer_for_All_Things_Necessary_to_Salvation." class="extiw" title="s:St. Vincent&#39;s Manual/Universal Prayer for All Things Necessary to Salvation.">Universal Prayer</a> and highly recommended it to others.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Construction_activity_and_patronage">Construction activity and patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Clement_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Construction activity and patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg/220px-Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg/330px-Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg/440px-Tomb_of_Pope_Clement_XI_requiem.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>The tomb of Pope Clement XI.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Pope Clement XI had a famous <a href="/wiki/Sundial" title="Sundial">sundial</a> added in the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_degli_Angeli_e_dei_Martiri" title="Santa Maria degli Angeli e dei Martiri">Santa Maria degli Angeli e dei Martiri</a> and had an obelisk erected in the <a href="/wiki/Piazza_della_Rotonda" title="Piazza della Rotonda">Piazza della Rotonda</a> in front of the <a href="/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome" title="Pantheon, Rome">Pantheon</a>, and a port built on the <a href="/wiki/Tiber_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiber River">Tiber River</a>, the beautiful <a href="/wiki/Porto_di_Ripetta" title="Porto di Ripetta">porto di Ripetta</a>, demolished at the end of the 19th century.
</p><p>He established a committee, overseen by his favourite artists, <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Maratta" title="Carlo Maratta">Carlo Maratta</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Fontana" title="Carlo Fontana">Carlo Fontana</a>, to commission statuary of the apostles to complete the decoration of <a href="/wiki/San_Giovanni_in_Laterano" class="mw-redirect" title="San Giovanni in Laterano">San Giovanni in Laterano</a>. He also founded an academy of painting and sculpture on the <a href="/wiki/Campidoglio" class="mw-redirect" title="Campidoglio">Campidoglio</a>.
</p><p>He also enriched the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_library" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatican library">Vatican library</a> with numerous Oriental codices and lent his patronage to the first archaeological excavations in the Roman <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">catacombs</a>. In his native Urbino he restored numerous edifices and founded a public library.
</p>
