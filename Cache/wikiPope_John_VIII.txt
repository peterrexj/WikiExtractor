					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Head of the Catholic Church from 872 to 882</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the Catholic pope. <p><b>Pope John VIII</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes VIII</i>; died 16 December 882) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 14 December 872 to his death. He is often considered one of the ablest <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">popes</a> of the 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John devoted much of his papacy attempting to halt and reverse the <a href="/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Sicily" title="Muslim conquest of Sicily">Muslim gains in southern Italy</a> and their march northwards. When his efforts to obtain assistance from either the Franks or the Byzantines failed, John strengthened the defences of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He supported <a href="/wiki/Methodius_of_Thessalonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Methodius of Thessalonica">Methodius of Thessalonica</a> in his mission to the <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavs</a>, defended him against the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> rulers and Bavarian clergy, and authorized the <a href="/wiki/Translation_of_the_Bible" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation of the Bible">translation of the Bible</a> into <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Slavonic</a>. John also extended diplomatic recognition to the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Croatia" title="Duchy of Croatia">Duchy of Croatia</a> and resolved the <a href="/wiki/Photian_schism" title="Photian schism">Photian schism</a>. John's pontificate ended with his assassination, and the papacy became significantly weaker in the aftermath.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Slavonic_liturgy">Slavonic liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Slavonic liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Letter_svatopluk.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Letter_svatopluk.jpg/170px-Letter_svatopluk.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="211" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Letter_svatopluk.jpg/255px-Letter_svatopluk.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Letter_svatopluk.jpg/340px-Letter_svatopluk.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1624" data-file-height="2016" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Letter_svatopluk.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>John VIII's letter to <a href="/wiki/Svatopluk_I_of_Moravia" title="Svatopluk I of Moravia">Svatopluk I of Moravia</a></div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_II" title="Pope Adrian II">Pope Adrian II</a> consecrated <a href="/wiki/Methodius_of_Thessalonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Methodius of Thessalonica">Methodius of Thessalonica</a> as archbishop and supported his mission to the Slavs. Unbeknownst to Rome, Methodius was imprisoned in 870 by the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> King <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> and Bavarian bishops, who objected to his use of the <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Slavonic language</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a> and his encroachment on their jurisdiction in <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoldberg2006300,_319_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldberg2006300,_319-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Adrian II died in 872, and John VIII, a Roman native,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKreutz199657_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKreutz199657-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> was <a href="/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059" title="Papal selection before 1059">selected</a> to succeed him. When Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Anno_of_Freising&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Anno of Freising (page does not exist)">Anno of Freising</a> visited Rome, John inquired about the whereabouts of the missing Methodius, but Anno lied to him. In the summer of 873, John finally learned the truth. Furious, he forbade the celebration of <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">mass</a> in Bavaria until Methodius was released.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoldberg2006319_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldberg2006319-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After his release, Methodius came to Rome and convinced John to allow him to <a href="/wiki/Bible_translations" title="Bible translations">translate the Bible</a> to Slavonic as well as to perform liturgy in Slavonic. John wrote: "He who made three main languages - <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Roman</a> - also made all other languages to sing his praise and glory."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Saracen_incursions">Saracen incursions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Saracen incursions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a young man, John witnessed the <a href="/wiki/Arab_raid_against_Rome" title="Arab raid against Rome">Arab raid against Rome</a>. Their expansion into Italy was severely affecting the economy of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKreutz199657_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKreutz199657-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After the raids against <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Hills" class="mw-redirect" title="Sabine Hills">Sabine Hills</a>, Pope John asked for military aid from Emperor <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a> and later Count <a href="/wiki/Boso_of_Provence" title="Boso of Provence">Boso of Provence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His efforts failed and he was forced to pay tribute to the <a href="/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily" title="Emirate of Sicily">Emirate of Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The threatening Muslim military presence (which he believed was God's punishment against "bad Christians"),<sup id="cite_ref-Tolan_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolan-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> coupled with alliances they formed with the local Christians, prompted John to promote "a new and uncompromisingly hostile view of the Saracens." This included a ban on forming alliances with the Muslims. However, his efforts proved unsuccessful,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> partly because Christian leaders viewed his calls for unity as an excuse to assert papal authority in southern Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Tolan_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tolan-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg/220px-EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="104" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg/330px-EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg/440px-EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg 2x" data-file-width="779" data-file-height="370" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:EB1911_Palaeography_-_Bull_of_Pope_John_VIII.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>An 876 bull of John VIII</div></div></div>
<p>In 876, John VIII traveled throughout <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a> in an effort to form an alliance among the cities of <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>, <a href="/wiki/Capua" title="Capua">Capua</a>, <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gaeta" title="Gaeta">Gaeta</a> and <a href="/wiki/Amalfi" title="Amalfi">Amalfi</a> against Muslim raids. By 877, all five cities sent delegates to Traietto to formalize an alliance.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKreutz_(1991),_p._58_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKreutz_(1991),_p._58-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Pope John VIII urged Charles to come to his defence in Italy. Charles again crossed the Alps, but this expedition was received with little enthusiasm by the nobles, and even by his regent in Lombardy, Boso, and they refused to join his army. At the same time <a href="/wiki/Carloman_of_Bavaria" title="Carloman of Bavaria">Carloman of Bavaria</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>, entered northern Italy. Charles, ill and in great distress, started on his way back to Gaul, but died while crossing the pass of Mont Cenis on 6 October 877.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Obtaining relatively little support from outside sources, John fell back on what resources he could command. He reinforced the walls previously restored by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a>. As the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls</a> was located outside the <a href="/wiki/Aurelian_Walls" title="Aurelian Walls">Aurelian Walls</a>, and had been damaged in a Saracen raid, the pope fortified the basilica, the monastery, and the nearby dwellings of the peasants. He also founded a papal fleet.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Constantinople_problems">Constantinople problems</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Constantinople problems">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 879, John recognised the reinstatement of <a href="/wiki/Photios_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Photios I">Photios I</a> as the legitimate <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">patriarch of Constantinople</a>. Photius had been condemned in 869 by Adrian II. This was undertaken mainly to appease the Byzantines, since in them he saw the only hope of removing the Arabs from Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> It was commonly believed that some time afterward John VIII re-confirmed the <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a> of Photius, which eventually enabled <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Leo_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Leo VI">Emperor Leo VI</a> to move against Photius.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However, modern scholarship particularly  influenced by Catholic scholar <a href="/wiki/Francis_Dvornik" title="Francis Dvornik">Francis Dvornik</a> has demonstrated this to be a Latin myth, as Photius died in visible perfect communion within the Roman Church.
</p><p>John was anxious that the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Croatia" title="Duchy of Croatia">Duchy of Croatia</a> would follow in the steps of <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgaria</a>, which had recently accepted the spiritual authority of Constantinople rather than that of Rome. After the overthrow of the pro-Byzantine <a href="/wiki/Zdeslav_of_Croatia" title="Zdeslav of Croatia">Zdeslav</a> in 879, John thanked the new duke, <a href="/wiki/Branimir_of_Croatia" title="Branimir of Croatia">Branimir</a>, for returning Croatia to papal jurisdiction. In return, John recognized Croatian independence from the Carolingians.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECurta2006140_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECurta2006140-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John VIII was assassinated in 882 by his own clerics; he was first poisoned, and then clubbed to death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The motives may have been his exhaustion of the papal treasury, his lack of support among the Carolingians, his gestures towards the Byzantines, and his failure to stop the Saracen raids.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Without the protection of powerful magnates or the Carolingian emperor, the papacy after John VIII's reign became increasingly subject to the machinations and greedy ambition of the rival clans of the local nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Malley200979-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
