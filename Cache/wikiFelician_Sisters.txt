<p>The <b>Felician Sisters</b>, officially known as the <b>Congregation of Sisters of St. Felix of Cantalice Third Order Regular of St. Francis of Assisi</b> (<b>CSSF</b>), is a religious institute of <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_right" title="Pontifical right">pontifical right</a> whose members profess public vows of <a href="/wiki/Chastity,_poverty,_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Chastity, poverty, and obedience">chastity, poverty, and obedience</a> and follow the evangelical way of life in common. This active-contemplative <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> was founded in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, in 1855, by <a href="/wiki/Sophia_Truszkowska" class="mw-redirect" title="Sophia Truszkowska">Sophia Truszkowska</a>, and named for a shrine of <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Cantalice" title="Felix of Cantalice">St. Felix</a>, a 16th-century <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> saint especially devoted to children.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Angela_Truszkowska" title="Angela Truszkowska">Angela Truszkowska</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation">Foundation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Foundation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When Sophia Camille Truszkowska was twelve years of age, her family moved to <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a> where her father took up the position of Registrar of Deeds. Initially, she wished to become a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitation</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, but in 1854 she joined the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_St._Vincent_de_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of St. Vincent de Paul">Society of St. Vincent de Paul</a> and began to work among the poor. With her father’s financial assistance, she rented a flat in order to care for several orphaned girls and aged women. Sophia was joined in her work by her cousin and close friend, Clothilde Ciechanowska. Later that year they became lay members of the Franciscan Third Order. On the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Presentation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Presentation of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Feast of the Presentation of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, November 21, 1855, while praying before an icon of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Czestochowa" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of Czestochowa">Our Lady of Czestochowa</a>, they solemnly dedicated themselves to do the will of Jesus Christ in all things. Hereafter this was recorded as the official founding day of the Congregation of the Sisters of St. Felix of Cantalice.<sup id="cite_ref-oloh_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oloh-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>People began calling them "Sisters of St. Felix." in reference to the shrine of St. Felix of Cantalice at a nearby Capuchin church. They were popularly referred to as "Felician Sisters," the name by which the community is still known. In 1857, she and several associates took the Franciscan habit. Sophia took the new name of Mary Angela.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1869 health problems caused her to withdraw from administration of the Congregation. She spent the next thirty years on assignments in the garden and greenhouse, tending flowers for the chapel and in the liturgical vestment sewing room, embroidering altar cloths and chasubles. She died at the provincial house in Kraków on October 10, 1899.<sup id="cite_ref-Felix_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Felix-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Mother Mary Angela Truszkowska was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1993.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Expansion">Expansion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Expansion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Felician sisters came to the United States in 1874, at the invitation of Rev. Joseph Dabrowski, pastor of St. Joseph Parish in <a href="/wiki/Polonia,_Wisconsin" title="Polonia, Wisconsin">Polonia, Wisconsin</a>. There they taught in the parish school. Eventually some relocated to Detroit, MI, where they taught school starting in 1880 at <a href="/wiki/St._Albertus_Roman_Catholic_Church" title="St. Albertus Roman Catholic Church">St. Albertus</a>'s school. By 1900, they were responsible for the teaching of two-thirds of all Polish Catholic children in Poletown as they staffed St. Albertus, St. Casimir, St. Josaphat, and St. Stanislav.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1947 Felician Sisters of Our Lady of the Angels Province, Enfield, Connecticut, accepted an offer to purchase the Paine Private Hospital located in Bangor, Maine; the name of the facility was changed to St. Joseph Hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eventually, their work spread to Canada and Haiti.
</p><p><br />
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_habit">Religious habit</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Religious habit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Most Felician Sisters maintain the religious garb of their Foundress, Blessed Mary Angela Truszkowska, consisting of a brown <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> (beige during summer months), <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a>, (jacket at specified times), <a href="/wiki/Headgear" title="Headgear">headdress</a>, black <a href="/wiki/Veil" title="Veil">veil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Collar_(clothing)" title="Collar (clothing)">collar</a>, Felician wooden <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a> suspended on tape or cord, and simple <a href="/wiki/Ring_(jewellery)" title="Ring (jewellery)">ring</a> received at final profession.  This remains a  discipline in the <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w,_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Kraków, Poland">Kraków</a>, <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl,_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Przemyśl, Poland">Przemyśl</a> and <a href="/wiki/Warsaw,_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Warsaw, Poland">Warsaw</a> <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">provinces</a> in Poland, and a treasured tradition in the former Livonia and Enfield provinces in North America.  At the 1994 General Chapter, a proposal passed allowing the <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">sisters</a> to wear an alternate habit consisting of a brown, black, beige or white skirt, blazer, suit or jumper along with a white blouse.  Sisters wearing the alternate habit wear the Felician Crucifix along with the ring received at final profession and may wear it with our without a veil.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Felician Sisters have always sought to harmonize a deep spiritual and community life with dedication to diverse <a href="/wiki/Works_of_Mercy" class="mw-redirect" title="Works of Mercy">acts of mercy</a>.  As of 2014, there were 1,800 professed members of the Felician Sisters, with about 700 in the North American Province.<sup id="cite_ref-fsna_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fsna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> They use the abbreviation/post-nominal <b>C.S.S.F.</b> (Congregation of the Sisters of St. Felix).
</p><p>They remain active in education, operating, among other facilities, the St. Mary Child Care Center in <a href="/wiki/Livonia,_Michigan" title="Livonia, Michigan">Livonia, Michigan</a>; <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_High_School_(Lodi,_New_Jersey)" title="Immaculate Conception High School (Lodi, New Jersey)">Immaculate Conception High School</a>, founded in 1915 in Lodi, New Jersey; and <a href="/wiki/Villa_Maria_College" title="Villa Maria College">Villa Maria College</a> in Buffalo, New York.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Built on the site of a former Felician orphanage, Our Lady of Grace Village in Newark, Delaware is a 60-unit affordable housing community.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The St. Felix Centre in Toronto, Canada offers  Respite services.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
In <a href="/wiki/Holly,_Michigan" title="Holly, Michigan">Holly, Michigan</a>, they run the Maryville Retreat Center.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Volunteers_in_Mission_Program">Volunteers in Mission Program</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Volunteers in Mission Program">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>As part of the Catholic Volunteer Network, the North American Province has a Felician Volunteers in Mission (VIM) program which offers both short and long-term service opportunities to lay men and women interested in partnering with the Felician Sisters to serve, with compassion, mercy and joy, the disadvantaged and underserved.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Provinces">Provinces</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_Sisters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Provinces">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Immaculate Heart of Mary, <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, Poland (1861)</li>
<li>Our Lady of Czestochowa, <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a>, Poland (1910)</li>
<li>Our Lady Queen of Poland, <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a>, Poland (1922)</li>
<li>Nossa Senhora Aparecida, <a href="/wiki/Curitiba" title="Curitiba">Curitiba</a>, Paraná, Brazil (1950)</li>
<li>Our Lady Mediatrix of Graces, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Italy (1953) (Generalate)</li></ul>
<p>In North America, the Felician Sisters have ministered primarily to <a href="/wiki/Polish_Americans" title="Polish Americans">Polish Americans</a> since their arrival from Poland in 1874.  The sisters provided <a href="/wiki/Social_mobility" title="Social mobility">social mobility</a> for young Polish women. Although the congregation was involved in the care of orphans, the aged, and the sick, teaching remained its primary concern.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
