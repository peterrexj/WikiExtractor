<p><b>Euplius</b> (<b>Euplus</b>) (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Euplo, Euplio</i>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Εὖπλος</span>) (d. c. AD 304) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Euplius_of_Catania&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB,_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg/220px-%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg/330px-%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg/440px-%D0%95%D0%B2%D0%BF%D0%BB%2C_%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>Saint Euplius Fresco from <a href="/wiki/Transfiguration_Church_in_Kovalyovo" title="Transfiguration Church in Kovalyovo">Transfiguration Church in Kovalyovo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod" title="Veliky Novgorod">Novgorod</a>, Russia, <a href="/wiki/1380" title="1380">1380</a></figcaption></figure><p>His name in Greek means "good sailing" which is played upon in the text of the Orthodox Christian Vespers service in his honor.
</p><p>The <i>Passion of Saint Euplius</i> states that he was a deacon and that he was arrested for owning and reading from a copy of the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>.  He was brought before the governor of the city, Calvinianus (Calvinian), who asked the saint to read him extracts from the book.  He was then <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.
</p><p>The remains of the Saint rest in the Cathedral of the Assumption of <a href="/wiki/Trevico" title="Trevico">Trevico</a>; it is plausible that they were brought just before the Arab invasion of Sicily in the 10th century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 5 February <a href="/wiki/1654" title="1654">1654</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trevico" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trevico">Bishop of Trevico</a>, Donato Pascasio, authorized the translation of a bone of the saint in favor of the catanese diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Euplius_of_Catania&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With <a href="/wiki/Saint_Agatha" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Agatha">Saint Agatha</a>, he is a co-<a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> of <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>. He is also the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Trevico and <a href="/wiki/Francavilla_di_Sicilia" title="Francavilla di Sicilia">Francavilla di Sicilia</a>. His feast day is August 12.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg/220px-Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="347" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg/330px-Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg/440px-Chiesa_di_Santo_Euplio_-_Interni.jpg 2x" data-file-width="457" data-file-height="720" /></a><figcaption>Interior of the Church of St Euplius in Catania before the bombing.</figcaption></figure>
<p>An ancient church, dating back to the 5th century, located in Catania near Piazza <a href="/wiki/Stesichorus" title="Stesichorus">Stesicoro</a>, was dedicated to him; various buildings stratified over the centuries, but during the <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> an american bombing reduced the new church, dating from the 18th century, to rubble. Today only the ancient crypt of the primitive church remains. This urban site coincides with the place of his martyrdom.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG"><img resource="/wiki/File:Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG/220px-Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="260" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG/330px-Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG/440px-Church_of_Saint_Euplius_in_Myasnitskaya.JPG 2x" data-file-width="771" data-file-height="911" /></a><figcaption>Church of St. Euplius the Archdeacon (<a href="/wiki/1882" title="1882">1882</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Euplio of Catania was also the object of a strong devotion in <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>. In <a href="/wiki/1471" title="1471">1471</a>, a wooden church was erected in honor of the saint to celebrate the peace between the Grand Prince of Muscovy <a href="/wiki/Ivan_III_of_Russia" title="Ivan III of Russia">Ivan III</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Novgorod_Republic" title="Novgorod Republic">Novgorod Republic</a>. In <a href="/wiki/1657" title="1657">1657</a>, Tsar <a href="/wiki/Alexis_of_Russia" title="Alexis of Russia">Alexei I Romanov</a> had the building rebuilt in stone. In the 18th century on the occasion of a renovation, a majestic facade was erected. The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Church_of_St._Euplius_the_Archdeacon&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Church of St. Euplius the Archdeacon (page does not exist)">Church of St. Euplius the Archdeacon</a>, on what is now <i>Myasnitskaya</i> Street in Moscow, was demolished, on <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Stalin" title="Joseph Stalin">Stalin</a>'s orders, in <a href="/wiki/1926" title="1926">1926</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
