<p><b>Fidelis of Como</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Fedele</i>) (died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;304</span>) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Soldier-saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldier-saint">soldier-saint</a>, according to Christian tradition.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Como&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fidelis' cult is associated with <a href="/wiki/Carpophorus,_Exanthus,_Cassius,_Severinus,_Secundus,_and_Licinius" title="Carpophorus, Exanthus, Cassius, Severinus, Secundus, and Licinius">Carpophorus and Exanthus</a>, two <a href="/wiki/Soldier-saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldier-saint">soldier-saints</a>. Variations on their legend are applied to Fidelis. The first says that he, with Carpophorus and Exanthus, were Roman soldiers, members of the <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legendary</a> <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a>, who deserted during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> by <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The three split up at Como, with Carpophorus and Exanthus going into hiding, while Fidelis took a boat across the lake to <a href="/wiki/Samolaco" title="Samolaco">Samolaco</a>. All were eventually
caught and beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The second says that Fidelis was an army officer who was guarding Christian prisoners at Milan, including <a href="/wiki/Alexander_of_Bergamo" title="Alexander of Bergamo">Alexander of Bergamo</a>. He managed to procure the freedom of five of these prisoners. With Carpophorus and Exanthus, he and these five attempted to make their way to the Alps, but were executed at Como.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1996_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1996-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>More likely, he was a Christian missionary sent by <a href="/wiki/Maternus_(bishop_of_Milan)" title="Maternus (bishop of Milan)">Maternus (bishop of Milan)</a> to convert the still pagan area around Lake Como, and there met his death.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>   
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Como&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Fedele,_Como.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg/170px-San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="246" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg/255px-San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg/340px-San_Fedele%2C_Como.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1832" data-file-height="2646" /></a><figcaption>The Basilica of San Fedele in Como.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The cult of Fidelis at Como is ancient.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1996_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1996-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Milan, Como, and <a href="/wiki/Arona_(NO)" class="mw-redirect" title="Arona (NO)">Arona</a> claim the relics of Fidelis.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Magnus_Felix_Ennodius" title="Magnus Felix Ennodius">Magnus Felix Ennodius</a> describes, in the early sixth century, a tomb at Como containing the relics of the martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1996_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1996-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Reports of <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">miracles</a> at the tomb fomented the popularity of his cult. Como's claim is based on an account of 964 that describes the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> of Fidelis' relics from the spot where he was killed to Como in that year.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Sometime before 1000, the church of Sanfedelino, which was dedicated to him, was built on the site of a much earlier simple <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>, from around the fourth century,<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1996_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1996-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> previously dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Euphemia" title="Euphemia">Saint Euphemia</a>.
</p><p>Relics are said to have been transferred to Arona during a time of war between Milan and <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a>. In Arona, the presence of the relics of Fidelis and Carpophrous is attested in documents dating back to 1259 and 1321.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg/220px-IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg/330px-IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg/440px-IMG_4370_-_Milano_-_Chiesa_di_San_Fedele_-_Foto_Giovanni_Dall%27Orto_20-jan_2007.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1360" /></a><figcaption>The church of <a href="/wiki/San_Fedele_(Milan)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Fedele (Milan)">San Fedele</a> in Milan.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1576, <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a> transferred to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> relics of Carpophorus and Fidelis. Though until then their veneration in Arona was minimal, Borromeo's proposed transfer angered the people of the city. As a compromise, Borromeo brought back to Arona the two left forearms of the saints. This occurred on March 13. The city council, which had been pressured by the populace to bring back the relics, decreed that an annual festival occur on that day, which in succeeding centuries has increased in importance. The purpose of the festival was extended to include the celebration for the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Felinus_and_Gratian" title="Felinus and Gratian">Felinus and Gratian</a>, thereby uniting their cult to that of Carpophorus and Fidelis.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Milan, Borromeo commissioned the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Fedele_(Milan)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Fedele (Milan)">San Fedele</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pellegrino_Tibaldi" title="Pellegrino Tibaldi">Pellegrino Tibaldi</a> (1559).
