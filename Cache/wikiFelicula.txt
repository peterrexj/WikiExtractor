<p><b>Felicula</b> was a probably fourth-century Roman martyr whose relics <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a> gave to Bishop John of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> in about 592. She is mentioned in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> on 13 June: "On the seventh milestone from the city of Rome on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a>, Saint Felicula, martyr".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The heavily romanticized <i>Acts of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus" title="Saints Nereus and Achilleus">Saints Nereus and Achilleus</a></i> make of Felicula one of the first virgin martyrs and assign her death to about 90 AD. In this legend she was the foster sister of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petronilla" title="Saint Petronilla">Saint Petronilla</a> and was arrested after Petronilla refused to marry a Roman official. After Petronilla's death, Felicula had no food and water in <a href="/wiki/Prison" title="Prison">prison</a>. She was thrown into a <a href="/wiki/Sanitary_sewer" title="Sanitary sewer">sewer</a>, where she eventually died. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicomedes" title="Saint Nicomedes">Saint Nicomedes</a> recovered her body.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
