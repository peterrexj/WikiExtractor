<p>The <b>Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ordo Fratrum Minorum Capuccinorum</i>; <a href="/wiki/Post-nominal_letters" title="Post-nominal letters">postnominal</a> abbr. <b>O.F.M. Cap.</b>) is a <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious order</a> of <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friars</a> within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, one of Three "<a href="/wiki/Religious_institute#Nomenclature" title="Religious institute">First Orders"</a> that reformed from the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan Friars Minor Observant</a> (OFM Obs., now OFM), the other being the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Conventual" title="Order of Friars Minor Conventual">Conventuals</a> (OFM Conv.). Franciscans reformed as Capuchins in 1525 with the purpose of regaining the original Habit (Tunic) of St. Francis of Assisi and also for returning to a stricter observance of the rule established by <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> in 1209.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins">Origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif/220px-Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif" decoding="async" width="220" height="308" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif/330px-Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif/440px-Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif 2x" data-file-width="1124" data-file-height="1575" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre-Matteo-de-Bascio.gif" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Matteo_Bassi" title="Matteo Bassi">Matteo Bassi</a> (1495–1552), co-founder of the Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:165px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernardino_Ochino.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/Bernardino_Ochino.jpg" decoding="async" width="163" height="252" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="163" data-file-height="252" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernardino_Ochino.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Bernardino_Ochino" title="Bernardino Ochino">Bernardino Ochino</a> (1487–1564), co-founder of the Capuchin Order</div></div></div>
<p>The Order arose in 1525 when <a href="/wiki/Matteo_da_Bascio" class="mw-redirect" title="Matteo da Bascio">Matteo da Bascio</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Observant Franciscan friar</a><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> native to the Italian region of <a href="/wiki/Marche" title="Marche">Marche</a>, said he had been inspired by God with the idea that the manner of life led by the friars of his day was not the one which their founder, <a href="/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Francis of Assisi">St. Francis of Assisi</a>, had envisaged.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He sought to return to the primitive way of life of solitude and penance, as practised by the founder of their Order.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Religious_superior" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious superior">religious superiors</a> tried to suppress these innovations<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and Friar Matteo and his first companions were forced into hiding from Church authorities, who sought to arrest them for having abandoned their religious duties. They were given refuge by the <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a> monks, in gratitude for which they later adopted the hood (or <a href="/wiki/Chaperon_(headgear)" title="Chaperon (headgear)"><i>cappuccio</i></a>, <i><a href="/wiki/Capuche" title="Capuche">capuche</a></i>) worn by that Order—which was the mark of a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in that region of Italy—and the practise of wearing a beard. The popular name of their Order originates from this feature of their <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a>.
</p><p>In 1528, Friar Matteo obtained the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> and was given permission to live as a hermit and to go about everywhere preaching to the poor. These permissions were not only for himself, but for all such as might join him in the attempt to restore the most literal observance possible of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Francis">Rule of St. Francis</a>. Matteo and the original band were soon joined by others. Matteo and his companions were formed into a separate <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">province</a>, called the Hermit Friars Minor, as a branch of the <a href="/wiki/Conventual_Franciscans" class="mw-redirect" title="Conventual Franciscans">Conventual Franciscans</a>, but with a <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">Vicar</a> Provincial of their own, subject to the jurisdiction of the Minister General of the Conventuals. The Observants, the other branch of the Franciscan Order at that time, continued to oppose the movement.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Rules_of_the_Order">Rules of the Order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Rules of the Order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1529, they had four houses and held their first <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a>, at which their particular rules were drawn up. The <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">eremitical</a> idea was abandoned, but the life was to be one of extreme austerity, simplicity and poverty—in all things as near an approach to St Francis' ideals as was practicable. Neither the monasteries nor the Province should possess anything, nor were any loopholes left for evading this law. No large provision against temporal wants should be made, and the supplies in the house should never exceed what was necessary for a few days. Everything was to be obtained by begging, and the friars were not allowed even to touch money.
</p><p>The communities were to be small, eight being fixed as the normal number and twelve as the limit. In furniture and clothing extreme simplicity was enjoined and the friars were <a href="/wiki/Discalced" title="Discalced">discalced</a>, required to go bare-footed—without even sandals. Like the Observants, the Capuchins wore a brown habit but of most simple form, i.e. only a <a href="/wiki/Tunic" title="Tunic">tunic</a>, with the distinctive large, pointed hood reaching to the waist attached to it, girdled by the traditional woolen <a href="/wiki/Rope" title="Rope">cord</a> with three knots. By visual analogy, the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_monkey" title="Capuchin monkey">Capuchin monkey</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Cappuccino" title="Cappuccino">cappuccino</a> style of coffee are both named after the shade of brown used for their habit.<sup id="cite_ref-Fragaszy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fragaszy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Besides the <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canonical</a> <a href="/wiki/Choir" title="Choir">choral</a> celebration of the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a>, a portion of which was recited at midnight, there were two hours of private prayer daily. The <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasts</a> and disciplines were rigorous and frequent. Their main external work was <a href="/wiki/Preaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Preaching">preaching</a> and spiritual ministrations among the poor. In <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> the Capuchins abandoned the later Franciscan School of <a href="/wiki/Scotism" title="Scotism">Scotus</a> and returned to the earlier school of <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">St. Bonaventure</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_setbacks">Early setbacks</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early setbacks">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the outset of its history, the Capuchins underwent a series of severe blows. Two of the founders left it: Matteo Serafini of Bascio (<a href="/wiki/Matteo_Bassi" title="Matteo Bassi">Matteo Bassi</a>) returning to the Observants, while his first companion, on being replaced in the office of Vicar Provincial, became so insubordinate that he had to be expelled from the Order. Even more scandalously, the third Vicar General, <a href="/wiki/Bernardino_Ochino" title="Bernardino Ochino">Bernardino Ochino</a>, left the Catholic faith in 1543 after fleeing to <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, where he was welcomed by <a href="/wiki/John_Calvin" title="John Calvin">John Calvin</a>, became a <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinist</a> <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> in <a href="/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich" title="Zürich">Zürich</a>, and married. Years later, claims that he had written in favor of <a href="/wiki/Polygamy" title="Polygamy">polygamy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Unitarianism" title="Unitarianism">Unitarianism</a> caused him to be exiled from that city and he fled again, first to Poland and then to Moravia, where he died.
</p><p>As a result, the whole province came under the suspicion of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical</a> tendencies and the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="Statement needs to be more specific about the content to which it refers. (March 2019)">specify</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> resolved to suppress it. He was dissuaded with difficulty, but the Capuchins were forbidden to preach.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Expansion">Expansion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Expansion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Despite earlier setbacks, the authorities were eventually satisfied as to the soundness of the general body of Capuchin friars and the permission to preach was restored. The movement then began to multiply rapidly, and by the end of the 16th century the Capuchins had spread all over the Catholic parts of Europe, so that in 1619 they were freed from their dependence on the Conventual Franciscans and became an independent Order. They are said to have had at that time 1500 houses divided into fifty provinces. They were one of the chief tools in the Catholic Counter-reformation, the aim of the order being to work among the poor, impressing the minds of the common people by the poverty and austerity of their life, and sometimes with sensationalist preaching such as their use of the supposedly possessed <a href="/wiki/Martha_Broissier" class="mw-redirect" title="Martha Broissier">Marthe Brossier</a> to arouse Paris against the <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The activities of the Capuchins were not confined to Europe. From an early date they undertook missions to non-Catholics in America, Asia and Africa, and a College was founded in Rome for the purpose of preparing their members for foreign missions. Due to this strong missionary thrust, a large number of Capuchins have suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> over the centuries. Activity in Europe and elsewhere continued until the close of the 18th century, when the number of Capuchin friars was estimated at 31,000.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cimitero_dei_Cappuccini:_The_Capuchin_Crypt">Cimitero dei Cappuccini: The Capuchin Crypt</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Cimitero dei Cappuccini: The Capuchin Crypt">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg/220px-Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg/330px-Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg/440px-Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2288" data-file-height="1712" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cripta_Cappuccini.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The remains of 4,000 <a href="/wiki/Friars" class="mw-redirect" title="Friars">friars</a> adorn the <a href="/wiki/Ossuary" title="Ossuary">ossuary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Concezione_dei_Cappuccini" title="Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini">Santa Maria della Concezione</a></div></div></div>
<p>The crypt is located just under the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Concezione_dei_Cappuccini" title="Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini">Santa Maria della Concezione</a> in Rome, a church commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> in 1626. The pope's brother, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Barberini" title="Antonio Barberini">Antonio Barberini</a>, who was of the Capuchin Order, in 1631 ordered the remains of thousands of Capuchin friars exhumed and transferred from the <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> on the Via dei Lucchesi to the crypt. The bones were arranged along the walls in varied designs, and the friars began to bury their own dead here, as well as the bodies of poor Romans whose tomb was under the floor of the present Mass chapel. Here the Capuchins would come to pray and reflect each evening before retiring for the night.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Ossuary" title="Ossuary">ossuary</a>, now contains the remains of 4,000 friars buried between 1500–1870, during which time the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> permitted burial in and under churches. The underground crypt is divided into five <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapels</a>, lit only by dim natural light seeping in through cracks, and small <a href="/wiki/Fluorescent_lamp" title="Fluorescent lamp">fluorescent lamps</a>. The crypt walls are decorated extensively with the remains, depicting various religious themes. Some of the skeletons are intact and draped with Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habits</a>, but for the most part, individual bones are used to create the elaborate ornamental designs.
</p><p>A <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/plaque" class="extiw" title="wikt:plaque">plaque</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> reads:
</p><p><i>What you are now, we used to be.</i><br /><i>What we are now, you will be.</i><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Mark_Twain" title="Mark Twain">Mark Twain</a> visited the crypt in the summer of 1867, and begins Volume 2, Chapter 1, of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Innocents_Abroad" title="The Innocents Abroad">The Innocents Abroad</a></i> with five pages of his observations.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_era">Modern era</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Modern era">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Confession(allg).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Confession%28allg%29.JPG/220px-Confession%28allg%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Confession%28allg%29.JPG/330px-Confession%28allg%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Confession%28allg%29.JPG/440px-Confession%28allg%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="742" data-file-height="1113" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Confession(allg).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A capuchin friar/priest ready to administer the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance" title="Sacrament of Penance">Sacrament of Reconciliation</a> (confession).</div></div></div>
<p>Like all other Orders, the Capuchins suffered severely from the <a href="/wiki/Secularization" title="Secularization">secularizations</a> and revolutions of the end of the 18th century and the first half of the 19th; but they survived the strain, and during the latter part of the 19th century rapidly recovered ground.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the beginning of the 20th century there were fifty provinces with some 500 friaries and 300 hospices or lesser houses; and the number of Capuchin friars, including <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brothers</a>, was reckoned at 9,500. The Capuchins still keep up their missionary work and have some 200 missionary stations in all parts of the world—notably <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ethiopia" title="Ethiopia">Ethiopia</a>, and parts of the former <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>. Though "the poorest of all Orders", it has attracted into its ranks an extraordinary number of the highest nobility and even of royalty. The celebrated <a href="/wiki/Theobald_Mathew_(temperance_reformer)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theobald Mathew (temperance reformer)">Theobald Mathew</a>, the apostle of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Temperance_movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Temperance movement">Temperance</a> in Ireland, was a Capuchin friar.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Crypt" title="Imperial Crypt">Imperial Crypt</a>, underneath the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Church,_Vienna" title="Capuchin Church, Vienna">Church of the Capuchins</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, over 140 members of the <a href="/wiki/Habsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Habsburg">Habsburg dynasty</a> are buried. The most recent burial in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> was in 2011 for <a href="/wiki/Otto_von_Habsburg" title="Otto von Habsburg">Otto von Habsburg</a>, the last <a href="/wiki/List_of_heirs_to_the_Austrian_throne" title="List of heirs to the Austrian throne">crown prince of Austria-Hungary</a> and eldest son of the last <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Austrian Emperor">Austrian Emperor</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <a href="/wiki/Karl_I_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Karl I of Austria">Charles of Austria</a>.
</p><p>As of June&#160;2018<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup>, there were 10,480 Capuchins worldwide, of whom 7,070 were <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priests</a>, living and working in 108 countries around the world: Africa: 1,357; South America: 1,657; North America: 664; Asia-Oceania: 2,339; Western Europe: 3,500; Central-Eastern Europe: 769.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain" title="Great Britain">Great Britain</a> there are currently five Capuchin friaries, and eight in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.
</p><p>The worldwide head of the Order, called the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General_(Franciscan)" title="Minister General (Franciscan)">Minister General</a>, is currently <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a> Roberto Genuin.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="India">India</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: India">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_and_colonialism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Church and colonialism">Catholic Church and colonialism</a></div>
<p>The Capuchin order, under the leadership of Italian Capuchin priest Giuseppe Maria Bernini, took part in the <a href="/wiki/Colonial_India" title="Colonial India">European colonization of India</a> as missionaries and founded the community of <a href="/wiki/Bettiah_Christians" title="Bettiah Christians">Bettiah Christians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-UCA2020_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UCA2020-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Bernini was invited by Maharaja Dhurup Singh of the <a href="/wiki/Bettiah_Raj" title="Bettiah Raj">Bettiah Raj</a>, an appointment that was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> on 1 May 1742.<sup id="cite_ref-UCA2020_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UCA2020-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States">United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG/220px-Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG/330px-Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG/440px-Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="1728" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Capuchinos_de_Paraguay.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Capuchin friars in <a href="/wiki/Paraguay" title="Paraguay">Paraguay</a>, wearing the traditional franciscan <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The United States has six provinces throughout the country. Together with the two provinces in Canada, the Province of Australia and the Custody of the Mariana Islands/Hawaii they form the North American-Pacific Capuchin Conference (NAPCC).
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation">Foundation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Foundation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The <b>Province of St. Joseph</b>, originally the Province of Calvary, headquartered in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit, Michigan</a>, was one of the first two Capuchin Provinces to be established in the country in 1882. It was founded by Francis Haas (1826–1895) and Bonaventure Frey (1831–1912), two <a href="/wiki/Swiss_people" title="Swiss people">Swiss</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocesan priest">diocesan priests</a> who arrived in the United States in September 1856, and were received into the then-<a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milwaukee" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milwaukee">Diocese of Milwaukee</a> by <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Henni" title="John Henni">John Henni</a>, also a Swiss immigrant, and given charge of St. Nicholas Parish which they renamed Mount Calvary. They were later admitted to the Capuchin Order on December 2, 1857, by Antoine Gauchet of the Swiss Province who had been sent to admit them in order to establish the Order in the United States.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The friars started <a href="/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seminary_High_School" title="St. Lawrence Seminary High School">St. Lawrence Seminary High School</a> in 1861 at <a href="/wiki/Mount_Calvary,_Wisconsin" title="Mount Calvary, Wisconsin">Mount Calvary, Wisconsin</a>, a school that is still owned and operated by the Capuchin Order.
</p><p>One of the friars of this province, <a href="/wiki/Solanus_Casey" title="Solanus Casey">Solanus Casey</a>, was noted for the holiness of his life, serving as the <a href="/wiki/Porter_(doorkeeper)" class="mw-redirect" title="Porter (doorkeeper)">porter</a> of several Capuchin friaries both in Michigan and <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> for decades. As a miraculous healing attributed to him was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> in mid-2017, he was beatified in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ford_Field" title="Ford Field">Ford Field</a> on November 18, 2017. This is significant because Casey could become the first male American-born Saint in the history of the Catholic Church. He had previously been declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 1995 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. His tomb is in <a href="/wiki/St._Bonaventure_Monastery" title="St. Bonaventure Monastery">St. Bonaventure Monastery</a> in Detroit, and is visited by thousands every year.
</p><p>As of 2011<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup>, the province has 23 communities spread throughout the <a href="/wiki/American_Midwest" class="mw-redirect" title="American Midwest">American Midwest</a>, reaching from <a href="/wiki/Michigan" title="Michigan">Michigan</a> to <a href="/wiki/Arizona" title="Arizona">Arizona</a>. Additionally, there are friars of this province working in <a href="/wiki/Central_America" title="Central America">Central America</a>, with a community serving in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-thecapuchins.org_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thecapuchins.org-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_jurisdictions">Other jurisdictions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Other jurisdictions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG/220px-St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG/330px-St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG/440px-St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Francis_Friary_DC.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Francis Friary – Capuchin College in Washington, D.C. (St. Augustine Province)</div></div></div>
<ul><li><b>St. Joseph</b> or <b>Calvary</b> (1882), based in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a>, <a href="/wiki/Michigan" title="Michigan">Michigan</a>, covering the upper Midwest, from Detroit to <a href="/wiki/Montana" title="Montana">Montana</a> and from which came Blessed <a href="/wiki/Solanus_Casey" title="Solanus Casey">Solanus Casey</a>.</li>
<li><b>St. Augustine</b> (1882), based in <a href="/wiki/Pittsburgh" title="Pittsburgh">Pittsburgh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pennsylvania" title="Pennsylvania">Pennsylvania</a>, to which Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Se%C3%A1n_Patrick_O%27Malley" title="Seán Patrick O&#39;Malley">Sean O'Malley</a> belongs.</li>
<li><b>Stigmata</b> (ca. 1925), based in <a href="/wiki/Union_City,_New_Jersey" title="Union City, New Jersey">Union City, New Jersey</a>, founded by friars from the <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscan</a> region of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> to minister to <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Immigrants" class="mw-redirect" title="Immigrants">immigrants</a>, with 9 communities on the <a href="/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States" title="East Coast of the United States">East Coast</a>, primarily in <a href="/wiki/New_Jersey" title="New Jersey">New Jersey</a>, with parishes also in <a href="/wiki/The_Bronx" title="The Bronx">the Bronx</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hendersonville,_North_Carolina" title="Hendersonville, North Carolina">Hendersonville, North Carolina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware" title="Wilmington, Delaware">Wilmington, Delaware</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida" title="Fort Lauderdale, Florida">Fort Lauderdale, Florida</a>.</li>
<li><b>St. Mary</b> of <a href="/wiki/New_York_(state)" title="New York (state)">New York</a> and <a href="/wiki/New_England" title="New England">New England</a> (1952), based in <a href="/wiki/White_Plains,_New_York" title="White Plains, New York">White Plains, New York</a>, with 18 fraternities on the East Coast, from <a href="/wiki/Vermont" title="Vermont">Vermont</a> to <a href="/wiki/Florida" title="Florida">Florida</a>. They supervise the Custody of Japan and the Custody of Guam.</li>
<li><b>St. Conrad</b> or Mid-America (1977), based in <a href="/wiki/Denver" title="Denver">Denver</a>, <a href="/wiki/Colorado" title="Colorado">Colorado</a>, serving <a href="/wiki/Illinois" title="Illinois">Illinois</a>, Colorado, <a href="/wiki/Kansas" title="Kansas">Kansas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Missouri" title="Missouri">Missouri</a> and Texas, with missionaries in <a href="/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea" title="Papua New Guinea">Papua New Guinea</a>; a friar of this province, <a href="/wiki/Charles_J._Chaput" title="Charles J. Chaput">Charles J. Chaput</a>, was installed as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Philadelphia" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Philadelphia">Archbishop of Philadelphia</a> on 8 September 2011.<sup id="cite_ref-capuchins.org_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-capuchins.org-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><b>Our Lady of Angels</b> (1979), Western America, based in <a href="/wiki/Burlingame,_California" title="Burlingame, California">Burlingame, California</a>. Seven communities in California, with four communities in <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a> which became the Custody of St. <a href="/wiki/Juan_Diego" title="Juan Diego">Juan Diego</a> in December 2011.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><b>Star of the Sea Custody</b> (1982),  <a href="/wiki/Hawaii" title="Hawaii">Hawaii</a> and <a href="/wiki/Guam" title="Guam">Guam</a>, this division is dependent on St. Mary Province.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Capuchin_Poor_Clares">Capuchin Poor Clares</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Capuchin Poor Clares">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Poor_Clares" title="Capuchin Poor Clares">Capuchin Poor Clares</a> are <a href="/wiki/Cloistered" class="mw-redirect" title="Cloistered">cloistered</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Clare">Order of St. Clare</a>, who form the female branch of the Capuchin Order. They were founded in 1538 in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> <a href="/wiki/Maria_Laurentia_Longo" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Laurentia Longo">Maria Laurentia Longo</a>, who was <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">Abbess</a> of the Poor Clare monastery of that city. She and the other nuns of that community embraced the then-new Capuchin reform movement, and so austere was the life that they were called "Sisters of Suffering". The Order soon spread to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and beyond. They live according to the same rules and regulations as the Capuchin friars, and are held as members of the friars' provinces.
</p><p>In the United States, as of 2012, there are five monasteries of this Order. There are about 50 nuns in these communities, which are located in: <a href="/wiki/Denver" title="Denver">Denver</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pueblo,_Colorado" title="Pueblo, Colorado">Pueblo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Colorado" title="Colorado">Colorado</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alamo,_Texas" title="Alamo, Texas">Alamo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Amarillo,_Texas" title="Amarillo, Texas">Amarillo</a> (the first, founded 1981) in <a href="/wiki/Texas" title="Texas">Texas</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware" title="Wilmington, Delaware">Wilmington, Delaware</a>. The monasteries were almost all founded from <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>, where there are some 1,350 Capuchin nuns in 73 monasteries. The monastery in Pueblo is a foundation of the monastery in Amarillo. Together they form the <b>Federation of Our Lady of the Angels</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Appearance">Appearance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Appearance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Capuchins are unique for a Catholic religious order in that the growing of natural, untrimmed <a href="/wiki/Beards" class="mw-redirect" title="Beards">beards</a> features as part of its first Constitution, which states as the reason, the beard is "manly, austere, natural, an imitation of Christ and the saints of our Order, and despised." This makes the Capuchin friars stand out in particular from the <a href="/wiki/Secular_clergy" title="Secular clergy">secular clergy</a> of the Latin Church, who have no rule on such matters. In more recent times, since the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, the beard has no longer been mandatory but is still common. Like other <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>, the friars wear a plain brown tunic with a hood, a cord fastened around the waist, and sandals (or shoes).
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>There does not appear to be any modern general history of the Capuchin order as a whole, though there are histories of various provinces and of the foreign missions. The references to this literature can be found in the article "Kapuzinerorden" in <i>Wetzer und Welte, Kirchenlexicon</i> (2nd ed.), a general sketch on the subject.</li>
<li>Shorter sketches, with the needful references, are given in Max Heimbucher, <i>Orden und Kongregationen</i> (1896), i. §4 and in Herzog-Hauck, <i>Realencyklopedie</i> (3rd ed.), art. "Kapuziner."</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pierre_Helyot" class="mw-redirect" title="Pierre Helyot">Helyot</a>'s <i>Hist. des ordres religieux</i> (1792), vii. c. 24 and c. 27, gives an account of the Capuchins up to the end of the 17th century.</li></ul>
