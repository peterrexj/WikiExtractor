<p><b>Saint John of Shanghai and San Francisco</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Иоанн Шанхайский и Сан Францисский</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Russian-language romanization"><i lang="ru-Latn">Ioann Shankhayskiyi i San Frantsiskyi</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Secular_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Secular name">secular name</a> <b>Mikhail Borisovich Maximovitch</b>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Михаил Борисович Максимович</span>; 4 June 1896 – 2 July 1966) was a prominent <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hierarch" class="mw-redirect" title="Hierarch">hierarch</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> (ROCOR) who was active in the mid-20th century. He was a pastor and spiritual father of high reputation and a reputed <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">wonderworker</a> to whom were attributed powers of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Clairvoyance" title="Clairvoyance">clairvoyance</a> and healing. He is often referred to as "<b>St. John the Wonderworker</b>".
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Mikhail Maximovitch was born in 1896 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Sloviansk_Raion" title="Sloviansk Raion">Adamovka</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Izyumsky_Uyezd" title="Izyumsky Uyezd">Izyumsky Uyezd</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate" title="Kharkov Governorate">Kharkov Governorate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a> (in present-day eastern <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>).
</p><p>Growing up Mikhail was a sickly child who was deeply devout and used to collect icons and church books. He was captivated by the lives of saints; even in play he would pretend toy soldiers were monks and fortresses were  monasteries. His piety  impressed his French caretaker so much that she converted from Catholicism to Orthodox Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maximovitch attended Poltava Military School from 1907 to 1914. Later he attended and received a degree in law from <a href="/wiki/Kharkiv_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Kharkiv University">Kharkov Imperial University</a> in 1918. He studied well and attended church in <a href="/wiki/Kharkiv" title="Kharkiv">Kharkiv</a>, where he was inspired by <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Antony_(Khrapovitsky)" title="Antony (Khrapovitsky)">Antony Khrapovitsky</a> to go deeper into his spiritual learnings.
</p><p>He later recalled that the local monastery had become more important in his life than the secular institutions. 
Maximovitch was a patriot of his fatherland and was profoundly disappointed by what he saw as human weakness and impermanence during the tragic events of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution" title="Russian Revolution">1917 revolution</a>. As a result, he made the decision to dedicate his life to serving God.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Yugoslavia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His family sought refuge in <a href="/wiki/Yugoslavia" title="Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</a> and brought him to <a href="/wiki/Belgrade" title="Belgrade">Belgrade</a> in 1921. In 1925 he graduated from <a href="/wiki/Belgrade_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Belgrade University">Belgrade University</a> with a degree in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathedral_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathedral-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> To support his impoverished family, he would sell newspapers.
</p><p>In 1926 he was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> a monk and ordained a <a href="/wiki/Hierodeacon" title="Hierodeacon">hierodeacon</a> by Russian <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Anthony_(Khrapovitsky)" class="mw-redirect" title="Anthony (Khrapovitsky)">Anthony (Khrapovitsky)</a>, who gave him the name of John after his saintly relative, <a href="/wiki/John_of_Tobolsk" title="John of Tobolsk">Saint John of Tobolsk</a>. 
</p><p>Later that same year, he was ordained to the priesthood by Russian Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gabriel_(Chepur)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gabriel (Chepur) (page does not exist)">Gabriel (Chepur)</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chelyabinsk" title="Chelyabinsk">Chelyabinsk</a>. Once ordained St. John would no longer sleep in a bed. He would nap in a chair or kneeling down in front of the icons, praying. He ate only once a day. For several years  he worked as an instructor and tutor in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia" title="Kingdom of Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</a>. He worked as a <a href="/wiki/Religious_education" title="Religious education">religious teacher</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gymnasium_(school)" title="Gymnasium (school)">Gymnasium</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kikinda" title="Kikinda">Velika Kikinda</a> between 1925 and 1927.<sup id="cite_ref-Le_Karo_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Le_Karo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
In 1929, the Holy Synod of the <a href="/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church" title="Serbian Orthodox Church">Serbian Orthodox Church</a> appointed him a teacher of the seminary in <a href="/wiki/Bitola" title="Bitola">Bitola</a> under principal <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">Nikolaj Velimirović</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-RTV_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RTV-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
John earned the respect and devotion at the seminary where he taught. His reputation grew as he started visiting hospitals, caring for patients with prayer and communion.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Shanghai">Shanghai</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Shanghai">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1934 he was ordained a bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> by Metropolitan Anthony and assigned to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> of <a href="/wiki/Shanghai" title="Shanghai">Shanghai</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RTV_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RTV-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
In Shanghai, bishop John found an <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas%27_Church,_Shanghai" title="Saint Nicholas&#39; Church, Shanghai">uncompleted cathedral</a> and an <a href="/wiki/Shanghai_Russians" title="Shanghai Russians">Orthodox community</a> deeply divided along ethnic lines. Making contact with all the various groups, he quickly became involved in local charitable institutions and also founded an orphanage and home for the children of the destitute.
</p><p>He set about restoring church unity, establishing ties with local Orthodox Serbs and Greeks. Here he first became known for miracles attributed to his prayer.
</p><p>As a public figure, it was impossible for him to completely conceal his ascetic way of life. During the <a href="/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War" title="Second Sino-Japanese War">Japanese occupation</a>, even when he routinely ignored the curfew in pursuit of his pastoral activities, the Japanese authorities never harassed him.
</p><p>As the only Russian hierarch in China who refused to submit to the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a>-dominated <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> under <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Alexy_I_of_Moscow" title="Patriarch Alexy I of Moscow">Patriarch Alexy I of Moscow</a>, he was elevated in 1946 to Archbishop of China by the Holy Synod of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathedral_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathedral-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the <a href="/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_People%27s_Republic_of_China" title="Proclamation of the People&#39;s Republic of China">Communists took power in China</a> in 1949, the Russian colony was forced to flee, first to a <a href="/wiki/Russian_settlement_in_the_Philippines" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian settlement in the Philippines">refugee camp</a> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Tubabao" title="Tubabao">Tubabao</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> and then mainly to the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> and <a href="/wiki/Australia" title="Australia">Australia</a>. Archbishop St. John personally traveled to <a href="/wiki/Washington,_D.C." title="Washington, D.C.">Washington, D.C.</a>, to ensure that his people would be allowed to enter the US.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Western_Europe">Western Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Western Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1951, St. John was assigned to the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Russian_Orthodox_churches_in_Western_Europe" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Russian Orthodox churches in Western Europe">Archdiocese of Western Europe</a> with his see first in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, then in <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>.
</p><p>Thanks to his work in compiling the lives of saints, numerous pre-<a href="/wiki/East-West_Schism" class="mw-redirect" title="East-West Schism">Schism</a> Western saints became known in Orthodoxy and continue to be venerated to this day. His charitable and pastoral work continued as it had in Shanghai, even among a much more widely scattered flock.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="San_Francisco">San Francisco</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: San Francisco">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1962 St. John was reassigned by the Holy Synod to the see of <a href="/wiki/San_Francisco" title="San Francisco">San Francisco</a>. Here too, he found a divided community and a cathedral in an unfinished state. Although he completed the building of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Virgin_Cathedral" title="Holy Virgin Cathedral">Holy Virgin Cathedral</a> and brought some measure of peace to the community, he became the target of slander from political enemies. They filed a lawsuit against him for alleged mishandling of finances related to the construction of the cathedral. He was exonerated, but this was a great cause of sorrow to him in his later life.
</p><p>Deeply reverent of <a href="/wiki/John_of_Kronstadt" title="John of Kronstadt">St. John of Kronstadt</a>, John played an active role in the preparation of his canonization in 1964.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On July 2, 1966 (June 19 on the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>), St. John died while visiting <a href="/wiki/Seattle" title="Seattle">Seattle</a>, at a time and place he was said to have foretold.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He was entombed in a sepulcher beneath the altar of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Virgin_Cathedral" title="Holy Virgin Cathedral">Holy Virgin Cathedral</a> he had built in San Francisco. It is dedicated to the <i>Theotokos</i>, Joy of All Who Sorrow, and located on <a href="/wiki/Geary_Boulevard" title="Geary Boulevard">Geary Boulevard</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Richmond_District_(San_Francisco)" class="mw-redirect" title="Richmond District (San Francisco)">Richmond district</a>.
</p><p>In 1994, he was solemnly <a href="/wiki/Glorification" title="Glorification">glorified</a> on the 28th anniversary of his death. His unembalmed, <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a> relics occupy a shrine in the cathedral's <a href="/wiki/Nave" title="Nave">nave</a>. His feast day is celebrated on the Saturday nearest to July 2.
</p><p>He is beloved and celebrated worldwide, with portions of his relics located in <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> (Church of Saint Anna in Katerini), <a href="/wiki/South_Korea" title="South Korea">South Korea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a>, United States (St. John Maximovitch Church, Eugene, Oregon and St. Innocent Orthodox Church, Redford, Michigan), Canada (Holy Trinity Serbian Orthodox Church, Kitchener), England (Dormition Cathedral of the Russian Orthodox Church, London) and other countries of the world.
</p>
