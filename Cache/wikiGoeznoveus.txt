<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Goeznovius</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Unknown<br /><a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 675?<br /><a href="/wiki/Quimper,_Finist%C3%A8re" class="mw-redirect" title="Quimper, Finistère">Quimper, Finistère</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">25 October</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Goeznovius</b> (died c. 675 according to one account but more probably fl. sixth century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), also known as <b>Goueznou</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornish</a>-born <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Quimper-et-L%C3%A9on" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Quimper-et-Léon">Bishop of Léon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, who is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the region around <a href="/wiki/Brest,_France" title="Brest, France">Brest</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_L%C3%A9on" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Léon">diocese of Léon</a>. According to his <i>Legenda</i> he was born in Cornwall and became one of many of his countrymen who moved to the continent in the wake of the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_invasion_of_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon invasion of Britain">Anglo-Saxon invasions</a> and helped found the <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historical)">Brittonic</a> settlement in <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorica</a> that became established as Brittany. His feast day is celebrated on 25 October.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legenda_Sancti_Goeznovii"><i>Legenda Sancti Goeznovii</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Goeznovius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legenda Sancti Goeznovii">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Goeznovius is known through his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, the <i>Legenda Sancti Goeznovii</i>. The author names himself as William, a <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> chaplain in the <i><a href="/wiki/Family" title="Family">familia</a></i> of the otherwise-unattested Bishop Eudo of Léon, and gives a date of 1019 for the work, though it is now dated to the late 12th or early 13th century AD.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Legenda</i> includes an unusual preface detailing the history of Brittany and including some episodes from <a href="/wiki/Matter_of_Britain" title="Matter of Britain">Britain's traditional history</a>.
</p><p>The preface describes the traditional story of <a href="/wiki/Vortigern" title="Vortigern">Vortigern</a>, who usurps the British throne and invites Saxon warriors into the country as protection. The Saxons caused great suffering among the Britons, until they were largely driven out by the new king, Arthur. Arthur proceeded to win battles in Britain and in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> but was eventually "summoned… from human activity," paving the way for the Saxons to return. The Saxon persecution caused many of the Britons to flee to Gaul, where they established Brittany.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/John_Strong_Perry_Tatlock" class="mw-redirect" title="John Strong Perry Tatlock">J. S. P. Tatlock</a> set out signs that the preface to William's <i>Legenda</i> followed the outlines of <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a>'s <i>Historia Regum Britanniae</i>, written around 1136, and therefore that the date 1019 was a fiction<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> but this conclusion has been challenged by <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Fleuriot" title="Léon Fleuriot">Léon Fleuriot</a>: the preface includes material that is found in early sources but not in Geoffrey, suggesting that the author had access to some earlier document. More recently André-Yves Bourgès has demonstrated that the author of the Life is most likely Guillaume le Breton (c. 1166-1226 AD).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For those who consider it independent of Geoffrey the <i>Legende</i>, as an early historical account that depicts <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">Arthur</a> without fantastic or legendary touches, is an important <a href="/wiki/Historical_basis_for_King_Arthur" class="mw-redirect" title="Historical basis for King Arthur">historical basis for King Arthur</a>. The text may imply that Arthur succeeds Vortigern directly whereas Geoffrey claims two kings reigned between them (<a href="/wiki/Ambrosius_Aurelianus" title="Ambrosius Aurelianus">Aurelius Ambrosius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Uther_Pendragon" title="Uther Pendragon">Uther Pendragon</a>). If so it places his activities during the period of Saxon unrest in the mid-5th century.
</p>
