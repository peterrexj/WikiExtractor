<p><b>William Harrington</b> (1566 &#8211; executed 18 February 1594) was an English <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest. He is a Roman Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Harrington_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His father had entertained <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> at the ancestral home, Mount St. John, early in 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Young William, inspired by Campion, went abroad to train as a priest. He was first at the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, then went to study under the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a> (1582–1584). He would have joined the order, but his health broke down and forced him to keep at home for the next six years.
</p><p>In February 1591, however, he was able to return once more to Reims, and, having been ordained, returned at midsummer 1592. The following May he fell into the hands of the English authorities, whereupon he was arrested and confined to the dungeons for several months.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  
Harrington was sentenced to be <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> for the crime of being a Catholic priest. He was given the chance to spare his life if he renounced the Catholic faith and were to attend Protestant services just once. William refused.  He was tortured on the rack, <a href="/wiki/Hanging" title="Hanging">hanged</a> until not quite dead, then disemboweled, before being beheaded.
</p><p>William's fate had an important literary side-effect. One of those who had sheltered him was Henry Donne, the brother of the poet <a href="/wiki/John_Donne" title="John Donne">John Donne</a>. Henry was arrested, and died of the plague in <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a>. John Donne was a Catholic too, but later embraced the Protestant Church of England, in an effort to spare his own life.<sup id="cite_ref-Schama_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Schama-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William Harrington was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929 by Pope Pius XI. His Feast Day is 18 February. He is also venerated on 22 November as one of the Martyrs of England, Scotland and Wales and on 29 October as one of the Martyrs of Douai.
</p>
