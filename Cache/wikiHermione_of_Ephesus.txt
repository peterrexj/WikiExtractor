<div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Christian martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Hermione of Ephesus</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><span class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Frameless"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg/220px-Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="149" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg/330px-Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg/440px-Menologion_of_Basil_011.jpg 2x" data-file-width="920" data-file-height="625" /></a></span><div class="infobox-caption">Beheading of St.&#160;Hermione. <p><b>Hermione of Ephesus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ερμιόνη της Εφέσου</span>; <abbr title="died">d.</abbr> <abbr title="anno Domini">A.D.</abbr> 117) is a 2nd-century <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> Churches. She was well known as a "great healer"<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 16">&#58;&#8202;16&#8202;</span></sup> and founded the first Christian hospital in Ephesus.<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 16">&#58;&#8202;16&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Hermione was born in <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Cæsarea</a> and was one of the four daughters of <a href="/wiki/Philip_the_Evangelist" title="Philip the Evangelist">Saint Philip the Evangelist</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Deacons" title="Seven Deacons">seven deacons</a> as described in chapter 6 in the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> chosen by the early <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">Christian church</a> to minister to the community of believers in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. Her name does not appear in the Bible, but she and her sisters are described as <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virgins</a> and "gifted with prophecy."<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 16">&#58;&#8202;16&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WJL_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJL-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Hermione also appears in the <a href="/wiki/Menaion" title="Menaion">Menaion</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_book" title="Liturgical book">liturgical book</a> used by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-IM_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 45">&#58;&#8202;45&#8202;</span></sup> She is often confused with a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Philip_the_Apostle" title="Philip the Apostle">St. Philip the Apostle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SBG_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SBG-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ABCD_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABCD-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to tradition, around the early 100s, after studying medicine, Hermione travelled with her sister Eukhilda to <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>, through <a href="/wiki/Anatolia" title="Anatolia">Anatolia</a>, to meet <a href="/wiki/John_of_Patmos" title="John of Patmos">St. John the Theologian</a> in the hopes that they could help him in his evangelization efforts. They found that he had already died, but met Petronius, a disciple of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Saint Paul</a>, and followed him instead. Hermione became well known for her healing and built a hospital in Ephesus. Soon, her reputation as a doctor and as a devout Christian attracted the attention of the Roman emperor <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a> who stopped in Ephesus on his way to a war with the Persians in 114 to convince her to renounce Christ. When she refused, he ordered that she be struck in the face for several hours, which she was able to withstand because she was "comforted by a vision of the Lord, in the form of Petronius, sitting upon the throne of judgment."<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Trajan freed her when he saw that she would not recant her faith and that she bore the torture with "patience and courage,"<sup id="cite_ref-ABCD_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABCD-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and after she prophesied that he would defeat the Persians and that his son-in-law <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a> would succeed him.<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 16">&#58;&#8202;16&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WJL_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJL-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-IM_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 45">&#58;&#8202;45&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Hadrian also tried to force Hermione to renounce Christ, by having her scourged and her feet pierced with wire<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-IM_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 45">&#58;&#8202;45&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ABCD_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABCD-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but "she endured these trials without complaint."<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup> Hadrian also ordered that Hermione be thrown into a cauldron full of burning tar; she made the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">Sign of the Cross</a> before entering and was unharmed. The fire was extinguished after she entered the cauldron and she "seemed to be standing in dew."<sup id="cite_ref-WJL_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJL-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Hadrian touched the cauldron himself, but his nails fell off and he was badly burned,<sup id="cite_ref-WJL_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJL-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> so he ordered his troops to "torture her without mercy;"<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup> they beat her and cut her feet with nails. They tried to fry her to death in an enormous pan, but a moment after she was tossed naked onto the pan, the fire under the pan exploded, burning several onlookers. After these miracles, Hermione pretended to want to sacrifice to idols and was taken to a pagan temple.<sup id="cite_ref-WJL_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WJL-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Khoury reported that she prayed and "caused the jeweled idols in Hadrian’s pagan temple to go crashing down into ruins,"<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup> so he had her beheaded. Before she died, her two executioners, named Theotimus and Theodulos, were briefly paralysed. They knelt at her feet and were converted to Christianity "on the spot."<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup> She healed them and promised that they would go to heaven, and they died soon afterwards.<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 17">&#58;&#8202;17&#8202;</span></sup> According to Khoury, Hermione "owns a special place among the <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> saints<span style="white-space: nowrap;">&#8201;—&#8201;</span>the place reserved for valiant women of unbreakable faith in Jesus Christ."<sup id="cite_ref-DK_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 16">&#58;&#8202;16&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Hermione might have been buried at the eastern slopes of Pion Hill in Ephesus, along with an impressive list of other saints, including <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Saint Timothy</a>, her father Saint Philip, <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Thebes" title="Paul of Thebes">Paul of Thebes</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aristobulus_of_Britannia" title="Aristobulus of Britannia">Aristobulus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Felix_and_Adauctus" title="Felix and Adauctus">Adauctus</a>, and Adauctus’ daughter, Callisthena, although "there is no archeological evidence to support even one sacred burial from the list."<sup id="cite_ref-IM_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 243">&#58;&#8202;243&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>Hermione's feast day is 4 September<sup id="cite_ref-ABCD_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABCD-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-OCA_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> except in <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">the Episcopal Church</a>, where she is honored on 14 April with <a href="/wiki/Zenaida_and_Philonella" title="Zenaida and Philonella">Zenaida and Philonella</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
