<p><b>George Grenville</b> (14 October 1712 – 13 November 1770) was a <a href="/wiki/British_Whig_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig Party">British Whig</a> statesman who rose to the position of <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of Great Britain</a>. Grenville was born into an influential political family and first entered Parliament in 1741 as an MP for <a href="/wiki/Buckingham_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Buckingham (UK Parliament constituency)">Buckingham</a>. He emerged as one of <a href="/wiki/Cobham%27s_Cubs" class="mw-redirect" title="Cobham&#39;s Cubs">Cobham's Cubs</a>, a group of young members of Parliament associated with <a href="/wiki/Richard_Temple,_1st_Viscount_Cobham" title="Richard Temple, 1st Viscount Cobham">Lord Cobham</a>.
</p><p>In 1754, Grenville became <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_Navy" title="Treasurer of the Navy">Treasurer of the Navy</a>, a position he held twice until 1761. In October 1761 he chose to stay in government and accepted the new role of <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons" title="Leader of the House of Commons">Leader of the Commons</a> causing a rift with his brother-in-law and political ally <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt</a> who had resigned. Grenville was subsequently made <a href="/wiki/Northern_Secretary" class="mw-redirect" title="Northern Secretary">Northern Secretary</a> and <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty" title="First Lord of the Admiralty">First Lord of the Admiralty</a> by the new Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute" title="John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute">Lord Bute</a>. On 8 April 1763, Lord Bute resigned, and Grenville assumed his position as Prime Minister.<sup id="cite_ref-Public_Opinion:_John_Wilkes_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Public_Opinion:_John_Wilkes-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His government tried to bring public spending under control and pursued an assertive foreign policy. His best-known policy is the <a href="/wiki/Stamp_Act_1765" title="Stamp Act 1765">Stamp Act</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stamp_act#England_and_United_Kingdom_Stamp_Acts" title="Stamp act">a long-standing tax in Great Britain</a> which Grenville extended to the colonies in America, but which instigated widespread opposition in <a href="/wiki/British_America" title="British America">Britain's American colonies</a> and was later <a href="/wiki/Repealed" class="mw-redirect" title="Repealed">repealed</a>. Grenville had increasingly strained relations with his colleagues and the King. In 1765, he was dismissed by <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">George III</a> and replaced by <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham</a>. For the last five years of his life, Grenville led a group of his supporters in opposition and staged a public reconciliation with Pitt.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life:_1712-1741">Early life: 1712-1741</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life: 1712-1741">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Grenville_arms.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Grenville_arms.svg/220px-Grenville_arms.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="242" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Grenville_arms.svg/330px-Grenville_arms.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Grenville_arms.svg/440px-Grenville_arms.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="563" /></a><figcaption>Coat of arms of Grenville of <a href="/wiki/Wotton_Underwood" title="Wotton Underwood">Wotton Underwood</a>, <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a>: <i>Vert, on a cross argent five torteaux</i></figcaption></figure>
<p>George Grenville was born at <a href="/wiki/Wotton_House" title="Wotton House">Wotton House</a> on 14 October 1712. He was the second son of <a href="/wiki/Richard_Grenville_(1678%E2%80%931727)" title="Richard Grenville (1678–1727)">Richard Grenville</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hester_Grenville,_1st_Countess_Temple" title="Hester Grenville, 1st Countess Temple">Hester Temple</a> (later the 1st <a href="/wiki/Hester_Grenville,_1st_Countess_Temple" title="Hester Grenville, 1st Countess Temple">Countess Temple</a>). He was one of five brothers, all of whom became MPs. His sister <a href="/wiki/Hester_Pitt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hester Pitt">Hester Grenville</a> married the leading political figure <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt</a>. His elder brother was <a href="/wiki/Richard_Grenville-Temple,_2nd_Earl_Temple" title="Richard Grenville-Temple, 2nd Earl Temple">Richard Grenville</a>, later the 2nd Earl Temple. It was intended by his parents that George Grenville should become a <a href="/wiki/Lawyer" title="Lawyer">lawyer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Grenville was educated at <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a> and at <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>, but did not graduate.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career:_1741-1756">Early political career: 1741-1756</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early political career: 1741-1756">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Member_of_Parliament">Member of Parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Member of Parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cobhamites" title="Cobhamites">Cobhamites</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg/220px-Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="145" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg/330px-Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg/440px-Stowe_North_front_in_1750_by_George_Bickham.jpg 2x" data-file-width="720" data-file-height="474" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Stowe_House" title="Stowe House">Stowe House</a> in <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a>, the political base of the <a href="/wiki/Cobham%27s_Cubs" class="mw-redirect" title="Cobham&#39;s Cubs">Cobham faction</a>; in 1749, ownership of the estate passed to Grenville's brother <a href="/wiki/Richard_Grenville-Temple,_2nd_Earl_Temple" title="Richard Grenville-Temple, 2nd Earl Temple">Richard</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>He entered <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain" title="Parliament of Great Britain">Parliament</a> in 1741 as one of the two members for <a href="/wiki/Buckingham_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Buckingham (UK Parliament constituency)">Buckingham</a>, and continued to represent that borough for the next twenty-nine years until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was disappointed to be giving up what appeared to be a promising legal career for the uncertainties of <a href="/wiki/Opposition_(politics)" title="Opposition (politics)">opposition politics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Parliament, he subscribed to the "Boy Patriot" party, which opposed Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In particular he enjoyed the patronage of <a href="/wiki/Richard_Temple,_1st_Viscount_Cobham" title="Richard Temple, 1st Viscount Cobham">Lord Cobham</a>, the leader of a faction that included George Grenville, his brother Richard, <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt</a> and <a href="/wiki/George_Lyttelton,_1st_Baron_Lyttelton" title="George Lyttelton, 1st Baron Lyttelton">George Lyttelton</a> that became known as <a href="/wiki/Cobham%27s_Cubs" class="mw-redirect" title="Cobham&#39;s Cubs">Cobham's Cubs</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Administration">Administration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Administration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In December 1744 he became a Lord of the <a href="/wiki/British_Admiralty" class="mw-redirect" title="British Admiralty">Admiralty</a> in the administration of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Pelham" title="Henry Pelham">Henry Pelham</a>. He allied himself with his brother Richard and with <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt</a> (who became their brother-in-law in 1754) in forcing Pelham to give them promotion by rebelling against his authority and obstructing business. In June 1747, Grenville became a <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_Treasury" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of the Treasury">Lord of the Treasury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1754 Grenville was made <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_Navy" title="Treasurer of the Navy">Treasurer of the Navy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of Great Britain">Privy Councillor</a>. Along with Pitt and several other colleagues he was dismissed in 1755 after speaking and voting against the government on a debate about a recent <a href="/wiki/Golden_Cavalry_of_St_George" title="Golden Cavalry of St George">subsidy treaty</a> with <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russia</a> which they believed was unnecessarily costly, and would drag Britain into <a href="/wiki/Continental_European" class="mw-redirect" title="Continental European">Continental European</a> disputes. Opposition to European entanglements was a <a href="/wiki/Cornerstone" title="Cornerstone">cornerstone</a> of Patriot Whig thinking.
</p><p>He and Pitt joined the opposition, haranguing the Newcastle government. Grenville and Pitt both championed the formation of a British <a href="/wiki/Militia" title="Militia">militia</a> to provide additional security rather than the deployment of <a href="/wiki/Hessian_mercenaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Hessian mercenaries">Hessian mercenaries</a> favoured by the government.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> As the military situation deteriorated following the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_Minorca" class="mw-redirect" title="Fall of Minorca">loss of Minorca</a>, the government grew increasingly weak until it was forced to resign in Autumn 1756.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Government:_1756-1763">In Government: 1756-1763</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In Government: 1756-1763">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years War">Great Britain in the Seven Years War</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Treasurer_of_the_Navy">Treasurer of the Navy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Treasurer of the Navy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pitt then formed a government led by the <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish,_4th_Duke_of_Devonshire" title="William Cavendish, 4th Duke of Devonshire">Duke of Devonshire</a>. Grenville was returned to his position as Treasurer of the Navy, which was a great disappointment as he had been expecting to receive the more prestigious and lucrative post of <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This added to what Grenville regarded as a series of earlier slights in which Pitt and others had passed him over for positions in favour of men he considered no more talented than he was. From then on Grenville felt a growing resentment towards Pitt, and grew closer to the young <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of Great Britain">Prince of Wales</a> and his advisor <a href="/wiki/Lord_Bute" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Bute">Lord Bute</a> who were both now opposed to Pitt.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1758, as Treasurer of the Navy, he introduced and carried a bill which established a fairer system of paying the wages of seamen and supporting their families while they were at sea which was praised for its humanity if not for its effectiveness.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He remained in office during the years of British victories, notably the <a href="/wiki/Annus_Mirabilis_of_1759" class="mw-redirect" title="Annus Mirabilis of 1759">Annus Mirabilis of 1759</a> for which the credit went to the government of which he was a member. However his seven-year-old son died after a long illness and Grenville remained by his side at their country house in Wotton and rarely came to London.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1761, when Pitt resigned upon the question of the war with Spain, and subsequently functioned as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons" title="Leader of the House of Commons">Leader of the House of Commons</a> in the administration of <a href="/wiki/John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute" title="John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute">Lord Bute</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Grenville's role was seen as an attempt to keep someone closely associated with Pitt involved in the government, in order to prevent Pitt and his supporters actively opposing the government. However, it soon led to conflict between Grenville and Pitt. Grenville was also seen as a suitable candidate because his reputation for honesty meant he commanded loyalty and respect amongst <a href="/wiki/Independent_(politician)" class="mw-redirect" title="Independent (politician)">independent</a> MPs.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Northern_Secretary">Northern Secretary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Northern Secretary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:JohnStuartBute.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/JohnStuartBute.jpg/220px-JohnStuartBute.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ce/JohnStuartBute.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="274" data-file-height="274" /></a><figcaption>Grenville served under <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">British Prime Minister</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute" title="John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute">Lord Bute</a> between 1762 and 1763 and later succeeded him.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In May 1762, Grenville was appointed <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Northern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Northern Department">Northern Secretary</a>, where he took an increasingly hard line in the negotiations with France and Spain designed to bring the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a> to a close.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Grenville demanded much greater compensation in exchange for the return of British conquests, while Bute favored a more generous position which eventually formed the basis of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)" title="Treaty of Paris (1763)">Treaty of Paris</a>. In spite of this, Grenville had now become associated with Bute rather than his former political allies who were even more vocal in their opposition to the peace treaty than he was. In October he was made <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty" title="First Lord of the Admiralty">First Lord of the Admiralty</a>. <a href="/wiki/Henry_Fox,_1st_Baron_Holland" title="Henry Fox, 1st Baron Holland">Henry Fox</a> took over as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons" title="Leader of the House of Commons">Leader of the Commons</a>, and forced the peace treaty through parliament.
</p><p>Bute's position grew increasingly untenable and stable as he was extremely unpopular, which led to him offering his resignation to George III on several occasions. Bute was the target of the radical <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a>’s criticism and satire and he ridiculed in Wilkes’ newspaper the <i><a href="/wiki/The_North_Briton" title="The North Briton">The North Briton</a></i>, stereotypical reference to the prime minister’s Scottish heritage.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister:_1763-1765">Prime Minister: 1763-1765 <span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Prime Minister: 1763-1765">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Grenville_ministry" title="Grenville ministry">Grenville ministry</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment">Appointment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Appointment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bute's intention to resign was genuine, and the hostile press attacks and the continuing unpopularity of his government finally led to <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">King George III</a> to reluctantly accept his resignation. Though the king was unsure whom should be appoint to the position and Bute highly recommended that the King should appoint Grenville as the new prime minister. Despite the King’s disapproval and distrust of his ministers, he considered appointed Grenville to office.
</p><p>When Grenville was asked about becoming the new prime minister, he agreed only on the condition that Bute would not take an active part in politics and be barred from voicing policies for the government. The King agreed and thus appointed Grenville as new Prime Minister. George III assured Grenville that 'he meant to put his government solely into his hand’. Grenville set to work and formed his government on 16 April 1763. He appointed two of his trusted allies <a href="/wiki/George_Montagu-Dunk,_2nd_Earl_of_Halifax" title="George Montagu-Dunk, 2nd Earl of Halifax">Lord Halifax</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_Wyndham,_2nd_Earl_of_Egremont" title="Charles Wyndham, 2nd Earl of Egremont">Charles Wyndham, 2nd Earl of Egremont</a> as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Northern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Northern Department">Northern Secretary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Southern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Southern Department">Southern Secretary</a> respectively. He also appointed the <a href="/wiki/Robert_Henley,_1st_Earl_of_Northington" title="Robert Henley, 1st Earl of Northington">Lord Northington</a> as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor" title="Lord Chancellor">Lord High Chancellor</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Carteret,_2nd_Earl_Granville" title="John Carteret, 2nd Earl Granville">Lord Granville</a> as <a href="/wiki/Lord_President_of_the_Council" title="Lord President of the Council">Lord President of the Council</a> as well.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Domestic_issues">Domestic issues</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Domestic issues">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_of_John_Wilkes">Arrest of John Wilkes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Arrest of John Wilkes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p><a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons" title="House of Commons">House of Commons</a> was thrown into doubt by the first case involving the <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament" class="mw-redirect" title="Member of Parliament">Member of Parliament</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a>, a radical reformer and political activist who promoted parliamentary representation and reform, whose critique of the former prime minister led to his resignation.
Wilkes was regarded by many as threat to the government and therefore was treated suspiciously. In 1763, it came to be one of Grenville's first acts to prosecute Wilkes for publishing in <i><a href="/wiki/The_North_Briton" title="The North Briton">The North Briton</a></i> newspaper an article deriding King George III's speech made on 23 April 1763.<sup id="cite_ref-Public_Opinion:_John_Wilkes_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Public_Opinion:_John_Wilkes-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Wilkes was prosecuted for "<a href="/wiki/Sedition" title="Sedition">seditious</a> <a href="/wiki/Libel" class="mw-redirect" title="Libel">libel</a>". That was a massive tactical blunder by the <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State">secretaries of state</a>, for it was perceived as a violation of individual liberty that raised the political discontent. 
</p><p>After fighting a <a href="/wiki/Duel" title="Duel">duel</a> with a supporter of the Grenville ministry, Samuel Martin, Wilkes fled to France for asylum. Despite, government officials calling on him to be arrested, Wilkes was later returned to <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> and was elected and re-elected by the <a href="/wiki/Middlesex" title="Middlesex">Middlesex</a> constituency. He was continually refused admission to parliament by parliament, and proved a problem to several successive governments.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Monetary_debt">Monetary debt</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Monetary debt">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>During the time of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a>, was a tumultuous period in the history of <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> and was fought on a global conflict scale. Even though Great Britain defeated <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and its allies and rose to the position as a dominant world power, the victory came at a great cost. In January 1763, Great Britain's national debt was more than £122 million pounds, an enormous sum for the time. Interest on the debt was more than £4.4 million a year. Figuring out how to pay the interest alone absorbed the attention of the King and his ministers.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Britain was trying to recover from the costs of the Seven Years' War and now in dire need of finances for the British army in the American colonies, Grenville's most immediate task was to restore the nation's finances. He also had to deal with the fall-out from <a href="/wiki/Pontiac%27s_Rebellion" class="mw-redirect" title="Pontiac&#39;s Rebellion">Pontiac's Rebellion</a>, which erupted in North America in 1763. Although, initially, prominent measures of his government included the prosecution of <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a>, focus shifted from arresting radicals to paying off the national debt. The passing of the tax measures in America had proved to be a disaster and would lead to the first symptoms of alienation between <a href="/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies" title="Thirteen Colonies">American colonies</a> and <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain" title="Great Britain">Great Britain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Colonial_reforms">Colonial reforms</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Colonial reforms">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg/275px-Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg" decoding="async" width="275" height="207" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg/413px-Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg/550px-Death_of_the_Stamp_Act_in_color.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="964" /></a><figcaption>Cartoon depicting the repeal of the <a href="/wiki/Stamp_Act_1765" title="Stamp Act 1765">Stamp Act</a> as a funeral, with Grenville carrying a child's coffin marked "Miss America Stamp born 1765, died 1766" (The Skulls refer to the Scottish Rebellions of 1715 and 1745)</figcaption></figure>

<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Measures_and_taxes">Measures and taxes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Measures and taxes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Many of the acts passed by the British were by the colonists as threatening to their liberties. Although, not a part of the Grenville government’s program, butthe itissue was generallymainly blamedattributed onto him by the colonists. Another one of the most controversial acts passed by Grenville was the <a href="/wiki/Quartering_Act" class="mw-redirect" title="Quartering Act">Quartering Act</a> on 15 May 1765. Enacted because of a request by <a href="/wiki/Major_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Major General">Major General</a> <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Gage" title="Thomas Gage">Thomas Gage</a>, commander-in-chief in North America, to give better housing for his troops stationed in the colonies.
</p><p>One of the more prominent measures of the Grenville's government occurred in March 1765 when Grenville authored the <a href="/wiki/Stamp_Act" class="mw-redirect" title="Stamp Act">Stamp Act</a>, enacted in November of that year. It was an exclusive tax placed on the colonies in America requiring that <a href="/wiki/Documents" class="mw-redirect" title="Documents">documents</a> and <a href="/wiki/Newspapers" class="mw-redirect" title="Newspapers">newspapers</a> be printed on stamped paper from London bearing an embossed revenue stamp that had to be paid for in British currency. It was met with general outrage and resulted in public acts of disobedience and <a href="/wiki/Riot" title="Riot">rioting</a> throughout the colonies in America.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foreign_policy">Foreign policy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Foreign policy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The signing of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)" title="Treaty of Paris (1763)">treaty in February 1763</a> formally marked the conclusion of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Seven Years War">Seven Years War</a> experienced by Britain and as such, foreign policy, along with military policy and diplomacy, no longer became the dominant concern of both domestic politics and government agenda. Focus thus shifted to seemingly more relevant issues such as the survival of the Grenville Ministry. But overall the British relations, after negotiating, with the French and the Spanish, remained hostile and suspicious of it’s enemies and that wariness concluded the entirety of Grenville’s policy.
</p><p>When seemingly, the French and the Spanish began to support Britain’s colonial dissenters in the Americas and beyond and threatening British allies in the continent, promoting disputes and resentment from British politicians who viewed them as a violation of British sovereignty. Britain begun a process of isolation, when Britain had no allies in the continent and the allies it did have were weak or less significant in military or political might.
</p><p>In disputes with Spain and France, Grenville managed to secure British objectives by deploying what was later described as <a href="/wiki/Gunboat_diplomacy" title="Gunboat diplomacy">gunboat diplomacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> During his ministry. Britain's international isolation increased, as Britain failed to secure alliances with other major European powers, a situation that subsequent governments were unable to reverse leading to Britain fighting several countries during the <a href="/wiki/American_War_of_Independence" class="mw-redirect" title="American War of Independence">American War of Independence</a> without a major ally.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Dismissal">Dismissal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Dismissal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The King made various attempts to induce Pitt to come to his rescue by forming a ministry, but without success, and at last had recourse to <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham</a>. When Rockingham agreed to accept office, the king dismissed Grenville in July 1765. He never again held office.<sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The nickname of "gentle shepherd" was given him because he bored the House by asking over and over again, during the debate on the <a href="/wiki/Cider_Bill_of_1763" title="Cider Bill of 1763">Cider Bill of 1763</a>, that somebody should tell him "where" to lay the new tax if it was not to be put on cider. Pitt whistled the air of the popular tune (by <a href="/wiki/William_Boyce_(composer)" title="William Boyce (composer)">Boyce</a>) <i>Gentle Shepherd, tell me where</i>, and the House laughed.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Though few surpassed him in knowledge of the forms of the House or in mastery of administrative details, he lacked tact in dealing with people and affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911580-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life:_1765-1770">Later life: 1765-1770</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=19" title="Edit section: Later life: 1765-1770">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Opposition">In Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=20" title="Edit section: In Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After a period of active opposition to the <a href="/wiki/Chatham_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Chatham Ministry">Chatham Ministry</a> led by Pitt between 1766 and 1768, Grenville became an elder <a href="/wiki/Politician" title="Politician">statesman</a> during his last few years – seeking to avoid becoming associated with any faction or party in the House of Commons.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> He was able to oversee the re-election of his core group of supporters in the <a href="/wiki/1768_General_Election" class="mw-redirect" title="1768 General Election">1768 General Election</a>. His followers included <a href="/wiki/Robert_Clive" title="Robert Clive">Robert Clive</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lord_George_Sackville" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord George Sackville">Lord George Sackville</a> and he received support from his elder brother <a href="/wiki/Richard_Grenville-Temple,_2nd_Earl_Temple" title="Richard Grenville-Temple, 2nd Earl Temple">Lord Temple</a>.
</p><p>In late 1768 he reconciled with Pitt and the two joined forces, re-uniting the partnership that had broken up in 1761 when Pitt had resigned from the government.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Grenville was successful in mobilising the opposition during the <a href="/wiki/Middlesex_election_dispute" class="mw-redirect" title="Middlesex election dispute">Middlesex election dispute</a>.
</p><p>Grenville prosecuted John Wilkes and the printers and authors for treason and sedition for publishing a bitter editorial about King George III's recent speech in "The North Briton" a weekly periodical. After losing the case Grenville lost favor from the public who regarded the act as an attempt to silence or control the press.<sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Britannia_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Britannia-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Advocate_and_critic">Advocate and critic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Advocate and critic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although personally opposed to Wilkes, Grenville saw the government's attempt to bar him from the Commons as unconstitutional and opposed it on principle.
</p><p>Following <a href="/wiki/French_Conquest_of_Corsica" class="mw-redirect" title="French Conquest of Corsica">a French invasion of Corsica</a> in 1768 Grenville advocated sending British support to the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Republic" title="Corsican Republic">Corsican Republic</a>. Grenville was critical of the <a href="/wiki/Grafton_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Grafton Ministry">Grafton Government</a>'s failure to intervene and he considered such weakness would encourage the French. In the House of Commons he observed "For fear of going to war, you will make a war unavoidable".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1770 Grenville steered a bill concerning the results of contested elections, a major issue in the eighteenth century, into law – despite strong opposition from the government.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=22" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Grenville died on 13 November 1770, aged 58. His personal following divided after his death, with a number joining the government of <a href="/wiki/Lord_North" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord North">Lord North</a>. In the long-term, the <a href="/wiki/Grenvillites" class="mw-redirect" title="Grenvillites">Grenvillites</a> were revived by <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a> who served as Prime Minister from 1784 and dominated British politics until his death in 1806. Grenville's own son, <a href="/wiki/William_Grenville" class="mw-redirect" title="William Grenville">William Grenville</a>, later served briefly as Prime Minister.<sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Grenville is buried at <a href="/wiki/Wotton_Underwood" title="Wotton Underwood">Wotton Underwood</a> in Buckinghamshire<sup id="cite_ref-GrenODNB_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GrenODNB-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>George Grenville's post mortem was carried up by <a href="/wiki/John_Hunter_(Surgeon)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Hunter (Surgeon)">John Hunter</a> who retained specimens in his collection which later became the <a href="/wiki/Hunterian_Museum_(London)" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunterian Museum (London)">Hunterian Museum</a>. Subsequent analysis of these specimens published by the Royal College of Surgeons of England suggests that George Grenville was affected by <a href="/wiki/Multiple_myeloma" title="Multiple myeloma">Multiple myeloma</a> at the time of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=23" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was one of the relatively few prime ministers (others include <a href="/wiki/Henry_Pelham" title="Henry Pelham">Henry Pelham</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Campbell-Bannerman" title="Henry Campbell-Bannerman">Henry Campbell-Bannerman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bonar_Law" title="Bonar Law">Bonar Law</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald" title="Ramsay MacDonald">Ramsay MacDonald</a>, <a href="/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain" title="Neville Chamberlain">Neville Chamberlain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Winston_Churchill" title="Winston Churchill">Sir Winston Churchill</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Perceval" title="Spencer Perceval">Spencer Perceval</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone" title="William Ewart Gladstone">William Ewart Gladstone</a>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Heath" title="Edward Heath">Edward Heath</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Major" title="John Major">John Major</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tony_Blair" title="Tony Blair">Tony Blair</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gordon_Brown" title="Gordon Brown">Gordon Brown</a>, <a href="/wiki/David_Cameron" title="David Cameron">David Cameron</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theresa_May" title="Theresa May">Theresa May</a>, <a href="/wiki/Boris_Johnson" title="Boris Johnson">Boris Johnson</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Liz_Truss" title="Liz Truss">Liz Truss</a>) who never acceded to the <a href="/wiki/Peerage" title="Peerage">peerage</a>.
</p><p>The town of <a href="/wiki/Grenville,_Quebec" title="Grenville, Quebec">Grenville, Quebec</a>, was named after George Grenville. The town is in turn the namesake for the <a href="/wiki/Grenville_orogeny" title="Grenville orogeny">Grenville orogeny</a>, a long-lived Mesoproterozoic mountain-building event associated with the assembly of the supercontinent <a href="/wiki/Rodinia" title="Rodinia">Rodinia</a>. Its record is a prominent orogenic belt which spans a significant portion of the North American continent, from Labrador to Mexico, and extends to Scotland.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_life">Family life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=24" title="Edit section: Family life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1749 Grenville married <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Wyndham" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth Wyndham">Elizabeth Wyndham</a> (1719 – 5 December 1769), daughter of Sir <a href="/wiki/Sir_William_Wyndham,_3rd_Baronet" title="Sir William Wyndham, 3rd Baronet">William Wyndham</a>, and the granddaughter of the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Seymour,_6th_Duke_of_Somerset" title="Charles Seymour, 6th Duke of Somerset">Duke of Somerset</a>. Somerset did not approve of their marriage and consequently left Elizabeth only a small sum in his will.<sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The couple had four sons and four daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> (One account states they had five daughters.)<sup id="cite_ref-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy_15-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-George_Grenville_(1712-1770),_Dr.Bloy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li>Richard Grenville (died 1759), died young</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/George_Nugent-Temple-Grenville,_1st_Marquess_of_Buckingham" title="George Nugent-Temple-Grenville, 1st Marquess of Buckingham">George Nugent-Temple-Grenville, 1st Marquess of Buckingham</a> (17 June 1753 – 11 February 1813), father of the <a href="/wiki/Richard_Temple-Nugent-Brydges-Chandos-Grenville,_1st_Duke_of_Buckingham_and_Chandos" title="Richard Temple-Nugent-Brydges-Chandos-Grenville, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos">1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Charlotte_Williams-Wynn_(aristocrat)" title="Charlotte Williams-Wynn (aristocrat)">Charlotte Grenville</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1754</span> – 29 September 1830), married <a href="/wiki/Sir_Watkin_Williams-Wynn,_4th_Baronet" title="Sir Watkin Williams-Wynn, 4th Baronet">Sir Watkin Williams-Wynn, 4th Baronet</a> (1749–1789) on 21 December 1771, and had eight children, six of whom survived to adulthood</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Thomas_Grenville" title="Thomas Grenville">Thomas Grenville</a> (31 December 1755 – 17 December 1846), MP and <a href="/wiki/Bibliophilia" title="Bibliophilia">bibliophile</a>, died unmarried</li>
<li>Elizabeth Grenville (24 October 1756 – 21 December 1842), married (as his second wife) <a href="/wiki/John_Proby,_1st_Earl_of_Carysfort" title="John Proby, 1st Earl of Carysfort">John Proby, 1st Earl of Carysfort</a> (1751–1828), on 12 April 1787, and had three daughters</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville" title="William Grenville, 1st Baron Grenville">William Grenville, 1st Baron Grenville</a> (25 October 1759 – 12 January 1834), later Prime Minister</li>
<li>Catherine Grenville (1761 – 6 November 1796), married Richard Neville-Aldworth (1750–1825), afterwards <a href="/wiki/Richard_Griffin,_2nd_Baron_Braybrooke" title="Richard Griffin, 2nd Baron Braybrooke">Richard Griffin, 2nd Baron Braybrooke</a>, on 19 June 1780, and had four children.</li>
<li>Hester Grenville (before 1767 – 13 November 1847), married <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Fortescue,_1st_Earl_Fortescue" title="Hugh Fortescue, 1st Earl Fortescue">Hugh Fortescue, 1st Earl Fortescue</a>, on 10 May 1782 and had nine children</li></ol>
<p>At the time of his death in 1770, he was the <a href="/wiki/Heir_presumptive" title="Heir presumptive">heir presumptive</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Earl_Temple" title="Earl Temple">Earldom of Temple</a> held by his elder brother Richard (who had succeeded their mother in that title in 1752, but had no sons). When Richard died in 1779, George's second (but eldest surviving) son, also George, therefore succeeded as 3rd Earl Temple, and was later created Marquess of Buckingham. His male line survived until the death of the <a href="/wiki/Richard_Temple-Nugent-Brydges-Chandos-Grenville,_3rd_Duke_of_Buckingham_and_Chandos" title="Richard Temple-Nugent-Brydges-Chandos-Grenville, 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos">3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos</a> in 1889.
</p>
