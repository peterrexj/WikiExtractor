<p><b>Pope Nicholas II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Nicholaus II</i>; c. 990/995 – 27 July 1061), otherwise known as <b>Gerard of Burgundy</b>, was the head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 24 January 1059 until his death in 27 July 1061. At the time of his election, he was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Florence">bishop of Florence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During his Papacy, Nicholas II successfully expanded the influence of the papacy in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and southern Italy. He was also responsible for passing papal election reforms.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gerard of Burgundy was born in Chevron,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in what is now <a href="/wiki/Savoy" title="Savoy">Savoy</a>. He was a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> at <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>. In 1045 he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Florence">bishop of Florence</a>, where he restored the canonical life among the clergy of numerous churches.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Antipope_Benedict_X" title="Antipope Benedict X">Benedict X</a> was elected in 1058, his election having been arranged by the <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Tusculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Tusculum">count of Tusculum</a>. However, a number of cardinals alleged that the election was irregular, and that votes had been bought; these cardinals were forced to flee <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Hildebrand</a>, archdeacon of the Roman church, was away on a diplomatic mission to Germany. When he heard of Benedict X's election, he decided to oppose it, and obtained support for the election of Gerard of Burgundy instead. In December 1058, those cardinals who had opposed Benedict X's election met at <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a> and elected Gerard as pope instead. He then took the name Nicholas II.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nicholas II proceeded towards Rome, along the way holding a synod at <a href="/wiki/Sutri" title="Sutri">Sutri</a>, where, in the presence of the Tuscan ruler <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_the_Bearded" title="Godfrey the Bearded">Godfrey the Bearded</a> and the imperial chancellor, Guibert of Parma, he pronounced Benedict X deposed and excommunicated.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The supporters of Nicholas II then gained control of Rome and forced Benedict X to flee to Gerard of Galeria. Having arrived in Rome, Nicholas II then proceeded to wage war against Benedict X and his supporters with <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> assistance. At an initial battle in <a href="/wiki/Campagna" title="Campagna">Campagna</a> in early 1059, Nicholas II was not wholly successful. But later that same year, his forces conquered <a href="/wiki/Praeneste" class="mw-redirect" title="Praeneste">Praeneste</a>, Tusculum, and <a href="/wiki/Mentana" title="Mentana">Numentanum</a>, and in the autumn took Galeria, forcing Benedict X to surrender and renounce the papacy. He died on 27 July 1061, less than three years after becoming Pope.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationship_with_the_Normans">Relationship with the Normans</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Relationship with the Normans">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Museo_nicolaiano,_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II,_1059.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg/220px-Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="172" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg/330px-Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg/440px-Museo_nicolaiano%2C_sigillo_di_papa_nicola_II%2C_1059.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1260" data-file-height="984" /></a><figcaption>A 1059 bullae seal of Nicholas II</figcaption></figure>
<p>To secure his position, Nicholas II at once entered into relations with the Normans. The pope wanted to re-take <a href="/wiki/Emirate_of_Sicily" title="Emirate of Sicily">Sicily</a> for Christianity, and he saw the Normans as the perfect force to crush the Muslims.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The Normans were by this time firmly established in southern Italy, and later in the year 1059 the new alliance was cemented at <a href="/wiki/Melfi" title="Melfi">Melfi</a>, where the pope, accompanied by Hildebrand, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Humbert" title="Humbert">Humbert</a>, and Abbot <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Desiderius</a> of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>, solemnly invested <a href="/wiki/Robert_Guiscard" title="Robert Guiscard">Robert Guiscard</a> with the duchies of <a href="/wiki/Apulia" title="Apulia">Apulia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Richard_of_Aversa" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard of Aversa">Richard of Aversa</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Capua" title="Principality of Capua">principality of Capua</a>, in return for oaths of fealty and the promise of assistance in guarding the rights of the Church. This arrangement, which was based on no firmer foundation than the forged "<a href="/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine" title="Donation of Constantine">Donation of Constantine</a>", was destined to give the papacy independence from both the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Eastern</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Western Empires</a>. Its first substantial result was Norman aid in taking Galeria, where Antipope Benedict X was hiding, and the end of the subordination of the papacy to the Roman nobles.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Subordination_of_Milan">Subordination of Milan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Subordination of Milan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>Meanwhile, Nicholas II sent <a href="/wiki/Peter_Damian" title="Peter Damian">Peter Damian</a> and Bishop <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Lucca" title="Anselm of Lucca">Anselm of Lucca</a> as legates to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, to resolve the conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Pataria" title="Pataria">Patarenes</a> and the archbishop and clergy. The result was a fresh triumph for the papacy. Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Wido" class="mw-redirect" title="Wido">Wido</a>, facing ruinous ecclesiastical conflict in Milan, submitted to the terms of the legates, which subordinated Milan to Rome. The new relation was advertised by the unwilling attendance of Wido and the other Milanese bishops at the council summoned to the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Palace" title="Lateran Palace">Lateran Palace</a> in April 1059. This council not only continued the Hildebrandine reforms by sharpening the discipline of the clergy, but marked an epoch in the history of the papacy by its famous regulation of future elections to the Holy See.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Election_reform">Election reform</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Election reform">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/In_nomine_Domini" title="In nomine Domini">In nomine Domini</a></div>
<p>Previously, <a href="/wiki/Papal_election" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election">papal elections</a> had effectively been controlled by the Roman aristocracy, unless the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman emperor">Holy Roman emperor</a> was strong enough to be able to intervene from a distance to impose his will. As a result of the battles with the Antipope Benedict X, Nicholas II wished to reform papal elections. At the synod held in the Lateran at Easter, 1059, Pope Nicholas brought 113 bishops to Rome to consider a number of reforms, including a change in the election procedure. The electoral reform adopted by that synod amounted to a declaration of independence on the part of the church. Henceforth, popes were to be selected by the cardinals in assembly at Rome.
</p>
