<p><b>Symphorosa</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sinforosa</i>; died circa AD 138) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. According to tradition, she was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> with her seven sons at <a href="/wiki/Tibur" class="mw-redirect" title="Tibur">Tibur</a> (present <a href="/wiki/Tivoli,_Lazio" title="Tivoli, Lazio">Tivoli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lazio" title="Lazio">Lazio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>) toward the end of the reign of the Roman <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Emperor Hadrian</a> (117–38).<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The story of their martyrdom is told in an ancient <i>Passio</i>, the credibility of which is seriously questioned by many modern <a href="/wiki/Hagiologists" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiologists">hagiologists</a>. According to the <i>Passio</i>, Symphorosa was a Tiburtine matron and the widow of <a href="/wiki/Getulius" title="Getulius">Getulius</a>, a native of Gabii in Sabina. He was a <a href="/wiki/Tribune" title="Tribune">tribune</a>  in the Roman army, but resigned upon being baptized a Christian, ultimately retiring to his estate near Tivoli, where he was joined by his brother, Amantius.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
Getulius and Amantius were subsequently beheaded under <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Hadrian" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Hadrian">Emperor Hadrian</a>. Symphorosa buried his  remains on their estate.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Not long after, Symphorosa and her children were arrested. When all of the Emperor's attempts to induce Symphorosa and her sons to sacrifice to the pagan <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a> were unsuccessful, he ordered her to be brought to the Temple of Hercules, where, after various <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortures</a>, she was thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Anio" class="mw-redirect" title="Anio">Anio River</a> with a heavy rock fastened to her neck. Her brother Eugenius, who was a member of the council of Tibur, buried her with her husband.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Her_seven_sons">Her seven sons</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Her seven sons">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The next day, the emperor summoned Symphorosa's seven sons, and being equally unsuccessful in his attempts to make them sacrifice to the gods, he ordered them to be tied to seven stakes erected for the purpose round the Temple of Hercules.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The emperor ordered all seven to be tortured, and finally pierced with the sword.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their bodies were thrown <a href="/wiki/Mass_grave" title="Mass grave">en masse</a> into a deep <a href="/wiki/Ditch" title="Ditch">ditch</a> at a place the pagan priests afterwards called <i>Ad septem Biothanatos</i> (the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Greek</a> word <i>biodanatos</i>, or rather <i>biaiodanatos</i>, was employed for <a href="/wiki/Self-murder" class="mw-redirect" title="Self-murder">self-murderers</a> and, by the pagans, applied to Christians who suffered martyrdom). Hereupon the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" title="Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire">persecution</a> ceased for one year and six months, during which period the bodies of the martyrs were recovered by the Christian community<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and buried on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Tiburtina" title="Via Tiburtina">Via Tiburtina</a>, eight or nine miles (14&#160;km) from <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Authenticity">Authenticity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Authenticity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is difficult to ascertain how much reliability these <i>Acts</i> possess. The opinion that they were written by <a href="/wiki/Julius_Africanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Julius Africanus">Julius Africanus</a> (3rd century) has been rejected almost universally, since neither <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> nor any other contemporary historian makes the least allusion to any <i>Acts</i> of Roman or Italian martyrs composed by this African writer.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Hieronymian_Martyrology" class="mw-redirect" title="Hieronymian Martyrology">Hieronymian Martyrology</a></i>, which was compiled by an unknown author in the second half of the 5th century, commemorates St. Symphorosa and her sons on 18 July, but here the names of her sons are entirely different from those given in the <i>Acts</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Since here the names of Symphorosa's sons are different from those of the <i>Acts</i> we possess, there must have existed some other <i>Gesta</i> to which the author of the martyrology refers. In the same martyrology, on 27 June, are commemorated seven brother-martyrs, whose names are identical with those our <i>Acts</i> assign to the sons of Symphorosa. It is probable that the author of the <i>Acts</i>, guided by the tradition that Symphorosa had seven sons who were martyred, made her the mother of the seven martyrs, whom he found mentioned in the martyrology on 27 June. If this is the case, we may infer, provided Symphorosa had seven sons at all, that their names were not those mentioned in the <i>Acts</i>. Whether they were those assigned to them in the <i>Hieronymian Martyrology</i> will also remain doubtful as long as we have no certainty that the <i>Gesta</i> to which the author refers are authentic. Some hagiologists consider the seven sons of Symphorosa, like those of <a href="/wiki/Felicitas_of_Rome" title="Felicitas of Rome">Felicitas of Rome</a>, a mere adaptation of the <a href="/wiki/Woman_with_seven_sons" title="Woman with seven sons">seven sons of the Maccabean Mother</a>. <a href="/wiki/Paul_Allard" title="Paul Allard">Paul Allard</a> dealt with her story uncritically in his work.
</p><p>In the 17th century, <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Bosio" title="Antonio Bosio">Bosio</a> discovered the ruins of a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> at the place popularly called "<i>le sette fratte</i>" (taken to be a corruption of words meaning "the seven brothers"), on the Via Tiburtina, nine miles (14&#160;km) from Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Acts</i> and the <i>Hieronymian Martyrology</i> agree in designating this spot as the tomb of Symphorosa and her sons. Further discoveries, that leave no room for doubt that the basilica was built over their tomb, were made by <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Stevenson" title="Joseph Stevenson">Stevenson</a>. The remains were transferred to the Church of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Angelo_in_Pescheria" title="Sant&#39;Angelo in Pescheria">Sant'Angelo in Pescheria</a> at Rome by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Pope Stephen II</a> in 752. A <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> was found here in 1610, bearing the <a href="/wiki/Early_Christian_inscriptions" title="Early Christian inscriptions">inscription</a>: <i>Hic requiescunt corpora SS. Martyrum Simforosae, viri sui Zotici (Getulii) et Filiorum ejus a Stephano Papa translata</i> (Here rest the bodies of the holy martyrs Symphorosa, her husband Zotius (Getulius) and her sons, transferred by Pope Stephen).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG/200px-Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG/300px-Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG/400px-Eglise_Santa_Sinforosa.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2909" data-file-height="2182" /></a><figcaption>Church of Santa Sinforosa in Tivoli</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Tivoli" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Tivoli">Diocese of Tivoli</a> honours them as <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> and they are mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> on 18 July. However, they are no longer included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>. The reason given for their removal was that the information given in their <i>Acts</i>, which are thought to be an imitation of the <i>Passio</i> of Saint Felicitas of Rome and her seven sons, is untrustworthy.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In medieval times <a href="/wiki/Settecamini" title="Settecamini">Settecamini</a> was called "Field of Seven Brothers" in relation to the legend of Saint Symphorosa and her seven children.
</p><p>There is a St. Symphorosa Elementary School in <a href="/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicago, Illinois">Chicago, Illinois</a>.
</p>
