					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Verecundus&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Verecundus">Verecundus</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Verecundus</b>  (<a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl</a>. 552) was a 6th-century writer and the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ounga,_Tunisia" title="Ounga, Tunisia">Iunca</a> (or Junca) in <a href="/wiki/Roman_North_Africa" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman North Africa">Roman North Africa</a> (the modern Tunisia).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was an ardent champion of the <a href="/wiki/Three_Chapters" class="mw-redirect" title="Three Chapters">Three Chapters</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Verecundus_of_Junca&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Verecundus attended the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">Synod</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> of 551 called by <a href="/wiki/Justinian" class="mw-redirect" title="Justinian">Justinian</a> where he sided with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Three-Chapter_Controversy" title="Three-Chapter Controversy">Three-Chapter Controversy</a> and went into self-imposed exile with him at the end of the synod<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> for refusing to sign the condemnation of <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Mopsuestia" title="Theodore of Mopsuestia">Theodore of Mopsuestia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theodoret_of_Cyrrhus" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodoret of Cyrrhus">Theodoret of Cyrrhus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ibas_of_Edessa" title="Ibas of Edessa">Ibas of Edessa</a>.
</p><p>When the question of the Three Chapters was raised at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>, in the beginning of 552. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a>'s <i>Judicatum</i> having excited almost universal discontent, both the pope and the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Justinian" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Justinian">Emperor Justinian</a> agreed the question should be settled in a general council to be held at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.  Verecundus, with <a href="/wiki/Primasius_of_Hadrumeta" class="mw-redirect" title="Primasius of Hadrumeta">Primasius of Hadrumeta</a>, went to represent the Province of Byzacena, and arrived at Constantinople towards the middle of 551. At once the Greek bishops set out to induce them by promises and threats to anathematize the Three Chapters.  Both resisted strenuously at first, and, in the grave difficulties then besetting Pope Vigilius, stood by his side; and when the latter had taken refuge in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Peter%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Peter&#39;s">Basilica of St. Peter's</a>, both, in union with him, issued a sentence of excommunication against Theodore Askidas and of deposition against <a href="/wiki/Menas_of_Constantinople" title="Menas of Constantinople">Mennas</a>, the patriarch of Constantinople (17 August, 551).  Soon, however, the conditions became so unbearable that on 23 December Pope Vigilius, although his residence was carefully watched, managed to escape across the <a href="/wiki/Bosphorus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bosphorus">Bosphorus</a> and to reach the Church of St. Euphemia at <a href="/wiki/Chalcedon" title="Chalcedon">Chalcedon</a>.  Primasius and Verecundus followed him a few days later. 
</p><p>Verecundus died shortly afterwards.  After Verecundus's death, Primasius was moved by ambition to relent from his unyielding attitude. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesiastical_writer">Ecclesiastical writer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Verecundus_of_Junca&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ecclesiastical writer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As an ecclesiastical author, Verecundus is little known. His works, edited by <a href="/wiki/J._B._Pitra" class="mw-redirect" title="J. B. Pitra">J. B. Pitra</a> (<i>Spicilegium Solesmense</i>, IV, Paris, 1858), comprise a collection of historical documents on the Council of Chalcedon, "Excerptiones de gestis Chalcedonensis Concilii", of which we possess two recensions; an exegetical commentary in nine books upon the Canticles of the Old Testament,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the poem <i>Carmen de satisfactione</i> ( "De satisfactione poenitentiae") in 212 <a href="/wiki/Hexameter" title="Hexameter">hexameters</a> ( in which exquisite thoughts are presented in a very incorrect form), and possibly another, the <i>Carmen ad Flavium Felicem de resurrectione mortuorum et de iudicio domini</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Verecundus also wrote excerpts of the proceedings of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville" title="Isidore of Seville">Isidore of Seville</a> (De vir. ill., vii) attributes to Verecundus another poem on resurrection and judgment, which is possibly none other than the "De iudicio Domini" or "De resurrectione mortuorum", found among the works of <a href="/wiki/Tertullian" title="Tertullian">Tertullian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cyprian" title="Cyprian">St. Cyprian</a>.
</p>
