<p><b>Saint Afra</b> (died 304) was martyred during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian persecution">Diocletian persecution</a>. Along with <a href="/wiki/Ulrich_of_Augsburg" title="Ulrich of Augsburg">Saint Ulrich</a>, she is a patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is August 7. Afra was dedicated to the service of the goddess <a href="/wiki/Venus_(mythology)" title="Venus (mythology)">Venus</a> by her mother, Hilaria. Through his teachings, Bishop Narcissus converted Afra and her family to Christianity. When it was learned that Afra was a Christian, she was brought before <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and ordered to sacrifice to the pagan gods. She refused and was condemned to <a href="/wiki/Death_by_burning" title="Death by burning">death by fire</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afra&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although many different accounts of her life exist, the most widely known is <i>The Acts of St. Afra</i>, which dates from the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> period (8th century AD). In the opinion of most critics, this is a compilation of two different accounts, the story of the conversion of St. Afra, and the story of her martyrdom. The former is of later origin and is merely a legendary narrative of Carolingian times, drawn up with the intention of connecting with St. Afra the organization of the church of Augsburg.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the late 3rd century, her pagan family journeyed from <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a> to Augsburg. Afra was dedicated to the service of the goddess Venus by her mother, Hilaria.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to this source, she was originally a <a href="/wiki/Prostitute" class="mw-redirect" title="Prostitute">prostitute</a> in <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Afra_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Afra-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> having gone there from Cyprus, maybe even as the daughter of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Cyprus" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Cyprus">King of Cyprus</a>. She is reputed either to have run a <a href="/wiki/Brothel" title="Brothel">brothel</a> in that town or worked as a <a href="/wiki/Hierodule" class="mw-redirect" title="Hierodule">hierodule</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Temple" title="Temple">Temple</a> of <a href="/wiki/Venus_(mythology)" title="Venus (mythology)">Venus</a>. As the <a href="/wiki/Persecution" title="Persecution">persecution</a> of Christians during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> began, <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> Narcissus of <a href="/wiki/Girona" title="Girona">Girona</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>) sought refuge in Augsburg and lodged with Afra and her mother, Hilaria. Through his teachings, Bishop Narcissus converted Afra and her family to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She continued to hide the bishop from the authorities. When it was learned that Afra was a Christian, she was brought before Diocletian and ordered to sacrifice to the pagan gods. She refused and was condemned to death by fire on a small island in the <a href="/wiki/Lech_(river)" title="Lech (river)">Lech River</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> with her remains being buried at a distance from the place of her martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her mother and her maids (viz., Ligna, Eunonia, and Eutropia) later suffered the same fate,<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> for <a href="/wiki/Burial" title="Burial">interring</a> her in a <a href="/wiki/Burial_vault_(tomb)" title="Burial vault (tomb)">burial vault</a>.
</p><p>According to an alternative account in an earlier document, Afra was beheaded, rather than having been burned. The <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> (a compilation of martyrs) mentions that Afra  "suffered in the city of Augsburg" and was "buried there".<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is August 5.<sup id="cite_ref-Afra_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Afra-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Carl Egger, it appears that the author of the <i>passio</i> blended the account of Afra with that of Venerea, a martyr of Antioch, who is mentioned on the same day in the <i>Martyrologium Hieronymianum</i>. Contrary to this, other ancient calendars portray Afra as a virgin.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afra&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_(Augsburg)_15.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG/220px-St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG/330px-St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG/440px-St._Ulrich_und_St._Afra_%28Augsburg%29_15.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3000" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Saint Afra in Augsburg</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_and_St._Afra%27s_Abbey" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s and St. Afra&#39;s Abbey">St. Ulrich's and St. Afra's Abbey</a>, Augsburg (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Kloster Sankt Ulrich und Afra Augsburg</i>) is a former Benedictine abbey dedicated to Saint Ulrich and Saint Afra in the south of the old city in Augsburg, Bavaria. From the medieval Saint Afra's church in <a href="/wiki/Meissen" title="Meissen">Meissen</a>, the name passed via a former monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Canons Regular</a> to the current <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A4chsisches_Landesgymnasium_Sankt_Afra_zu_Mei%C3%9Fen" title="Sächsisches Landesgymnasium Sankt Afra zu Meißen">Sächsisches Landesgymnasium Sankt Afra zu Meißen</a>, a boarding school in Saxony.
</p><p>Her exact birthdate is unknown
</p><p>There was a Church of Saint Afra in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> (that was destroyed during the bombing of World War II).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
