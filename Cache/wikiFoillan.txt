<p><b>Saint Foillan</b> (<i>Faélán, Faolán, Foélán, French: <i lang="fr">Feuillen</i></i>) is an Irish saint of the seventh century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Foillan was the brother of Saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ultan" title="Saint Ultan">Ultan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fursey" title="Saint Fursey">Fursey</a>. He is described as the 'uterine brother' of Fursa, meaning that they had the same mother but not the same father.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Certain Latin <i>Lives</i> of Foillan therefore incorporate the Fursa ancestry into Foillan's origins: his mother is stated to have been Gelges, the Christian daughter of 'Aed Finn' (possibly meaning <a href="/wiki/%C3%81ed_mac_Echach" title="Áed mac Echach">Áed mac Echach</a>), <a href="/wiki/List_of_kings_of_Connacht" title="List of kings of Connacht">King of Connacht</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Fursey's father is stated to be Fintan son of Finlog (though whether of Momonia or of Mag Murthemni, the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> editor finds the sources not in agreement).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_to_East_Anglia">Mission to East Anglia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mission to East Anglia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Foillan, probably in company with Ultan, went with his brother Fursa when the latter retired to a lonely island, escaping from the multitudes who gathered around him, some of whom harboured ill-feeling towards him. From there, around 633, Fursa went through <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historic)">British</a> territory to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">Kingdom of East Anglia</a> with a group of followers including Foillan and Ultan and priests named Gobán and Dicuill. There they were received kindly by King <a href="/wiki/Sigeberht_of_East_Anglia" title="Sigeberht of East Anglia">Sigeberht of East Anglia</a>, who gave Fursa the site of a Roman shore-fort at a place called <i>Cnobheresburg</i>, to build a monastery. The monastery was built at the site usually identified as <a href="/wiki/Burgh_Castle_Roman_Site" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgh Castle Roman Site">Burgh Castle</a> or <a href="/wiki/Gariannonum" title="Gariannonum">Gariannonum</a> (formerly in <a href="/wiki/Suffolk" title="Suffolk">Suffolk</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a>), and it flourished between c. 634 and c. 650. The earliest source for Fursa and Foillan in East Anglia is the <i>Vita Sancti Fursei</i>:<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> this was the primary source quoted by the <a href="/wiki/Venerable_Bede" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable Bede">Venerable Bede</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Ecclesiastica_Gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia Ecclesiastica Gentis Anglorum">Historia Ecclesiastica Gentis Anglorum</a></i> of 731.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Cnobheresburg">Abbot of <i>Cnobheresburg</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Abbot of Cnobheresburg">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Seized again with the desire for solitude, about 643 Fursa left the monastery of <i>Cnobheresburg</i> in the care of Foillan, while he (Fursa) went off to find his brother Ultan, who had previously gone to live in the East Anglian wilderness as a hermit: Fursa and Ultan lived together for a year in austerity and prayer.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> At the end of the year Fursa, seeing that East Anglia and the monastery were threatened by hostile invasions, decided to take his leave of East Anglia, and went into Gaul leaving Foillan now fully in charge of the monastery.
</p><p>The <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i> states that Fursa made a return visit to the brethren in East Anglia c. 650. This is derived from the <i>Virtutes Fursei</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> which states that Fursa decided that he would return to visit his brothers, and set out to do so, but then adds that he died on the journey at his estate at <i>Macerias</i> (<a href="/wiki/M%C3%A9zerolles" title="Mézerolles">Mézerolles</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ponthieu" title="Ponthieu">Ponthieu</a>), so in fact did not reach them. Neither the early <i>Transitus Fursei</i> nor <i>Bede</i> have the story.
</p><p>Around 651 there was (as Fursa had foreseen) a disastrous assault on East Anglia by <a href="/wiki/Penda" class="mw-redirect" title="Penda">Penda</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercian</a> king. King <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna of East Anglia</a> was put to flight, and the monastery of <i>Cnobheresburg</i> fell into the hands of the enemies. It was pillaged, and its superior, Foillan, barely escaped death. He hastened to ransom the captive monks, recovered the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, put the holy books and objects of veneration on board ship, and departed for <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9ronne,_Somme" title="Péronne, Somme">Péronne</a> in Frankish <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, where his brother Fursa was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="At_P.C3.A9ronne_and_Nivelles"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="At_Péronne_and_Nivelles">At Péronne and Nivelles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: At Péronne and Nivelles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He and his companions were well received at Péronne by <a href="/wiki/Erchinoald" title="Erchinoald">Erchinoald</a>, Mayor of the Palace, who with King <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> had previously befriended Fursa. But soon, turning against these visitors, that nobleman expelled Foillan and his companions from Péronne, and they went to <a href="/wiki/Nivelles" title="Nivelles">Nivelles</a>, where they were made welcome by (Saint) <a href="/wiki/Itta" class="mw-redirect" title="Itta">Itta</a> (also called Iduberg) and her daughter (Saint) <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude</a>, and their protector (Gertrude's brother) <a href="/wiki/Grimoald_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Grimoald I">Grimoald I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Itta and Gertrude, wife and daughter of Duke <a href="/wiki/Pepin_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin I">Pepin I</a> respectively, were the foundresses of the Nivelles monastery in Belgium.
</p><p>Foillan seems to have been a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He was therefore of great assistance in the organisation of worship, and the holy books and relics which he brought were great treasures for St. Itta and St. Gertrude. As the monastery of Nivelles was under Irish discipline, the companions of Foillan were well received and lived side by side with the holy women, occupying themselves with the details of worship under the general direction of the abbess.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Foundation_of_Fosses.2C_and_martyrdom"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_Fosses,_and_martyrdom">Foundation of Fosses, and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Foundation of Fosses, and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Through the liberality of Itta, Foillan was enabled to build a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Fosses-la-Ville" title="Fosses-la-Ville">Fosses-la-Ville</a>, not far from Nivelles, in the province of <a href="/wiki/Namur_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Namur (city)">Namur</a>. After the death of Itta in 652, Foillan came one day to Nivelles and sang <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>, on the eve of the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quentin" title="Saint Quentin">Saint Quentin</a>. The ceremony being finished, he resumed his journey, doubtless undertaken in the interests of his monastery. In the Sonian Forest the saint and his companions fell into a trap set by <a href="/wiki/Bandit" class="mw-redirect" title="Bandit">bandits</a> who inhabited the dense forest. They were slain, stripped, and their bodies concealed. Foillan's head, still speaking prayers, was thrown into a nearby pigsty. The bodies were recovered by St. Gertrude, and when she had taken some relics of the saint, his body was borne to the monastery of Fosses-la-Ville, where it was buried about 655.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Foillan was one of the numerous Irish missionaries who, in the course of the seventh century, evangelised in Neustria, bringing thither the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a> and sacred vessels, founding prosperous monasteries, and sharing considerably in the propagation of the faith in these countries. Owing to the friendship which united him with Erchinoald, Mayor of the Palace (who, however, expelled him from <a href="/wiki/Lagny-sur-Marne" title="Lagny-sur-Marne">Lagny</a>), and with the members of Pepin's family, Foillan played a significant part in Frankish ecclesiastical history, as shown by his share in the direction of Nivelles and by the foundation of the monastery of Fosses-la-Ville.
</p><p>It is not surprising, therefore, that he should be honoured and venerated both at Nivelles and Fosses-la-Ville and to find at <a href="/wiki/Le_Roeulx" class="mw-redirect" title="Le Roeulx">Le Roeulx</a> (Belgium) a monastery bearing his name. As late as the twelfth century the veneration in which he was held inspired <a href="/wiki/Philippe_de_Harvengt" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippe de Harvengt">Philippe de Harvengt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Bonne-Esperance" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Bonne-Esperance">Abbot of Bonne-Esperance</a>, to compose a lengthy biography of the saint. He is the patron of Fosses, near <a href="/wiki/Charleroi" title="Charleroi">Charleroi</a>. In the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Namur" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Namur">Diocese of Namur</a> his feast is celebrated on 31 October, in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Mechelen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Mechelen">Diocese of Mechelen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Tournai">Diocese of Tournai</a> on 5 November.
</p><p>There are several Latin <i>Lives</i> of Foillan of varying authority, reproduced by the Society of <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Around 1100 Hillinus, a deacon and cantor of the church of Fosses, wrote a metrical life of Foillan for his master Sigebert, the patron of Fosses.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Soon afterwards, between 1102 and 1112, Hillinus also wrote a prose <i>In Miraculis Sancti Foyllani Martyris</i>, a book of the miracles associated with St. Foillan at Fosse.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Musician">Musician</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Foillan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Musician">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Gratten Flood, <i>"About the year 653, St. Gertrude, of Brabant, (daughter of Pepin, Mayor of the Palace), abbess of Nivelle, in Brabant, sent for St. Foillan and St. Ultan, brothers of our celebrated St. Fursey (Patron of Perrone), to teach <a href="/wiki/Psalmody" class="mw-redirect" title="Psalmody">psalmody</a> to her nuns. These two Irish monks complied with her request, and built an adjoining monastery at Fosse, in the diocese of Liege."</i> (* <i>A History of Irish Music</i>, p.&#160;12, William H. Gratten Flood, Dublin, 1906.)
</p>
