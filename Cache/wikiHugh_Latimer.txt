<p><b>Hugh Latimer</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1487</span> – 16 October 1555) was a <a href="/wiki/Fellow" title="Fellow">Fellow</a> of <a href="/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge" title="Clare College, Cambridge">Clare College, Cambridge</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a> during the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">Reformation</a>, and later <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> chaplain to <a href="/wiki/King_Edward_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="King Edward VI">King Edward VI</a>. In 1555 under the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Queen_Mary_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Mary I">Queen Mary I</a> he was <a href="/wiki/Burned_at_the_stake" class="mw-redirect" title="Burned at the stake">burned at the stake</a>, becoming one of the three <a href="/wiki/Oxford_Martyrs" title="Oxford Martyrs">Oxford Martyrs</a> of <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglicanism</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Latimer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Latimer was born into a family of farmers in <a href="/wiki/Thurcaston" title="Thurcaston">Thurcaston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Leicestershire" title="Leicestershire">Leicestershire</a>. His birthdate is unknown. Contemporary biographers including <a href="/wiki/John_Foxe" title="John Foxe">John Foxe</a> placed the date somewhere between 1480 and 1494.He later recalled that "my father...kept me to schole" <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and he started his studies in Latin grammar at the age of four, but not much else is known of his childhood. He attended the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cambridge" title="University of Cambridge">University of Cambridge</a> and was elected a fellow of <a href="/wiki/Clare_College" class="mw-redirect" title="Clare College">Clare College</a> on 2 February 1510.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He received the <a href="/wiki/MA_degree" class="mw-redirect" title="MA degree">Master of Arts degree</a> in April 1514 and he was ordained a priest on 15 July 1515. In 1522, Latimer was nominated to the positions of university preacher and university chaplain. While carrying out his official duties, he continued with theological studies and received the <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Divinity" title="Bachelor of Divinity">Bachelor of Divinity</a> degree in 1524. The subject of his <a href="/wiki/Disputation" title="Disputation">disputation</a> for the degree was a refutation of the new ideas of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a> emerging from the Continent, in particular the doctrines of <a href="/wiki/Philipp_Melanchthon" class="mw-redirect" title="Philipp Melanchthon">Philipp Melanchthon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Up to this time, Latimer described himself as "obstinate a papist as any was in England". A recent convert to the new teachings, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Bilney" title="Thomas Bilney">Thomas Bilney</a> heard his disputation and later came to him to give his <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Bilney's words had a great impact on Latimer and from that day forward he accepted the reformed doctrines.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg/220px-Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg/330px-Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg/440px-Latimer%27s_Pulpit_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="4608" /></a><figcaption>The pulpit from which Latimer preached in <a href="/wiki/St_Edward%27s_Church,_Cambridge" class="mw-redirect" title="St Edward&#39;s Church, Cambridge">St Edward's Church, Cambridge</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Latimer joined a group of reformers including Bilney and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Barnes_(martyr)" title="Robert Barnes (martyr)">Robert Barnes</a> that met regularly at the <a href="/wiki/White_Horse_Tavern,_Cambridge" title="White Horse Tavern, Cambridge">White Horse Tavern</a>. He began to preach publicly on the need for the translation of the Bible into English. This was a dangerous move as the first translation of the New Testament by <a href="/wiki/William_Tyndale" title="William Tyndale">William Tyndale</a> had recently been banned. In early 1528, Latimer was called before Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Wolsey" title="Thomas Wolsey">Thomas Wolsey</a> and he was given an admonition and a warning. The following year, Wolsey fell from <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s favour when he failed to expedite the annulment of Henry's marriage to <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon" title="Catherine of Aragon">Catherine of Aragon</a>. In contrast, Latimer's reputation was in the ascendant as he took the lead among the reformers in Cambridge. During Advent in 1529, he preached his two "Sermons on the Card" at <a href="/wiki/St_Edward_King_and_Martyr,_Cambridge" title="St Edward King and Martyr, Cambridge">St Edward's Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1535, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a>, in succession to an Italian absentee, and promoted reformed teachings and <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclasm</a> in his diocese. On 22 May 1538, at the insistence of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Cromwell</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> he preached the final sermon before <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Forest" title="John Forest">John Forest</a> was burnt at the stake, in a fire said to have been fueled partly by a Welsh image of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Derfel" title="Saint Derfel">Saint Derfel</a>. In 1539, he opposed <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Six_Articles_(1539)" class="mw-redirect" title="Six Articles (1539)">Six Articles</a>, with the result that he was forced to resign his bishopric and imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> (where he was again in 1546).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg/220px-Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg/330px-Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg/440px-Joseph_Martin_Kronheim_-_Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs_Plate_V_-_Latimer_before_the_Council.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2324" data-file-height="3272" /></a><figcaption>"Latimer before the Council", from an 1887 edition of <a href="/wiki/Foxe%27s_Book_of_Martyrs" title="Foxe&#39;s Book of Martyrs">Foxe's <i>Book of Martyrs</i></a>, illustrated by Kronheim.</figcaption></figure><p> He then served as chaplain to <a href="/wiki/Catherine_Willoughby" class="mw-redirect" title="Catherine Willoughby">Katherine Duchess of Suffolk</a>.  However, when Edward VI's sister <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Mary I</a> came to the throne, he was tried for his beliefs and teachings in <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prison" title="Prison">imprisoned</a>. In October 1555 he was <a href="/wiki/Execution_by_burning" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution by burning">burned at the stake</a> outside <a href="/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford" title="Balliol College, Oxford">Balliol College, Oxford</a>.
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trial">Trial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Latimer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Trial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 14 April 1554, commissioners from the papal party (including Edmund Bonner and Stephen Gardiner) began an examination of Latimer, Ridley, and Cranmer.  Latimer, hardly able to sustain a debate at his age, responded to the council in writing.  He argued that the doctrines of the <a href="/wiki/Real_presence_of_Christ_in_the_Eucharist" title="Real presence of Christ in the Eucharist">real presence of Christ</a> in the mass, transubstantiation, and the propitiatory merit of the mass were unbiblical.  The commissioners tried to demonstrate that Latimer did not share the same faith as eminent Fathers, to which Latimer replied, "I am of their faith when they say well... I have said, when they say well, and bring Scripture for them, I <i>am</i> of their faith; and further Augustine requireth not to be believed."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Latimer believed that the welfare of souls demanded he stand for the Protestant understanding of the gospel.  The commissioners also understood that the debate involved the very message of salvation itself, by which souls would be saved or damned:
</p>
<blockquote><p>After the sentence had been pronounced, Latimer added, 'I thank God most heartily that He hath prolonged my life to this end, that I may in this case glorify God by that kind of death'; to which the prolocutor replied, 'If <i>you go</i> to heaven in this faith, then <i>I</i> will never come hither, as I am thus persuaded.'<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Latimer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg/300px-Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="252" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg/450px-Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg/600px-Latimer_Ridley_Foxe_burning.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1546" data-file-height="1297" /></a><figcaption>The burning of Hugh Latimer and <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Ridley_(martyr)" title="Nicholas Ridley (martyr)">Nicholas Ridley</a>, from John Foxe's <i>Book of Martyrs</i> (1563)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Latimer was burned at the stake along with <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Ridley_(martyr)" title="Nicholas Ridley (martyr)">Nicholas Ridley</a>. He is quoted as having said to Ridley:
</p>
<blockquote><p>"Play the man, Master Ridley; we shall this day light such a candle, by God's grace, in England, as I trust shall never be put out."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The deaths of Latimer, Ridley and later <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cranmer" title="Thomas Cranmer">Cranmer</a>&#160;&#8211;&#32; now known as the <a href="/wiki/Oxford_Martyrs" title="Oxford Martyrs">Oxford Martyrs</a>&#160;&#8211;&#32; are commemorated in Oxford by the Victorian-era <a href="/wiki/Martyrs%27_Memorial" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrs&#39; Memorial">Martyrs' Memorial</a> near the actual execution site, which is marked by a cross in <a href="/wiki/Broad_Street,_Oxford" title="Broad Street, Oxford">Broad Street</a>, formerly the ditch outside the city's North Gate.
</p><p>Hugh Latimer said, "It may come in my days, old as I am, or in my children's days, the saints shall be taken up to meet Christ in the air, and so shall come down with Him again" (cf. 1 Thessalonians 4).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Latimer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Latimer and Nicholas Ridley are honoured with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Church_of_England)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (Church of England)">commemoration</a> on 16 October by the Church of England, the <a href="/wiki/American_Episcopal_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="American Episcopal Church">American Episcopal Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Canada" title="Anglican Church of Canada">Anglican Church of Canada</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The Latimer room in Clare College, Cambridge is named after him, as is <a href="/wiki/Latimer_Square" title="Latimer Square">Latimer Square</a> in central <a href="/wiki/Christchurch" title="Christchurch">Christchurch</a>, New Zealand.
</p>
