<p><b>Denise (Dionysia, Dionisia), Dativa, Leontia, Tertius, Emilianus, Boniface, Majoricus, and Servus</b> are venerated as martyrs by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.  They were killed in the late 5th century during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinitarian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> in <a href="/wiki/Proconsular_Africa" class="mw-redirect" title="Proconsular Africa">Proconsular Africa</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> <a href="/wiki/Vandals" title="Vandals">Vandals</a>, according to <a href="/wiki/Victor_of_Vita" class="mw-redirect" title="Victor of Vita">Victor of Vita</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  These martyrs were killed during the reign of Arian king <a href="/wiki/Hunneric" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunneric">Hunneric</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Victor, Denise was a beautiful and widowed noblewoman, who was killed during this persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Denise's son <b>Majoricus</b> was killed during the same persecution, as well as Denise's sister <b>Dativa</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Denise was brutally whipped by the authorities.
and then died at the stake with her little child, Majoricus, and her sister Dativa.<sup id="cite_ref-latin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-latin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Also killed were <b>Leontia</b>, daughter of Bishop Germanus of Perada (Paradana);<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> a doctor<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> named <b>Emilius</b> or Emelius ("Emilianus" according to <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>), brother-in-law of Dativa; <b>Tertius</b>, a monk of <a href="/wiki/Byzacena" title="Byzacena">Byzacena</a>; and <b>Boniface</b>, surnamed Sibidense, who has been identified as Boniface, bishop of Sicilibba in Proconsular Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Emilius and Tertius were flayed alive.<sup id="cite_ref-latin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-latin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two other figures associated with the same persecution were venerated on the same <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>: <b>Servus</b> or Servius, who was martyred at <a href="/wiki/Tuburbium" class="mw-redirect" title="Tuburbium">Tuburbium</a>; and <b>Victoria</b> or Victrix, who was tortured at <a href="/wiki/Culusi" title="Culusi">Cucusa</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (Colusitana, Culcitana).<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Servus was hung and dropped from ropes and then dragged through the streets, while Victoria was hung from the wrists above a fire. Victoria's husband begged her to abjure her faith and to think of her young children but she refused. Thinking her dead, the authorities left her on the ground. According to the legend, Victoria later recovered and stated that she had been miraculously cured by a young maiden.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Denise,_Dativa,_Leontia,_Tertius,_Emilianus,_Boniface,_Majoricus,_and_Servus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their cult was mentioned in the martyrologies of various ancient churches and in the martyrologies associated with <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a>, <a href="/wiki/Florus_of_Lyon" title="Florus of Lyon">Florus</a>, and Usuard.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The <i>Legendarium</i> associated with the convent of the canons regular at Böddeken records their legend in a <a href="/wiki/Codex" title="Codex">codex</a> dating from the 15th century (Biblioteca Teodoriana, <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The <i>Roman Martyrology</i> commemorates their feast day on December 6, although Servius’ name is recorded on December 7, and Victoria's name is completely left out.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
There was a cult in the late <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> devoted only to Emilianus, which was popular especially in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, and at the end of the 14th century, he was chosen as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> for a local society of pharmacists along with <a href="/wiki/Peregrine_of_Auxerre" title="Peregrine of Auxerre">Peregrine of Auxerre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  A church was built in Emilianus’ honor at Naples.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
