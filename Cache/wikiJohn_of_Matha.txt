<p><b>John of Matha</b>, OSST (1160–1213) was a French Catholic priest and cofounder of the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarians" title="Trinitarians">Order of the Most Holy Trinity</a>, initially dedicated to ransoming Christians who had been captured by <a href="/wiki/Barbary_pirates" title="Barbary pirates">marauders</a> from North Africa.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Between the eighth and the 15th centuries, <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Medieval Europe</a> was in a state of intermittent warfare between the Christian kingdoms of Southern Europe and the Muslim polities of North Africa, Southern France, Sicily and portions of Spain. According to James W. Brodman, the threat of capture or kidnapping, either by Muslim pirates or coastal raiders, or during one of the region's intermittent wars, was a continual concern for residents of <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Languedoc" title="Languedoc">Languedoc</a>, and other coastal provinces of mediaeval Christian Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-brodman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brodman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Raids by Muslim bands and armies was a near-annual occurrence.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The redemption of captives is listed among the corporal <a href="/wiki/Works_of_mercy" title="Works of mercy">works of mercy</a>. The period of the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>, during which so many Christians were in danger of falling into the hands of Muslims, witnessed the rise of religious orders vowed exclusively to this pious work.<sup id="cite_ref-moeller_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moeller-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg/250px-Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="375" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg/375px-Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg/500px-Sv_Jan_z_Malty.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1500" /></a><figcaption>Statues of John of Matha, <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Valois" title="Felix of Valois">Felix of Valois</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Ivan&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Ivan (page does not exist)">Saint Ivan</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_Work">Life and Work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life and Work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Most of the story of John of Matha's life is based on legends that circulated after his death. It is reasonably certain that he was born to noble parents Euphemius and Martha at <a href="/wiki/Faucon-de-Barcelonnette" title="Faucon-de-Barcelonnette">Faucon-de-Barcelonnette</a>, on the borders of <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a> on 23 June 1169. He was baptised as John, in honour of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>. His father Euphemius sent him to <a href="/wiki/Aix-en-Provence" title="Aix-en-Provence">Aix-en-Provence</a>, where he learned grammar, fencing, riding, and other skills fit for a young nobleman. It is said that while there, he gave the poor a considerable part of the money his parents sent him, and he visited the hospital every Friday to assist the penniless sick.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He studied theology at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Holy orders in the Catholic Church">ordained</a> a priest at the age of 32 in December 1192.<sup id="cite_ref-dematha_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dematha-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to Trinitarian tradition, John celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 28 January 1193. During that Mass, he had a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(spirituality)" title="Vision (spirituality)">vision</a> of Christ holding by the hand two chained captives, one a <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moor</a>, the other a Christian (the Crusades were in full force at the time). The Christian captive carried a staff with a red and blue cross. After the Mass, John decided to devote himself to the task of ransoming Christian captives from the Moors.<sup id="cite_ref-dematha_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dematha-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Before entering upon this work, he thought it needful to spend some time in retirement, prayer, and mortification; having heard of the holy hermit <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Valois" title="Felix of Valois">Felix of Valois</a>, who lived in a great wood near Gandelu in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Meaux" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Meaux">Diocese of Meaux</a>, he went to him and requested instruction in the practice of perfection.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Order_of_the_Most_Holy_Trinity">Order of the Most Holy Trinity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Order of the Most Holy Trinity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>One day while walking with Felix, John had another <a href="/wiki/Vision_(spirituality)" title="Vision (spirituality)">vision</a>—a white stag appeared at a stream with a red and blue cross between its antlers.<sup id="cite_ref-dematha_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dematha-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> John disclosed to Felix the plans inspired by the vision during his first Mass of succouring captive Christians under slavery, and Felix offered his help to carry it out. They set out for Rome in the severe winter of 1197, to obtain the Pope's approval.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Trynitarze.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Trynitarze.svg/200px-Trynitarze.svg.png" decoding="async" width="200" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Trynitarze.svg/300px-Trynitarze.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Trynitarze.svg/400px-Trynitarze.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1000" /></a><figcaption>Emblem of the Trinitarian Order</figcaption></figure>
<p>On 17 December 1198, they obtained the preliminary approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> for a new order dedicated in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Blessed Trinity</a> for the redemption of Christian captives. The Order was fully approved in 1209; its first monastery was established at <a href="/wiki/Cerfroid" class="mw-redirect" title="Cerfroid">Cerfroid</a> north of Paris (at the site of Felix’s old hermitage), and the second in Rome at the Church of <a href="/wiki/San_Tommaso_in_Formis" title="San Tommaso in Formis">San Tommaso in Formis</a>. Christian slaves were first rescued by the Order in 1201. In 1202 and 1210, John travelled to <a href="/wiki/Tunisia" title="Tunisia">Tunisia</a> himself and brought back countless Christian slaves.
</p><p>Before his death, Trinitarian tradition says he met <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> and introduced Francis to the <a href="/wiki/Frangipani_family" title="Frangipani family">Frangipani family</a>, who became benefactors of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>. John of Matha died on 17 December 1213 in Rome, in the house of Thomas In Formis on the <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Caelian Hill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-trinitarians_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trinitarians-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Our_Lady_of_Good_Remedy">Our Lady of Good Remedy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Our Lady of Good Remedy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John founded the Trinitarians to visit slave markets, buy Christian slaves, and set them free in the name of the Holy Trinity. To do this, the Order needed large amounts of money, so they placed their fundraising efforts under the <a href="/wiki/Patronage_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Patronage of the Blessed Virgin Mary">patronage</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a>. In gratitude for her assistance, John of Matha honored Mary with the <a href="/wiki/Titles_of_Mary" title="Titles of Mary">title</a> of "Our Lady of Good Remedy." Devotion to Mary under this ancient title is widely known in Europe and <a href="/wiki/Latin_America" title="Latin America">Latin America</a>, and the Catholic Church celebrates her feast day on 8 October. Our Lady of Good Remedy is often depicted as the Virgin Mary handing a bag of money to John of Matha.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg/220px-John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg/330px-John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg/440px-John_of_Matha_sculpture_Mafra.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>A statue of John of Matha Saint at the main entrance of <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Mafra" title="Palace of Mafra">Mafra Palace</a>, Portugal</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1655, his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> from Rome to <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a> was approved in 1665, and his feast day on the current <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> is 17 December. <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholicism" title="Traditionalist Catholicism">Traditional Catholics</a> that observe the <a href="/wiki/Extraordinary_Form" class="mw-redirect" title="Extraordinary Form">Extraordinary Form of the Mass</a> still observe his old feast day on 8 February.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Matha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Order of the Most Holy Trinity is active on five continents and in many countries.<sup id="cite_ref-trinitarians_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trinitarians-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/DeMatha_Catholic_High_School" title="DeMatha Catholic High School">DeMatha Catholic High School</a> located in <a href="/wiki/Hyattsville,_Maryland" title="Hyattsville, Maryland">Hyattsville, Maryland</a>, <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">US</a> is named after him. It is also sponsored by the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarians" title="Trinitarians">Trinitarians</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint-Jean-de-Matha,_Quebec" title="Saint-Jean-de-Matha, Quebec">Saint-Jean-de-Matha</a> is a village in the province of Quebec.
</p><p>In 1865 American poet <a href="/wiki/John_Greenleaf_Whittier" title="John Greenleaf Whittier">John Greenleaf Whittier</a> composed an anti-slavery poem entitled "The Mantle of St. John De Matha".
</p>
