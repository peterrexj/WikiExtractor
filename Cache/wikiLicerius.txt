<p><b>Saint Lycerius</b> (sometimes also <b><a href="/wiki/Glycerius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Glycerius (disambiguation)">Glycerius</a></b>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Lizier</i>; <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Lliceri</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 548) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Couserans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Couserans">bishop of Couserans</a> in the late 5th and 6th centuries.
</p><p>Bishop Glycerius is recorded as having attended the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Agde" title="Council of Agde">Council of Agde</a> in 506.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his death in 548 he was buried in what is now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Lizier_Cathedral" title="Saint-Lizier Cathedral">Saint-Lizier Cathedral</a> and honored as a saint. He is the patron of a number of places in south-western France, notably <a href="/wiki/Saint-Lizier" title="Saint-Lizier">Saint-Lizier</a>, where the cathedral is also dedicated to him; Saint-Lizier d'Ustou (Ariège); and <a href="/wiki/Saint-Lizier-du-Plant%C3%A9" title="Saint-Lizier-du-Planté">Saint-Lizier-du-Planté</a> (Gers). His feast day is 27 August.
</p><p>Lycerius is also linked with <a href="/wiki/Lleida" title="Lleida">Lleida</a> (Lérida) in <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>, where there is a tradition (undocumented) of a bishop of the same name in the 3rd century. It is not clear to what extent the Catalan traditions reflect a confusion between two separate individuals, if indeed there is any foundation to them. In Lleida his feast is celebrated on 1 September. He is the secondary patron of <a href="/wiki/Lleida" title="Lleida">Lleida</a>, and the patron of <a href="/wiki/Sant_Antoni_de_Vilamajor" title="Sant Antoni de Vilamajor">Sant Antoni de Vilamajor</a>.
</p><p>According to tradition, which cannot be verified, he was a native of <i>Civitas Bigorra</i>, the present <a href="/wiki/Saint-L%C3%A9zer" title="Saint-Lézer">Saint-Lézer</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Tarbes" title="Tarbes">Tarbes</a>. He studied with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tarbes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tarbes">bishop of Tarbes</a>, and later with Quintí, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rodez" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rodez">bishop of Rodez</a>, who ordained him priest. On account of the sanctity of his life he was elected bishop of Couserans, and also administered the diocese of Tarbes during its vacancy from 506 to 541.
</p><p>He is credited with many miracles of healing.
</p><p>Lycerius is shown wearing bishop's robes standing between two bulls.
</p>
