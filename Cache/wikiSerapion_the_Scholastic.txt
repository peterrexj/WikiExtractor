<p><b>Serapion of Nitria</b> or <b>Serapion of Thmuis</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Σεραπίων</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Serapíon</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Серапион</span>) was an early <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">Christian monk</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nitria_(monastic_site)" title="Nitria (monastic site)">Nitria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Egypt (region)">Lower Egypt</a>, born in the third century AD. He was a companion of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the Monastery of Arsina in Nitria, and the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Thmuis" title="Thmuis">Thmuis</a>. His feast day in <a href="/wiki/March_21_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="March 21 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">March 21</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known about Serapion. He is quoted in four sections of the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>, where he is called <a href="/wiki/Ab_(Semitic)" title="Ab (Semitic)">Abba</a> Serapion,<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and is commemorated on March 21 in the <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://archive.org/details/ThePrologueFromOhrid_BishopNikolaiVelimirovich/page/n169/mode/2up?q=serapion">Prologue of Ohrid</a></i>, an Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">synaxarium</a> written by Saint <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">Nikolaj Velimirović</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Serapion (a variant of the name <i>Seraphim</i> derived from <i><a href="/wiki/Seraph" title="Seraph">Seraph</a></i>) was the abbot of the Monastery of Arsina (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Arseonita</i>), which at one point held as many as eleven-thousand monks.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was given the title "the Great" by the early Christian historians <a href="/wiki/Sozomen" title="Sozomen">Sozomen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius</a>.
</p><p>Serapion died c. 358.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Story">Story</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Story">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A famous story of Serapion is recounted in the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>. He is said of have paid a prostitute. But instead of engaging with her, he prayed for a long time in front of her and eventually <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity" title="Conversion to Christianity">converted</a> her to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. She later became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> at a monastery and practiced extreme <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> labors.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The same story also exists in a poetic Hymn of Praise in the <i>Prologue of Ohrid.</i><sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotes">Quotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Nitria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Quotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From the <i>Prologue of Ohrid</i>:
</p>
<ul><li>"Do not think that sickness is difficult; only sin is difficult. Sickness follows the sinners only in life, but sin follows the sinner into the grave."</li></ul>
<p>From the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>:
</p>
<ul><li>"When the soldiers of the emperor are standing at attention, they cannot look to the right or left; it is the same for the man who stands before God and looks towards Him in fear at all time; he cannot then fear anything from the enemy."</li></ul>
