<p><b>Matthew Carrieri</b>, (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it"><b>Matteo Carrieri</b></i>; ca 1420 &#8211; 5 October 1470) was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> noted for the "austerity of his life."<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the spiritual instructor of <a href="/wiki/Stephana_de_Quinzanis" title="Stephana de Quinzanis">Stephana de Quinzanis</a>, and like her, an alleged <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmatic</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthew_Carrieri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Giovanni Francesco Carrieri</b> in the city of <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> some time around 1420.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He took the name <b>Matthew</b> when he entered the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. His later success as a preacher was inarguable, attributable to the significant time he spent in spiritual exercises and meditation between preaching.<sup id="cite_ref-santoral_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santoral-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of the major events in Carrieri's life was his capture by a <a href="/wiki/Turkish_people" title="Turkish people">Turkish</a> <a href="/wiki/Privateer" title="Privateer">corsair</a>, while on a voyage from <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mores_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mores-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When asked to explain their purpose for being on board the ship, Carrieri spoke up so forcefully and convincingly, that the captain set him and the two friars accompanying him free. The friar then saw that the pirates were still keeping a woman and her daughter captive, planning to sell them into slavery in Algeria. He proposed to the captain that they keep him in their place. So shocked was the pirate captain by Carrieri's willingness to sacrifice himself, that he freed all five captives.<sup id="cite_ref-santoral_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santoral-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Carrieri was a very close associate of a noted <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Dominican tertiary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stephana_de_Quinzanis" title="Stephana de Quinzanis">Stephana de Quinzanis</a>, in her youth. He was responsible for her <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a>, and predicted that she would be his "spiritual heiress." The meaning of this statement was illuminated when Stephana also began to experience pains similar to Carrieri's, which, according to those concerned, were the result of his devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion of Christ</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santoral_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santoral-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Carrieri's <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> states that on 5 October 1470 he asked his superior for permission to die, and upon receiving this, he died.<sup id="cite_ref-santoral_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santoral-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthew_Carrieri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1482, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a> authorized the solemn transfer of Matthew's <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> and allowed formal liturgical celebration of Blessed Matthew Carrieri's <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)"><i>cultus</i></a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santoral_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santoral-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
