<p><b>Saint Tatiana</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> in 3rd-century <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Emperor_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor of Rome">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Severus_Alexander" title="Severus Alexander">Severus Alexander</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tatiana_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, she was the daughter of a Roman civil servant who was secretly Christian, and raised his daughter in the faith. This was dangerous, and one day the <a href="/wiki/Jurist" title="Jurist">jurist</a> <a href="/wiki/Ulpian" title="Ulpian">Ulpian</a> captured Tatiana and attempted to force her to make a sacrifice to <a href="/wiki/Apollo" title="Apollo">Apollo</a>. She prayed, and <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miraculously</a>, an earthquake destroyed the Apollo statue and part of the temple.
</p><p>Tatiana was then blinded, and beaten for two days, before being brought to a circus and thrown into the pit with <a href="/wiki/Damnatio_ad_bestias" title="Damnatio ad bestias">a hungry lion</a>. But the lion did not touch her and lay at her feet. She was then sentenced to death, and after being tortured, Tatiana was beheaded with a sword on January 12, around <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a> 225 or 230.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tatiana_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tatiana_of_Rome_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/260px-Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="174" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/390px-Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/520px-Tatiana_of_Rome_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1890" data-file-height="1265" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tatiana_of_Rome_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting showing the beheading of Tatiana of Rome from the <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></i> (c. 1000 AD)</div></div></div>
<p>Tatiana is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, and her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is on January 12 (for those churches which follow the traditional <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, January 12 currently falls on January 25 of the modern <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>). The miracles performed by Saint Tatiana are said to have converted many people to the fledgling religion. Her skull was translated in 1955 from <a href="/wiki/Bistri%C8%9Ba_Monastery" title="Bistrița Monastery">Bistrița Monastery</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Demetrius,_Craiova" title="Cathedral of Saint Demetrius, Craiova">Cathedral of Saint Demetrius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Craiova" title="Craiova">Craiova</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a>, and is enshrined with the relics of Saints <a href="/wiki/Sergius_and_Bacchus" title="Sergius and Bacchus">Sergius and Bacchus</a> and those of Patriach <a href="/wiki/Nephon_II_of_Constantinople" title="Nephon II of Constantinople">Nephon II of Constantinople</a>. 
</p><p>Saint Tatiana is <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of students. In <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tatiana_Day" title="Tatiana Day">Tatiana Day</a> is semi-formally celebrated as "Students' Day."
</p><p>The similarity of her life with those of <a href="/wiki/Martina_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Martina (saint)">Martina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prisca_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Prisca (saint)">Prisca</a> has led some<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2011)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> to question whether they may even all be the same person, or if perhaps similar <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> were assigned to them posthumously. There is no early evidence of <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> of either Martina or Tatiana in Rome, and Prisca (or Priscilla) is hard to identify.
</p>
