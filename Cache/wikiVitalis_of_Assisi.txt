<p><b>Vitalis of Assisi</b>, O.S.B. (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Vitale de Assisi</i>) (1295 – 31 May 1370) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin">Origin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Assisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Bastia_Umbra" title="Bastia Umbra">Bastia Umbra</a>, Vitalis as a youth was licentious and immoral; however, he attempted to expiate his sins by going on <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to various sanctuaries in Italy and Europe. When he returned to <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, he became a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monk at <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Subiaco, Italy">Subiaco</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Assisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vitalis then spent the rest of his life in the hermitage of Santa Maria di Viole, near <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, in utter poverty. His one possession was an old container that he used to drink water from a nearby spring. His reputation for holiness soon spread after his death. He was known as a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> against sicknesses and diseases affecting the bladder and genitals.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Assisi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 29 May 2011, a head preserved as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a>, allegedly that of Vitalis, was offered at auction with an estimate of €1,000 at Annesbrook House, Duleek, <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was sold to <a href="/wiki/Billy_Jamieson" title="Billy Jamieson">Billy Jamieson</a> for €3,500.
</p><p>In November 2016 the relic was once again purchased by an antiquities and oddities collector, and permanently resides in a private gallery located in the Eastern United States.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
