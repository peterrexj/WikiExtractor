					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
Saint <b>Acarius</b> (died 14 March 642) was a monk of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil Abbey</a>, who became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Doornik_and_Noyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Doornik and Noyon">bishop of Doornik and Noyon</a>, which today are located on either side of the <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">Franco</a>-<a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgian</a> border.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Acarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Acarius was born to a noble family of Burgundy. He entered the Abbey of Luxeuil and was a pupil of <a href="/wiki/Eustace_of_Luxeuil" title="Eustace of Luxeuil">Abbot Eustace</a>. <a href="/wiki/Audomar" title="Audomar">Audomar</a>, future <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Th%C3%A9rouanne" title="Ancient Diocese of Thérouanne">bishop of Thérouanne</a> was a classmate.
</p><p>While the date of his election is not known, it is assured that he was bishop of Doornik and Noyon in 627. He was an adviser to King <a href="/wiki/Clothar_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clothar II">Clothar II</a>. Apparently, Acarius had great influence on the spreading of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgic_Gaul" class="mw-redirect" title="Belgic Gaul">Belgic Gaul</a>. It was Acarius that recommended to Dagobert that <a href="/wiki/Audomar" title="Audomar">Audomar</a> be appointed <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Th%C3%A9rouanne" title="Ancient Diocese of Thérouanne">Bishop of Thérouanne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a strong supporter of the missionary <a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a>, but despite his influence could not prevent King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> from banishing Amandus for chastising him.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Amandus then went to Gascony. Along with Audomar and the King's counselor, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eligius" title="Saint Eligius">Éloi</a>, they were eventually able to persuade Dagobert to allow Amandus to return, and Acarius sent him to preach in the area of Tournai.
</p><p>As bishop, Acarius was especially attentive to the poor and afflicted, whose needs he enjoyed relieving and calming their suffering. He built a number of monasteries.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Acarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Soon after his death in November 640, he was <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a>. He was succeeded as bishop by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eligius" title="Saint Eligius">Eligius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His memorial is 27 Nov.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Acarius is invoked against a "cantankerous" mood.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Église Saints-Hugues-et-Achaire de Haspres
</p>
