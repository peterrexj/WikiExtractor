<p><b>Alice of Schaerbeek</b> (or <b>Adelaide</b> or <b>Aleydis</b>) (also known as <b>Alice the Leper</b>) (<a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Sint Aleydis</i>, <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Sainte Alix</i>), (c. 1220–1250) was a <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/Laybrother#laysister" class="mw-redirect" title="Laybrother">laysister</a> who is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the blind and paralyzed. Her feast day is 15 June.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alice_of_Schaerbeek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alice was born at <a href="/wiki/Schaerbeek" title="Schaerbeek">Schaerbeek</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant" title="Duchy of Brabant">Duchy of Brabant</a>. A frail child, at the age of seven, she was sent to be boarded and educated at the Cistercian <a href="/wiki/La_Cambre_Abbey" title="La Cambre Abbey">La Cambre Abbey</a>, where she remained for the rest of her life. The name of the abbey is derived from the <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i>Camera Sanctae Mariae</i> (Chamber of Our Lady) and is recalled in the park southeast of Brussels called "<a href="/wiki/Bois_de_la_Cambre" title="Bois de la Cambre">Ter Kamerenbos / Bois de la Cambre</a>" ("Chamber Woods").<sup id="cite_ref-McNamara_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNamara-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alice was a very pretty girl and lovable child, and soon showed a high intelligence and a great love for God. She became a laysister at the abbey. However, at some 20 years of age (c. 1240), she contracted <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a> and had to be isolated in a small hut. The disease caused her intense suffering, which she offered for the salvation of sinners and the souls in purgatory.<sup id="cite_ref-coming_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coming-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eventually she became paralyzed and afflicted with blindness. Her greatest consolation came from reception of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Eucharist" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Eucharist">Holy Eucharist</a>, although she was not allowed to drink from the <a href="/wiki/Chalice" title="Chalice">chalice</a> because of the presumed danger of contamination. However, it is said that the Lord appeared to her with assurance that He was in both the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">consecrated bread and the wine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McNamara_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNamara-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  She died in 1250, at the age of c. 30.
</p><p>The little we know about Alice's life comes from a Latin biography, composed c. 1260–1275.<sup id="cite_ref-Latin_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Latin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Authorship of the work is unknown. Scholars have typically believed that the author was an anonymous chaplain at La Cambre Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Roisin1947_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Roisin1947-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, Martinus Cawley suggests that Arnulf II of Ghistelles, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Villers_Abbey" title="Villers Abbey">Villers Abbey</a> 1270–1276, is its likely author.<sup id="cite_ref-CawleyIntro_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CawleyIntro-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Alice's biography was also translated into Middle Dutch, as witnessed by one extant manuscript.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By decree of 1 July 1702, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a> granted to the monks of the Congregation of St. Bernard Fuliensi the faculty to celebrate the cultus of Alice.<sup id="cite_ref-coming_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coming-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Devotion to Alice as a saint was approved in 1907 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Responses_to_Alice.27_Life"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Responses_to_Alice'_Life">Responses to Alice' Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alice_of_Schaerbeek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Responses to Alice&#039; Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alice' biography has been upheld as a model of Cistercian spirituality. Writing in 1954, <a href="/wiki/Trappist" class="mw-redirect" title="Trappist">Trappist</a> monk <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Merton" title="Thomas Merton">Thomas Merton</a>, for example, called the text "a practical and concise treatise of Cistercian asceticism."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Nevertheless, Alice of Schaerbeek was not particularly well known. Chyrsogone Waddell, reflecting on his entry into the Cistercian life in the 1950s, remarked on her obscurity, with Alice being mostly unknown even in devout Cistercian communities of the time.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In recent years, Alice has become more well known in medieval scholarship as a member of the so-called "Holy Women of <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>" corpus of thirteenth-century Latin biographies.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This situates Alice, and her spirituality, in terms of the <a href="/wiki/Beguine" class="mw-redirect" title="Beguine">beguine</a> movement, an innovation in medieval women's piety that saw women taking up an active religious life outside of monastic enclosure.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Primary_Sources:_Manuscripts">Primary Sources: Manuscripts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alice_of_Schaerbeek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Primary Sources: Manuscripts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margot H. King and Ludo Jongen detail 5 extant manuscripts of Alice's biography (4 in Latin; 1 in Middle Dutch)<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> These are:
</p>
<ul><li>Brussels, Bibliothèque Royale 4459–70 (3161), ff. 48–57: “Vita sanctimonialis Aleidis de Scarenbeke.” (Cistercian: Villers-la-Ville; Augustinian: Louvain; Val Saint Martin; 14th century; Latin). Also contains biography of <a href="/wiki/Christina_the_Astonishing" title="Christina the Astonishing">Christina the Astonishing</a>.</li>
<li>Brussels, Bibliothèque Royale 8609–20 (3206), ff. 139–146: "Vita sanctimonialis Aleidis de Scharenbeka". (Cistercian: Cambre St. Marie; after 1250; Latin). Also includes biographies of Christina the Astonishing and <a href="/wiki/Ida_of_Nivelles" title="Ida of Nivelles">Ida of Nivelles</a>.</li>
<li>Brussels, Bibliothèque Royale IV. 778, 11 ff.: <i>Vita sanctae Aleydis</i>. (ca. 1500. La Cambre; c. 1500; Latin).</li>
<li>Vienna, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek 12706–12707, ff. 248–252v: "Vita sanctimonialis Aleidis de Scarenbeke". (Rouge Cloître. Gielemans; 15th century; Latin). Also includes biographies of <a href="/wiki/Marie_of_Oignies" title="Marie of Oignies">Marie of Oignies</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ida_of_Louvain" title="Ida of Louvain">Ida of Louvain</a>, Ida of Nivelles, Ida of Léau, and <a href="/wiki/Lutgardis" title="Lutgardis">Lutgard of Aywières</a>.This manuscript has been digitized and is available to view online.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>‘s-Gravenhage, Koninklijke Bibliotheek 71 H 7, ff. 1–8v: "Sinte Aleijdes van Scarembeke leuen". (Brabant; 15th century; Middle Dutch).</li></ul>
