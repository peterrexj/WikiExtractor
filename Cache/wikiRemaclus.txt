<p><b>Saint Remaclus</b> (Remaculus, Remacle, Rimagilus; died 673) was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> missionary <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Remaclus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Remaclus grew up at the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Aquitaine" title="Duchy of Aquitaine">Aquitanian ducal court</a> and studied under <a href="/wiki/Sulpitius_the_Pious" title="Sulpitius the Pious">Sulpitius the Pious</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bourges" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bourges">bishop of Bourges</a>. In 625 he became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> at <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil Abbey</a> and was then ordained a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. Around 631 <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eligius" title="Saint Eligius">Saint Eligius</a> founded <a href="/wiki/Solignac_Abbey" title="Solignac Abbey">Solignac Abbey</a> and sent for monks from Luxeil, including Remaclus, who became the first abbot. The abbey followed the rule as at Luxeil. <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Audoin</a> wrote that Solignac quickly gained importance. It became particularly known for its silversmith's workshop.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Remaclus was then given charge as well of the abbey of Cougnon, in the duchy of Luxemburg. He served as an advisor to <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_III" title="Sigebert III">Sigebert III</a> of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> and persuaded him to establish the <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Stavelot-Malmedy" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Stavelot-Malmedy">double-monastery</a> of Malmedy in 648 and Stavelot in 650. Remaclus served as abbot of <a href="/wiki/Princely_Abbey_of_Stavelot-Malmedy" title="Princely Abbey of Stavelot-Malmedy">Stavelot and Malmedy</a>. Erected as an ecclesiastical principality attached to the Holy Roman Empire, its head bore the title of Prince-Abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stavelot_JPG01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Stavelot_JPG01.jpg/200px-Stavelot_JPG01.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Stavelot_JPG01.jpg/300px-Stavelot_JPG01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Stavelot_JPG01.jpg/400px-Stavelot_JPG01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stavelot_JPG01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Remacle Square, in Stavelot</div></div></div>
<p>In 650 <a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Maastricht" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Maastricht">bishop of Maastricht</a>, resigned his see to resume missionary work.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Remaclus was appointed in his stead. He brought with him <a href="/wiki/Saint_Hadelin" title="Saint Hadelin">Hadelin</a>, then abbot of Visé. His student <a href="/wiki/Theodard_of_Maastricht" title="Theodard of Maastricht">Theodard</a> succeeded him as abbot of the double monastery of Stavelot-Malmedy.
</p><p>Inhabitants of this troubled diocese had murdered some of his predecessors. However, Remaclus successfully spread monasticism in the region. He served as the spiritual teacher to <a href="/wiki/Trudo" title="Trudo">Saint Trudo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Babolen" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Babolen">Saint Babolen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theodard_of_Maastricht" title="Theodard of Maastricht">Saint Theodard of Maastricht</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Maastricht" title="Lambert of Maastricht">Saint Lambert</a>. Remaclus served as bishop for twelve years before resigning in favor of Theodard, and retiring to Stavelot around 662.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He died at Stavelot in 664. <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His relics lie in St. Sebastian Church in <a href="/wiki/Stavelot" title="Stavelot">Stavelot</a>. His feast day is September 3.
</p><p>There is a St. Remaclus Parish Church in <a href="/wiki/Cochem" title="Cochem">Cochem</a>, Germany. The abbeys of Stavelot-Malmedy ceased to exist at the time of the French Revolution. In 1950 St. Remaclus' Priory, Wavreumont was established between Stavelot and Malmedy.
</p>
