<p><b>Saints Cyrus and John</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Ciro e Giovanni</i>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">أباكير ويوحنا</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">Abākīr wa-Yūḥannā</i></span>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;304</span> or 311 AD<sup id="cite_ref-orthodoxengland_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodoxengland-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-onk_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-onk-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>. They are especially venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Church</a> and surnamed <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturge" class="mw-redirect" title="Thaumaturge">Wonderworking</a> <a href="/wiki/Unmercenaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Unmercenaries">Unmercenaries</a> (<i>thaumatourgoi anargyroi</i>) because they healed the sick free of charge.
</p><p>Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated by the <a href="/wiki/Copts" title="Copts">Copts</a> on the sixth day of <a href="/wiki/Month_of_Tobi" class="mw-redirect" title="Month of Tobi">Tobi</a>, corresponding to <a href="/wiki/January_31_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="January 31 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">31 January</a>, the day also observed by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>; on the same day they are commemorated in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>.  The Eastern Orthodox Church celebrate also the finding and <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> of their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> on 28 June.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_historicity">Life and historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyrus_and_John&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The principal source of information regarding the life, passion and miracles of Sts. John and Cyrus is the <i><a href="/wiki/Encomium" title="Encomium">encomium</a></i> written by <a href="/wiki/Sophronius_of_Jerusalem" title="Sophronius of Jerusalem">Sophronius</a>, Patriarch of Jerusalem (d. 638). Of the birth, parents, and first years of the saints we know nothing. According to the <a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a> "<a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxarium</a>", compiled by Michael, Bishop of Athrib and Malig, Cyrus and John were both <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandrians</a>; this, however, is contradicted by other documents in which it is said that Cyrus was a native of Alexandria and John of <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cyrus">Cyrus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyrus_and_John&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cyrus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cyrus practised the art of <a href="/wiki/Medicine" title="Medicine">medicine</a>, and had a workshop (<i>ergasterium</i>) which was afterwards transformed into a <a href="/wiki/Temple" title="Temple">temple</a> (church) dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Shadrach,_Meshach,_and_Abednego" title="Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego">Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego</a>. He ministered to the sick gratis and at the same time laboured with all the ardour of an apostle of the Faith, and won many from pagan superstition. He would say, “Whoever wishes to avoid being ill should refrain from sin, for sin is often the cause of bodily illness.” <sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This took place under the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Emperor Diocletian</a>. Denounced to the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> of the city he fled to <a href="/wiki/Arabia" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabia">Arabia</a> where he took refuge in a town near the sea called Tzoten. There, having received the <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> and assumed the <a href="/wiki/Monastic_habit" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic habit">monastic habit</a>, he abandoned medicine and began a life of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="John">John</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyrus_and_John&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: John">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John belonged to the army, in which he held a high rank; the "Synaxarium" cited above adds that he was one of the familiars of the emperor. Hearing of the virtues and wonders of Cyrus, he went to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> in fulfillment of a vow, and thence passed to Alexandria and then to Arabia where he became the companion of St. Cyrus in the ascetic life.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom_of_Cyrus_and_John">Martyrdom of Cyrus and John</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyrus_and_John&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom of Cyrus and John">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg/220px-Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="144" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg/330px-Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg/440px-Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="1772" data-file-height="1159" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cyrus_and_John_of_Alexandria_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Miniature from the <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></i></div></div></div>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Diocletian" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecution of Diocletian">persecution of Diocletian</a> three holy virgins, fifteen-year-old Theoctista (Theopista), Theodota (Theodora), thirteen years old, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodossia&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Theodossia (page does not exist)">Theodossia</a> (Theodoxia), eleven years old, together with their mother <a href="/w/index.php?title=Athanasia_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Athanasia (saint) (page does not exist)">Athanasia</a>, were arrested at <a href="/wiki/Canopus,_Egypt" title="Canopus, Egypt">Canopus</a> and brought to Alexandria. Cyrus and John, fearing lest these girls, on account of their youth, might, in the midst of torments, deny the Faith, resolved to go into the city to comfort them and encourage them in undergoing martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This fact becoming known they also were arrested and after dire torments they were all <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on the 31st of January.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyrus_and_John&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The bodies of the two martyrs were placed in the church of St. Mark the Evangelist in Alexandria.
</p><p>At the time of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_of_Alexandria" title="Cyril of Alexandria">St. Cyril</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">Patriarch of Alexandria</a> (412-444), there existed at <a href="/wiki/Menouthis" title="Menouthis">Menuthis</a> (Menouthes or Menouthis) near <a href="/wiki/Canopus" title="Canopus">Canopus</a> and present-day <a href="/wiki/Abu_Qir" title="Abu Qir">Abu Qir</a>, a pagan temple reputed for its oracles and cures which attracted even some simple Christians of the vicinity.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> St. Cyril thought to extirpate this idolatrous cult by establishing in that town the <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a> of <i>Saints Cyrus and John</i>. For this purpose he <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">moved</a> their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> (28 June, 414) and placed them in the church built by his predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Theophilus_I_of_Alexandria" title="Theophilus I of Alexandria">Theophilus</a>, in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Four_Evangelists" title="Four Evangelists">Four Evangelists</a>.
</p><p>Before the finding and transfer of the relics by St. Cyril it seems that the names of the two saints were unknown; it is certain that no written records of them were known prior to then.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In the fifth century, during the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a>, their relics were brought to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by two monks, Grimaldus and Arnulfus—this according to a manuscript in the archives of the deaconry of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Via_Lata" title="Santa Maria in Via Lata">Santa Maria in Via Lata</a>, cited by <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Bosio" title="Antonio Bosio">Antonio Bosio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bosio_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bosio-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Mai" title="Angelo Mai">Angelo Mai</a>, however, for historical reasons, justly assigns a later date, namely 634, under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I" title="Pope Honorius I">Pope Honorius I</a> and the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Heraclius" title="Heraclius">Heraclius</a> (Spicilegium Rom., III, V). The relics were placed in the suburban church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Passera" title="Santa Passera">Santa Passera</a> (a linguistic corruption of "Abbas Cyrus") on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Portuense" class="mw-redirect" title="Via Portuense">Via Portuense</a>. In the time of Bosio the pictures of the two saints were still visible in this church.<sup id="cite_ref-Bosio_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bosio-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Upon the door of the <a href="/wiki/Hypogeum" title="Hypogeum">hypogeum</a>, which still remains, is the following inscription in marble:
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Corpora sancta Cyri renitent hic atque Joannis</i></dd>
<dd><i>Quæ quondam Romæ dedit Alexandria magna</i><sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Their tomb became a <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> and place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>.  In <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a> Cyrus' name became <i>Difnar, Apakiri, Apakyri, Apakyr</i>; in <a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a>, '<i>Abaqir, 'Abuqir</i>. The city of <a href="/wiki/Abu_Qir" title="Abu Qir">Abu Qir</a>, now a suburb of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, was named after him.
</p><p>At Rome three churches were dedicated to these martyrs, <i>Abbas Cyrus de Militiis, Abbas Cyrus de Valeriis, and Abbas Cyrus ad Elephantum</i> — all of which were transformed afterwards by the vulgar pronunciation into S. Passera, a corruption of <i>Abbas Cyrus</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and those <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> which follow the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a>, Cyrus and John are among the saints who are commemorated during the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_Preparation" title="Liturgy of Preparation">Liturgy of Preparation</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a>.
</p>
