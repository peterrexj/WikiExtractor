<p><b>Agrippina of Mineo</b>, also known as <b>Saint Agrippina</b> (flourished 3rd century, died 262) was venerated as a virgin <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and Orthodox Christianity.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agrippina_of_Mineo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her legend states that she was a <a href="/wiki/Blonde" class="mw-redirect" title="Blonde">blonde</a> princess born of a noble <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> family, and that she was martyred during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Valerian_(emperor)" title="Valerian (emperor)">Valerian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bsb_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bsb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was either <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> or <a href="/wiki/Flagellation" title="Flagellation">scourged</a> to death.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her body was said to have been taken to <a href="/wiki/Mineo" title="Mineo">Mineo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, by three devout Christian women named Bassa, Paula,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and Agatonica,<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> their travels aided by angels.<sup id="cite_ref-jones_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jones-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> says that the reputed acts in the Greek <i>Menaia</i> are quite unreliable and no evidence is forthcoming of any cultus of early date.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her canonization date was 783 AD.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agrippina_of_Mineo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Agrippina is greatly honored in Sicily and, to a lesser degree, in Greece, where it is said that her <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> from Sicily to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-acoa_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acoa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her tomb became a popular pilgrimage destination, and she was invoked as a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> against evil spirits, <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Thunderstorm" title="Thunderstorm">thunderstorms</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bsb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bsb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is no longer celebrated in the Catholic Church, however it is celebrated in the Orthodox Church on June 23.<sup id="cite_ref-acoa_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acoa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are three Catholic Churches named after Saint Agrippina; the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Agrippina" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint Agrippina">Church of Saint Agrippina</a> is located in Mineo, Saint Agrippina'a parish church located in Rosario (Argentina) and the Chapel of Saint Agrippina di Mineo is located in <a href="/wiki/Boston" title="Boston">Boston, Massachusetts</a>. Immigrants from Mineo to the <a href="/wiki/North_End,_Boston" title="North End, Boston">North End</a> of Boston have celebrated their patron saint annually since 1914 during the first week of August.<sup id="cite_ref-jones_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jones-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
