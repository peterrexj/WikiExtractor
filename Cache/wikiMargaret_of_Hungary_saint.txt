<p><b>Margaret of Hungary</b>, OP (January 27, 1242 – January 18, 1270) was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican nun</a> and the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary" title="Béla IV of Hungary">Béla IV of Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maria_Laskarina" title="Maria Laskarina">Maria Laskarina</a>. She was the younger sister of <a href="/wiki/Kinga_of_Poland" title="Kinga of Poland">Kinga of Poland</a> (Kunegunda) and <a href="/wiki/Yolanda_of_Poland" title="Yolanda of Poland">Yolanda of Poland</a> and, through her father, the niece of the famed <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Hungary_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret was born in <a href="/wiki/Klis_Fortress" class="mw-redirect" title="Klis Fortress">Klis Fortress</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia_(medieval)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Croatia (medieval)">Kingdom of Croatia</a>, the eighth and last daughter (9th of 10 children) of the royal couple. They resided there during the Mongol invasion of Hungary (1241–42) as her father was also ruler of this land.<sup id="cite_ref-građevinar_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-građevinar-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her parents vowed that if <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> was liberated from the <a href="/wiki/Mongols" title="Mongols">Mongols</a>, they would dedicate the child to religion.
</p><p>The three-year-old Margaret was entrusted by her parents to the Dominican monastery at <a href="/wiki/Veszpr%C3%A9m" title="Veszprém">Veszprém</a> in 1245. Six years later she was transferred to the Monastery of the Blessed Virgin founded by her parents on Nyulak Szigete (Rabbit Island) near Buda (today <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Island" title="Margaret Island">Margaret Island</a>, named after her, and a part of <a href="/wiki/Budapest" title="Budapest">Budapest</a>; the ruins of the monastery can still be seen). She spent the rest of her life there,<sup id="cite_ref-WDL1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WDL1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> dedicating herself to religion and opposing all attempts of her father to arrange a political marriage for her with King <a href="/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia" title="Ottokar II of Bohemia">Ottokar II of Bohemia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Aldasy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldasy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She appears to have taken <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vow" title="Solemn vow">solemn vows</a> when she was eighteen years old.<sup id="cite_ref-Aldasy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldasy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  In marked contrast to the customs of her Order, she received<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> the <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">Consecration of Virgins</a> along with some other royals to prevent further attempts on the part of her father to have her vows dispensed by the pope for marriage.
</p><p>Many of the details of her life are known from the <i><a href="/wiki/Legend_of_Saint_Margaret" title="Legend of Saint Margaret">Legend of Saint Margaret</a></i>, written probably in the 14th century and translated from <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> to <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a> in the 15th. The only remaining copy of the legend is in the <i><a href="/wiki/Margaret_Codex" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret Codex">Margaret Codex</a></i> copied by the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> <a href="/wiki/Lea_R%C3%A1skay" title="Lea Ráskay">Lea Ráskay</a> around 1510. According to the legend, Margaret chastised herself from early childhood, wore an iron <a href="/wiki/Girdle" title="Girdle">girdle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hairshirts</a> and shoes spiked with nails and performed the most menial work in the convent.<sup id="cite_ref-Aldasy_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aldasy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The extravagance of the penances she undertook may have shortened her life.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She died on 18 January 1270.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Hungary_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was venerated as a saint soon after her death, e.g., a church dedicated to her in <a href="/wiki/Bocfolde" class="mw-redirect" title="Bocfolde">Bocfolde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zala_County" title="Zala County">Zala County</a>, appears in documents dated 1426. Steps were taken to procure her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> shortly after her death, at the request of her brother <a href="/wiki/Stephen_V_of_Hungary" title="Stephen V of Hungary">King Stephen V</a>. The necessary investigations were taken up between 1270 and 1276, but the canonization process was not successful, even though 74 <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> were ascribed to her intercession, most of them referring to curing illnesses, even someone coming back from the dead. Among those giving testimony were 27 people for whom miracles had been wrought. Unsuccessful attempts to canonize her were also made in 1640 and 1770. She was finally canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on November 19, 1943,<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> at that time the feast day of her aunt, Saint Elizabeth of Hungary.
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated by the Dominican Order.  Raised by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a> to a <i><a href="/wiki/Totum_duplex" title="Totum duplex">festum duplex</a>,</i> it is the day of her death, January 18.
</p><p>Her monastery was among those suppressed in 1782, part of the suppression of all monastic orders by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Joseph II, Holy Roman Emperor">Joseph II</a>. At that time, her remains were given to the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>. They were kept in Pozsony (today <a href="/wiki/Bratislava" title="Bratislava">Bratislava</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Buda" title="Buda">Buda</a>. The relics were partly destroyed in 1789 but some portions were preserved and are now kept in <a href="/wiki/Esztergom" title="Esztergom">Esztergom</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gy%C5%91r" title="Győr">Győr</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Pannonhalma" title="Pannonhalma">Pannonhalma</a>.
</p><p>In art Margaret is usually depicted in a Dominican nun's <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a>, holding a white lily and a book.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
