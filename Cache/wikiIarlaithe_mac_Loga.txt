<p><b>Jarlath</b>,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Pronunciation" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Pronunciation"><span title="Irish IPA transcription needed for this term.">needs  IPA</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> also known as <b>Iarlaithe mac Loga</b> (<i>fl.</i> 6th century), was an Irish priest and scholar from <a href="/wiki/Connacht" title="Connacht">Connacht</a>, remembered as the founder of the monastic <a href="/wiki/School_of_Tuam" title="School of Tuam">School of Tuam</a> and of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Tuam_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Tuam (Roman Catholic)">Archdiocese of Tuam</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> of which he is the patron saint. No medieval <i>Life</i><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag is a non sequitur, and needs to be integrated into the text in a way that is meaningful to the reader. (January 2022)"><i>non sequitur</i></span></a></i>&#93;</sup> for Jarlath is extant, but sources for his life and cult include genealogies, martyrologies, the Irish <i>Lives of <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">St Brendan of Clonfert</a></i>, and a biography compiled by <a href="/wiki/John_Colgan" title="John Colgan">John Colgan</a> in the 17th century.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Jarlath&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Irish genealogies record the existence of two saints named Jarlath: Jarlath son of Lugh (<i>Iarlaithe m. Loga</i>), founder of Tuam, and Jarlath son of Trian (<i>Iarlaithe m. Trena</i>), bishop of Armagh.<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jarlath of Tuam is said to have belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Conmha%C3%ADcne" class="mw-redirect" title="Conmhaícne">Conmhaícne</a>, who ruled over the greater part of what would become the parish of Tuam.<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Donegal_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donegal-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The other saint is said to have belonged to the <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_Fiatach" title="Dál Fiatach">Dál Fiatach</a> in east <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is identified as the third <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Armagh" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Armagh">Bishop of Armagh</a>, that is after <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Patrick</a>'s heir <a href="/wiki/Benignus_of_Armagh" title="Benignus of Armagh">Benignus</a> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Innisfallen" class="mw-redirect" title="Annals of Innisfallen">Innisfallen</a></i> record his death in the year 481.<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the two Irish <i>Lives</i> of <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan</a>, possibly of the 12th century, Jarlath is called a son of Lug, son of Trén, son of Fiacc, son of Mochta, and the First <i>Life</i> in the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Lismore" title="Book of Lismore">Book of Lismore</a> continues the pedigree by calling Mochta a son of Bresal, son of Siracht, son of Fiacha the Fair.<sup id="cite_ref-BrendanStokes_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BrendanStokes-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BrendanPlummer_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BrendanPlummer-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Both <i>Lives</i> substitute Imchada for Mochta and on this basis, Séamus Mac Mathúna argues that they go back to an original which conflates the genealogy of Iarlaithe mac Loga with that of his namesake in Armagh.<sup id="cite_ref-Mathuna134_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mathuna134-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<p><a href="/wiki/D%C3%B3nall_Mac_Giolla_Easpaig" title="Dónall Mac Giolla Easpaig">Dónall Mac Giolla Easpaig</a> suggests that the saints could refer to one and the same person:</p><blockquote><p>[...] both are given as the third bishop of <a href="/wiki/Armagh" title="Armagh">Armagh</a> [...] placename evidence from the Tuam area would tend to corroborate [this] view [...] the evidence suggests that there was a strong Patrician and, consequently, a strong Armagh influence in the Tuam area from the earliest <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> period [...] the fact that Iarlaithe was a bishop like Benignus of Kilbennan and Felartus of Donaghpatrick, would further indicate that Tuam [...] would have predated Brendan of Clonfert by almost a century.<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Brendan.27s_Irish_Lives"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Brendan's_Irish_Lives">Brendan's Irish <i>Lives</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Jarlath&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Brendan&#039;s Irish Lives">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jarlath appears briefly as a prominent figure in the medieval Irish <i>Lives</i> of Brendan of Clonfert.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Brendan is said to have visited Connacht to study under the famous Jarlath. One day, when Jarlath was in his old age, Brendan advised his mentor to leave the school and to depart in a newly built chariot until its two hind shafts broke, because there would be the place of his resurrection (<i>esséirge</i>) and that of many after him. Because Jarlath acknowledged the divinity and superior wisdom of his pupil, saying "take me into thy service for ever and ever", he gladly accepted his advice. His travel did not take him very far, as the shafts broke at <i>Tuaim da Ghualann</i> ("Mound of two shoulders"), that is, at Tuam.<sup id="cite_ref-BrendanStokes_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BrendanStokes-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BrendanPlummer_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BrendanPlummer-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jarlath died, "full of days", on 26 December, circa 540, aged about 90 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-Rev._John_Healy_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rev._John_Healy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In attributing a leading role to St Brendan in the foundation of Tuam, the <i>Lives</i> suggest that the see of Tuam was united with but subordinate to that of <a href="/wiki/Annaghdown" title="Annaghdown">Annaghdown</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mathuna157-8_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mathuna157-8-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tuam achieved the status of the principal see of Connacht only in 1152 at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Kells-Mellifont" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Kells-Mellifont">Synod of Kells-Mellifont</a>, while <a href="/wiki/Annaghdown" title="Annaghdown">Annaghdown</a> became an independent diocesan seat at the Synod of Dublin in 1192. In this light, the assertion in the <i>Lives</i> has been read as reflecting circumstances in the 12th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Mathuna157-8_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mathuna157-8-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_Tuam">Foundation of Tuam</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Jarlath&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundation of Tuam">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg/200px-Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg.png" decoding="async" width="200" height="222" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg/300px-Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg/400px-Tuam_Coat_of_Arms.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="379" data-file-height="420" /></a><figcaption>Jarlath's broken wheel on Tuam Coat of Arms</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/John_Colgan" title="John Colgan">John Colgan</a> drew up a memoir of the saint in his  <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum_Hiberniae" title="Acta Sanctorum Hiberniae">Acta Sanctorum Hiberniae</a></i> (1645). Jarlath is said to have studied under St <a href="/wiki/Benignus_of_Kilbannon" class="mw-redirect" title="Benignus of Kilbannon">Benignus</a> at <a href="/wiki/Kilbennen" title="Kilbennen">Kilbennen</a>, disciple of St Patrick.
</p><p>
Afterwards, he founded his first monastery at Cluainfois (<a href="/wiki/Cloonfush" title="Cloonfush">Cloonfush</a>), near <a href="/wiki/Tuam" title="Tuam">Tuam</a>, while his principal seat came to be at Tuam. His monastic school is said to have attracted scholars from all parts of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, including such students as <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ardfert" title="Ardfert">Ardfert</a> and <a href="/wiki/Colman_of_Cloyne" class="mw-redirect" title="Colman of Cloyne">Colman</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cloyne" title="Cloyne">Cloyne</a>. On the significance of the place-name Tuam, <a href="/wiki/D%C3%B3nall_Mac_Giolla_Easpaig" title="Dónall Mac Giolla Easpaig">Dónall Mac Giolla Easpaig</a> posits:</p><blockquote><p>"[t]he first element in the placename <i>Tuaim Dá Ualann/Ghualann</i> referred to a <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> burial-ground similar to that designated by the second element of <i>Cluain Fearta</i> (see <a href="/wiki/Clonfert" title="Clonfert">Clonfert</a>). If so Tuam offers another example of an early church being built on or near a pre-Christian sacred site."<sup id="cite_ref-Easpaig802-3_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easpaig802-3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Despite his fame, Jarlath left Cloonfush to study under <a href="/wiki/Enda_of_Aran" title="Enda of Aran">Enda of Aran</a> around 495.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In the 520s, he retired to Tuam. He chose Tuam because the wheel of his chariot broke there. Jarlath is included in the second order of Irish saints, which implies that he must have lived prior to the year 540.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A poem ascribed to Cuimmín of Coindeire, which is also cited in Ó Cléirigh's <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Donegal" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrology of Donegal">Martyrology of Donegal</a></i>, states that Jarlath was known for his generosity and devotion to prayer ("three hundred <a href="/wiki/Genuflexions" class="mw-redirect" title="Genuflexions">genuflexions</a> every night, and three hundred genuflexions every day").<sup id="cite_ref-Donegal_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donegal-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In the <i>Martyrology of Donegal</i>, he is credited with having predicted the names of his successors, including those of three 'heretical' bishops and one Máel.<sup id="cite_ref-Donegal_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donegal-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Similarly, his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> in the <i>"Great <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxaristes</a> of the Orthodox Church"</i> records that as a result of his great <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> and devotion to prayer he was granted the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_gift" title="Spiritual gift">gift of prophecy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MEGAS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MEGAS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast-day">Feast-day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Jarlath&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Feast-day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Jarlath's feast day is 6 June, which is the date of the <a href="/wiki/Translation" title="Translation">translation</a> of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> to a church specially built in his honour next to the Cathedral of Tuam.<sup id="cite_ref-MEGAS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MEGAS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His remains were encased in a silver shrine, from which the 13th-century church gained the name <i>Teampul na scrín</i>, that is the "church of the shrine", a perpetual vicarage united to the <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebend</a> of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Kilmainemore&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Kilmainemore (page does not exist)">Kilmainemore</a> in 1415.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In a note added to the <i><a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9lire_%C3%93engusso" class="mw-redirect" title="Félire Óengusso">Félire Óengusso</a></i> and in other martyrologies, Jarlath's feast-day was recorded as 25 or 26 December.<sup id="cite_ref-Donegal_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donegal-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Jarlath_in_the_21st_century">Jarlath in the 21st century</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Jarlath&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Jarlath in the 21st century">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first St Jarlath's Festival in Tuam, organised by the Energise Tuam community group, was organised for Saturday 7 June 2008. This included a pageant/parade from <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Assumption_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary,_Tuam" title="Cathedral of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Tuam">Tuam Cathedral</a> through the streets of the town, a school's art competition to raise awareness of the saint and local cultural heritage, and street entertainment.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Jarlath's broken wheel is a heraldic symbol of Tuam, and is included on the crest of many local organisations, including Tuam Town Council.
</p><p>St Jarlath Road, a residential street in <a href="/wiki/Cabra,_Dublin" title="Cabra, Dublin">Cabra</a> in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> 7 is named in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oakland,_California" title="Oakland, California">Oakland, California</a> in the United States and one in <a href="/wiki/Yarragon,_Victoria" title="Yarragon, Victoria">Yarragon, Australia</a> are named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
