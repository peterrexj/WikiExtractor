<p><b>Saint Innocent of Alaska</b> (August 26, 1797 &#8211; March 31, 1879, O.S.), also known as <b>Saint Innocent Metropolitan of Moscow</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <b>Святитель Иннокентий Митрополит Московский</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox">Russian Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christianity)">missionary</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest#Catholicism_and_Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Priest">priest</a>, then the first <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Orthodox_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Orthodox Church)">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> in the Americas, and finally the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Moscow_and_all_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Moscow and all Russia">Metropolitan of Moscow and all Russia</a>. Remembered for his missionary work, scholarship, and leadership in <a href="/wiki/Alaska" title="Alaska">Alaska</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Far_East" title="Russian Far East">Russian Far East</a> during the 19th century, he is known for his abilities as a scholar, <a href="/wiki/Linguist" class="mw-redirect" title="Linguist">linguist</a>, and administrator, as well as his great zeal for his work.
</p><p>As a missionary priest he took his wife and family with him. In these territories he learned several languages and dialects of the indigenous peoples. He wrote many of the earliest scholarly works about the native peoples of Alaska, including dictionaries and grammars for their languages for which he devised writing systems; also, he wrote religious works in, and translated parts of the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a> into, several of these languages. His books were published beginning in 1840.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Innocent was born Ivan Evseyevich Popov (Иван Евсеевич Попов) on August 26, 1797, into the family of a church server in the village of Anginskoye, Verkholensk District, <a href="/wiki/Irkutsk_Oblast" title="Irkutsk Oblast">Irkutsk Province</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>. His father, Evsey Popov, died when Ivan was six, and Ivan lived with his uncle, the parish deacon, in Anga.<sup id="cite_ref-Ioanna_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ioanna-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 1807 at age 10, Ivan entered the <a href="/wiki/Irkutsk" title="Irkutsk">Irkutsk</a> <a href="/wiki/Theological_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Theological Seminary">Theological Seminary</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-oca_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> where the rector renamed him Veniaminov in honor of the recently deceased Bishop Veniamin of Irkutsk.
</p><p>In 1817, he married a local priest's daughter named Catherine. On May 18 that year, Ivan Veniaminov was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> of the Church of the Annunciation in <a href="/wiki/Irkutsk" title="Irkutsk">Irkutsk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After completing his studies in 1818, Veniaminov was appointed a teacher in a <a href="/wiki/Parochial_school" title="Parochial school">parish school</a>. On May 18, 1821, he was ordained a priest to serve in the Church of the Annunciation in Irkutsk. In Russian he was known as Father <i>Ioann,</i> the religious version of Ivan.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry_in_Alaska">Ministry in Alaska</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ministry in Alaska">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the beginning of 1823, Bishop Michael of Irkutsk received instructions to send a priest to the island of <a href="/wiki/Unalaska" class="mw-redirect" title="Unalaska">Unalaska</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Aleutian_Islands" title="Aleutian Islands">Aleutian Islands</a> of Alaska. Father Ioann Veniaminov volunteered to go and on May 7, 1823, he departed from Irkutsk, accompanied by his aging mother, his brother Stefan, his wife, and their son, Innocent, an infant. After a difficult yearlong journey by land and water, they arrived at Unalaska on July 29, 1824.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Father Ioann and his family built and moved into an earthen hut, he set about studying the local languages and dialects. He trained some of his new parishioners in Russian building techniques. With them he undertook the construction of Holy Ascension Church,<sup id="cite_ref-Ioanna_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ioanna-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> which was finished the following July.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg/220px-Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="257" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg/330px-Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg/440px-Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg 2x" data-file-width="554" data-file-height="646" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Innokenty_Kamchatsky.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Innocent as <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Father Ioann's <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> included the island of Unalaska and the neighboring groups of <a href="/wiki/Fox_Islands_(Alaska)" title="Fox Islands (Alaska)">Fox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pribilof_Islands" title="Pribilof Islands">Pribilof islands</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  occupied by indigenous people who had been converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> before his arrival. They also retained many of their earlier religious beliefs and customs. Father Ioann often traveled between the islands in a <a href="/wiki/Canoe" title="Canoe">canoe</a>, battling the stormy ocean in the <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Alaska" title="Gulf of Alaska">Gulf of Alaska</a>.
</p><p>By his travels through the islands, Father Ioann Veniaminov became familiar with the local dialects.  In a short time he mastered six of the dialects. He devised an alphabet using <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic" class="mw-redirect" title="Cyrillic">Cyrillic</a> letters for the most widely used dialect, the Unangan dialect of <a href="/wiki/Aleut_language" title="Aleut language">Aleut</a>. In 1828, he translated portions of the Bible and other church material into that dialect. In 1829, he journeyed to the <a href="/wiki/Bering_Sea" title="Bering Sea">Bering Sea</a> coast of the Alaskan mainland and preached to the people there.
</p><p>In 1834, Father Ioann was transferred to <a href="/wiki/Baranof_Island" title="Baranof Island">Sitka Island</a>, to the town of Novoarkhangelsk, later called <a href="/wiki/Sitka,_Alaska" title="Sitka, Alaska">Sitka</a>. He devoted himself to the <a href="/wiki/Tlingit_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Tlingit people">Tlingit people</a> and studied their <a href="/wiki/Tlingit_language" title="Tlingit language">language</a> and customs. From his studies there, he wrote the scholarly works <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.asna.ca/alaska/research/zamechaniya.pdf"><i>Notes on the Kolushchan and Kodiak Tongues</i></a> and <i>Other Dialects of the Russo-American Territories, with a Russian-Kolushchan Glossary</i>.
</p><p>In 1836, Father Veniaminov made the journey south on a pastoral tour of the southernmost extent of <a href="/wiki/Russian_America" title="Russian America">Russian America</a>, landing at <a href="/wiki/Fort_Ross" class="mw-redirect" title="Fort Ross">Fort Ross</a> in Northern California. While there he conducted a census and performed the sacraments of marriage and baptism for the Russian population and local natives.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Kamchatka_ministry">Kamchatka ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Kamchatka ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg/200px-Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="256" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg/300px-Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg/400px-Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2902" data-file-height="3710" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Innocent_of_Alaska_4.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Contemporary portrait of Bishop Innocent.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1838, Father Ioann journeyed to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg" title="Saint Petersburg">St. Petersburg</a> (where on Christmas Day 1839 he was promoted to <a href="/wiki/Archpriest#Byzantine_Christianity" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a>), <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> to report on his activities and request an expansion of the Church's activities in <a href="/wiki/Russian_America" title="Russian America">Russian America</a>. While he was there, he received notice that his wife had died during her visit to Irkutsk. He requested permission to return to his hometown. Instead, church officials suggested that he take <a href="/wiki/Vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Vows">vows</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>.  Father Ioann at first ignored these suggestions, but, on November 29, 1840, he was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> a monk with the name <i>Innocent</i> in honor of <a href="/wiki/Innocent_of_Irkutsk" title="Innocent of Irkutsk">Saint Innocent, the first bishop of Irkutsk</a> (†1731, commemorated on November 26). He was elevated to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">Archimandrite</a>.
</p><p>On December 15, 1840, Archimandrite Innocent was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Kamchatka_Oblast" title="Kamchatka Oblast">Kamchatka</a>  and <a href="/wiki/Kuril_Islands" title="Kuril Islands">Kuril Islands</a> in Russia and the Aleutian Islands in Russian America.  His <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> was located in <a href="/wiki/Sitka,_Alaska" title="Sitka, Alaska">Novoarkhangelsk</a> (Sitka), to which he returned in September 1841. He spent the next nine years administering his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> as well as taking several long missionary journeys to its remote areas.
</p><p>On April 21, 1850, Bishop Innocent was elevated to <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a>. In 1852 the Yakut area was admitted to the Kamchatka Diocese. In September 1853 Archbishop Innocent took up permanent residence in the town of <a href="/wiki/Yakutsk" title="Yakutsk">Yakutsk</a>. Innocent took frequent trips throughout his enlarged diocese.  He devoted much energy to the translation of the scriptures and service books into the <a href="/wiki/Yakut_language" title="Yakut language">Yakut</a> (Sakha) language.
</p><p>In April 1865 Archbishop Innocent was appointed a member of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Governing_Synod" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Governing Synod">Holy Governing Synod</a> of the Russian Orthodox Church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Metropolitan_of_Moscow">Metropolitan of Moscow</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Metropolitan of Moscow">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On November 19, 1867, he was appointed the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Moscow">Metropolitan of Moscow</a>, succeeding his friend and mentor, <a href="/wiki/Filaret,_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Filaret, Metropolitan of Moscow">Saint Filaret</a>, who had died.  As metropolitan, he undertook revisions of many church texts that contained errors, raised funds to improve the living conditions of impoverished priests, and established a retirement home for clergy.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Innocent died on March 31, 1879. He was buried on April 5, 1879, at <a href="/wiki/Trinity-St._Sergius_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra">Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra</a>, outside <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Innocent_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On October 6&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> September 23&#93;&#160;1977 the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>, acting on the official request of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a>, <a href="/wiki/Glorification#Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Glorification">glorified</a> (<a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>) Innocent as a <a href="/wiki/Saint#Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Saint">saint</a>, giving him the title "Enlightener of the Aleuts, Apostle to America."
</p><p>Innocent's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated by the Orthodox Church three times a year: March 31, the date of his repose according to the Julian Calendar (April 13 N.S.); October 6, the anniversary of his canonization (September 23 O.S.); and October 18, the Synaxis of the Moscow Hierarchs (October 5 O.S.).
</p><p>Innocent is widely venerated with the epithet <a href="/wiki/Equal-to-apostles" title="Equal-to-apostles">Equal-to-apostles</a><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
as the Orthodox apostle of America.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">liturgical calendar of the Episcopal Church (USA)</a>, Innocent is honored with a feast day on March 30.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
