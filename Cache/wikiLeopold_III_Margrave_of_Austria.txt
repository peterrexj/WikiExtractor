<p><b>Leopold III</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Luitpold</i>, 1073 – 15 November 1136), known as <b>Leopold the Good</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Margrave_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Margrave of Austria">Margrave of Austria</a> from 1095 to his death in 1136. He was a member of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Babenberg" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Babenberg">House of Babenberg</a>. He was canonized on 6 January 1485 and became the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Upper_Austria" title="Upper Austria">Upper Austria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 15 November.<sup id="cite_ref-lingelbach_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lingelbach-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leopold_III,_Margrave_of_Austria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leopold was born at Babenberg castle in <a href="/wiki/Gars_am_Kamp" title="Gars am Kamp">Gars am Kamp</a>, the son of Margrave <a href="/wiki/Leopold_II,_Margrave_of_Austria" title="Leopold II, Margrave of Austria">Leopold II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ida_of_Formbach-Ratelnberg" title="Ida of Formbach-Ratelnberg">Ida of Formbach-Ratelnberg</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (April 2019)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Leopold_III,_Margrave_of_Austria#_Ida" title="Talk:Leopold III, Margrave of Austria">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> The Babenbergs had come to Austria from Bavaria where the family had risen to prominence in the 10th century. He grew up in the diocese of Passau under the influence of the reformer bishop <a href="/wiki/Altmann_of_Passau" class="mw-redirect" title="Altmann of Passau">Altmann of Passau</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-augcan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augcan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1096, Leopold succeeded his father as margrave of Austria at the age of 23. He married twice. His first wife, who died in 1105, may have been one of the von Perg family. The following year he married <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Waiblingen" title="Agnes of Waiblingen">Agnes</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the widowed sister of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry V, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry V</a> whom he had supported against her father <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>. This connection to the <a href="/wiki/Salian_dynasty" title="Salian dynasty">Salians</a> raised the importance of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Babenberg" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Babenberg">House of Babenberg</a>, to which important royal rights over the margraviate of Austria were granted. Also, Agnes had influential connections through her previous marriage to <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Duke_of_Swabia" title="Frederick I, Duke of Swabia">Frederick of Hohenstaufen</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-augcan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augcan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> one of her sons being <a href="/wiki/Conrad_III_of_Germany" title="Conrad III of Germany">Conrad III of Germany</a>.
</p><p>Leopold called himself "Princeps Terræ", a reflection of his sense of territorial independence. He was considered a candidate in the election of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Romans" title="King of the Romans">Kaiser</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> in 1125, but declined this honour.
</p><p>He is mainly remembered for the development of the country and, in particular, the founding of several monasteries. His most important foundation is <a href="/wiki/Klosterneuburg" title="Klosterneuburg">Klosterneuburg</a> (1108).<sup id="cite_ref-augcan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augcan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to legend, the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a> appeared to him and led him to a place where he found the veil of his wife Agnes, who had lost it years earlier. He established the <a href="/wiki/Klosterneuburg_Monastery" title="Klosterneuburg Monastery">Klosterneuburg Monastery</a> there. He subsequently expanded the settlement to become his residence.
</p><p>Leopold also founded the monasteries of <a href="/wiki/Heiligenkreuz_Abbey" title="Heiligenkreuz Abbey">Heiligenkreuz</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kleinmariazell" title="Kleinmariazell">Kleinmariazell</a> and <a href="/wiki/Seitenstetten" title="Seitenstetten">Seitenstetten</a> which developed a territory still largely covered by forest. All of these induced the church to <a href="/wiki/Canonize" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonize">canonize</a> him in 1485.
</p><p>Leopold also fostered the development of cities, such as Klosterneuburg, <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> and <a href="/wiki/Krems_an_der_Donau" title="Krems an der Donau">Krems</a>. The last one was granted the right to <a href="/wiki/Mint_(coin)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mint (coin)">mint</a> but never attained great importance.
</p><p>The writings of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_of_Melk&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Henry of Melk (page does not exist)">Henry of Melk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ava_(poet)" title="Ava (poet)">Ava of Göttweig</a>, which are the first literary texts from Austria, date back to Leopold's time.
</p><p>He is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Klosterneuburg_Monastery" title="Klosterneuburg Monastery">Klosterneuburg Monastery</a>, which he founded. His skull is kept in an embroidered reliquary, which leaves the forehead exposed; it also wears an <a href="/wiki/Archducal_hat" title="Archducal hat">archducal hat</a>.
</p><p>In 1663, under the rule of his namesake Emperor <a href="/wiki/Leopold_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Leopold I, Holy Roman Emperor">Leopold I</a>, he was declared patron saint of Austria instead of <a href="/wiki/Coloman_of_Stockerau" title="Coloman of Stockerau">Coloman of Stockerau</a>.
</p><p>The brothers <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Haydn" title="Joseph Haydn">Joseph</a> and <a href="/wiki/Michael_Haydn" title="Michael Haydn">Michael Haydn</a>, each of whom sang in the choir of <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen%27s_Cathedral,_Vienna" title="St. Stephen&#39;s Cathedral, Vienna">St. Stephen's Cathedral</a>, both sang in that capacity at Klosterneuburg on this day. Joseph Haydn later became the more famous composer of the two. Michael Haydn later (in 1805) wrote a mass in honour of Leopold, the <i>Missa sub titulo Sancti Leopoldi.</i>
</p><p>Since the death of Emperor Leopold I, the <a href="/wiki/King%27s_Feast" title="King&#39;s Feast">King's Feast</a> is celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> on Leopold's feast day.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Children">Children</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leopold_III,_Margrave_of_Austria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Children">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His first marriage, possibly to a lady of the von Perg family, was childless.
</p><p>By his second wife, <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Agnes of Germany">Agnes of Germany</a>, widow of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Duke_of_Swabia" title="Frederick I, Duke of Swabia">Frederick I, Duke of Swabia</a>:
</p>
<ul><li>Adalbert</li>

<li><a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Duke_of_Austria" title="Henry II, Duke of Austria">Henry II Jasomirgott</a> (1107–1177)<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Berta, m. Henry III, Burgrave of <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Babenberg" title="Agnes of Babenberg">Agnes</a> (c. 1108/13 – c. 1160/63), m. <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_the_Exile" title="Władysław II the Exile">Władysław II of Poland</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>

<li>Uta, wife of Liutpold von Plain</li>

<li><a href="/wiki/Conrad_of_Babenberg" title="Conrad of Babenberg">Conrad</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Passau" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Passau">Bishop of Passau</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Salzburg" title="Prince-Archbishopric of Salzburg">Archbishop of Salzburg</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>

Duke (later King) <a href="/wiki/Vladislaus_II,_Duke_and_King_of_Bohemia" title="Vladislaus II, Duke and King of Bohemia">Vladislaus II of Bohemia</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreed2016xiv-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>According to the <i>Continuation of the Chronicles of Klosterneuburg</i>, there may have been up to seven others (possibly from multiple births) stillborn or who died in infancy.
</p><p>In 2013, documentation regarding the results of DNA testing of the remains of the family buried in Klosterneuburg Abbey strongly favor that Adalbert was the son of Leopold and Agnes.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
