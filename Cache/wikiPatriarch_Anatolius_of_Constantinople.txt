<p><b>Anatolius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Late_Greek" title="Late Greek">Greek</a>: Ανατόλιος,&#160;? – 3 July 458) was a <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> (451 – 3 July 458). He is regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, by both the Orthodox and Roman Catholic Churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anatolius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anatolius was born at Alexandria. He was ordained a deacon by <a href="/wiki/Cyril_of_Alexandria" title="Cyril of Alexandria">Cyril of Alexandria</a>, and was present at the Third Ecumenical Council at Ephesus in the year 431.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He became Patriarch through the influence of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Dioscorus_I_of_Alexandria" title="Pope Dioscorus I of Alexandria">Pope Dioscorus I of Alexandria</a> with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a>, after the deposition of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Flavian_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Flavian of Constantinople">Flavian</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Ephesus" title="Second Council of Ephesus">Second Council of Ephesus</a>, having previously been the <a href="/wiki/Apocrisiarius" title="Apocrisiarius">apocrisiarius</a> or representative of Dioscorus with the emperor at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After his consecration, being under suspicion of <a href="/wiki/Eutyches" title="Eutyches">Eutychianism</a>, Anatolius publicly condemned the teachings not only of Eutyches, but also those of <a href="/wiki/Nestorius" title="Nestorius">Nestorius</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> subscribing to the letters of <a href="/wiki/Cyril" title="Cyril">Cyril</a> against Nestorius and of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_I" title="Pope Leo I">Pope Leo I</a> against Eutyches.
</p><p>In conjunction with Pope Leo, according to Zonaras (<i>Annals</i> iii), he requested that the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcian" title="Marcian">Marcian</a> summon a general council against Dioscorus and the Eutychians, but the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Imperial</a> letter instructing Anatolius in the preparations for the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a> only mentions Pope Leo (<a href="/wiki/Philippe_Labbe" title="Philippe Labbe">Philippe Labbe</a>, <i>Conc. Max. Tom.</i> iv.). In this council Anatolius presided in conjunction with the Roman legates (Labbe, Conc. Max. iv.; Evagr. H. E. ii. 4, 18; Niceph. H. E. xv. 18). By the famous 28th canon, passed at the conclusion of the council, Constantinople was made equal in dignity with Rome, "second in eminence and power to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome" title="Diocese of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-Noble214_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Noble214-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This displaced the traditional order of authority of the much older sees of Antioch and Alexandria. Hence arose the controversy between Anatolius and the Roman <a href="/wiki/Pontiff" title="Pontiff">pontiff</a>. However, the third canon of the earlier <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople" title="First Council of Constantinople">First Council of Constantinople</a> of 381 stated that "The Bishop of Constantinople, however, shall have the prerogative of honour after the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a> because <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> is <a href="/wiki/New_Rome" title="New Rome">New Rome</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Eastern position could be characterized as being political in nature, as opposed to a doctrinal view.
</p><p>Leo complained to Marcian (Ep. 54) and to <a href="/wiki/Pulcheria" title="Pulcheria">Pulcheria</a> (Ep. 55) that Anatolius had outstepped his jurisdiction by consecrating <a href="/w/index.php?title=Patriarch_Maximinus_II_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Patriarch Maximinus II of Antioch (page does not exist)">Maximinus II</a> as <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">Patriarch of Antioch</a>, as well as protesting to Anatolius (Ep. 53).
</p><p>Following the council of Chalcedon, Anatolius received a letter signed by several Egyptian bishops, asking his assistance against <a href="/wiki/Pope_Timothy_II_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Timothy II of Alexandria">Timothy</a>, who was usurping the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">Patriarch of Alexandria</a> (Labbe, Conc. Max. iv. iii. 23, p.&#160;897), as a result Anatolius wrote to the emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_I_of_the_Byzantine_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo I of the Byzantine Empire">Leo</a> against Timothy (Labbe, iii. 26, p.&#160;905). The circular of the emperor requesting the advice of Anatolius on the turbulent state of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> is given by <a href="/wiki/Evagrius_Scholasticus" title="Evagrius Scholasticus">Evagrius</a> (H. E. ii. 9), and by <a href="/wiki/Nicephorus_Callistus_Xanthopoulos" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos">Nicephorus</a> (H. E. xv. 18). <a href="/wiki/Edward_Gibbon" title="Edward Gibbon">Edward Gibbon</a> states that the crowning of Leo on his accession by Anatolius is the first instance of the kind on record (<a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Confessor" title="Theophanes the Confessor">Theophanes</a>, <i>Chronicle</i> p.&#160;95). When he was in danger of death he was restored to health by <a href="/wiki/Daniel_the_Stylite" title="Daniel the Stylite">Daniel the Stylite</a>, who came to Constantinople to see him.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The followers of Dioscorus are said to have killed him in 458.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anatolius was credited with composing a few <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymns</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Strong_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Strong-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
