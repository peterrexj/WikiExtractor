<p><b>Margaret Mary Alacoque</b>, VHM (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Marguerite-Marie Alacoque</i>) (22 July 1647 – 17 October 1690), was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_France" title="Catholic Church in France">French Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitation</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a> who promoted <a href="/wiki/Catholic_devotions" title="Catholic devotions">devotion</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> in its modern form.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Alacoque was born in 1647 in <a href="/wiki/Hautecour,_Jura" title="Hautecour, Jura">L'Hautecour, Burgundy, France</a>, now part of the <a href="/wiki/Commune_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Commune in France">commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Verosvres" title="Verosvres">Verosvres</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy" title="Duchy of Burgundy">Duchy of Burgundy</a>. She was the fifth of seven children, and only daughter of Claude and Philiberte Lamyn Alacoque.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her father was a well-to-do notary. Her godmother was the Countess of Corcheval. Margaret was described as showing intense love for the Blessed Sacrament from early childhood.
</p><p>When Margaret was eight years old, her father died of pneumonia. She was sent to a convent school run by the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> in Charolles, where she made her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> at the age of nine. She later contracted <a href="/wiki/Rheumatic_fever" title="Rheumatic fever">rheumatic fever</a> which confined her to bed for four years. At the end of this period, having made a vow to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> to consecrate herself to religious life, it is said she was instantly restored to perfect health.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In recognition of this favor, she added the name "Mary" to her baptismal name of Margaret. According to her later account of her life, she had <a href="/wiki/Visions_of_Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Visions of Jesus Christ">visions of Jesus Christ</a>, which she thought were a normal part of human experience and continued to practice austerity.
</p><p>With the death of Alacoque's father, the family's assets were held by an uncle who refused to hand them over, plunging her family into poverty. During this time, her only consolation was frequent visits to pray before the Blessed Sacrament in the local church. When she was 17, however, her brother came of age, took undisputed possession of the home and things improved. Her mother encouraged her to socialize, in the hopes of her finding a suitable husband. Out of obedience, and believing that her childhood vow was no longer binding, she began to accompany her brothers in the social events, attending dances and balls.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One night, after returning home from a ball for <a href="/wiki/Carnival" title="Carnival">Carnival</a> dressed in her finery, she experienced a vision of Christ, scourged and bloody. He reproached her for her forgetfulness of him; yet he also reassured her by demonstrating that his heart was filled with love for her, because of the childhood promise she had made to his Blessed Mother. As a result, she determined to fulfill her vow and entered, when almost 24 years of age, the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitation</a> Convent at <a href="/wiki/Paray-le-Monial" title="Paray-le-Monial">Paray-le-Monial</a> on 25 May 1671, intending to become a nun.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg/280px-San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="199" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg/420px-San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg/560px-San_Michele_Santa_Margherita_Maria_Alacoque2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1652" data-file-height="1173" /></a><figcaption>Jesus Christ appearing to St. Margaret Mary, Church of San Michele, <a href="/wiki/Cortemilia" title="Cortemilia">Cortemilia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Alacoque was subjected to many trials to prove the genuineness of her vocation. She was admitted to wearing the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> on 25 August 1671, but was not allowed to make her religious profession on the same date of the following year, which would have been the usual course.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A fellow novice described Margaret Mary as humble, simple and frank, but above all kind and patient.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was finally admitted to profession on 6 November 1672. It is said that she was assigned to the infirmary and was not very skillful at her tasks.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Visions">Visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the monastery, Alacoque reportedly received several <a href="/wiki/Private_revelation" title="Private revelation">private revelations</a> of the Sacred Heart, the first on 27 December 1673 and the final one 18 months later. The visions revealed to her the form of the devotion, the chief features being reception of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Communion">Holy Communion</a> on the <a href="/wiki/First_Friday_Devotions" class="mw-redirect" title="First Friday Devotions">first Friday</a> of each month, <a href="/wiki/Eucharistic_adoration" title="Eucharistic adoration">Eucharistic adoration</a> during a "<a href="/wiki/Holy_hour" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy hour">Holy hour</a>" on Thursdays, and the celebration of the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Feast of the Sacred Heart">Feast of the Sacred Heart</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She stated that in her vision she was instructed to spend an hour every Thursday night in meditation on Jesus' <a href="/wiki/Agony_in_the_Garden" title="Agony in the Garden">Agony in the Garden</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gethsemane" title="Gethsemane">Gethsemane</a>. The Holy Hour practice later became widespread among Catholics.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 27 December 1673, the feast of St. John, Margaret Mary said that Jesus had permitted her to rest her head upon his heart, and then disclosed to her the wonders of his love, telling her that he desired to make them known to all mankind and to diffuse the treasures of his goodness, and that he had chosen her for this work.
</p><p>Initially discouraged in her efforts to follow the instruction she had received in her visions, Alacoque was eventually able to convince her superior, Mother de Saumaise, of the authenticity of her visions. She was unable, however, to convince a Benedictine and a Jesuit, whom Saumaise had consulted. Nor was she any more successful with many of the members of her own community.
</p><p>On 16 June 1675, Alacoque reported three specific requests of France, directly from his spiritual talks.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> These will have political and religious repercussions and will successively be realized under the royal, imperial and republican French regimes:<sup id="cite_ref-:0_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li>The first message is addressed to kings: "He desires to enter pompously and magnificently into the house of princes and kings, to be honored, as much as he has been outraged, despised and humiliated in his passion ... that the adorable Heart of his divine Son was received ... to establish his empire in the heart of our Great Monarch, from which he wants to serve for the execution of his designs."</li>
<li>The second message is: "to build a building where the painting of this divine Heart will be, to receive the consecration and the homage of the King and of the whole court ..."</li>
<li>The third message asks the King: "to be painted on his standards and engraved on his weapons to make him victorious over all his enemies, by bringing down at his feet the proud and superb heads, in order to make him triumphant to all the enemies of the Holy Church".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li></ol>
<p>Sometime around 1681 Alacoque felt compelled to write a personal testament, passionately donating her life completely to Jesus with her own blood. With the permission of her superior she used a pocket knife to carve the name of Jesus into her breast and used the blood to sign the document. The following account recalls this event:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>She herself wrote out the donation, and signed this humble formula: <i><span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span>Sister Peronne-Rosalie Greyfie, at present Superioress, and for whom Sister Margaret Mary daily asks conversion with the grace of final penitence.<span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span></i> This done, Sister Margaret Mary implored Mother Greyfie to allow her, in turn, to sign, but with her blood. The Mother having assented, Sister Margaret Mary went to her cell, bared her breast, and, imitating her illustrious and saintly foundress, cut with a knife the name of Jesus above her heart. From the blood that flowed from the wound she signed the act in these words: <i><span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span>Sister Margaret Mary, Disciple of the Divine Heart of the Adorable Jesus<span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span></i><sup id="cite_ref-:1_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Upset by the fact that the wounds which she had cut into her breast were beginning to fade, she attempted to reopen the original wounds on more than one occasion using a knife. But, having failed to open them to her liking, she decided to burn her chest with fire. This incident placed her in the infirmary: "Trembling and humbled, she went to acknowledge her fault. Mother Greyfie, true to her custom, apparently paid little attention to what Margaret said, but ordered her in a few dry words to go to the infirmary and show her wound to Sister Augustine Marest, who would dress it."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She eventually received the support of <a href="/wiki/Claude_de_la_Colombi%C3%A8re" class="mw-redirect" title="Claude de la Colombière">Claude de la Colombière</a>, the community's <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> for a time, who declared that the visions were genuine. In 1683, opposition in the community ended when Mother Melin was elected Superior and named Margaret Mary her assistant. She later became <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Novice Mistress</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and saw the monastery observe the Feast of the Sacred Heart privately, beginning in 1686. Two years later, a chapel was built at <a href="/wiki/Paray-le-Monial" title="Paray-le-Monial">Paray-le-Monial</a> to honor the Sacred Heart. Observation of the feast of the Sacred Heart spread to other Visitation convents.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1689, Alacoque received a private request from Jesus to urge the King of France, <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIV" title="Louis XIV">Louis XIV</a>, to consecrate the nation to the Sacred Heart, so that he may be "triumphant over all the enemies of Holy Church." Either Louis XIV never received the letter or he refused to reply.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Alacoque died on 17 October 1690.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg/240px-HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="339" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg/360px-HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg/480px-HerzJesu_mit_Droste_zu_Vischering_und_MMA.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1658" data-file-height="2342" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Mary_of_the_Divine_Heart" title="Mary of the Divine Heart">Blessed Mary of the Divine Heart</a> and St. Margarette Mary Alacoque adoring the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Heart of Jesus">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window,_Clonmel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg/220px-Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg/330px-Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg/440px-Margaret_Mary_Alacoque_window%2C_Clonmel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3888" data-file-height="5184" /></a><figcaption>Stained glass image of Mary Alacoque in <a href="/wiki/Clonmel" title="Clonmel">Clonmel</a>, Ireland</figcaption></figure>
<p>After Alacoque's death, the devotion to the Sacred Heart was fostered by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> even as it remained controversial within the Catholic Church. The practice was not officially recognized for 75 years.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The discussion of Alacoque's own mission and qualities continued for years. All her actions, her <a href="/wiki/Revelation" title="Revelation">revelations</a>, her spiritual <a href="/wiki/Maxim_(philosophy)" title="Maxim (philosophy)">maxims</a>, her teachings regarding the devotion to the Sacred Heart, of which she was the chief exponent as well as the <a href="/wiki/Apostle" title="Apostle">apostle</a>, were subjected to the most severe and minute examination. The <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Congregation_of_Rites" title="Sacred Congregation of Rites">Sacred Congregation of Rites</a> eventually recognized her <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtues</a>, granting her the title "servant of God". In March 1824, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XII" title="Pope Leo XII">Pope Leo XII</a> pronounced her <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> and on 18&#160;September 1864 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> declared her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blessed</a>. When her tomb was opened in July 1830, two instantaneous cures were recorded. Her body rests above the side altar in the Chapel of the Apparitions, located at the Visitation Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Paray-le-Monial" title="Paray-le-Monial">Paray-le-Monial</a>, which draws pilgrims from all parts of the world.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alacoque was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> in 1920, and in 1929 her <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgical</a> commemoration was included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="General Roman calendar">General Roman calendar</a> for celebration on 17&#160;October, the day of her death. In the reforms of 1969, the feast day was moved to 16 October. It is an optional memorial in the United States.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his 1928 <a href="/wiki/Encyclical" title="Encyclical">encyclical</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Miserentissimus_Redemptor" title="Miserentissimus Redemptor">Miserentissimus Redemptor</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> affirmed the Catholic Church's position regarding the credibility of her <a href="/wiki/Visions_of_Jesus_and_Mary" title="Visions of Jesus and Mary">visions of Jesus Christ</a> by speaking of Jesus as having "manifested Himself" to Alacoque and having "promised her that all those who rendered this honour to His Heart would be endowed with an abundance of heavenly graces".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alacoque's short devotional work, <i>La Devotion au Sacré-Coeur de Jesus</i> (Devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus), was published posthumously by J. Croiset in 1698 and has been popular among <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Catholics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the Caribbean island of Saint Lucia there are two flower festivals supported by their respective Societies. Each society has a patron saint on whose feast day the grande fete is celebrated. For the Roses it is the feast of St. Rose of Lima on 30 August; and for the Marguerites it is that of St. Margaret Mary Alacocque, 17 October.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Mary_Alacoque&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es"><a href="/wiki/Detente_bala" title="Detente bala">detente bala</a></i></span> amulet used by Spanish soldiers is said to derive from Alacoque's emblems.<sup id="cite_ref-Príncipe_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Príncipe-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/James_Joyce" title="James Joyce">James Joyce</a>'s short story "<a href="/wiki/Eveline_(short_story)" title="Eveline (short story)">Eveline</a>", in his book <i><a href="/wiki/Dubliners" title="Dubliners">Dubliners</a>,</i> a "coloured print of the promises made to Blessed Margaret Mary Alacoque" is mentioned as part of the decorations of an Irish home at the turn of the 20th century, testifying to Joyce's fine eye for the details of Irish Catholic piety.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
