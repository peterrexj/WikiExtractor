<p><b>Agnes of Montepulciano</b> (28 January 1268 &#8211; 20 April 1317)<sup id="cite_ref-Tiepolo_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tiepolo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a> in medieval <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, who was known as a <a href="/wiki/Miracle_worker" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracle worker">miracle worker</a> during her lifetime. She is honored as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Montepulciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agnes was born in 1268 into the noble Segni family in <a href="/wiki/Gracciano,_Montepulciano" title="Gracciano, Montepulciano">Gracciano</a>, <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> a <a href="/wiki/Frazione" title="Frazione">frazione</a> of <a href="/wiki/Montepulciano" title="Montepulciano">Montepulciano</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. At the age of nine, she convinced her parents to allow her to enter a Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of women in the city known as the "Sisters of the Sack", after the rough <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> they wore. They lived a simple <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplative</a> life. She received the permission of the pope to be accepted into this life at such a young age, which was normally against <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">Church law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1281, the lord of the castle of <a href="/wiki/Proceno" title="Proceno">Proceno</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Fief" title="Fief">fief</a> of <a href="/wiki/Orvieto" title="Orvieto">Orvieto</a>, invited the <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of Montepulciano to send some of their sisters to Proceno to found a new monastery. Agnes was among the nuns sent to found this new community.  At the age of fourteen she was appointed bursar.<sup id="cite_ref-dorcy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dorcy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1288 Agnes, despite her youth at only 20 years of age, was noted for her devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a> and deep life of prayer, and was elected as the prioress of the community. <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> There she gained a reputation for performing miracles; people suffering from mental and physical ailments seemed cured by her presence.  She was reported to have "multiplied loaves", creating many from a few on numerous occasions, recalling the Gospel <a href="/wiki/Miracle_of_the_loaves_and_fishes" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracle of the loaves and fishes">miracle of the loaves and fishes</a>. She herself, however, suffered severe bouts of illness which lasted long periods of time.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1306, Agnes was recalled to head the monastery in Montepulciano. Agnes reached a high degree of contemplative prayer and is said to have been favoured with many visions. After her return, she proceeded to build a church, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Novella" title="Santa Maria Novella">Santa Maria Novella</a> in <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, to honour the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Blessed Mother</a>, as she felt she had been commanded to do in a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(spirituality)" title="Vision (spirituality)">mystical vision</a> several years earlier.  She also had a vision of <a href="/wiki/Dominic_Guzman" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominic Guzman">Dominic Guzman</a>, under the inspiration of which she led the nuns of her monastery to embrace the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Augustine" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Augustine">Rule of St. Augustine</a> as members of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dorcy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dorcy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  She was frequently called upon to bring peace to the warring families of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1316, Agnes' health had declined so greatly that her doctor suggested taking the cure at the thermal springs in the neighbouring town of <a href="/wiki/Chianciano_Terme" title="Chianciano Terme">Chianciano Terme</a>. The nuns of the community prevailed upon her to take his recommendation. While many of the other bathers reported being cured of their illnesses, Agnes received no benefit from the springs. Her health deteriorated to such a degree that she had to be carried back to the monastery on a stretcher.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Montepulciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agnes died the following 20 April, at the age of 49. The Dominican friars attempted to obtain balsam (or <a href="/wiki/Myrrh" title="Myrrh">myrrh</a>) to embalm her body. It was found, however, to be producing a sweet odor on its own, and her limbs remained supple.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When her body was moved years after her death to the monastery church, it was found to be incorrupt.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her tomb became the site of pilgrimages.  
</p><p>Some fifty years after her death, a Dominican <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Raymond_of_Capua" title="Raymond of Capua">Raymond of Capua</a>, who served as <a href="/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrament of Penance (Catholic Church)">confessor</a> to <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>, wrote an account of Agnes' life. He described her body as still appearing as if she were alive. Catherine herself referred to her as "Our mother, the glorious Agnes".<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Catherine made a pilgrimage to Montepulciano while visiting her niece, Eugenie, who was a nun there.<sup id="cite_ref-dorcy_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dorcy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Another Dominican, Lorenzo Mariani of Rome, wrote another early biography of Agnes, in which he already referred to her as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1435  her remains were moved to the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Domenico,_Orvieto" title="San Domenico, Orvieto">San Domenico, Orvieto</a>. <sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agnes was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1726.<sup id="cite_ref-Tiepolo_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tiepolo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated within the Dominican Order on 20 April. "In Montepulciano, Tuscany, St. Agnes, Virgin, of the Order of St. Dominic, noted for her miracles." – Roman Martyrology for April 20.<sup id="cite_ref-Stracke_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stracke-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agnes of Montepulciano is depicted as a Dominican nun with a cross or crucifix, lilies, and a lamb (a play upon the name "Agnes").<sup id="cite_ref-Stracke_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stracke-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
