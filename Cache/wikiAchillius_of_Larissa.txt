<p><b>Saint Achillius of Larissa</b>, also known as <b>Achilles</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-cathonline_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathonline-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <b>Ailus</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-ochrid_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ochrid-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <b>Achillas</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-cathonline_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathonline-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Achilius</b><sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Άγιος Αχίλλειος</span>, <i>Ágios Achílleios</i>) (died 330 AD), was a 4th century <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Larissa" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Larissa">bishop of Larissa</a> and one of the 318 persons present at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">First Council of Nicaea</a>. His feast day is on 15 May.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Achillius_of_Larissa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Achillius was <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a> of <a href="/wiki/Larissa" title="Larissa">Larissa</a> in <a href="/wiki/Thessaly" title="Thessaly">Thessaly</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>. Achillius is mainly remembered for his vehement defense of orthodoxy during the Council of Nicea and a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> he performed in testimony against <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>.
</p>
<blockquote><p>Taking up a stone, Achillius called to the Arians: 'If <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> is a creature of God, as you say, tell <a href="/wiki/Oil" title="Oil">oil</a> to flow from this stone.' The <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretics</a> kept silent, amazed at this demand by St. Achillius. Then the saint continued: 'And if the <a href="/wiki/Son_of_God" title="Son of God">Son of God</a> is equal to the <a href="/wiki/God_the_Father" title="God the Father">Father</a>, as we believe, then let oil flow from this stone.' And oil flowed out, to the amazement of all.<sup id="cite_ref-ochrid_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ochrid-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Upon returning from the Council, Achillius is reputed to have "cast down many <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> temples, built many churches, [and] <a href="/wiki/Exorcism" title="Exorcism">cast out many demons</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-godsaints_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-godsaints-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The 1910 <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a> makes issue to reference Achillius in its article about the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/Larissa" title="Larissa">Larissa</a>.
</p>
<blockquote><p>We must mention especially, St. Achilius, in the fourth century, whose feast is on 15 May, and who is celebrated for his miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Achillius_of_Larissa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Achillius died in Larissa in the year 330. When <a href="/wiki/Samuil_of_Bulgaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Samuil of Bulgaria">Samuel, Emperor of Bulgaria</a>, conquered Thessaly, he <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated the relics</a> of Achillius to <a href="/wiki/Prespa_(medieval_town)" title="Prespa (medieval town)">Prespa</a>, to an island in a lake that was subsequently named after the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-maps_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maps-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  A district of Larissa is called <i>Saint Achellios</i> after this saint.
</p>
