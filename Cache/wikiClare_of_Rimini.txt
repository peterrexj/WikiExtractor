<p><b>Clare of Rimini</b> (c. 1260 – 10 February c. 1326) was an Italian holy woman who was beatified.
</p><p>She was born to a wealthy family and married at a young age. She was sent into exile when her husband died, witnessed the political execution of her father and brother, and remarried. She received a <a href="/wiki/Religious_calling" title="Religious calling">calling</a> after attending Mass and founded a penitential community. Her feast day is 10 February.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clare_of_Rimini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chiara was born to a wealthy family of Rimini. Married at a young age, she was sent into exile with her family. Upon her return, she witnessed the execution of her father and brother by a rival political faction. She remarried and lived a life of pleasure and dissipation.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the Stephen Donovan, one day when she was 34, she attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in the church of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> Friars, she seemed to hear a mysterious voice that bade her say an <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Our Father</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Hail_Mary" title="Hail Mary">Hail Mary</a> at least once with fervour and attention. Clare obeyed the command, not knowing from where it came, and then began to reflect upon her life.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She  became a model of every virtue, but especially of charity towards the destitute and afflicted. She abandoned her life of luxury and established an informal community for a group of women under her direction.<sup id="cite_ref-Delaney_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Delaney-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> were compelled to leave Regno on account of the prevailing wars, it was mainly through the exertions of Clare that they were able to obtain a means of sustenance at Rimini.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later, Clare is believed to have worked numerous miracles and towards the close of her life to have been favored in an extraordinary manner with the gift of contemplation. Her body is now in the cathedral of Rimini.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 22 December 1784, the cult of Blessed Clare was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a>, who permitted her feast to be celebrated in the city and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rimini" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Rimini">Diocese of Rimini</a> on 10 February.<sup id="cite_ref-Delaney_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Delaney-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Critique">Critique</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clare_of_Rimini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Critique">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1751 <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Garampi" title="Giuseppe Garampi">Giuseppe Garampi</a> was appointed Prefect of the Vatican Archives. He published an anonymous fourteenth century <i>vita</i> of Clare of Rimini, which had served as the basis of subsequent biographies. Through the use of careful philological and historical analysis he disputed that Clare had been a Franciscan tertiary and later a Poor Clare. He also argued against her having founded the monastery of Santa Maria degli Angeli in Rimini. Garampi described her as similar to a <a href="/wiki/Beguines_and_Beghards" title="Beguines and Beghards">Beguine</a>, and was a devout laywoman who dressed in a religious habit and practiced poverty and penance, but never took vows or was under a rule. With the details of her <i>vita</i> disputed, the promotion of her <i>cultus</i> (Catholic veneration) to canonization effectively ended.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The most important modern scholarship on Clare has been the work of Jacques Dalarun, including a new critical edition of her fourteenth-century Italian vita,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and a recent English translation.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
