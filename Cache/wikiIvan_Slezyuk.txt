<p><b>Ivan Slezyuk</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Іван Слезюк</span>; 14 January 1896 – 2 December 1973) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">Ukrainian Greek Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hieromartyr" title="Hieromartyr">hieromartyr</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Slezyuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born on 14 January 1896 in the village of Zhyvachiv, <a href="/wiki/Austrian-Hungarian_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Austrian-Hungarian Empire">Austrian-Hungarian Empire</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk_Oblast" title="Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast">Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>). After graduating from the seminary in 1923, he was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> on 1923 by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Hryhoriy_Khomyshyn" title="Hryhoriy Khomyshyn">Hryhoriy Khomyshyn</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HKV_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HKV-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 1945 Khomyshyn <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> him to the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_polity" title="Episcopal polity">Episcopate</a> as his coadjutor with the right of succession as a precaution in case Khomyshyn should be arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-v_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, shortly thereafter on 2 June 1945, Slezyuk was arrested and deported for ten years to the labour camps in <a href="/wiki/Vorkuta" title="Vorkuta">Vorkuta</a>. In 1950 he was transferred to the labour camps in <a href="/wiki/Mordovia" title="Mordovia">Mordovia</a>. After his release on 15 November 1954, he returned to <a href="/wiki/Stanislaviv" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanislaviv">Stanislaviv</a>. In 1962, he was arrested for the second time and imprisoned for five years in a <a href="/wiki/Strict_regimen_colony" class="mw-redirect" title="Strict regimen colony">camp of strict regimen</a>. After his release on 30 November 1968, he had to often go to the <a href="/wiki/KGB" title="KGB">KGB</a> for regular "talks." The last visit was two weeks before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-v_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died on 2 December 1973 in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Ivano-Frankivsk</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Testimony_of_Bishop_Sofron_Dmyterko">Testimony of Bishop Sofron Dmyterko</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Slezyuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Testimony of Bishop Sofron Dmyterko">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>“As the deceased himself said, they locked him in a separate isolated area, and no one visited him. He stayed there for two hours. Then they told him: ‘You’re free to go.’ It was difficult for him to walk because, as he himself said, after this he felt dizzy, as if he had a fever, his skin was burning. The Sisters of St. Vincent, who helped him out, also said that the bishop returned from this ‘conversation’ with a very red face, he felt exhausted, stayed in bed and died two weeks later. There was and still is a suspicion that the KGB used radiation to get rid of one more Uniate bishop.” – From the testimony of Bishop Sofron Dmyterko.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></i>
</p>
