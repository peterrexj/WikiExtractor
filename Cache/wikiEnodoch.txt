<p><b>Saint Enodoc</b>, originally <b>Wenedoc</b>, was a sub-Roman <a href="/wiki/Pre-congregational_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Pre-congregational saint">Pre-congregational saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>.
</p><p>Enodoc was originally recorded as a man. Historian <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Orme" title="Nicholas Orme">Nicholas Orme</a> says that in the 16th century the name was apparently misunderstood as that of a woman.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Enodoc's feast day was observed at <a href="/wiki/St_Petroc%27s_Church,_Bodmin" title="St Petroc&#39;s Church, Bodmin">Bodmin Priory</a> on 7 March.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/St_Enodoc%27s_Church,_Trebetherick" title="St Enodoc&#39;s Church, Trebetherick">St Enodoc's Church</a>, the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Trebetherick" title="Trebetherick">Trebetherick</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> is dedicated to this saint, and its churchyard is home to the grave of <a href="/wiki/John_Betjeman" title="John Betjeman">John Betjeman</a>.
</p><p>Enodoc should not be confused with Saint <a href="/wiki/Enoder" title="Enoder">Enoder</a>, who was probably a son of King <a href="/wiki/Brychan" title="Brychan">Brychan</a> Brycheiniog, originally called Wenheden.<sup id="cite_ref-EBK_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EBK-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
