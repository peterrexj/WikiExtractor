<p><b>Philastrius</b> (also <b>Philaster</b> or <b>Filaster</b>) <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a>, was one of the bishops present at a <a href="/wiki/Synod_held_in_Aquileia_in_381" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod held in Aquileia in 381">synod held in Aquileia in 381</a>. <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> met him at <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> about 383, or perhaps a little later (St. Augustine, <i>Ep.</i> ccxxii). He composed a catalogue of <a href="/wiki/Heresies" class="mw-redirect" title="Heresies">heresies</a> (<i>Diversarum Hereseon Liber</i>) about 384.  He died before 397. 
</p><p>Among the writings of <a href="/wiki/Gaudentius_of_Brescia" title="Gaudentius of Brescia">Gaudentius of Brescia</a> was a <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermon</a> purporting to be preached on the fourteenth anniversary of Philastrius's death. According to this sermon, Philastrius's life began with a great act of renunciation, for which he might fitly be compared to <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a>. Later he was ordained priest, and travelled over nearly the whole Roman world <i>(circumambiens Universum pene ambitum Romani Orbis</i>), preaching against pagans, Jews, and heretics, especially the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a>. Like <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul of Tarsus</a> he carried in his body the "stigmata" of Christ, having been scourged for his zeal against the last-named heretics. In Milan he was a great pillar of the Catholic party in the time of <a href="/wiki/Ambrose_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Ambrose of Milan">Ambrose's</a> Arian predecessor. At Rome he held both private and public <a href="/wiki/Disputation" title="Disputation">disputations</a> with heretics, and converted many. His wanderings ceased when he was made <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a>. 
</p><p>Doubts were first raised by <a href="/wiki/Louis_Ellies_du_Pin" title="Louis Ellies du Pin">Louis Ellies du Pin</a> as to the genuineness of this sermon, and these have been reiterated by Marx, the latest editor of Philastrius, who thinks the sermon a forgery of the eighth or ninth century. The chief objection to its genuineness, rather a weak one, seems to be that it is not found in the manuscripts containing the undoubted sermons of Gaudentius. Marx was answered by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Knappe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Knappe (page does not exist)">Knappe</a>, "Ist die 21 Rede des hl. Gaudentius (Oratio B. Gaudentii de Vita et Obitu B. Filastrii episcopi prædecessoris sui) echt? Zugleich ein Betrag zur Latinität des Gaudentius" (Osnabrück), who endeavours to prove the genuineness of the sermon in question by linguistic arguments. His <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> reviewer thinks he has made a strong case (<i>Anal. Boll.</i>, XXVIII, 224). 
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Richard_Adelbert_Lipsius" title="Richard Adelbert Lipsius">Richard Adelbert Lipsius</a> discovered that in Philastrius's "Catalogue" of heresies, for the <a href="/wiki/Christian_heresies" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian heresies">Christian heresies</a> up to <a href="/wiki/Noetus" title="Noetus">Noetus</a>, the compiler drew from the same source as <a href="/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis" title="Epiphanius of Salamis">Epiphanius of Salamis</a>, i. e. the lost <i>Syntagma of Hippolytus</i>. By the aid, therefore, of these two and the <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Tertullian" title="Pseudo-Tertullian">Pseudo-Tertullian</a> <i>Adversus Omnes Haereses</i> it has been possible in great measure to reconstruct the lost treatise of Hippolytus.
</p><p>Philastrius' comments and spellings do not always accord with those of Epiphanius or Pseudo-Tertullian, for example his description of <i><a href="/wiki/Nazaraei" class="mw-redirect" title="Nazaraei">Nazaraei</a></i> does not match well with either the <i>Nasaraioi</i> or <i><a href="/wiki/Nazarene_(sect)" title="Nazarene (sect)">Nazoraioi</a></i> which Epiphanius attempts to distinguish.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
