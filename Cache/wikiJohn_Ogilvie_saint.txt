<p><b>John Ogilvie</b>, SJ (1580 – 10 March 1615) was a <a href="/wiki/Scottish_people" title="Scottish people">Scottish</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuit</a> martyr. For his work as a priest in service to a persecuted <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> community in 17th century <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, and in being hanged for his faith, he became the only post-<a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a> Scottish saint.
</p><p>Ogilvie was brought up a <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinist</a> and sent to continental Europe to further his education. His interest piqued by the popular debates going on between Catholic and Calvinist scholars, he took up studies with the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a>, and then with the Jesuits. He became a Jesuit and was sent to Scotland, where he worked among the few Catholics in the area of <a href="/wiki/Glasgow" title="Glasgow">Glasgow</a>. Arrested after less than a year, he was hanged at <a href="/wiki/Glasgow_Cross" title="Glasgow Cross">Glasgow Cross</a> in 1615.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Ogilvie_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was the eldest son of Walter Ogilvie, a respected <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> who owned the estate of Drumnakeith in <a href="/wiki/Banffshire" title="Banffshire">Banffshire</a>. His family was partly <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and partly <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian" class="mw-redirect" title="Presbyterian">Presbyterian</a>. At the age of twelve he was sent to continental Europe to be educated. He attended a number of Catholic educational establishments, under the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictines</a> at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> in Germany and with the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Olomouc" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Olomouc">Olmutz</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brno" title="Brno">Brunn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravia</a>.
</p><p>In the midst of the religious controversies and turmoil that engulfed the Europe of that era, he decided to become a Catholic. In 1597, aged seventeen, he was received into the Catholic Church by <a href="/wiki/Cornelius_a_Lapide" title="Cornelius a Lapide">Cornelius a Lapide</a> S.J., professor of sacred scripture at <a href="/wiki/Leuven" title="Leuven">Leuven</a>, Belgium. Ogilvie joined the Society of Jesus in 1599 and was ordained a priest at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> in 1613.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrett_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrett-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After ordination he served in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> where he made repeated requests to be sent to Scotland to minister to the few remaining Catholics in the <a href="/wiki/Glasgow" title="Glasgow">Glasgow</a> area. (After 1560 it had become illegal there to preach, proselytise for, or otherwise endorse Catholicism.)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg/170px-Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="246" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg/255px-Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg/340px-Melchior_k%C3%BCsel-john_ogilvie.jpg 2x" data-file-width="729" data-file-height="1055" /></a><figcaption>The Jesuit John Ogilvie, who was publicly hanged and drawn on 10 March 1615 in Glasgow</figcaption></figure>
<p>It was his hope that some Catholic nobles there would aid him, given his lineage. Finding none, he went to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, then back to Paris, and finally returned to Scotland in November 1613 disguised as a horse trader named John Watson. Thereafter he began to preach in secret, celebrating <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> clandestinely in private homes. This ministry was to last less than a year. In October 1614, Ogilvie was discovered and arrested in Glasgow under the orders of <a href="/wiki/John_Spottiswoode" title="John Spottiswoode">Archbishop Spottiswood</a>, and was imprisoned. He was initially treated well, but after continually refusing to confess, was tortured by sleep deprivation. He aggravated his position by refusing to pledge allegiance to <a href="/wiki/King_James_VI_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="King James VI of Scotland">King James</a>, and it was for this crime that he was tried. During the trial he accused the king of 'playing the runagate from God' and stated he would acknowledge him no more than an 'old hat'. Found guilty, he was hanged at <a href="/wiki/Glasgow_Cross" title="Glasgow Cross">Glasgow Cross</a> on 10 March 1615, aged thirty-six.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The customary beheading and quartering were omitted owing to undisguised popular sympathy, and his body was hurriedly buried in the churchyard of Glasgow cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-Barrett_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barrett-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ogilvie's <a href="/wiki/Last_words" title="Last words">last words</a> were: "If there be here any hidden Catholics, let them pray for me but the prayers of heretics I will not have." After he was pushed from the stairs, he threw his concealed <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> out into the crowd. According to legend, one of his enemies caught it and subsequently became a devout, lifelong Catholic. After his execution Ogilvie's followers were rounded up and put in jail. They suffered heavy fines, but none received the death penalty.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Ogilvie_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Reformation" title="Scottish Reformation">Reformation</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a> he was declared Venerable in the seventeenth century. Ogilvie was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929 and <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> in 1976 on 17 October, becoming the only post-Reformation Scottish saint.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is celebrated on 10 March in the Catholic Church in Scotland. In the rest of the world it is celebrated on 14th October. In <a href="/wiki/Corby" title="Corby">Corby</a>, Northamptonshire — an English town with a strong Scottish heritage — a Catholic church registered in March 1980 is dedicated to John Ogilvie.<sup id="cite_ref-LG-48154-5584-Supp_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-48154-5584-Supp-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the Scottish Highlands there is the Parish of Saint John Ogilvie comprising the Churches of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a>’s in <a href="/wiki/Invergordon" title="Invergordon">Invergordon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_De_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vincent De Paul">Saint Vincent De Paul</a>’s in Tain. At the service to mark the quadricentenary of his death, he was described as "Scotland's only Catholic martyr".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
