<p><b>Athanasius the Athonite</b> (c. 920 &#8211; c. 1003; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἀθανάσιος ὁ Ἀθωνίτης</span>), was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk#Eastern_Christianity" title="Monk">monk</a> who is considered the founder of the monastic community on the peninsula of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>; which has since evolved into the greatest centre of <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> monasticism.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athanasius_the_Athonite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His parents were from <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Trabzon" title="Trabzon">Trebizond</a> and patronized by <a href="/wiki/Michael_Maleinos" title="Michael Maleinos">Michael Maleinos</a>, he studied at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and became famous there as Abraham, a fervent preacher who held great authority with Michael's nephew, <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_II_Phokas" title="Nikephoros II Phokas">Nicephoros Phocas</a>. By the time Phocas ascended the imperial throne, Abraham, ill at ease with the lax morals of the monks living in the capital, changed his name to Athanasios and joined the monks at <a href="/wiki/Mount_Kyminas" title="Mount Kyminas">Mount Kyminas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a>. In 958, he relocated to Mount Athos.
</p><p>He helped defend the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a>, or <i>sketes</i>, there against the <a href="/wiki/Saracen" title="Saracen">Saracens</a>, and also started to incorporate the sketes already there into what would eventually become known as the <a href="/wiki/Great_Lavra" title="Great Lavra">Great Lavra</a>, which Athanasios built with the financial assistance of Nicephoros. This monastery was dedicated in 963. It is still in use today, and is often referred to by people of the area simply as "Lavra", or "The Monastery". Three other foundations followed shortly thereafter, with all three of them remaining in place to the present. Athanasios met with considerable opposition from the hermits already at Mount Athos in the construction of his monasteries. They resented his intrusion and his attempts to bring order and discipline to their lives.
</p><p>Upon Nicephoros' death the enemies of Athanasios prevailed and he had to leave Athos for <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, where he lived until the new emperor, <a href="/wiki/John_Tzimisces" class="mw-redirect" title="John Tzimisces">John Tzimisces</a>, resumed the patronage of the Great Lavra and bestowed upon the monastery its first charter in 972. This charter, which is kept at the <a href="/wiki/Protaton" title="Protaton">Protaton</a> in <a href="/wiki/Karyes,_Mount_Athos" title="Karyes, Mount Athos">Karyes</a> today, is also known as the <i>Tragos</i> ('goat'), since it was written on goatskin parchment.<sup id="cite_ref-Renewal_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Renewal-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Athanasios, spurred by a divine vision, returned at once to Athos as a <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> (abbot) and introduced a <a href="/wiki/Typicon" class="mw-redirect" title="Typicon">typicon</a> for <a href="/wiki/Cenobite" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenobite">cenobites</a>, based on those compiled by <a href="/wiki/Theodore_Studites" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodore Studites">Theodore Studites</a> and <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a>.
</p><p>He died during an accident, killed by a falling masonry, when the <a href="/wiki/Cupola" title="Cupola">cupola</a> of his church collapsed. Upon his death, Athanasios was <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Eastern_Orthodox_Practice--Glorification" title="Canonization">glorified</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. His feast day is 5 July.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sites">Sites</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athanasius_the_Athonite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sites">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Athanasius_Bridge&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Athanasius Bridge (page does not exist)">Saint Athanasius Bridge</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%B2_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82" class="extiw" title="bg:Светиатанасов мост">bg</a>&#93;</span> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Γεφύρι του Αγίου Αθανασίου του Αθωνίτη</span>) is a historic bridge located about 5–6 km from the main compound of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Lavra" title="Great Lavra">Great Lavra</a>. Its construction is traditionally attributed to Athanasius the Athonite.
</p>
