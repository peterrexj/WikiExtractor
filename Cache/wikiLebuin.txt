<p><b>Lebuinus</b> (also known as <i>Lebuin</i>, <i>Lebwin</i> or <i>Liafwin[e]</i>) is the Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Frisians" title="Frisians">Frisians</a> and patron of <a href="/wiki/Deventer" title="Deventer">Deventer</a> (born in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> parents, date unknown; died at Deventer c. 775).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lebuinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lebuinus was a monk in <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>'s monastery at <a href="/wiki/Ripon" title="Ripon">Ripon</a>. Inspired by the example of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Willibrord" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Willibrord">Saint Willibrord</a> and other great English missionaries, he resolved to devote his life to the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germans</a>. After his ordination, he proceeded in 754 to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Utrecht" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Utrecht">Utrecht</a>, and was welcomed by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Utrecht" title="Gregory of Utrecht">Saint Gregory</a>, acting bishop of that place, who entrusted him with the mission of <a href="/wiki/Overijssel" title="Overijssel">Overijssel</a> on the borders of <a href="/wiki/Westphalia" title="Westphalia">Westphalia</a>, and gave him a companion - Marchelm (or Marcellinus), a disciple of Saint Willibrord.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lebuinus preached the Gospel among the tribes of the district, and erected a little chapel at Wilp (see: <a href="/wiki/Voorst" title="Voorst">Voorst</a>) (<i>Wilpa</i>) on the west bank of the <a href="/wiki/IJssel" title="IJssel">IJssel</a>. His venerable personality and deep learning quickly won many to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, even among the nobles, and it soon became necessary to build at Deventer on the east bank of the river a larger church. However, Lebuinus's great success aroused hostility among the pagans. Ascribing his conversions to witchcraft, they formed an alliance with the anti-Christian <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a>, burned the church at Deventer and dispersed the converts.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After escaping with difficulty, Lebuinus determined to voice the claims of Christianity at the national assembly (<i>Allthing</i>) of the Saxons at Marklo near the <a href="/wiki/Weser" title="Weser">Weser</a> (Northwestern Germany).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Vitae</i> of Lebuinus describes in great details, his appearance before the assembly, where, it is claimed, he pointed out to the Saxons the inefficacy of their deities. It also describes how he warned them of impending destruction at the hands of a powerful king unless they converted to Christianity. With the intercession of the nobleman Buto, he persuaded them sufficiently of the power of his mission that they not only allowed him to escape with his life, but allowed him to preach unmolested in the territory allotted him. His life may have been a source of inspiration in the creation of the cultus on <a href="/wiki/Saint_Livinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Livinus">Saint Livinus</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lebuinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On his return to Friesland, Lebuinus rebuilt the church at Deventer where he was later buried. The exact date of his death is unknown, however it is almost certain that it occurred before 776, because during that year, the Saxons attacked and burnt the church at Deventer and yet could not identify his remains inside of the church after three days. His body and a copy of the Gospels presumed to have been written by his hand were still in Deventer, in a church bearing his name, until 882 when it was again destroyed by the Normans. The relics of St. Livinus (whose feast also is on 12 November) are probably those of Lebuinus. <a href="/wiki/Ludger" title="Ludger">Saint Ludger</a> rebuilt the church a few years later, and in doing so rediscovered the saint's remains beneath the site.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKennedy1913-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lebuinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Lebuinus is commemorated by the Church on 12 November, mostly in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>.</li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/Lebu%C3%AFnuskerk,_Deventer" title="Lebuïnuskerk, Deventer">Lebuïnuskerk, Deventer</a> was consecrated in his name.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHunt1892_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHunt1892-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
