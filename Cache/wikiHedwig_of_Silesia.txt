<p><b>Hedwig of Silesia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Święta Jadwiga Śląska</i>), also <b>Hedwig of Andechs</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Heilige Hedwig von Andechs</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Hedvigis</i>; 1174 – 15 October 1243), a member of the <a href="/wiki/Bavarians" title="Bavarians">Bavarian</a> comital <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Andechs" title="Counts of Andechs">House of Andechs</a>, was Duchess of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Silesia" title="Duchy of Silesia">Silesia</a> from 1201 and of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Greater_Poland" title="Duchy of Greater Poland">Greater Poland</a> from 1231 as well as <a href="/wiki/List_of_Polish_consorts" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Polish consorts">High Duchess consort of Poland</a> from 1232 until 1238.  She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1267 by Pope Clement IV.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedwig_of_Silesia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The daughter of Count <a href="/wiki/Berthold,_Duke_of_Merania" title="Berthold, Duke of Merania">Berthold IV of Andechs</a>, margrave of <a href="/wiki/March_of_Carniola" title="March of Carniola">Carniola</a> and <a href="/wiki/March_of_Istria" title="March of Istria">Istria</a> and his second wife <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Rochlitz" title="Agnes of Rochlitz">Agnes of Wettin</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> she was born at <a href="/wiki/Andechs_Abbey" title="Andechs Abbey">Andechs Castle</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria" title="Duchy of Bavaria">Duchy of Bavaria</a>. Her elder sister, <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Merania" title="Agnes of Merania">Agnes</a>, married King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_France" title="Philip II of France">Philip II of France</a> (annulled in 1200) and her sister <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Merania" title="Gertrude of Merania">Gertrude</a> (killed in 1213) married King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_II_of_Hungary" title="Andrew II of Hungary">Andrew II of Hungary</a>, while the youngest Matilda, (Mechtild) became abbess at the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Kitzingen" title="Kitzingen">Kitzingen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a>, where Hedwig also received her education. Hedwig's brother was Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ekbert_of_Bamberg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ekbert of Bamberg (page does not exist)">Ekbert of Bamberg</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekbert_von_Andechs-Meranien" class="extiw" title="de:Ekbert von Andechs-Meranien">de</a>&#93;</span>, Count of Andechs-Meranien. Another brother was <a href="/wiki/Berthold_(patriarch_of_Aquileia)" title="Berthold (patriarch of Aquileia)">Berthold</a>, Archbishop of Kalocsa and <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Aquileia">Patriarch of Aquileia</a>, while her brother <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Margrave_of_Istria" title="Henry II, Margrave of Istria">Henry, Margrave of Istria</a> was the first lord of <a href="/wiki/Carniola" title="Carniola">Carniola</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Through her sister Gertrude, she was the aunt of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Duchess_consort">Duchess consort</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedwig_of_Silesia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Duchess consort">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the age of twelve, Hedwig married <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_the_Bearded" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I the Bearded">Henry I the Bearded</a>, son and heir of the <a href="/wiki/Silesian_Piasts" title="Silesian Piasts">Piast</a> duke <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_I_the_Tall" title="Bolesław I the Tall">Boleslaus the Tall</a> of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Silesia" title="Duchy of Silesia">Silesia</a>. As soon as Henry succeeded his father in 1201, he had to struggle with his <a href="/wiki/Piast_dynasty" title="Piast dynasty">Piast</a> relatives, at first with his uncle Duke <a href="/wiki/Mieszko_IV_Tanglefoot" title="Mieszko IV Tanglefoot">Mieszko IV Tanglefoot</a> who immediately seized the Upper Silesian <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Opole" title="Duchy of Opole">Duchy of Opole</a>. In 1206 Henry and his cousin Duke <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_III_Spindleshanks" title="Władysław III Spindleshanks">Władysław III Spindleshanks</a> of <a href="/wiki/Greater_Poland" title="Greater Poland">Greater Poland</a> agreed to swap the Silesian <a href="/wiki/Lubusz_Land" title="Lubusz Land">Lubusz Land</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Kalisz" title="Kalisz">Kalisz</a> region, which met with fierce protest by Władysław's III nephew <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_Odonic" title="Władysław Odonic">Władysław Odonic</a>. When Henry went to <a href="/wiki/G%C4%85sawa" title="Gąsawa">Gąsawa</a> in 1227 to meet his Piast cousins, he narrowly saved his life, while High Duke <a href="/wiki/Leszek_I_the_White" class="mw-redirect" title="Leszek I the White">Leszek I the White</a> was killed by the men of the <a href="/wiki/Pomerelia" title="Pomerelia">Pomerelian</a> Duke <a href="/wiki/Swietopelk_II,_Duke_of_Pomerania" title="Swietopelk II, Duke of Pomerania">Swietopelk II</a>, instigated by Władysław Odonic.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg/160px-Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="243" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg/240px-Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg/320px-Sw_Jadwiga_godzi_Henryka_Brodatego_z_Konradem_Mazowieckim.jpg 2x" data-file-width="792" data-file-height="1205" /></a><figcaption>Hedwig intercedes between Henry and Konrad, 19th century depiction</figcaption></figure>
<p>The next year Henry's ally <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_III_Spindleshanks" title="Władysław III Spindleshanks">Władysław III Spindleshanks</a> succeeded Leszek I as <a href="/wiki/Seniorate_Province" title="Seniorate Province">High Duke</a>; however as he was still contested by his nephew in Greater Poland, he made Henry his governor at <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, whereby the Silesian duke once again became entangled in the dispute over the <a href="/wiki/Seniorate_Province" title="Seniorate Province">Seniorate Province</a>. In 1229 he was captured and arrested at <a href="/wiki/P%C5%82ock" title="Płock">Płock</a> Castle by rivaling Duke <a href="/wiki/Konrad_I_of_Masovia" title="Konrad I of Masovia">Konrad I of Masovia</a>. Hedwig proceeded to Płock pleading for Henry and was able to have him released.
</p><p>Her actions promoted the reign of her husband: upon the death of the Polish High Duke Władysław III Spindleshanks in 1231, Henry also became Duke of <a href="/wiki/Greater_Poland" title="Greater Poland">Greater Poland</a> and the next year prevailed as High Duke at Kraków. He thereby was the first of the Silesian Piast descendants of <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_the_Exile" title="Władysław II the Exile">Władysław II the Exile</a> to gain the rule over Silesia and the Seniorate Province in accord with the 1138 <a href="/wiki/Testament_of_Boles%C5%82aw_III_Krzywousty" class="mw-redirect" title="Testament of Bolesław III Krzywousty">Testament of Bolesław III Krzywousty</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Widow">Widow</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedwig_of_Silesia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Widow">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Upon his death in 1238, Henry was buried at a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monastery of nuns, <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_St._Jadwiga_in_Trzebnica" class="mw-redirect" title="Sanctuary of St. Jadwiga in Trzebnica">Trzebnica Abbey</a> (<i>Kloster Trebnitz</i>), which he had established in 1202 at Hedwig's request. Hedwig accepted the death of her beloved husband with faith. She said:<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Would you oppose the will of God? Our lives are His.</p></blockquote>
<p>The widow moved into the monastery, which was led by her daughter Gertrude, assuming the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay sister</a>, but she did not take vows. She invited numerous <a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> religious people from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> into the Silesian lands, as well as German settlers who founded numerous cities, towns and villages in the course of the <i><a href="/wiki/Ostsiedlung" title="Ostsiedlung">Ostsiedlung</a></i>, while cultivating barren parts of Silesia for agriculture.
</p><p>Hedwig and Henry had several daughters, though only one surviving son, <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_the_Pious" title="Henry II the Pious">Henry II the Pious</a>, who succeeded his father as Duke of Silesia and Polish High Duke. The widow, however, had to witness the killing of her son, vainly awaiting the support of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Poland" title="First Mongol invasion of Poland">Mongol invasion of Poland</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Legnica" title="Battle of Legnica">Battle of Legnica</a> (<i>Wahlstatt</i>) in 1241. The hopes for a re-united Poland were lost, and even Silesia fragmented into numerous Piast <a href="/wiki/Duchies_of_Silesia" title="Duchies of Silesia">duchies</a> under Henry II's sons. Hedwig and her daughter-in-law, Henry II's widow <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Bohemia_(1204%E2%80%931265)" class="mw-redirect" title="Anne of Bohemia (1204–1265)">Anna of Bohemia</a>, established a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine abbey">Benedictine abbey</a> at the site of the battle in <a href="/wiki/Legnickie_Pole" title="Legnickie Pole">Legnickie Pole</a>, settled with monks coming from <a href="/wiki/Opatovice_nad_Labem" title="Opatovice nad Labem">Opatovice</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:HedwigAltarXXX.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/HedwigAltarXXX.jpg/160px-HedwigAltarXXX.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="221" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/HedwigAltarXXX.jpg/240px-HedwigAltarXXX.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/HedwigAltarXXX.jpg/320px-HedwigAltarXXX.jpg 2x" data-file-width="439" data-file-height="605" /></a><figcaption>Scene from an altar of St. Hedwig of Silesia, <a href="/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw" title="Wrocław">Breslau</a>, around 1430, restored in 1929, <a href="/wiki/National_Museum,_Warsaw" title="National Museum, Warsaw">National Museum Warsaw</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Hedwig and Henry had lived very pious lives, and Hedwig had great <a href="/wiki/Diligence" title="Diligence">zeal</a> for her faith. She had supported her husband in donating the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> provostry at <a href="/wiki/Nowogr%C3%B3d_Bobrza%C5%84ski" title="Nowogród Bobrzański">Nowogród Bobrzański</a> (<i>Naumburg</i>) and the commandery of the <a href="/wiki/Knights_Templar" title="Knights Templar">Knights Templar</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ole%C5%9Bnica_Ma%C5%82a" title="Oleśnica Mała">Oleśnica Mała</a> (<i>Klein Oels</i>). Hedwig always helped the poor, the widows and the orphans, founded several hospitals for the sick and the lepers and donated all her fortune to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Church</a>. She allowed no one to leave her uncomforted, and one time she spent ten weeks teaching the <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Our Father</a> to a poor woman. According to legend, she went barefoot even in winter, and when she was urged by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Wroc%C5%82aw" title="Bishop of Wrocław">Bishop of Wrocław</a> to wear shoes, she carried them in her hands.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On 15 October 1243, Hedwig died and was buried in Trzebnica Abbey with her husband, while <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of her are preserved at <a href="/wiki/Andechs_Abbey" title="Andechs Abbey">Andechs Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Hedwig%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Hedwig&#39;s Cathedral">St. Hedwig's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Berlin" title="Berlin">Berlin</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedwig_of_Silesia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG/160px-%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG" decoding="async" width="160" height="231" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG/240px-%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG/320px-%C5%9Aw._Jadwiga_przy_Mo%C5%9Bcie_Tumskim_DSC00285.JPG 2x" data-file-width="554" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption>Monument in Wrocław</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hedwig was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1267 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_IV" title="Pope Clement IV">Pope Clement IV</a>, a supporter of the Cistercian order, at the suggestion of her grandson Prince-Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_of_Salzburg" title="Ladislaus of Salzburg">Władysław of Salzburg</a>. She is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Silesia" title="Silesia">Silesia</a>, of <a href="/wiki/Andechs" title="Andechs">Andechs</a>, and of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Wroc%C5%82aw" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Wrocław">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Wrocław</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_G%C3%B6rlitz" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Görlitz">Roman Catholic Diocese of Görlitz</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> on 16 October. The <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Paul_the_First_Hermit" title="Order of Saint Paul the First Hermit">Order of Saint Paul the First Hermit</a>, who count her as a great benefactor, celebrate it on 8 June.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A 17th-century legend has it that Hedwig, while on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, stopped at <a href="/wiki/Bad_Zell" title="Bad Zell">Bad Zell</a> in <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Austria" title="Duchy of Austria">Austria</a>, where she had healing waters spring up at a source which today still bears her name.
</p><p>In 1773 the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia" title="Kingdom of Prussia">Prussian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Frederick_the_Great" title="Frederick the Great">Frederick the Great</a>, having conquered and annexed the bulk of Silesia in the <a href="/wiki/Silesian_Wars" title="Silesian Wars">First Silesian War</a>, had <a href="/wiki/St._Hedwig%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Hedwig&#39;s Cathedral">St. Hedwig</a> in Berlin built for the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Upper_Silesia" title="Upper Silesia">Upper Silesian</a> immigrants, since 1930 the cathedral of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Berlin" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Berlin">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Berlin</a>. After the <a href="/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_from_Poland_during_and_after_World_War_II" title="Flight and expulsion of Germans from Poland during and after World War II">expulsion of almost all Germans from Silesia</a>, German Silesians carried Hedwig's worship to all over remaining Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In March 2020 the discovery of Hedwig's remains, that had been missing for centuries, was reported. The remains were found in <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_St._Jadwiga,_Trzebnica" title="Sanctuary of St. Jadwiga, Trzebnica">her sanctuary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Trzebnica" title="Trzebnica">Trzebnica</a>, in a silver casket bearing a lead tablet with an inscription confirming Hedwig's identity.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Hedwig_glass" title="Hedwig glass">Hedwig glasses</a> are named after Hedwig of Silesia.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Children">Children</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedwig_of_Silesia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Children">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hedwig and Henry I had seven children:
</p>
<ol><li>Agnes (ca. 1190 – before 11 May 1214).</li>
<li>Bolesław (ca. 1191 – 10 September 1206/08).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Henry_II_the_Pious" title="Henry II the Pious">Henry II the Pious</a> (ca. 1196 – killed in <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Legnica" title="Battle of Legnica">Battle of Legnica</a>, 9 April 1241).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Konrad_the_Curly" title="Konrad the Curly">Konrad the Curly</a> (ca. 1198 – Czerwony Kosciol, 4 September 1213).</li>
<li>Sophie (ca. 1200 – before 22/23 March 1214).</li>
<li>Gertrude (ca. 1200 – Trebnitz, 6/30 December 1268), Abbess of Trebnitz.</li>
<li>A son [Władysław?] (before 25 December 1208 – 1214/17).</li></ol>
