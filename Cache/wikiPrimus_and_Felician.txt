<p><b>Saints Primus and Felician (Felicianus)</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Primo e Feliciano</i>) were brothers who suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> about the year 304 during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution. The <i>"<a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a>"</i> gives under June 9 the names of <i>Primus and Felician</i> who were buried at the fourteenth milestone of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Nomentana" title="Via Nomentana">Via Nomentana</a> (near <a href="/wiki/Nomentum" class="mw-redirect" title="Nomentum">Nomentum</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Mentana" title="Mentana">Mentana</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They were evidently from <a href="/wiki/Nomentum" class="mw-redirect" title="Nomentum">Nomentum</a>. This notice comes from the catalogue of <a href="/wiki/Roman_martyrology" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman martyrology">Roman martyrs</a> of the fourth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Acts">Acts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Primus_and_Felician&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Acts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their "<a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Acts</a>" relate that Sts Felician and Primus were brothers and patricians who had converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and devoted themselves to caring for the poor and visiting prisoners.<sup id="cite_ref-Delehaye_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Delehaye-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Arrested, they both refused to sacrifice to the public gods. They were imprisoned and <a href="/wiki/Scourge" title="Scourge">scourged</a>. They were brought separately before the judge Promotus, who tortured them together and endeavored to deceive them that the other had <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostatized</a> by offering sacrifice. This had no effect on the brothers, and the two were subsequently <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> at <a href="/wiki/Nomentum" class="mw-redirect" title="Nomentum">Nomentum</a> (12 miles from Rome).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> St Primus was eighty years old at the time of his death.  A <a href="/wiki/Church_(building)" title="Church (building)">church</a> was built over their tombs on the Via Nomentana.<sup id="cite_ref-Delehaye_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Delehaye-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Burial">Burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Primus_and_Felician&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG/220px-Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG/330px-Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG/440px-Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2868" data-file-height="1916" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ss_Primo_e_Feliciano_S_Stefano_Rotondo_Roma.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ss Primo e Feliciano S Stefano Rotondo, Roma</div></div></div>
<p>They appear to be the first martyrs of whom it is recorded that their bodies were subsequently reburied within the walls of Rome.  In 648 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Theodore_I" title="Pope Theodore I">Pope Theodore I</a> <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> the bones of the two saints (together with the remains of his father) to the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Stefano_Rotondo" class="mw-redirect" title="Santo Stefano Rotondo">Santo Stefano Rotondo</a>, under an <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> erected in their honor where they remain.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The Chapel of <i>Ss. Primo e Feliciano</i> contains <a href="/wiki/Mosaic" title="Mosaic">mosaics</a> from the 7th century. The chapel was built by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Theodore_I" title="Pope Theodore I">Pope Theodore I</a>. One mosaic shows the martyrs St Primus and St Felician flanking a <a href="/wiki/Crux_gemmata" title="Crux gemmata">crux gemmata</a> (jeweled cross).
</p><p>Other depictions of the saints can be found at <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/St_Mark%27s_Basilica" title="St Mark&#39;s Basilica">St Mark's Basilica</a> (13th century) and at <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Cappella_Palatina" title="Cappella Palatina">Cappella Palatina</a> (12th century).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Primus_and_Felician&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their feast day is 9 June. In the past they were also included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, but because of the limited worldwide interest in them, it was decided in 1969 to leave to individual dioceses the decision whether to include them in their local calendars.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_in_Bavaria">Veneration in Bavaria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Primus_and_Felician&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration in Bavaria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavarian</a> tradition holds that Sts Primus and Felician were Roman <a href="/wiki/Legionary" title="Legionary">legionaries</a> who became missionaries in the region of <a href="/wiki/Chiemgau" title="Chiemgau">Chiemgau</a>, where Primus found in a forest a <a href="/wiki/Fountain" title="Fountain">fountain</a> with curative properties.  The two brothers preached the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> there and cured the sick by virtue of their prayers and the virtue of the source.  When they returned to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, they were martyred under Diocletian.  The fountain, known as the Fountain of Saint Primus, can still be seen at <a href="/wiki/Adelholzener_Alpenquellen" title="Adelholzener Alpenquellen">Adelholzen</a>, an area of hot springs where a chapel constructed in 1615 can be found, dedicated to these saints, who are much venerated in the area.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_at_Agen">Veneration at Agen</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Primus_and_Felician&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration at Agen">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During the 9th century, the cult of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Faith" title="Saint Faith">Saint Faith</a> was fused with that of <a href="/wiki/Caprasius_of_Agen" title="Caprasius of Agen">Caprasius of Agen</a> (Caprais) and <a href="/wiki/Alberta_of_Agen" title="Alberta of Agen">Alberta of Agen</a>, also associated with <a href="/wiki/Agen" title="Agen">Agen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Caprasius' cult in turn was fused with that of Primus and Felician, who are called Caprasius' brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One legend states that during the persecutions of Christians by the prefect Dacian, Caprasius fled to Mont-Saint-Vincent, near Agen.  He witnessed the execution of Faith from atop the hill.  Caprasius was condemned to death, and was joined on his way to execution by Alberta, Faith's sister (also identified as Caprasius' mother<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>), and two brothers, named Primus and Felician.  All four were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.
</p>
