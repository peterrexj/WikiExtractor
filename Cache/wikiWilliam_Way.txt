<p><b>William Way</b> (alias May, alias Flower) (died 1588) was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> executed under <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a>. He is venerated in the Roman Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Way&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William Way was born in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Exeter" title="Diocese of Exeter">Diocese of Exeter</a> about c. 1560.  Bishop <a href="/wiki/Richard_Challoner" title="Richard Challoner">Richard Challoner</a> said he was born in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, and earlier authorities say in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devonshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Since the Protestant Reformation had closed Catholic seminaries in England, Way went to France to study. On 31 March 1584, he received his first <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Reims">Cathedral of Reims</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Louis_II,_Cardinal_of_Guise" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis II, Cardinal of Guise">Cardinal of Guise</a>.  On 22 March he was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a>, on 5 April <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, and priest on 18 September 1586, at <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a>, probably by Bishop Valentine Douglas (<i>Valentine Duglas</i>), <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">O.S.B.</a><sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Way&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Way departed for England on 9 December 1586, and by June 1587, was imprisoned. He was indicted at <a href="/wiki/Newgate" title="Newgate">Newgate</a> in September 1588, for being a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He declined to be tried by a secular judge, whereupon the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a> was sent for. Way, refusing to acknowledge him as a bishop or <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a> as head of the Church, was immediately condemned as a traitor and to death.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was austere. When called to trial at the Sessions in August, "he had so much joy that he seemed past himself".<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Way was "<a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hung, drawn and quartered</a>" at <a href="/wiki/Kingston_upon_Thames" title="Kingston upon Thames">Kingston upon Thames</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The date is variously given as either 23 September or 1 October 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
