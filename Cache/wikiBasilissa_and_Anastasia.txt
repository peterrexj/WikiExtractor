<p><b>Basilissa and Anastasia</b> (died 68 AD) are early Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, put to death during the reign of Nero.<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>   They were among the first converts to Christianity in the 1st century after Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-holweck_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holweck-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Basilissa and Anastasia were described as "Roman matrons of high rank and great wealth".<sup id="cite_ref-saintlywomen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saintlywomen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  They were disciples of and might have been baptized by the apostles <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and might have given them "honorable burials"<sup id="cite_ref-holweck_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holweck-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> after Peter and Paul's martyrdom in Rome on the same day in 67 AD.  The location of the two tombs eventually became <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of St. Paul Outside the Walls</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The burials might have exposed Basilissa and Anastasia to more persecution, and they were arrested for collecting the relics of and burying the bodies of other martyred Christians.  They refused to recant their Christian faith and were beheaded with swords by order of Nero in 68 AD, after being tortured, including having their tongues torn out, their skins scraped with hooks, being burned with fire, and their breasts and feet cut off.<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-holweck_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holweck-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-saintlywomen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saintlywomen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Their <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> are at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Pace" title="Santa Maria della Pace">Santa Maria della Pace Church</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their feast is April 15, are venerated by both the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and are honored as the patron saints of tailors.  A statue of Basilissa is among the statues that line the <a href="/wiki/Colonnade" title="Colonnade">colonnade</a> overlooking <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-dailycompass_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dailycompass-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
