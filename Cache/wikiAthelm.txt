<p><b>Athelm</b> (or <b>Æthelhelm</b>; died 926) was an English churchman, who was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Wells" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Wells">Bishop of Wells</a>, and later <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Translation_(ecclesiastical)" title="Translation (ecclesiastical)">translation</a>, or moving from one bishopric to another, was a precedent for later translations of ecclesiastics, because prior to this time period such movements were considered illegal. While archbishop, Athelm crowned King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelstan" title="Æthelstan">Æthelstan</a>, and perhaps wrote the coronation service for the event.  An older relative of <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>, a later Archbishop of Canterbury, Athelm helped promote Dunstan's early career. After Athelm's death, he was considered a saint.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Athelm was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> of <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a><sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> before his elevation in 909 to the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Wells" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Wells">see of Wells</a>, of which he was the first occupant.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook222_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook222-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The see was founded to divide up the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Sherborne" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Sherborne">diocese of Sherborne</a>, which was very large, by creating a bishopric for the county of Somerset. Wells was likely chosen as the seat because it was the center of the county.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson5_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson5-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Some scholarly works suggest that Athelm may be the same person as <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelhelm" title="Æthelhelm">Æthelhelm</a>, son of King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelred of Wessex">Æthelred of Wessex</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Dolley75_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dolley75-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> but this is not accepted by most historians.<sup id="cite_ref-AethelredDNB_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AethelredDNB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A few sources state that Athelm was <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Glastonbury" title="Abbot of Glastonbury">Abbot of Glastonbury</a> before he became bishop,<sup id="cite_ref-DictSaint_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictSaint-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> but other sources disagree and do not give him that office.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This traces to later medieval chroniclers, not to contemporary accounts. His brother was Heorstan, who held land near Glastonbury.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson6_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson6-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishopric">Archbishopric</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Archbishopric">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Between August 923 and September 925 he became archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> His translation from the see of Wells set a precedent for the future, and marks a break with historical practice. Previously the moving of a bishop from one see to another had been held to be against canon, or ecclesiastical, law. Recently, however, the popes had themselves been translated, and this practice was to become common in England after Athelm's time.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks214_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks214-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was West Saxon, unlike his predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Plegmund" title="Plegmund">Plegmund</a>, who was Mercian, reflecting the shift in power to <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nelson124_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nelson124-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Athelm was a paternal uncle of <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who later became Archbishop of Canterbury. It was Athelm who brought Dunstan to the king's court.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE446_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE446-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Athelm presided at the coronation of King <a href="/wiki/Athelstan_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Athelstan of England">Athelstan of England</a> on 4 September 925, and probably composed or organised the new <i>Ordo</i> (order of service) in which for the first time the king wore a crown instead of a helmet. He also attested the king's first grant to <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a> in Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-Nelson124_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nelson124-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> It is unclear if the reason that no coins were minted with his name was his short term of office or a change in policy towards the Archbishop of Canterbury minting coins in his own name. Nothing else is known of Athelm's brief time as archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks214_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks214-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Athelm died on 8 January 926.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He was later considered a saint, with a feast day of 8 January.<sup id="cite_ref-CathOnline_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOnline-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He was buried at first the church of St John the Baptist near the Saxon-era <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury Cathedral</a>. When a new cathedral was constructed under Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Norman_Conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman Conquest of England">Norman Conquest of England</a>, the earlier archbishops of Canterbury were moved to the north <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a> of the new cathedral. Later, Athelm and his successor as archbishop <a href="/wiki/Wulfhelm" title="Wulfhelm">Wulfhelm</a> were moved to a chapel dedicated to St Benedict, which later was incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Lady_Chapel_(Canterbury)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lady Chapel (Canterbury)">Lady Chapel</a> constructed by <a href="/wiki/Prior_of_Christ_Church" title="Prior of Christ Church">Prior</a> Thomas Goldstone (d. 1468).<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson58_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson58-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
