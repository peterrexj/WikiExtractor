<p>Saint <b>Athanasius I</b> (c.832–872) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Naples">bishop of Naples</a> from 850 to his death. This Athanasius should not be confused with his nephew, <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Naples" title="Athanasius of Naples">Athanasius II</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Athanasius_I_(bishop_of_Naples)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Athanasius was the second son of <a href="/wiki/Sergius_I_of_Naples" title="Sergius I of Naples">Sergius I of Naples</a> and not quite twenty years old when he became Bishop of Naples in 849, at the same time his brother, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_III_of_Naples" title="Gregory III of Naples">Gregory</a>, became co-duke. He was consecrated bishop in Rome by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a>. Athanasius led a life of austerity and prayer, and was particular concerned with the proper education and training of the clergy in his diocese. He oversaw the restoration of the monastery of the Most Holy Saviour, and established a monastery dedicated to Sts. Januarius and Agrippinus. He also established a hospice for pilgrims, and set up a process for ransoming prisoners captured by the Saracens.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He attended the Lateran Council of 863.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Athanasius was an intimate of both the court of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Western Emperor</a> and that of the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a>. He was a <i><a href="/wiki/Familiaris" title="Familiaris">familiaris</a></i> of emperors <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Louis_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Louis II">Louis II</a> and was made a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">Papal legate</a> because of his connections with the <a href="/wiki/Curia_romana" class="mw-redirect" title="Curia romana">Roman <i>curia</i></a>.
</p><p>Before dying, Sergius bade Gregory to follow the counsel of his brother the bishop. Gregory did, but his son, <a href="/wiki/Sergius_II_of_Naples" title="Sergius II of Naples">Sergius II</a>, did not. He persecuted Athanasius, who was opposed to the alliance with the <a href="/wiki/Aghlabids" title="Aghlabids">Aghlabids</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and took possession of the treasures of the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 870 Duke Sergius II imprisoned his uncle <a href="/wiki/Caesar_of_Naples" title="Caesar of Naples">Caesar</a>, admiral of the Neapolitan fleet, who similarly opposed his closeness to the Aghlabids, Caesar died in prison.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bishop was exiled to a small island, only to be rescued by <a href="/wiki/Amalfi" title="Amalfi">Amalfitan</a> ships sent by the Emperor Louis. While travelling to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Athanasius died at Veroli, and was buried at Monte Cassino. His body was soon afterwards translated to the Cathedral of Naples.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He is regarded among Roman Catholics as the co-<a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the city of Naples, and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 15 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A <i>vita</i> of Athanasius was written by <a href="/wiki/John_the_Deacon_(Neapolitan_historian)" title="John the Deacon (Neapolitan historian)">John the Deacon</a> in 872.
</p>
