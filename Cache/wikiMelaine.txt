					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">6th-century Bishop of Rennes</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the Bishop of Rennes. For the 4th-century, possibly legendary, Bishop of Rouen, see <a href="/wiki/Mellonius" title="Mellonius">Mellonius</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Rennes_(35)_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg/220px-Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg/330px-Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg/440px-Rennes_%2835%29_M%C3%A9tropole_Saint-Pierre_-_Int%C3%A9rieur_-_Procession_des_saints_de_Bretagne_-_Saint_Amand_et_Saint_Melaine.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="1000" /></a><figcaption>Detail of a fresco in Rennes Cathedral representing St Melaine (right) with Amand of Rennes (left)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint <b>Melaine</b> (Latin: <b>Melanius</b> or <b>Mellanus</b>; Breton: <b>Melani</b>; Cornish: <b>Melan</b>; Welsh: <b>Mellon</b>) was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rennes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rennes">Bishop of Rennes</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> (now in France).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Traditional_history">Traditional history</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Melaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Traditional history">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Melaine grew up at Plaz in <a href="/wiki/La_Chapelle-de-Brain" title="La Chapelle-de-Brain">Brain</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Redon,_Ille-et-Vilaine" title="Redon, Ille-et-Vilaine">Redon</a>. He was a pious child, often being punished for spending too long at his prayers. He became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and then <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. He was nominated the successor to Bishop Amand of Rennes. Traditions recounted by <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Baring-Gould</a> state that on the death of Amand, he was compelled by the local population to become the next Bishop, accepting the role with great reluctance; that he performed many miracles and put an end to heathen practices; and that following his death at La Vilaine, his body was placed on a boat which then returned to Rennes against the current without the assistance of rowers or sails.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (However, <a href="/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" title="Louis Duchesne">Louis Duchesne</a> is of opinion that the <a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a> reckoned among the bishops of Rennes at the end of the fifth century is the same as Amand of Rodez. He therefore excludes him from his list of authentic bishops of Rennes.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>)
</p><p>During his rule, <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis</a> took over the area and Melaine became his trusted advisor.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He opposed immigration from <a href="/wiki/Sub-Roman_Britain" title="Sub-Roman Britain">Britain</a> and attended the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="First Council of Orléans">First Council of Orléans</a> in 511. He died at Plaz in 530<sup id="cite_ref-monks_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Pro-cath%C3%A9drale_Notre-Dame-en-Saint-Melaine_de_Rennes" class="mw-redirect" title="Pro-cathédrale Notre-Dame-en-Saint-Melaine de Rennes">Abbey Church of Notre-Dame en Saint-Mélaine</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rennes" title="Rennes">Rennes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Doble_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doble-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Melaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Melaine quickly became revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, especially after the wooden tower above his grave burnt down and his <a href="/wiki/Tomb" title="Tomb">tomb</a> miraculously survived. He has three feast days: 6 November (death), 6 January (burial)<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (May 2023)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Melaine#https://nominis.cef.fr/contenus/saint/380/Saint-Melaine-de-Rennes.html_says_that_he_was_buried_Nov._6_and_elected_third_bishop_of_Rennes_Jan._6_(different_years!)._R._Martyrology_seems_to_imply_first_death_on_6_Jan.,_then_6_Nov." title="Talk:Melaine">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> and 11 October <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">(translation)</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, his feast is celebrated locally on 10 October rather than 11 October at <a href="/wiki/St_Mellons" title="St Mellons">St Mellons</a>, in modern-day Cardiff, though there is ambiguity over whether Melaine is the Saint '<a href="/wiki/Mellonius" title="Mellonius">Mellonius</a>' said to have been born there.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, he is the patron of the villages of <a href="/wiki/St_Mellion" title="St Mellion">St Mellion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mullion,_Cornwall" title="Mullion, Cornwall">Mullion</a>, where there is a tradition of his visit.<sup id="cite_ref-Doble_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Doble-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the English translation of the 1956 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, he is listed under 6 January with the citation: At Rennes, in France, St Melanius, Bishop and Confessor, who displayed innumerable virtues, and with his thoughts ever fixed on heaven, passed from the world in glory.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 2004 edition of the Roman Martyrology, Melaine is listed under 6 November, with the Latin name <i>Melánii</i>. He is mentioned as follows: 'At <i>Rhedónibus</i> (Rennes) in Brittany, bishop, who passed to God in the place called <i>Plácium</i> on the River <i>Vicenóniam</i> (<a href="/wiki/Vilaine" title="Vilaine">Vilaine</a>), where with his own hands he built a church and gathered a congregation of monks and servants of God'.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The abbey church of Notre-Dame-en-Saint-Melaine in Rennes was dedicated to him.
</p>
