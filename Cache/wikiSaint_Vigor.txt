<p><b>Saint Vigor</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Vigor, Vigeur</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vigor, Vigorus</i>) (died circa 537 AD) was a French bishop and <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> missionary.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Vigor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born into the nobility in <a href="/wiki/Artois" title="Artois">Artois</a>, he studied at <a href="/wiki/Arras" title="Arras">Arras</a> under <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vedast" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vedast">Saint Vedast</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father would not grant approval for him to become a priest, so he ran away from home, taking nothing with him, accompanied by an acolyte, Theodimir. Thereafter, he became a hermit preacher at <a href="/wiki/Reviers" title="Reviers">Reviers</a>, Calvados, and worked as a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>. Vigor was named <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bayeux">bishop of Bayeux</a> in 514.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He fervently opposed <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">paganism</a> and reputedly founded a monastery, later known as <a href="/wiki/Saint-Vigor-le-Grand" title="Saint-Vigor-le-Grand">Saint-Vigor-le-Grand</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Bayeux" title="Bayeux">Bayeux</a>, Normandy, he destroyed a pagan temple that was still in use and built a church on the grounds.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_vigor.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Saint_vigor.png/220px-Saint_vigor.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="431" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Saint_vigor.png/330px-Saint_vigor.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Saint_vigor.png/440px-Saint_vigor.png 2x" data-file-width="442" data-file-height="866" /></a><figcaption>Saint Vigor, Saint-Vigor-des-Monts (Manche)</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Vigor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vigor was venerated from an early date in Bayeux, where he had been bishop. His cult as a saint is attested in 834 by mention of a relic at <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans" title="Le Mans">Le Mans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Around 987 Vigor's relics came to the Abbey of Saint-Riquier in Ponthieu. <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ouen_Abbey,_Rouen" title="Saint-Ouen Abbey, Rouen">Saint-Ouen Abbey, Rouen</a> held a copy of the <i>Vita Vigoris</i>; John Howe dates the "vita"s composition to the beginning of the eleventh century.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1032 <a href="/wiki/Robert_the_Magnificent" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert the Magnificent">Robert the Magnificent</a>, Duke of Normandy, founded <a href="/wiki/Cerisy_Abbey" title="Cerisy Abbey">an abbey dedicated to Saint Vigor</a> on the site of an older monastery destroyed by the Normans during their invasion.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The successful <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest</a> brought his cult to England. His name appears in an eleventh-century breviary at Worcester, possibly introduced by bishop <a href="/wiki/Samson_(bishop_of_Worcester)" title="Samson (bishop of Worcester)">Samson</a> or <a href="/wiki/Theulf" title="Theulf">Theulf</a>, both of whom had been canons at Bayeux.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Two English churches have been dedicated to Vigor; one in <a href="/wiki/Fulbourn" title="Fulbourn">Fulbourn</a>, Cambridgeshire, the other in <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Vigor,_Stratton-on-the-Fosse" title="Church of St Vigor, Stratton-on-the-Fosse">Stratton-on-the-Fosse</a>, Somerset.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His feast falls on <a href="/wiki/All_Saints%27_Day" title="All Saints&#39; Day">All Saints' Day</a> (November 1), and as a result is often moved to another date. Saint Vigor is mentioned in the life (<i>vita</i>) of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paternus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Paternus">Saint Paternus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
