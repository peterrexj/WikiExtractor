<p>

<b>William Tirry</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Liam Tuiridh</i>) OSA (1609 – 12 May 1654) was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Order of Saint Augustine">Order of Saint Augustine</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Cromwellian_conquest_of_Ireland" title="Cromwellian conquest of Ireland">Cromwellian conquest of Ireland</a>. He was captured by the <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a> at <a href="/wiki/Fethard,_County_Tipperary" title="Fethard, County Tipperary">Fethard, County Tipperary</a> while continuing his priestly ministry covertly and was hanged at <a href="/wiki/Clonmel" title="Clonmel">Clonmel</a>, officially for <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England" title="Commonwealth of England">Commonwealth of England</a>, but in reality as part of <a href="/wiki/The_Protectorate" title="The Protectorate">The Protectorate</a>'s systematic <a href="/wiki/Religious_persecution" title="Religious persecution">religious persecution</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland" title="Catholic Church in Ireland">Catholic Church in Ireland</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> Friar William Tirry as one of the 24 officially recognized <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> in 1992.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Tirry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tirry was born into a well-to-do family of <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Norman" class="mw-redirect" title="Hiberno-Norman">Hiberno-Norman</a> merchants in <a href="/wiki/Cork_(city)" title="Cork (city)">Cork</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> in 1608,<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the son of Robert and Joan Tirry. He was named after his uncle, the elder William Tirry, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cork_and_Cloyne" title="Bishop of Cork and Cloyne">Bishop of Cork and Cloyne</a>. Tirry was the grandson of Edmond Terry, or Tirry, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_Cork" title="Lord Mayor of Cork">Lord Mayor of Cork</a>, and his wife Catherine Galway. His aunt Joan married <a href="/wiki/Dominick_Sarsfield,_1st_Viscount_Sarsfield" title="Dominick Sarsfield, 1st Viscount Sarsfield">Dominick Sarsfield, 1st Viscount Sarsfield</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Irish_Common_Pleas" class="mw-redirect" title="Chief Justice of the Irish Common Pleas">Chief Justice of the Irish Common Pleas</a>: their son William, the 2nd Viscount, played an important role in Tirry's life as his patron and protector.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Well-educated, he learned <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_Latin" title="Ecclesiastical Latin">Ecclesiastical Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Koine_Greek" title="Koine Greek">Koine Greek</a>, but also spoke the <a href="/wiki/Classical_Gaelic" title="Classical Gaelic">Classical Gaelic</a> <a href="/wiki/Literary_language" title="Literary language">literary language</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Munster_Irish" title="Munster Irish">Munster Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Vernacular" title="Vernacular">vernacular</a>. At the age of eighteen, he left to study for the priesthood in <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Europe" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Europe">Catholic Europe</a>. He is known to have studied for the priesthood first at the <a href="/wiki/Irish_College" title="Irish College">Irish College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a> and then at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Coll%C3%A8ge_des_Grand_Augustins&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Collège des Grand Augustins (page does not exist)">Collège des Grand Augustins</a> in Paris, and that he was ordained around 1634. He and then spent five years (1636–1641) in <a href="/wiki/Brussels,_Belgium" class="mw-redirect" title="Brussels, Belgium">Brussels, Belgium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his return to Ireland, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Augustinian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustinian Order">Augustinian Order</a> at <a href="/wiki/Red_Abbey,_Cork" title="Red Abbey, Cork">St. Austin's Abbey</a> in Cork City.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He then spent about four months working as secretary to his uncle, Bishop William Tirry. Although he relinquished this post to return to St Austin's Abbey, he was then persuaded to act as chaplain to his cousin Lord Kilmallock and as tutor to the latter's son and heir.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Tirry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tirry was elected Provincial Secretary in 1646. Lord Sarsfield of Kilmallock's death in 1648 deprived Tirry of his chief protector. In 1649, he was chosen as <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the Augustinian convent in <a href="/wiki/Skreen" title="Skreen">Skreen</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Mayo" title="County Mayo">County Mayo</a>, but was unable to assume his duties there because of the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Cromwellian_conquest_of_Ireland" title="Cromwellian conquest of Ireland">Cromwellian conquest of Ireland</a>. A law was enacted on 6 January 1653 declaring that any Roman Catholic priest in Ireland was guilty of high treason. Tirry was accordingly forced into hiding like all other priests. 
</p><p>All sources are agreed that for three years prior to his capture, Friar William Tirry found shelter at <a href="/wiki/Fethard,_County_Tipperary" title="Fethard, County Tipperary">Fethard, County Tipperary</a>. There, he stayed with his distant cousin, a local <a href="/wiki/Old_English_(Ireland)" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English (Ireland)">Old English</a> noblewoman and elderly widow named Mrs. Amy Everard of <a href="/wiki/Fethard,_County_Tipperary" title="Fethard, County Tipperary">Fethard, County Tipperary</a>. Aside from acting as Mrs. Everard's son's tutor and continuing his covert priestly ministry, "to all who came seeking the sacraments", Friar William spent most of his time in secret prayer and acts of penance.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_Execution">Imprisonment and Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Tirry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After being arrested at <a href="/wiki/Fethard,_County_Tipperary" title="Fethard, County Tipperary">Fethard</a> while vested for <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Mass" title="Tridentine Mass">Mass</a> on <a href="/wiki/Holy_Saturday" title="Holy Saturday">Holy Saturday</a>, <a href="/wiki/25_March" class="mw-redirect" title="25 March">25 March</a>, 1654, Fr. Tirry was taken to Clonmel Gaol (on the future location of the <a href="/wiki/Clonmel_Borstal" title="Clonmel Borstal">Clonmel Borstal</a>) and held there pending trial. Three men had reported his whereabouts to the <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a> in return for the £5 bounty. On 26 April, he was tried by a jury and a panel of Commonwealth judges, including <a href="/wiki/New_Model_Army" title="New Model Army">New Model Army</a> Colonel <a href="/wiki/Solomon_Richards_(soldier)" title="Solomon Richards (soldier)">Solomon Richards</a>, for violating the Proclamation of 6 January 1653, which defined it as <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_of_England" title="Commonwealth of England">Commonwealth of England</a> for <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic priest">priests</a> to remain in Great Britain or Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Like all other <a href="/wiki/British_subject" title="British subject">British subjects</a> tried for the same offence prior to the <a href="/wiki/Treason_Act_1695" title="Treason Act 1695">Treason Act 1695</a>, William Tirry and his codefendant, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a> Matthew Fogarty, were forbidden the services of a defence counsel and were forced to act as their own attorneys.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his own defense, Fr. Tirry replied that while he viewed the Commonwealth as the lawful Government, he had no choice but to disobey it's laws, as both the Pope and his Augustinian superiors had ordered him to remain in Ireland and to continue his pastoral ministry covertly. Fr. Tirry was according found guilty and sentenced to <a href="/wiki/Death_by_hanging" class="mw-redirect" title="Death by hanging">death by hanging</a>, which was carried out in <a href="/wiki/Clonmel" title="Clonmel">Clonmel</a> on 2 May 1654.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Friar Matthew Fogarty later recalled: "William, wearing his Augustinian habit, was led to the gallows praying the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a>. He blessed the crowd which had gathered, pardoned his betrayers and affirmed his faith. It was a moving moment for Catholics and Protestants alike."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite the efforts of a Puritan minister to silence him, Fr. Tirry told the assembled crowd, "there is only one true Church, whose head is the pope: Pope and Church are to be obeyed. He publicly forgave the three men who had betrayed him, and... stated explicitly that he had been offered life and favour, it would renounce his religion." Fr. Tirry was then hanged, after which the crowd surged forward to soak pieces of cloth in the blood that ran from his nose, which were seen as relics of a martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Friar William was then buried, with some ceremony, in the ruins of the Augustinian friary in nearby Fethard. The evidence is that he was buried in the grounds, rather than inside the ruins of the church, but it has not yet been possible to locate his grave.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Friar Matthew Fogarty was later released and banished from the Commonwealth.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Tirry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Filippo_Visconti_(bishop)" title="Filippo Visconti (bishop)">Filippo Visconti</a>, the Augustinian Prior General, was interested enough in the details of Fr. William Tirry's life and death to order the Irish Provincial, James O'Mahoney, to publish an account of Tirry's martyrdom in <i>Sanguinea Eremus</i> in 1655. His name drew little attention outside the Augustinian Order, however, until the revival of interest in the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> following <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Emancipation">Catholic Emancipation</a> in 1829.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tirry's Cause was submitted to Rome in 1904, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> authorized the introduction of his cause for <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonization</a> in 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Friar William Tirry was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> along with 16 other <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> on 22 September 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Augustinian order celebrates his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 12 May.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
