<p><b>Pontianus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Pontianus</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Ponziano</i>) (alternatively anglicized as <b>Pontian</b>) was a second century <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He was martyred during the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>. He is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>,  the <a href="/wiki/Old_Catholic_Church" title="Old Catholic Church">Old Catholic Church</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>, Italy, he is invoked for protection against earthquakes.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">Passio</a></i> preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Spoleto">Cathedral of Spoleto</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FSP_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FSP-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Pontianus was a young man from a local noble family of Spoleto, 18 years of age, who had been denounced as a Christian to the Roman authorities. Brought before a judge named Flavian, he chose torture and death rather than renounce his faith. He was condemned to death and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on 14 January 175.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Basilica_of_San_Ponziano">Basilica of San Ponziano</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Basilica of San Ponziano">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pontianus' body was buried in the local cemetery, called <i>di Sincleta</i>, outside the city walls. The <a href="/wiki/San_Ponziano,_Spoleto" title="San Ponziano, Spoleto">Basilica of San Ponziano</a> was eventually built over his grave as a shrine to his memory.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He has become the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of that city.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A monastery was built attached to the basilica for a community of <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> to administer it. Over the centuries, the monks were replaced by nuns of the same order. The monastery was suppressed in 1810 during the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_invasion_of_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleonic invasion of Italy">occupation of Italy by Napoleonic forces</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Every year a festival is held in the city to honor Pontianus, its patron. Various services are held starting on the eve of the feast, which culminate in a procession through the streets of the city. Pontianus' skull, preserved in the basilica, is processed for <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> by the people of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-FSP_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FSP-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today the complex of basilica and monastery is operated by a community of <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular_of_the_Lateran" title="Canons Regular of the Lateran">Canonesses Regular of the Lateran</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The canonesses operate the ancient monastery as a religious guesthouse, open to all.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_saint_of_Utrecht">Patron saint of Utrecht</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patron saint of Utrecht">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Devotion to Pontianus grew throughout the region. In 966, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Baldrick_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Baldrick (disambiguation)">Balderic of Utrecht</a> travelled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to present an account of his administration, as required by Church law. He took the opportunity to tour various churches and monasteries, from which he obtained numerous <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of the saints for the churches of his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a>. In the course of this journey, he obtained one of Pontianus' arms, which he had enshrined in his cathedral. As a result, Pontianus was named a co-patron of the diocese.
</p><p>At the time that the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation#Netherlands" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> took hold in <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a>, <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> mobs attacked the Catholic cathedral there. Members of the Old Catholic Church, headquartered in that city, sought to protect his relics, which they were given and then preserved.
</p><p>In 1994 the <a href="/wiki/Primate_(bishop)" title="Primate (bishop)">Primate</a> of the Old Catholic Church returned this relic in a solemn manner to the <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of the monastery which administers the basilica.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Protection_against_earthquakes">Protection against earthquakes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Protection against earthquakes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Spoleto, Pontianus is invoked for protection against earthquakes. This developed from an ancient tradition that, before his death, the young martyr had predicted that "Spoleto will shake but not will collapse".
</p><p>In 1703, the first of a <a href="/wiki/1703_Apennine_earthquakes" title="1703 Apennine earthquakes">series of devastating earthquakes</a> occurred on his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>. It affected the entire region of Italy, lasting nearly three weeks. While thousands of people died as a result, there were no deaths in Spoleto.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Artwork">Artwork</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontianus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Artwork">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pontianus is one of the 140 saints whose statues adorn <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a> on top of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square#The_Colonades" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">Bernini's colonnades</a>.
