<p>Saint <b>Gontrand</b> (c. 532 in <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a> – 28 March 592 in <a href="/wiki/Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Chalon-sur-Saône">Chalon-sur-Saône</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also called <b>Gontran</b>, <b>Gontram</b>, <b>Guntram</b>, <b>Gunthram</b>, <b>Gunthchramn</b>, and <b>Guntramnus</b>, was the king of the Kingdom of Orléans from AD 561 to AD 592.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was the third eldest and second eldest surviving son of <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_I" title="Chlothar I">Chlothar I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ingunda" class="mw-redirect" title="Ingunda">Ingunda</a>. On his father's death in 561, he became king of a fourth of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the Franks">Kingdom of the Franks</a>, and made his capital at <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The name "Gontrand" denotes "<a href="/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_ravens" title="Cultural depictions of ravens">War Raven</a>".
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guntram&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>King Gontrand had something of that fraternal love which his brothers lacked; the preeminent chronicler of the period, <a href="/wiki/St._Gregory_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory of Tours</a>, often called him "good king Gontrand", as noted in the quotation below from the former's <i>Decem Libri Historiarum</i>, in which St. Gregory discussed the fate of Gontrand's three marriages:
</p>
<blockquote><p>The good king Gontrand first took a concubine Veneranda, a slave belonging to one of his people, by whom he had a son Gundobad. Later he married Marcatrude, daughter of Magnar, and sent his son Gundobad to <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>. But after she had a son Marcatrude was jealous, and proceeded to bring about Gundobad's death. She sent poison, they say, and poisoned his drink. And upon his death, by God's judgment she lost the son she had and incurred the hate of the king, was dismissed by him, and died not long after. After her he took <a href="/wiki/Austregilde" title="Austregilde">Austerchild</a>, also named Bobilla. He had by her two sons, of whom the older was called Clothar and the younger Chlodomer.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Gontrand had a period of intemperance. He was eventually overcome with remorse for the sins of his past life, and spent his remaining years repenting of them, both for himself and for his nation. In atonement, he fasted, prayed, wept, and offered himself to God. Throughout the balance of his prosperous reign he attempted to govern by Christian principles. According to <a href="/wiki/St._Gregory_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory of Tours</a>, he was the protector of the oppressed, caregiver to the sick, and the tender parent to his subjects. He was generous with his wealth, especially in times of plague and famine. He strictly and justly enforced the law without respect to person, yet was ever ready to forgive offences against himself, including two attempted assassinations. Gontrand munificently built and endowed many churches and monasteries. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory</a> related that the king performed many miracles both before and after his death, some of which St. Gregory claimed to have witnessed himself.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Politics">Politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guntram&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg/150px-Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="476" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg/225px-Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg/300px-Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg 2x" data-file-width="882" data-file-height="2796" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_de_saint_Gontran.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Later statue depicting Gontrand</div></div></div>
<p>In 567, his elder brother <a href="/wiki/Charibert_I" title="Charibert I">Charibert I</a> died and his lands of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Paris">Kingdom of Paris</a> were divided between the surviving brothers: Gontrand, <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_I" title="Sigebert I">Sigebert I</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Chilperic_I" title="Chilperic I">Chilperic I</a>. They shared his realm, agreeing at first to hold <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> in common. Charibert's widow, Theudechild, proposed a marriage with Gontrand, the eldest remaining brother, though a council convened at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> as late as 557 had forbidden such tradition as incestuous. Gontrand decided to house her more safely, though unwillingly, in a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a>.
</p><p>In 573, Gontrand was caught in a <a href="/wiki/Civil_war" title="Civil war">civil war</a> with his brother Sigebert I of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>, and in 575 summoned the aid of their brother Chilperic I of <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>. He reversed his allegiance later, due to the character of Chilperic, if we may give him the benefit of the doubt in light of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory's</a> commendation, and Chilperic retreated.  He thereafter remained an ally of Sigebert, his wife, and his sons until his death. When Sigebert was assassinated later in 575, Chilperic invaded the kingdom, but Gontrand sent his general <a href="/wiki/Mummolus" title="Mummolus">Mummolus</a>, who was always Gontrand's greatest weapon, for he was the greatest general in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> at the time, to remove him. Mummolus defeated Chilperic's general <a href="/wiki/Desiderius_of_Aquitaine" title="Desiderius of Aquitaine">Desiderius</a> and the Neustrian's forces retreated from <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>.
</p><p>In 577, Chlothar and Clodomir, his two surviving children, died of <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a> and he adopted as his son and heir <a href="/wiki/Childebert_II" title="Childebert II">Childebert II</a>, his nephew, Sigebert's son, whose kingdom he had saved two years prior. However, Childebert did not always prove faithful to his uncle. In 581, Chilperic took many of Gontrand's cities and in 583, he allied with Childebert and attacked Gontrand.  This time Gontrand made peace with Chilperic and Childebert retreated. In 584, he returned Childebert's infidelity by invading his land and capturing <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a> and <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, but he had to leave to attend the <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">Baptism</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a>, his other nephew, who now ruled in Neustria. Supposed to take place on 4 July, the feast of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">St. Martin of Tours</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>, it did not and Gontrand turned to invade <a href="/wiki/Septimania" title="Septimania">Septimania</a>. Peace was soon made.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:227px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG/225px-Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG" decoding="async" width="225" height="247" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG/338px-Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG/450px-Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="562" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gontran_et_Childebert_II.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. King Gontrand and Childebert II, from the <i><a href="/wiki/Grandes_Chroniques_de_France" title="Grandes Chroniques de France">Grandes Chroniques de France</a></i>.</div></div></div>
<p>In 584 or 585, one <a href="/wiki/Gundowald" class="mw-redirect" title="Gundowald">Gundowald</a> claimed to be an illegitimate son of <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_I" title="Chlothar I">Chlothar I</a> and proclaimed himself king, taking some major cities in southern <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> and <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>, which belonged to Gontrand. Gontrand marched against him, calling him nothing more than a miller's son named Ballomer. Gundowald fled to <a href="/wiki/Comminges" title="Comminges">Comminges</a> and Gontrand's army proceeded to besiege the citadel. He could not capture it, but did not need to: Gundowald's followers gave him over and he was executed.
</p><p>In 587, <a href="/wiki/Fredegund" title="Fredegund">Fredegund</a> attempted to assassinate him, but failed.  He went, on 28 November, to <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a> to conclude a treaty with Childebert; <a href="/wiki/Brunhilda_of_Austrasia" title="Brunhilda of Austrasia">Brunhilda</a>, his sister-in-law, Sigebert's wife, whose ally he had always been; Chlodosind, Childebert's sister; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Faileuba&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Faileuba (page does not exist)">Faileuba</a>, Childebert's queen; <a href="/wiki/Magneric" class="mw-redirect" title="Magneric">Magneric</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trier">Bishop of Trier</a>; and Ageric, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Verdun" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Verdun">Bishop of Verdun</a>. This was called the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Andelot" title="Treaty of Andelot">Treaty of Andelot</a> and it endured until Gontrand died.
</p><p>Also in 587, Gontrand compelled obedience from <a href="/wiki/Waroch_II" title="Waroch II">Waroch II</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a> ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Vannetais" class="mw-redirect" title="Vannetais">Vannetais</a>. He forced the renewal of the oath of 578 in writing and demanded 1,000 <i><a href="/wiki/Solidi" class="mw-redirect" title="Solidi">solidi</a></i> in compensation for raiding the <a href="/wiki/Nantais" class="mw-redirect" title="Nantais">Nantais</a>. In 588, the compensation was not yet paid, as Waroch promised it to both Gontrand and <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a>, who probably had suzerainty over Vannes.
</p><p>In 589 or 590, Gontrand sent an expedition against Waroch under Beppolem and Ebrachain, mutual enemies. Ebrachain was also enemy of Fredegund, who sent the <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bayeux" title="Bayeux">Bayeux</a> to aid Waroch.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Beppolem fought alone for three days before dying, at which point Waroch tried to flee to the <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>, but Ebrachain destroyed his ships and forced him to accept a peace,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> the renewal of the oath, and the surrender of a nephew as a hostage. This was all to no effect. The Bretons maintained their independent mindedness.
</p><p>In 589, Gontrand made a final advance on <a href="/wiki/Septimania" title="Septimania">Septimania</a>, to no avail. He fought against the barbarians who menaced the kingdom and quelled a rebellion of his niece <a href="/wiki/Basina,_daughter_of_Chilperic" class="mw-redirect" title="Basina, daughter of Chilperic">Basina</a> at a Poitevin monastery with the aid of many of his bishops in 590.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guntram&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He died at <a href="/wiki/Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Chalon-sur-Saône">Chalon-sur-Saône</a> in 592, and his nephew Childebert II succeeded him.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Marcellus_of_Chalons&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Marcellus of Chalons (page does not exist)">Church of Saint Marcellus</a>, which he had founded in <a href="/wiki/Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Chalon-sur-Saône">Chalon</a>. Almost immediately, his subjects proclaimed Gontrand a saint and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> celebrates his feast day on 28 March. The <a href="/wiki/Huguenot" class="mw-redirect" title="Huguenot">Huguenots</a>, who scattered his ashes in the 16th century, left only his skull untouched in their fury. It is now kept there in a silver case.
</p>
