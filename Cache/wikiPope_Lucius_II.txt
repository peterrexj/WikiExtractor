<p><b>Pope Lucius II</b> (died 15 February 1145), born <b>Gherardo Caccianemici dal Orso</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 9 March 1144 to his death in 1145. His pontificate was notable for the unrest in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> associated with the <a href="/wiki/Commune_of_Rome" title="Commune of Rome">Commune of Rome</a> and its attempts to wrest control of the city from the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a>. He supported <a href="/wiki/Empress_Matilda" title="Empress Matilda">Empress Matilda</a>'s claim to England in <a href="/wiki/The_Anarchy" title="The Anarchy">the Anarchy</a>, and had a tense relationship with King <a href="/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily" title="Roger II of Sicily">Roger II of Sicily</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Lucius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gherardo Caccianemici dal Orso, the son of Orso Caccianemici<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._114_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._114-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was born in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was for many years a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Frediano" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di San Frediano">Basilica di San Frediano</a><sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> before his elevation by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_II" title="Pope Honorius II">Pope Honorius II</a> to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal priest">cardinal priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Croce_in_Gerusalemme" title="Santa Croce in Gerusalemme">Santa Croce in Gerusalemme</a> in 1124.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During this time there he renovated the basilica, attached a body of regular canons and improved its revenue stream.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._114_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._114-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After he was elevated as pope, he presented to the church a copy of the <a href="/wiki/Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospels">Gospels</a> bound with plates of gold and adorned with jewels, as well as an altar-cover and two chased silver-gilt ampullae for use at <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._115_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._115-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Honorius also appointed him the librarian of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rome" title="Diocese of Rome">Diocese of Rome</a><sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._114_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._114-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> before appointing him <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> in 1125.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While there, he helped support the candidacy of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Lothair_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Lothair III, Holy Roman Emperor">Lothair III</a><sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> as well as appointing Saint <a href="/wiki/Norbert_of_Xanten" title="Norbert of Xanten">Norbert of Xanten</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg" title="Magdeburg">Magdeburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1128, Gherardo was sent to <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a> to govern the city, which had overthrown the previous rector.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1130 he was again appointed legate to Germany by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II" title="Pope Innocent II">Pope Innocent II</a>, where he was instrumental in convincing Lothair III to make two expeditions to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> for the purpose of protecting Pope Innocent II against the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Anacletus_II" title="Antipope Anacletus II">Antipope Anacletus II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had a further period as legate to Germany in 1135–36.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was one of the principal negotiators with Lothair III in attempting to force the monks of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a> to submit themselves to the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In addition, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a> to negotiate the end of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_schism_of_1130" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal schism of 1130">schism</a> involving Anacletus II with King <a href="/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily" title="Roger II of Sicily">Roger II of Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As a principal supporter of Pope Innocent II,<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the pope rewarded him for his efforts by appointing him papal <a href="/wiki/Chancery_of_Apostolic_Briefs" class="mw-redirect" title="Chancery of Apostolic Briefs">chancellor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._115_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._115-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After the <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1144" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1144">papal election of 1144</a>, Gherardo was <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">elected</a> as Lucius II and consecrated on 12 March 1144.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._91_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._91-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He probably took his name in honor of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_I" title="Pope Lucius I">Pope Lucius I</a>, who was commemorated a few days prior to Gherardo's consecration.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._959_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._959-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Lucius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_England_and_Portugal">Relations with England and Portugal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Lucius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Relations with England and Portugal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Lucius was involved in the usual running of church business throughout medieval <a href="/wiki/Christendom" title="Christendom">Christendom</a>. In <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, he granted a number of privileges to bishops, <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> and churches, including exempting the monastery of St. Edmund from all subjection to the secular authorities.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._119_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._119-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He also dispatched a papal legate, Igmarus (or Hincmar), to England, charged to investigate the request of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_(Bishop_of_St_David%27s)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernard (Bishop of St David&#39;s)">Bernard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_St_David%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of St David&#39;s">Bishop of St David's</a>, to elevate his see to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan bishop</a>, and to take the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> to Archbishop <a href="/wiki/William_of_York" title="William of York">William of York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._119_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._119-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Regarding the political situation in England, he took the side of the <a href="/wiki/Empress_Matilda" title="Empress Matilda">Empress Matilda</a> over the rights to the English crown.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Early in his reign, Lucius received a request from prominent members of the town of <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a> to become the <a href="/wiki/Suzerain" class="mw-redirect" title="Suzerain">suzerain</a> of the castle within the town in order to protect it from the war between Lucca and <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>. Lucius received it on 18 March 1144 and, for a payment of ten pounds of gold, agreed to defend it on his behalf. Lucius then returned the castle to them as a <a href="/wiki/Fief" title="Fief">fief</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._121_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._121-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Meanwhile, in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, King <a href="/wiki/Afonso_I_of_Portugal" title="Afonso I of Portugal">Afonso I</a>, eager to maintain the newly established independence of Portugal from the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Kingdom of León">Kingdom of León</a>, offered to do <a href="/wiki/Homage_(feudal)" title="Homage (feudal)">homage</a> to Lucius, as he had done to Pope Innocent II, and to make the pope the feudal suzerain of his lands. He offered Lucius his territory and a yearly tribute of four ounces of gold in exchange for the defence and support of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic See">Apostolic See</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._121_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._121-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Although Lucius accepted Afonso's feudal homage on 1 May 1144, and excused him from appearing in person, he did not acknowledge Afonso as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Portugal">King of Portugal</a>, but instead as <i>Dux Portugallensis</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The royal title would eventually be conferred by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._123_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._123-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Finally, the city of <a href="/wiki/Tarquinia" title="Tarquinia">Corneto</a>, formally belonging to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, was restored to the papacy during Lucius’ pontificate by a formal deed on 20 November 1144.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._123_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._123-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Conflict_with_Roger_II_of_Sicily">Conflict with Roger II of Sicily</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Lucius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Conflict with Roger II of Sicily">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although Lucius had been the friend of King <a href="/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily" title="Roger II of Sicily">Roger II of Sicily</a> and <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godparent</a> to one of his children,<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._115_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._115-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> the situation between the two deteriorated. The two parties met at <a href="/wiki/Ceprano" title="Ceprano">Ceprano</a> in June 1144<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._116_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._116-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> to clarify the duties of Roger as a <a href="/wiki/Vassal" title="Vassal">vassal</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. Lucius demanded the return of the principality of <a href="/wiki/Capua" title="Capua">Capua</a>, while Roger instead wanted additional territory that formed part of the Papal States in the south.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._116_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._116-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Lucius II, on the advice of his cardinals,<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._92_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._92-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> was unwilling to accept Roger's demands and rejected them. Infuriated, King Roger returned to <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and asked his son Duke <a href="/wiki/Roger_III_of_Apulia" class="mw-redirect" title="Roger III of Apulia">Roger III of Apulia</a> to invade <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._116_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._116-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Duke Roger did as he was asked, and sent his general <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Selby" title="Robert of Selby">Robert of Selby</a> against Lucius, ravaging the country as far north as <a href="/wiki/Ferentino" title="Ferentino">Ferentino</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._116_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._116-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This forced the Romans to capitulate, and in September 1144, Lucius agreed to Roger's terms, negotiating a seven-year truce.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a> in return withdrew back to their conquered territories<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._116_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._116-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and promised not to attack <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a> or any other papal territory.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Emergence_of_the_Roman_Commune">Emergence of the Roman Commune</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Lucius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Emergence of the Roman Commune">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Commune_of_Rome" title="Commune of Rome">Commune of Rome</a></div>
<p>This surrender on the part of Lucius II gave an opportunity for members of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senate" title="Roman Senate">Roman Senate</a> to reassert their ancient independence and authority and to erect a <a href="/wiki/Revolutionary_republic" title="Revolutionary republic">revolutionary republic</a> at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> which sought to deprive the pope of his temporal power. The principal groups involved in this movement were the merchants and artisans, while the urban nobility kept their neutrality.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Senate, which practically took all temporal power from the pope during the pontificate of Innocent II, had been managed with considerable skill and firmness by Lucius at the beginning of his pontificate, convincing many senators to either leave the <a href="/wiki/Capitoline_Hill" title="Capitoline Hill">Capitoline Hill</a> or to lay down their <a href="/wiki/Magisterium" title="Magisterium">magisterium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._117_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._117-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Now, encouraged by Lucius II's defeat, the Senate, led by <a href="/wiki/Giordano_Pierleoni" title="Giordano Pierleoni">Giordano Pierleoni</a>, brother of the former <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Anacletus_II" title="Antipope Anacletus II">Antipope Anacletus II</a>, rebelled against Lucius II, driving out the papal prefects<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and establishing the <a href="/wiki/Commune_of_Rome" title="Commune of Rome">Commune of Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._117_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._117-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> They demanded the pope abandon all governmental duties, and that he would retain only ecclesiastical taxes and voluntary tributes.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Senate took over powers to elect magistrates and strike its own coinage.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> At first, Lucius asked for Roger II's aid, but Roger, still annoyed that Lucius had not fully recognised his kingship, withheld his assistance.<sup id="cite_ref-Levillain,_pg._960_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Levillain,_pg._960-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Lucius then turned for help to <a href="/wiki/Conrad_III_of_Germany" title="Conrad III of Germany">Conrad</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Romans" title="King of the Romans">King of the Romans</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._92_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._92-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and on December 1144 wrote to him pleading for military assistance against the Senate and the <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(post-Roman_Europe)" title="Patrician (post-Roman Europe)">Patrician</a> Giordano Pierleoni.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._117_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._117-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Lucius was supported by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>, who also wrote to Conrad, asking for him to intervene.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._118_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._118-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While waiting for Conrad's reply, Lucius decided to take matters into his own hands.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._92_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._92-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Turning to the Roman aristocracy, in particular the <a href="/wiki/Frangipani_family" title="Frangipani family">Frangipani family</a>, he gave them the fortress of the <a href="/wiki/Circus_Maximus" title="Circus Maximus">Circus Maximus</a> on 31 January 1145, allowing them complete control of the southern portion of the <a href="/wiki/Palatine_Hill" title="Palatine Hill">Palatine Hill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._118_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._118-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Forum" title="Roman Forum">Roman Forum</a> had become a battleground, and the confusion prevented Lucius from travelling to the <a href="/wiki/Aventine_Hill" title="Aventine Hill">Aventine Hill</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ordain" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordain">ordain</a> the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/San_Saba,_Rome" title="San Saba, Rome">San Saba</a> on 20 January 1145.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._118_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._118-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Finally, Lucius marched against the Senatorial positions on the Capitol with a small army. He was driven back by Giordano,<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._118_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._118-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and according to <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_of_Viterbo" title="Godfrey of Viterbo">Godfrey of Viterbo</a>, he was seriously injured during this battle (by a thrown stone).<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._118_15-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._118-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He did not recover from his injuries and died on 15 February 1145<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas,_pg._92_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas,_pg._92-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> at <a href="/wiki/San_Gregorio_Magno_al_Celio" title="San Gregorio Magno al Celio">San Gregorio Magno al Celio</a>, where he was under the protection of the neighbouring Frangipani fortress.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._119_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._119-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lucius II was buried at <a href="/wiki/St_John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="St John Lateran">St John Lateran</a> in the circular portico behind the apse.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._119_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._119-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Heraldic_badge" title="Heraldic badge">heraldic badge</a> was a <a href="/wiki/Escutcheon_(heraldry)" title="Escutcheon (heraldry)">shield</a> of argent, with a bear rampant of proper sable.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
