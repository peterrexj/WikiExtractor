<p><b>Galgano Guidotti</b> (1148 &#8211; 3 December 1181)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> was a Catholic saint from <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a> born in <a href="/wiki/Chiusdino" title="Chiusdino">Chiusdino</a>, in the modern <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Siena" title="Province of Siena">province of Siena</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. His mother's name was Dionigia, while his father's name (Guido or Guidotto) only appeared in a document dated in the 16th century, when the last name Guidotti was attributed.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> process to declare Galgano a saint started in 1185, only a few years after his death, and his canonization was the first conducted with a formal process by the Roman Church.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A lot of Galgano's life is known through the documents of the canonization process in 1185<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and other <i>Vitae: Legenda beati Galgani</i><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> by anonymous,  <i>Legenda beati Galgani confessoris</i> by an unknown Cistercian monk,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <i>Leggenda di Sancto Galgano</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <i>Vita sancti Galgani de Senis</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <i>Vita beati Galgani</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galgano_Guidotti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The son of a feudal lord, Galgano became a <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a>, and is said to have led a ruthless life in his early years.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Galgano died in 1181. Soon after, in 1184, a round chapel was built over his claimed tomb to commemorate him;<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> pilgrims came there in large numbers, and miracles were claimed. In that year, <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monks took over Montesiepi at the request of Hugh, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Volterra" title="Volterra">Volterra</a>, but most of Galgano's monks left, scattered over Tuscany, and became Augustinian hermits. By 1220, San Galgano Abbey, a large Cistercian monastery, had been built below Galgano's hermitage: he was then claimed and recognized as a Cistercian saint. His cult was lively in Siena and Volterra, where numerous representations survive. The ruins of his hermitage can still be seen, while his cloak is kept in the church of Santuccio at Siena.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_sword_in_the_stone">The sword in the stone</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galgano_Guidotti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The sword in the stone">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG/220px-San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG/330px-San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG/440px-San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Galgano_Spada_nella_roccia.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The sword in the stone at Montesiepi Chapel</div></div></div>
<p>The sword in the stone can be seen at the Rotonda at Montesiepi, near the ruins of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_San_Galgano" title="Abbey of San Galgano">Abbey of San Galgano</a>. The handle of a sword protrudes from a stone, and is said to be the sword of Galgano. An analysis of the metal done in 2001<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> by Luigi Garlaschelli confirmed that the "composition of the metal and the style are compatible with the era of the legend". The analysis also confirmed that the upper piece and the invisible lower one are authentic and belong to one and the same artifact.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_media">In the media</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galgano_Guidotti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In the media">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Television">Television</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Galgano_Guidotti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Television">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Galgano's "sword in the stone" story was featured in a season 7 episode of TV series, <i><a href="/wiki/Forged_in_Fire_(TV_series)" class="mw-redirect" title="Forged in Fire (TV series)">Forged in Fire</a></i>. Bladesmiths had to recreate "Excalibur", a medieval <a href="/wiki/Broadsword" class="mw-redirect" title="Broadsword">broadsword</a> possibly inspired by Galgano's story.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The episode explained the story as follows: the actual Sword in the Stone is located in <a href="/wiki/Siena,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Siena, Italy">Siena, Italy</a>, and believed to have belonged to Galgano.
</p><p>An episode of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ancient_X-files&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ancient X-files (page does not exist)">Ancient X-files</a> looked at the sword in the stone and the connections between it, and St Galgano, to Sir <a href="/wiki/Gawain" title="Gawain">Gawain</a> and King Arthur. It included some scientific exploration of the stone. <sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
