font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Daniel and Companions</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Martyrdom daniele fasanella.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg/220px-Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="158" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg/330px-Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg/440px-Martyrdom_daniele_fasanella.jpg 2x" data-file-width="620" data-file-height="444" /></a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Franciscan Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="dday deathdate">1227-10-10</span>)</span>October 10, 1227<br /><a href="/wiki/Ceuta" title="Ceuta">Ceuta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1516 by <a href="/wiki/Leo_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo X">Leo X</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">October 13</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Daniel and companions</b> were seven <a href="/wiki/Friars_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Friars Minor">Friars Minor</a> martyred at <a href="/wiki/Ceuta" title="Ceuta">Ceuta</a> on 10 October 1227, according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_the_Twenty-Four_Generals_of_the_Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Chronicle of the Twenty-Four Generals of the Order of Friars Minor">Chronicle of the Twenty-Four Generals of the Order of Friars Minor</a></i> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1370</span>). Their story is likely a legend or fabrication. The names of Daniel's companions are borrowed from the authentic account of the martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Berard_of_Carbio" title="Berard of Carbio">Berard of Carbio</a> and his companions in 1220.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daniel and his companions are venerated as saints by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Chalippe2007_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chalippe2007-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Story">Story</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_and_companions&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Story">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Berard_of_Carbio" title="Berard of Carbio">Berard of Carbio</a> and his companions in 1219 had inflamed many of the religious of the Order of Friars Minor with the desire of preaching the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">gospel</a> in non-Christian lands; and in 1227, the year following <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>'s death, six religious of <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, <b>Agnellus</b> (Agnello), <b>Samuel</b>, <b>Donulus</b>, <b>Leo</b>, <b>Hugolinus</b> (Ugolino), and <b>Nicholas</b>, petitioned <a href="/wiki/Elias_of_Cortona" title="Elias of Cortona">Elias of Cortona</a>, then vicar-general of the Order, for permission to preach the gospel to the <a href="/wiki/Muslims" title="Muslims">Muslims</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate" title="Almohad Caliphate">Maghreb</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The six missionaries went first to <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, where they were joined by <b>Daniel</b>, Minister Provincial of <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, who became their superior. They set sail from Spain and on 20 September reached the coast of <a href="/wiki/Africa" title="Africa">Africa</a>, where they remained for a few days in a small village inhabited mostly by Christian merchants just beyond the walls of the <a href="/wiki/Saracen" title="Saracen">Saracen</a> city of Ceuta.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Finally, very early on Sunday morning, they entered the city, and immediately began to <a href="/wiki/Evangelism" title="Evangelism">preach the gospel</a> and to <a href="/wiki/Criticism_of_Islam" title="Criticism of Islam">denounce Islam</a>. They were soon apprehended and brought before the <a href="/wiki/Sultan" title="Sultan">sultan</a> who, thinking that they were mad, ordered them to be cast into prison. Here they remained until the following Sunday when they were again brought before the sultan, who, by promises and threats, endeavoured in vain to make them deny the Christian religion. They were all <a href="/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Islam" title="Capital punishment in Islam">executed under the Islamic death penalty</a>. Each one approached Daniel, the superior, to ask his <a href="/wiki/Blessing" title="Blessing">blessing</a> and permission to die for Christ. They were all <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Daniel and his companions were canonized by <a href="/wiki/Leo_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo X">Leo X</a> in 1516. Their feast is kept in the Franciscan Order on the thirteenth of October.
</p>
