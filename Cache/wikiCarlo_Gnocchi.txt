<p><b>Carlo Gnocchi</b> (25 October 1902&#160;&#8211;&#32;28 February 1956) was an <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, educator and writer. He is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blessed</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>During <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, he was a <a href="/wiki/Military_chaplain" title="Military chaplain">military chaplain</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a>, the elite mountain warfare soldiers of the <a href="/wiki/Italian_Army" title="Italian Army">Italian Army</a>, and after the tragic experience of the war, he strove to ease the wounds of suffering and misery created by the war.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"Two of my sons you have already taken, Lord. The third I offer to You, so that You bless him and keep him always at Your service."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>Clementina Pasta, mother of Father Gnocchi <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Don_Gnocchi_giovane.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/Don_Gnocchi_giovane.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="225" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="180" data-file-height="225" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Don_Gnocchi_giovane.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Picture of young don Gnocchi</div></div></div>
<p>Gnocchi was born in <a href="/wiki/San_Colombano_al_Lambro" title="San Colombano al Lambro">San Colombano al Lambro</a>, a few miles from <a href="/wiki/Lodi,_Lombardy" title="Lodi, Lombardy">Lodi</a>, to Enrico Gnocchi and Clementina Pasta, a seamstress.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  The youngest of three brothers, when he was age five, he lost his father in 1907, from <a href="/wiki/Silicosis" title="Silicosis">silicosis</a>, caused by the unhealthy job as a <a href="/wiki/Marble" title="Marble">marble worker</a>.
</p><p>After moving to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> with family, he lost his two older brothers in a few years&#160;&#8211;&#32;Mario, in 1908, and Andrea, in 1915, from <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He grew up in a very devout and fervent environment, in the village of <a href="/wiki/Besana_in_Brianza" title="Besana in Brianza">Montesiro</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a>, where he often went to his relatives because of poor health. There, he was approached by the priest Luigi Ghezzi, who helped him in his choice to enter <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He received his <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> as priest in 1925 from <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> <a href="/wiki/Eugenio_Tosi" title="Eugenio Tosi">Eugenio Tosi</a>, and the same year he celebrated his first Mass in Montesiro.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Educator">Educator</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Educator">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"How nice it is to play in the snow when it is clean and white. Jesus also likes to play with the souls of the children when they are white and clean, but if they get spoiled, Jesus doesn't like them any more..."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>Carlo Gnocchi <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Gnocchi's first passion, since the first years of his priesthood, was the upbringing and education of the youth, by getting them closer to the Catholic Church and the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a>.
</p><p>First, he was entrusted to the parish of <a href="/wiki/Cernusco_sul_Naviglio" title="Cernusco sul Naviglio">Cernusco sul Naviglio</a> and then in 1926, the densely populated <a href="/w/index.php?title=San_Pietro_in_Sala&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="San Pietro in Sala (page does not exist)">San Pietro in Sala</a> of Milan, he continued his vocation for years, creating a deep bond with his parishioners.
</p><p>His fame as an educator came to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster" title="Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster">Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</a> who, in 1936,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> appointed him as spiritual director of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gonzaga_Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_Christian_Schools&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gonzaga Institute of the Brothers of Christian Schools (page does not exist)">Gonzaga Institute of the Brothers of Christian Schools</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_war">The war</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The war">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"I can say to have finally seen the man in those fatal days. The naked man, completely stripped, because of the violence of the events so much bigger than him, from any restraint and convention, in complete mercy of the most basic instincts emerged from the depths of being."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>Carlo Gnocchi, "Cristo con gli Alpini"</cite></div></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Reverberi_don_gnocchi.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Reverberi_don_gnocchi.jpg/220px-Reverberi_don_gnocchi.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="255" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/be/Reverberi_don_gnocchi.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="248" data-file-height="287" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Reverberi_don_gnocchi.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Father Gnocchi in departure for the Russian front, in the company of general Luigi Reverberi</div></div></div>
<p>At the end of the 1930s, Gnocchi was named chaplain of the second legion of Milan, composed of students of the <a href="/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_Cattolica_del_Sacro_Cuore" title="Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore">Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore</a> and the Gonzaga Institute. His mother, to whom he was very attached, died in 1939. At the start of World War II, Gnocchi volunteered in the <i>Val Tagliamento</i> battalion of the Alpini, sent to the <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greek</a>&#8211;<a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albanian</a> front. After the <a href="/wiki/Balkans_Campaign_(World_War_II)" class="mw-redirect" title="Balkans Campaign (World War II)">Balkans Campaign</a> in 1941, in 1942 Gnocchi left for the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)" title="Eastern Front (World War II)">Russian front</a> as chaplain of the <a href="/wiki/2nd_Alpine_Division_Tridentina" class="mw-redirect" title="2nd Alpine Division Tridentina">2nd Alpine Division Tridentina</a>, where he participated in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Nikolayevka" title="Battle of Nikolayevka">Battle of Nikolayevka</a>. During the dire retreat in the Russian <a href="/wiki/Steppe" title="Steppe">steppe</a>, he nearly fell victim to the freezing cold: dazed by <a href="/wiki/Frostbite" title="Frostbite">frostbite</a>, he would certainly have met the fate of thousands of other Italian servicemen, had not <a href="/wiki/Physician" title="Physician">medical officer</a> Rolando Prada (an offspring of the <a href="/wiki/Prada" title="Prada">famous leather-working family</a>) recognized him and put him on a passing military sled.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Surviving the conflict, he gathered last wishes of the wounded, that he would bring back to Italy, in a journey all over the country, as a messenger for the families of the deceased. He went into the <a href="/wiki/Alpine_Valleys" title="Alpine Valleys">Alpine Valleys</a> to find the relatives of fallen comrades. After becoming part of the O.S.C.A.R., Catholic association for aid to refugees, he helped Jews and escaped Allied POWs flee to <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>. He wrote articles in the illegal magazine <i>Il Ribelle</i> (<i>Rebel</i>) and in the diocesan newspaper <i>L'Italia</i> (<i>Italy</i>). He was imprisoned more than once in the San Vittore prison, but obtained the liberation by the intervention of the archbishop of Milan, <a href="/wiki/Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster" title="Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster">Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</a>. In those years the idea arose to create a charitable center that would take care of the victims of this war, which in the future developed as the origin of the <i>Pro Juventute</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Postwar_period">Postwar period</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Postwar period">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the war, Gnocchi felt as his duty to aid that part of childhood that had been affected by the war the most. First, he directed his charitable work to the orphans of <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a>, housing them in Istituto Arosio (Institute Arosio) and subsequently devoted himself to the "mutilated" and to children invalids of war and civilians, establishing for them a vast network of colleges in many cities of Italy (<a href="/wiki/Inverigo" title="Inverigo">Inverigo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pessano_con_Bornago" title="Pessano con Bornago">Pessano con Bornago</a>, <a href="/wiki/Torino" class="mw-redirect" title="Torino">Torino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>, <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Firenze</a>, <a href="/wiki/Genova" class="mw-redirect" title="Genova">Genova</a>, ...); and, in the end, opened the doors of modern <i>Centri di rieducazione</i> ("Re-educations centers") for children affected by <a href="/wiki/Poliomyelitis" class="mw-redirect" title="Poliomyelitis">polio</a>. For this forsaken and handicapped children, to whom he had devoted all of his young life, Gnocchi dedicated one of his most significant writings: <i>Pedagogia del dolore innocente</i> (<i>Pedagogy of Innocent Suffering</i>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Juvenile_Foundation">Juvenile Foundation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Juvenile Foundation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"A dream, after the war, to be able to devote myself to a work of charity, whatever it is, or better the one that God would want from me."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>father Carlo Gnocchi</cite></div></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg/200px-Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="155" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg/300px-Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg/400px-Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg 2x" data-file-width="482" data-file-height="374" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Don_Gnocchi_mutilatini.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Father Gnocchi in the company of mutilated children</div></div></div>
<p>The dramatic experience of the retreat from Russia, lived as <a href="/wiki/Military_ordinariate" title="Military ordinariate">military ordinariate</a> always on the front, matured in Gnocchi the idea and the focus of his charitable mission; assisting the victims of war, in search of redemption for their "innocent suffering". In 1945, he was nominated as director of the <i>Istituto Grandi Invalidi</i> (Institute of Registered Invalids) of <a href="/wiki/Arosio" title="Arosio">Arosio</a>, thus accepting the first orphans and adults disabled by the war. In 1948, he founded the "Fondazione Pro Infanzia Mutilata" ("Foundation For Mutilated Infants"), acknowledged in the next year with a decree by the <a href="/wiki/President_of_Italy" title="President of Italy">President of Italy</a>. The same year, the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Italy" title="Prime Minister of Italy">Italian Prime Minister</a> <a href="/wiki/Alcide_De_Gasperi" title="Alcide De Gasperi">Alcide De Gasperi</a> nominated Gnocchi as adviser to the Presidency of the Council for the mutilated by the war. In 1951, the foundation was dissolved, and all its goods and structure were donated to the newly created <i>Fondazione Pro Juventute</i> (Juvenile Foundation).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"Thank you for everything..."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>Last words of Carlo Gnocchi <sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"...Others will serve them better that I knew and could do; no one else, perhaps, to love them more than I did."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>From Testament of Father Carlo Gnocchi<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:141px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Funerali_Don_Gnocchi.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Funerali_Don_Gnocchi.jpg" decoding="async" width="139" height="110" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="139" data-file-height="110" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Funerali_Don_Gnocchi.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Procession of the <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpines</a> and "mutilated" at the funeral of Father Gnocchi</div></div></div>
<p>He died in Milan, age 53, with a <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a> in his hands, given to him by his mother years before, and very beloved in his last hours.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Metastasis" title="Metastasis">metastasis</a> of the tumor that had stricken him had reached the skeleton and the respiratory system. Three crises that occurred between noon and the evening before had announced the fourth, fatal, and no one had delusions about his possible recovery anymore: Don Gnocchi's constitution had been too hardly tested and accompanied by suffering and fasting, as well as by the unrelenting advancing of the illness, even though his face, often smiling, deceived visitors. While dying, he donated his <a href="/wiki/Cornea" title="Cornea">corneas</a> to two blind children, guests of his foundations, <i>Silvio Coangrande</i> and <i>Amabile Battistello</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Long_road_to_beatification">Long road to beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Long road to beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"Before, I used to tell you: goodbye, Father Carlo. Now I tell you: goodbye, Saint Carlo."</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>A mutilated child, during the funeral held in the <a href="/wiki/Milan_Cathedral" title="Milan Cathedral">Milan Cathedral</a><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>After his death, there were many people and the believers who called for his help, say they received <i>graces</i> by the priest. On 17 August 1979, an <i>Alpino</i>, a specialist electrician by profession, from <a href="/wiki/Villa_d%27Adda" title="Villa d&#39;Adda">Villa d'Adda</a>, survived a serious accident at work. The <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> is attributed to Gnocchi, invoked by the victim. For these reasons, thirty years after his death, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">archbishop of Milan</a> <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Maria_Martini" title="Carlo Maria Martini">Carlo Maria Martini</a> established the <i>Processo sulla vita, virtù e fama di santità</i> (<i>Process on the life, virtues and reputation for holiness</i>) (Diocesan process) on 6 May 1987, that was concluded positively on 23 February 1991.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 199 sessions was held deposition of 178 texts and numerous documentation that was collected. This investigation material (for a total of 4321 pages) was presented to the Congregazione per le Cause dei Santi di Roma (Congregation for the Causes of Saints in Rome), where Brother Leo Luigi Morelli was appointed postulator of the cause of canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his death, which occurred in 2002, replaces brother Rodolfo Cosimo Meoli.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> After years of careful investigation and analysis, on 20 December 2002, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> declared him <i>venerable</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> On 17 January 2009, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>, with a papal decree recognized a miracle attributed to Gnocchi, a decisive step towards the beatification. On 2 March 2009, cardinal <a href="/wiki/Dionigi_Tettamanzi" title="Dionigi Tettamanzi">Dionigi Tettamanzi</a> announced the beatification for the 25 October 2009; the rite for beatification was presided by the archbishop of Milan, cardinal <a href="/wiki/Dionigi_Tettamanzi" title="Dionigi Tettamanzi">Dionigi Tettamanzi</a> in the presence of many ambrosian priests and bishops. Among these were the cardinal Prefect of the Congregation of Bishops <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Re" title="Giovanni Battista Re">Giovanni Battista Re</a>, the former Master of Pontifical Liturgical Celebrations Msgr. <a href="/wiki/Piero_Marini" title="Piero Marini">Piero Marini</a> and the Prefect of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>, bishop <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Awards">Awards</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlo_Gnocchi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Awards">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>"The debt to him will never be repaid."</i></p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><a href="/wiki/Giulio_Andreotti" title="Giulio Andreotti">Giulio Andreotti</a><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>His charitable work goes under the name of Juvenile Foundation Don Gnocchi, today known as Don Carlo Gnocchi - ONLUS Foundation, that was awarded in 2003 with gold medal of merit of Public Health.
</p>
